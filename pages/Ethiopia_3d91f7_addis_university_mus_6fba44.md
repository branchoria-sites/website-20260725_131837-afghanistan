## Introduction

Addis Ababa University contains two of [Ethiopia]({{ 'what-makes-ethiopias-collections-so/' | relative_url }})'s most distinctive collections, and together they offer an unusual way of understanding the country. One is the Ethnological Museum, housed inside the former palace of Emperor Haile Selassie, where royal rooms, ceremonial objects and everyday cultural artefacts are displayed side by side. The other is the Zoological Natural History Museum, a quieter but equally remarkable institution that preserves hundreds of mounted animals and scientific specimens documenting Ethiopia's wildlife. Rather than presenting curiosities for entertainment, these collections reveal how an academic institution has preserved both the cultural life of people and the biological life of the country, making them among Ethiopia's most compelling "cabinets of culture and wildlife".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnological_Museum%2C_Addis_Ababa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span></span></span>

## The imperial palace turned ethnological museum

The Ethnological Museum occupies the former Genete Leul Palace, once the residence of Emperor Haile Selassie before the building became the main campus of Addis Ababa University. The palace itself is part of the collection. Visitors move through imperial reception rooms, private apartments and ceremonial spaces while simultaneously exploring exhibitions about Ethiopia's many cultures. This unusual combination means the building functions both as a historic royal residence and as an academic museum dedicated to anthropology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnological_Museum%2C_Addis_Ababa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span></span></span>

Unlike many ethnographic museums that arrange objects by region or ethnic group, the permanent exhibition is organised around the stages of human life. Displays explore birth, childhood, adulthood, marriage, work, belief and death through artefacts gathered from communities across Ethiopia. Household objects, clothing, agricultural tools, jewellery, musical instruments, religious items and ceremonial objects are presented as parts of lived experience rather than isolated works of art. This structure encourages visitors to compare shared human experiences across Ethiopia's many cultural traditions instead of treating each community as entirely separate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.musicinafrica.net/directory/ethnographic-museum-addis-ababa-university?language=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musicinafrica.net">[Music In Africa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musicinafrica.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Music In Africa Ethnographic Museum (Addis Ababa University) &#124; Music In Africa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Music In AfricaEthnographic Museum (Addis Ababa University) &#124; Music In AfricaSeptember 2, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 2, 2015</span></span></span>

The museum also preserves elements of imperial history that would otherwise have disappeared. Haile Selassie's bedroom, personal furnishings, ceremonial robes and other royal possessions remain in place, allowing visitors to experience the palace as both a historic residence and a museum exhibit. Some traces of political upheaval have also been preserved, including damage associated with the failed 1960 coup attempt, giving the palace an atmosphere that combines royal memory with modern political history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/ethnological-museum/455951" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lonelyplanet.com">[Lonely Planet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lonelyplanet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lonely Planet Ethnological Museum &#124; Addis Ababa, Ethiopia &#124; Attractions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lonely PlanetEthnological Museum &#124; Addis Ababa, Ethiopia &#124; Attractions - Lonely Planet...</span></span></span>

Beyond anthropology, the museum forms part of the Institute of Ethiopian Studies and contains collections relating to traditional art, ethnomusicology, coins and postage stamps. Together these collections exceed 13,000 objects, making it one of Ethiopia's most important university museums and one of the earliest museums established within an Ethiopian university.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnological_Museum%2C_Addis_Ababa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span></span></span>

## Mounted wildlife and the national biodiversity record

Only a short distance away on another part of Addis Ababa University's campus is the Zoological Natural History Museum, a collection that receives far fewer visitors but plays an equally important role in preserving Ethiopia's heritage.

Established during the 1950s, the museum remains Ethiopia's only dedicated natural history museum. Its origins partly lie in zoological collections assembled during the Italian occupation and later expanded by university staff, biology students and visiting researchers working throughout the country. As the university's collections grew, the zoological specimens were separated from the ethnographic collections that eventually became today's Ethnological Museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.musicinafrica.net/directory/ethnographic-museum-addis-ababa-university?language=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musicinafrica.net">[musicinafrica.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musicinafrica.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Music In Africa Ethnographic Museum (Addis Ababa University) &#124; Music In Africa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Music In AfricaEthnographic Museum (Addis Ababa University) &#124; Music In AfricaSeptember 2, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 2, 2015</span></span></span>

The museum now houses specimens representing more than 1,100 animal species. These include mounted mammals, birds, reptiles, fish and other preserved specimens collected from Ethiopia's diverse ecosystems, from highland plateaus to lowland savannahs. While the displays may appear old-fashioned compared with modern interactive natural history museums, their scientific value remains significant because they preserve physical records of species distribution, morphology and biodiversity collected over many decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bwppearladdis.com/discover-the-city/zoological-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bwppearladdis.com">[bwppearladdis.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bwppearladdis.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zoological Museum – Best Western Plus Pearl Addis Hotel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zoological Museum – Best Western Plus Pearl Addis Hotel</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in unusual collections, the appeal lies partly in the atmosphere. Rows of taxidermied animals and preserved specimens evoke the nineteenth- and twentieth-century tradition of natural history cabinets, where scientific research and public display overlapped. Rather than presenting dramatic special effects, the museum offers an authentic glimpse into how zoological collections were assembled for teaching and research before digital databases and wildlife photography became widespread.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bwppearladdis.com/discover-the-city/zoological-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bwppearladdis.com">[bwppearladdis.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bwppearladdis.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zoological Museum – Best Western Plus Pearl Addis Hotel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zoological Museum – Best Western Plus Pearl Addis Hotel</span></span></span>

## How academic collections differ from tourist museums

Both university museums differ noticeably from Ethiopia's better-known tourist attractions because their primary purpose remains education and research rather than entertainment.

The Ethnological Museum interprets culture through scholarship. Objects are used to explain social life, religion, music, craftsmanship and historical change, while the palace setting illustrates how Ethiopia's imperial past intersects with academic study. Visitors are encouraged to understand artefacts within wider cultural systems rather than treating them simply as exotic curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.musicinafrica.net/directory/ethnographic-museum-addis-ababa-university?language=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musicinafrica.net">[Music In Africa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musicinafrica.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Music In Africa Ethnographic Museum (Addis Ababa University) &#124; Music In Africa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Music In AfricaEthnographic Museum (Addis Ababa University) &#124; Music In AfricaSeptember 2, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 2, 2015</span></span></span>

The Zoological Natural History Museum serves a similar role for biology. Its specimens support teaching, taxonomy and biodiversity research, while simultaneously providing one of the country's few public opportunities to encounter preserved examples of Ethiopia's remarkable wildlife. The displays may lack the theatrical presentation found in larger international natural history museums, but they retain considerable scientific importance because they document species collected across different periods of Ethiopia's environmental history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bwppearladdis.com/discover-the-city/zoological-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bwppearladdis.com">[bwppearladdis.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bwppearladdis.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zoological Museum – Best Western Plus Pearl Addis Hotel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zoological Museum – Best Western Plus Pearl Addis Hotel</span></span></span>

Together, these collections illustrate an older museum tradition in which universities acted as custodians of both cultural memory and scientific evidence. One preserves the material lives of Ethiopia's people; the other preserves the physical record of its fauna. Seen together, they form an unusually complete cabinet of national identity, combining anthropology, royal history and natural history within a single academic institution.

## Why these collections matter in Ethiopia's curiosity landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Within Ethiopia's broader landscape of unusual collections, Addis Ababa University's museums stand apart because they preserve two very different forms of inheritance under one institutional roof. The Ethnological Museum demonstrates how objects associated with ordinary life, ritual and monarchy can coexist within a former imperial palace, while the Zoological Natural History Museum preserves animal bodies as scientific records rather than hunting trophies or decorative displays.

</div>

Neither museum trades on mystery or sensationalism. Their fascination comes instead from the survival of older museum practices: carefully assembled university collections, taxidermied wildlife, royal apartments left largely intact, and exhibitions that invite visitors to understand Ethiopia through both its people and its biodiversity. Together they remain among the country's most distinctive academic collections and offer one of the clearest examples of how Ethiopia preserves curiosity through scholarship rather than spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnological_Museum%2C_Addis_Ababa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ethnological Museum, Addis Ababa</span></span></span>
