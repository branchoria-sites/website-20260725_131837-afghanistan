## Introduction

The Sarajevo Haggadah is one of [Bosnia and Herzegovina]({{ 'bosnia-and-herzegovina/' | relative_url }})'s most remarkable cultural treasures: a richly illuminated Jewish manuscript created in medieval Spain that survived exile, war, occupation and political upheaval before becoming one of the country's most carefully protected museum objects. Unlike many famous museum pieces that are permanently visible, the Haggadah is deliberately difficult to see. It is preserved under strict environmental and security controls at the National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina, and public display of the fragile original is tightly managed to reduce the risk of damage. This combination of extraordinary artistic value, dramatic survival and careful stewardship has turned a single manuscript into one of the country's defining heritage objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/sarajevo-haggadah-manuscript" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Sarajevo Haggadah manuscript</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sarajevo Haggadah manuscript - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>

## Why one manuscript became Bosnia's great treasure

The Sarajevo Haggadah is an illuminated Passover prayer book produced around the middle of the fourteenth century, probably in the region of Barcelona in the Crown of Aragon. It belongs to a rare group of lavishly illustrated Sephardic Haggadot and is celebrated for its vivid biblical miniatures, gold decoration and exceptionally high-quality craftsmanship. UNESCO has recognised it as documentary heritage on its Memory of the World Register, reflecting both its artistic importance and its remarkable historical journey.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/sarajevo-haggadah-manuscript" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Sarajevo Haggadah manuscript</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sarajevo Haggadah manuscript - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>

Its significance extends well beyond Jewish religious history. The manuscript has become a symbol of Bosnia and Herzegovina's multicultural past because it links medieval Spain, the Sephardic Jewish diaspora, the Ottoman Balkans and modern Sarajevo. Rather than representing only one community, it has come to embody the preservation of cultural heritage across religious and political boundaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/secret-story-sarajevo-haggadah" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Secret Story of Sarajevo Haggadah &#124; UNESCO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Story of Sarajevo Haggadah &#124; UNESCO...</span></span></span>

Within Bosnia and Herzegovina's landscape of unusual collections, the Haggadah occupies a distinctive place. The object itself is not mysterious or occult; what makes it extraordinary is the way successive generations repeatedly treated it as something worth risking their lives to protect.

## Its journeys, rescues and symbolic status

The manuscript's documented story resumes in the nineteenth century, when the National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina purchased it in 1894 from the Sarajevo Sephardic Koen family. Marginal notes suggest an earlier passage through Italy after the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492, although parts of its early history remain uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[zemaljskimuzej.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

Its reputation, however, rests largely on its repeated escapes from destruction.

During the Second World War, occupying German authorities sought the manuscript after the invasion of Yugoslavia. Museum director Jozo Petrović and curator Derviš Korkut refused to surrender it. The most widely accepted account is that the manuscript was secretly hidden in a mosque in a village on Mount Bjelašnica until the war ended, illustrating an extraordinary episode in which Muslim, Croat and Jewish histories intersected in the protection of a shared cultural treasure. While some details have entered legend, the museum itself confirms that staff concealed the manuscript and prevented its seizure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[Zemaljski muzej BiH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

The Haggadah faced danger again during the siege of Sarajevo in the 1990s. As the National Museum came under shellfire, the manuscript was removed to secure storage, preserving it while much of the city's cultural infrastructure was under threat. The survival of the book through both the Holocaust era and the Bosnian War transformed it from an important manuscript into an international symbol of cultural endurance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[zemaljskimuzej.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

Because of these repeated rescues, discussions of the Sarajevo Haggadah often focus as much on the people who protected it as on the manuscript itself. It has become a powerful example of heritage being defended across ethnic and religious divisions during periods of violence.

## How visitors can see a fragile original

Visitors should not expect the Sarajevo Haggadah to be displayed like an ordinary museum exhibit. Its preservation takes precedence over continuous public access.

The manuscript is housed in a specially secured, climate-controlled environment within the National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Light exposure, humidity and temperature are carefully managed because medieval pigments and parchment deteriorate when subjected to prolonged display. For that reason, the original is only exhibited under controlled conditions and may not always be available for viewing during a normal museum visit. The museum occasionally presents it during special exhibitions or designated display periods, while high-quality reproductions and interpretive material help explain its significance when the original is not on show.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[Zemaljski muzej BiH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

Prospective visitors should therefore check the museum's current arrangements before planning a visit. Access policies can change depending on conservation requirements, exhibitions or museum operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[Zemaljski muzej BiH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

This controlled approach reflects modern conservation practice rather than exclusivity. Every additional hour of exposure to light and environmental fluctuation slightly accelerates the ageing of a manuscript that is already around seven centuries old.

## More than a museum exhibit

The Sarajevo Haggadah illustrates how a single artefact can accumulate meanings far beyond its original purpose. It remains a Jewish liturgical manuscript, an outstanding work of medieval book art and one of the finest surviving examples of Sephardic illumination. At the same time, it has become a national monument of Bosnia and Herzegovina and a recognised part of the world's documentary heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/sarajevo-haggadah-manuscript" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Sarajevo Haggadah manuscript</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sarajevo Haggadah manuscript - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>

For travellers interested in unusual collections, its appeal lies not in spectacle but in stewardship. The manuscript is fascinating precisely because so much effort has gone into limiting access, protecting it from conflict and ensuring that future generations can still encounter the original. In a country where many museums preserve memories of war, secrecy and survival, the Sarajevo Haggadah stands apart as a guarded treasure whose greatest story is not simply where it came from, but how many times it was saved.
