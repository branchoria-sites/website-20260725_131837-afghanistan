## Introduction

The Dead Sea Scrolls are among the most important archaeological discoveries ever made in [Israel]({{ 'where-israels-strangest-collections/' | relative_url }}), not because they reveal a single hidden secret, but because they changed what historians know about ancient Judaism, biblical texts and desert communities. Discovered in caves near Qumran on the north-western shore of the Dead Sea between 1947 and 1956, the scrolls preserve fragments of hundreds of ancient manuscripts dating mainly from the third century BCE to the first century CE. Today they are studied as historical evidence, displayed at the Shrine of the Book in Jerusalem, and remain a source of fascination because they sit at the meeting point of archaeology, religion and unresolved historical questions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/discovery-sites" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deadseascrolls.org.il">[The Dead Sea Scrolls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deadseascrolls.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dead Sea ScrollsThe Dead Sea Scrolls - Discovery Sites...</span></span></span>

The “mystery” of the scrolls is therefore less about lost supernatural knowledge and more about unanswered human questions: who wrote them, who hid them, how they relate to the communities of the Second Temple period, and what they reveal about the development of sacred texts. Their discovery transformed a remote desert landscape into one of the world’s most significant archaeological sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/discovery-and-publication?locale=en_US" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deadseascrolls.org.il">[The Dead Sea Scrolls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deadseascrolls.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dead Sea ScrollsThe Dead Sea Scrolls - Discovery and Publication...</span></span></span>

## Discovery in the Judean Desert caves

### How a chance find became a historic excavation

The first scrolls were discovered in 1947 when Bedouin shepherds found ancient manuscripts in a cave near Khirbet Qumran. The discovery led archaeologists and researchers to investigate the surrounding cliffs, eventually identifying eleven caves containing thousands of fragments from more than 900 manuscripts. The fragile remains included biblical writings, religious works, legal texts, prayers and community documents, creating an extraordinary archive of Jewish life during the Second Temple period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dss.collections.imj.org.il/discovery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dss.collections.imj.org.il">[Israel Museum Digital Collections]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dss.collections.imj.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Israel Museum Digital CollectionsDigital Dead Sea Scrolls at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem - Discovery...</span></span></span>

The caves themselves became part of the mystery. The dry climate of the Judean Desert helped preserve parchment and papyrus for roughly two thousand years, allowing scholars to study texts that would normally have disappeared. Cave 4 became one of the most important locations because it contained thousands of fragments from around 500 manuscripts, turning the discovery from a handful of unusual objects into a vast archaeological collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/discovery-sites" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deadseascrolls.org.il">[The Dead Sea Scrolls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deadseascrolls.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dead Sea ScrollsThe Dead Sea Scrolls - Discovery Sites...</span></span></span>

The physical journey of the scrolls was also unusual. Some of the first seven manuscripts passed through a complicated chain of discovery, purchase and scholarly investigation before eventually becoming part of the collection displayed at the Shrine of the Book. The early history of the scrolls involved Bedouin finders, collectors, archaeologists and religious institutions, adding a human story of negotiation and interpretation to the archaeological one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.imj.org.il/en/exhibitions/faces-behind-scrolls-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imj.org.il">[imj.org.il]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imj.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Faces behind the Scrolls &#124; The Israel Museum, Jerusalem</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 15, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 15, 2018</span></span></span>

## Why the scrolls changed historical understanding

The Dead Sea Scrolls matter because they provide some of the earliest surviving evidence for biblical texts. They include copies or fragments of almost every book of the Hebrew Bible, along with writings that were previously unknown or preserved only through later traditions. The Great Isaiah Scroll, for example, is a nearly complete manuscript of Isaiah dating from the Second Temple period and became one of the most famous objects connected with the discovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://deadsea.com/attraction/dead-sea-scrolls-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deadsea.com">[DeadSea.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deadsea.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dead Sea.com Shrine of the Book: Dead Sea Scrolls Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shrine of the Book: Dead Sea Scrolls Museum...</span></span></span>

Before the scrolls were found, scholars relied on much later manuscript traditions when studying the history of biblical texts. The Qumran discoveries showed that ancient Jewish writings existed in a more complex environment, with different versions, interpretations and religious debates developing before later forms of Judaism became established. Rather than proving a simple unchanged transmission of texts, the scrolls reveal a dynamic period of copying, editing and interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/biblical-artifacts/dead-sea-scrolls/what_are_the_dead_sea_scrolls/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblicalarchaeology.org">[Biblical Archaeology Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblical Archaeology Society What Are the Dead Sea Scrolls?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblical Archaeology SocietyWhat Are the Dead Sea Scrolls? - Biblical Archaeology SocietyAugust 29, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 29, 2022</span></span></span>

The manuscripts also changed views of ancient Jewish communities. Some texts describe strict religious practices, rules for communal life, biblical interpretation and expectations about the future. Many scholars associate the scroll collection with the community that lived at Qumran, although the exact relationship between the settlement, the caves and the wider Jewish world remains debated. Some researchers argue that the caves may have preserved a broader library gathered from different places rather than a collection produced entirely by the residents of Qumran.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dss.collections.imj.org.il/discovery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dss.collections.imj.org.il">[Israel Museum Digital Collections]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dss.collections.imj.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Israel Museum Digital CollectionsDigital Dead Sea Scrolls at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem - Discovery...</span></span></span>

## Mystery, belief and scholarly interpretation

The most famous question surrounding the scrolls is simple: who created them? For decades, many scholars linked them with the Essenes, a Jewish group described by ancient writers as living a disciplined communal life. However, the evidence is not considered decisive, and modern scholarship continues to examine whether the scrolls came from one particular sect, several groups, or a wider collection of Jewish writings hidden during a period of conflict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.livescience.com/24595-qumran-dead-sea-scrolls.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livescience.com">[Live Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livescience.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">First found by a shepherd in 1946 or 1947, over 900 texts were eventually located in 11 caves, with a 12th recently identified in 2017. Another enduring mystery concerns the Copper Scroll. Unlike the other manuscripts, which were mainly written on parchment or papyrus, this object was engraved on copper and lists locations where valuable objects were supposedly hidden. Some have interpreted it as a genuine treasure record connected with the Jerusalem Temple, while others question whether it represents an actual hidden cache or a symbolic text. The treasure described in the scroll has never been found, keeping it one of the most intriguing puzzles connected with the collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/archaeology/a71796916/dead-sea-copper-scroll/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popularmechanics.com">[Popular Mechanics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popularmechanics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Over the next five years, 250 scroll samples will undergo advanced analysis—including chemical testing, artificial intelligence, paleogra...</span></span></span>

Popular stories have sometimes presented the Dead Sea Scrolls as evidence of forbidden secrets or suppressed discoveries. Scholars, however, generally see their importance differently. Their real significance lies in the information they provide about ancient writing, religious diversity and the formation of Jewish traditions. The mystery is not that the scrolls overturn history, but that they reveal how complicated history already was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/biblical-artifacts/dead-sea-scrolls/what_are_the_dead_sea_scrolls/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblicalarchaeology.org">[Biblical Archaeology Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblical Archaeology Society What Are the Dead Sea Scrolls?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblical Archaeology SocietyWhat Are the Dead Sea Scrolls? - Biblical Archaeology SocietyAugust 29, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 29, 2022</span></span></span>

## New technologies are revealing more about the manuscripts

Research on the Dead Sea Scrolls has continued long after their discovery. Modern conservation methods, digital imaging and scientific analysis allow researchers to examine fragments without damaging them. The Israel Museum and other institutions have created digital access projects that allow scholars and the public to study high-resolution images of important manuscripts, including the Great Isaiah Scroll and other major texts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dss.collections.imj.org.il/discovery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dss.collections.imj.org.il">[Israel Museum Digital Collections]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dss.collections.imj.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Israel Museum Digital CollectionsDigital Dead Sea Scrolls at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem - Discovery...</span></span></span>

Recent studies have also used radiocarbon dating and computer analysis of handwriting to refine understanding of when and by whom some manuscripts may have been produced. Artificial intelligence tools are being tested alongside traditional palaeography, the study of ancient handwriting, to identify possible differences between scribes and improve dating methods. These techniques do not replace historical interpretation, but they provide new evidence for studying how ancient manuscripts were created.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12013" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dating ancient manuscripts using radiocarbon and AI-based writing style analysisJune 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2024</span></span></span>
