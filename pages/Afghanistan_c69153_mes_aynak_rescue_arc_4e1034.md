## Introduction

Mes Aynak is one of [Afghanistan]({{ 'what-makes-afghanistans-collections-so/' | relative_url }})’s most remarkable archaeological landscapes and one of its most endangered. About 40 kilometres south-east of Kabul in Logar Province, the site contains the remains of a large Buddhist settlement built above rich copper deposits that had already attracted miners in antiquity. Monasteries, stupas, workshops, homes, statues and traces of ancient metal production spread across a complex that helped connect religious life, trade and mining along routes linked to the wider Silk Roads. ## Can Mes Aynak's Buddhist City Be Saved?

The debate over Mes Aynak is not merely about individual statues or buildings. Archaeologists regard the site as one of the most important discoveries made in Afghanistan in recent decades because it preserves evidence of how religious communities and mining economies interacted. Excavations have revealed fortified monasteries, Buddhist shrines, market areas, workshops, residential structures and ancient copper workings spread across a large archaeological zone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/en/a-propos/ainak" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archéologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchéologieMes Aynak &#124; Archéologie &#124; culture.gouv.frMes Aynak, a few dozen kilometres to the south of Kabul, has been excavated since 200...</span></span></span>

What makes the site particularly unusual within Afghanistan’s landscape of curious collections is that the archaeology is inseparable from the geology. The settlement appears to have grown around copper extraction, meaning the very resource now attracting industrial mining was also a foundation of the ancient community. Modern researchers therefore see Mes Aynak not as a single monastery but as a layered historical environment linking religion, technology and commerce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aliph-foundation.org/en/projects/protecting-the-mes-aynak-archaeological-site-and-its-works-of-art" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aliph-foundation.org">[aliph-foundation.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aliph-foundation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aliph FoundationProtecting the Mes Aynak Archaeological Site and its...The settlement was initially established by Buddhists as a mining...</span></span></span>

The conflict emerged sharply after a major mining lease was awarded in 2007 to a Chinese-led consortium seeking access to the copper deposit. The project promised jobs, infrastructure and potentially significant revenue for Afghanistan, but it also raised the prospect that large portions of the archaeological landscape could be destroyed by open-pit mining.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mes_Aynak" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mes Aynak</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mes Aynak</span></span></span>

## Monasteries Above an Ancient Copper Landscape

Mes Aynak’s most famous remains are its Buddhist monasteries and sculptures dating mainly from the period between the third and seventh centuries CE. Excavations have uncovered hundreds of statues and reliefs, richly decorated religious buildings and evidence of extensive monastic activity. Researchers have also identified older archaeological layers beneath the Buddhist settlement, including traces of much earlier copper production.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aliph-foundation.org/en/projects/protecting-the-mes-aynak-archaeological-site-and-its-works-of-art" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aliph-foundation.org">[aliph-foundation.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aliph-foundation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aliph FoundationProtecting the Mes Aynak Archaeological Site and its...The settlement was initially established by Buddhists as a mining...</span></span></span>

The landscape differs from many famous Buddhist sites because it was also an industrial zone. Archaeologists have documented ancient mines, smelting areas and settlements connected to metal extraction. Rather than existing in isolation, the monasteries appear to have been embedded within a working economy built around copper. This combination gives Mes Aynak a special place among Afghanistan’s archaeological curiosities: it is simultaneously a religious centre, a mining settlement and an archive of ancient technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mes_Aynak" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mes Aynak</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mes Aynak</span></span></span>

French archaeological researchers have described the site as crucial for understanding the development of Buddhism in Afghanistan and the relationship between monastic communities and local economic life. That broader context helps explain why scholars have repeatedly argued that preserving only a handful of statues would not preserve the site’s full historical value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/en/a-propos/ainak" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archéologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchéologieMes Aynak &#124; Archéologie &#124; culture.gouv.frMes Aynak, a few dozen kilometres to the south of Kabul, has been excavated since 200...</span></span></span>

## Rescue Excavation and Objects Removed for Display

Because mining plans continued to advance, archaeologists adopted a rescue-excavation strategy. Instead of conducting the slower, more comprehensive investigations normally preferred at a major site, teams worked under pressure to document structures and remove portable artefacts before potential destruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mes_Aynak" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mes Aynak</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mes Aynak</span></span></span>

The scale of the effort has been enormous. Excavators recorded monasteries, sculptures, wall paintings, coins, tools and everyday objects while creating maps, photographs and digital records of the landscape. Hundreds of artefacts were recovered, and many were transferred for protection and conservation, including material destined for museum collections in Kabul. Archaeologists also used increasingly sophisticated recording techniques to preserve information even where complete excavation proved impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mes_Aynak" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mes Aynak</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mes Aynak</span></span></span>

Yet rescue archaeology is an imperfect solution. Removing statues and portable objects saves individual pieces, but it cannot fully preserve the relationships between buildings, workshops, pathways, mines and religious spaces that make the site meaningful. Archaeologists often compare this to saving pages from a book while losing the order in which they were originally arranged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/859311467993737697/pdf/768520IPR0P1160lPN0REQUEST0RQ013001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents1.worldbank.org">[World Bank]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents1.worldbank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World BankWorld Bank Document23 Apr 2013 — Mes Aynak is with the archaeological term, “context”. For visitors who eventually encounter Mes Aynak artefacts in museums, this creates an unusual situation. The objects survive, but many have been separated from the wider landscape that explains how they functioned. ## Mining, Preservation and the Loss of Context

The most important issue at Mes Aynak is not whether artefacts can be rescued. It is whether the archaeological context can survive. In archaeology, context refers to the precise relationships between objects, buildings, layers of soil and human activity. Once these relationships are destroyed, much historical information disappears permanently. World Bank documentation connected with the site emphasised the enormous number of archaeological contexts already recorded there, illustrating the scale of information embedded within the landscape itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/859311467993737697/pdf/768520IPR0P1160lPN0REQUEST0RQ013001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents1.worldbank.org">[World Bank]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents1.worldbank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World BankWorld Bank Document23 Apr 2013 — Mes Aynak is with the archaeological term, “context”.     if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
