## Introduction

In Majuro, the most remarkable canoe collection is not found behind display cases. At Waan Aelõñ in Majel (WAM, meaning "Canoes of the [Marshall Islands]({{ 'marshall-islands/' | relative_url }})"), traditional Marshallese canoe building remains a working practice, with trainees carving timber, assembling hulls, learning to sail and passing on knowledge that might otherwise disappear. For visitors, WAM offers something closer to a living workshop than a conventional museum: models, historic tools and interpretation sit alongside active construction, training and launches on the lagoon. This combination makes WAM one of the Marshall Islands' most distinctive heritage attractions because it preserves canoe culture by using it rather than simply exhibiting it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span></span></span>

## Why Majuro's Canoes Are More Than Exhibits

Traditional Marshallese canoes were once essential for travel, fishing and communication across widely scattered atolls. As imported boats and engines became more common during the twentieth century, fewer young people learned the specialised skills needed to build, sail and maintain them. WAM was established in the 1990s to address this loss while also helping young Marshallese gain practical employment skills and confidence through vocational training centred on canoe culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1080/17457823.2026.2672935" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[doi.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Full article: Lessons from canoe school: reimagining space and curriculum for a sustainable future in the Marshall IslandsMay 17, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 17, 2026</span></span></span>

That approach means visitors encounter heritage in motion. A canoe under construction is not treated as an archaeological reconstruction but as a vessel expected to sail. Experienced builders teach trainees by demonstration rather than written manuals, reflecting the traditional way knowledge was transmitted within families and island communities. Alongside woodworking, trainees study navigation, canoe terminology, maintenance, Marshallese stories, chants and the cultural values connected with life at sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/program/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Program – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Program – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span></span></span>

For anyone interested in unusual collections, WAM stands apart because the collection includes skills, routines and relationships as much as physical objects. The workshop itself becomes part of the heritage.

## Inside WAM's Visitor Experience

WAM maintains a dedicated Visitors Centre beside its distinctive A-frame canoe house on the Majuro lagoon. Rather than presenting a large national museum, it offers an intimate introduction to Marshallese canoe culture during normal weekday opening hours.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/2014/09/visitors-center/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visitors’ Center – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 1, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 2014</span></span></span>

Visitors typically encounter:

* A large suspended canoe model illustrating traditional design.
* Authentic canoe-building tools and equipment.
* Displays explaining canoe history and construction.
* Hand-crafted model canoes, [stick charts]({{ 'stick-charts/' | relative_url }}) and other locally made products.
* Opportunities to observe an active training environment, depending on the day's work programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/2014/09/visitors-center/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visitors’ Center – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 1, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 2014</span></span></span>

One of WAM's most distinctive visitor experiences is the opportunity to sail on a traditional Marshallese canoe on Majuro Lagoon through organised trips. Instead of merely reading about traditional seamanship, visitors can experience the balance, sail handling and quiet movement that defined inter-island travel for centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span></span></span>

The atmosphere differs noticeably from a conventional museum. Noise from woodworking, conversations between instructors and trainees, and vessels moving between workshop and lagoon reinforce that this is a functioning cultural centre rather than a preserved historical exhibit.

## How Canoe Skills Are Passed On

The heart of WAM is education rather than exhibition. Its programmes combine traditional canoe building with literacy, numeracy, counselling and vocational development, particularly for young people who may have struggled within formal education. Canoes become the teaching medium through which wider life skills are learned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/program/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Program – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Program – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span></span></span>

Training generally includes:

* Safe use and maintenance of woodworking tools.
* Building authentic model and full-sized canoes.
* Traditional sailing techniques.
* Boat maintenance.
* Marshallese cultural knowledge connected with the sea.
* Teamwork, punctuality and workplace skills.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/program/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Program – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Program – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span></span></span>

Experienced canoe builders such as Binton Daniel contribute knowledge learned from recognised master builders, while trainers like Isocker Anwel progressed from student to instructor, creating a continuous chain of transmission rather than a one-off heritage project.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/wam-team/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">WA M Team – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WAM Team – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in MajelAugust 30, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 30, 2014</span></span></span>

## Models, Workshops and Living Heritage

Although WAM displays attractive model canoes and historical objects, these are not ends in themselves. The models are teaching tools that help explain hull design, rigging and construction methods before trainees move on to full-sized vessels. Likewise, traditional woodworking tools remain relevant because they continue to be used alongside modern equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/2014/09/visitors-center/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visitors’ Center – Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 1, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 2014</span></span></span>

The workshop also adapts heritage to present-day needs. Alongside preserving traditional canoe construction, WAM has collaborated on projects exploring sustainable small-scale transport inspired by Marshallese canoe design, demonstrating that traditional maritime knowledge can contribute to modern environmental challenges rather than existing solely as historical nostalgia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://changing-transport.org/publications/low-carbon-sea-transport-prototype-workshop/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: changing-transport.org">[Changing Transport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">changing-transport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">low carbon sea transport prototype workshop</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">low carbon sea transport prototype workshop</span></span></span>

This practical adaptation illustrates why WAM is better understood as a living heritage institution than a static museum. Canoe culture is preserved because it continues to evolve and remain useful.

## Why It Matters in the Marshall Islands' Curiosity Landscape

Among the Marshall Islands' unusual heritage attractions, WAM occupies a distinctive place. While the Alele Museum preserves important canoe artefacts, navigation charts and archival collections, WAM preserves the knowledge needed to make sense of those objects. One institution safeguards physical evidence; the other safeguards the craft itself.

For travellers interested in unusual museums and cultural collections, WAM demonstrates that the rarest heritage is sometimes intangible. The most valuable collection is not simply a row of historic canoes but an active community capable of building, sailing and teaching them. In a country whose history has been shaped by migration, colonialism and profound environmental change, keeping those skills alive makes WAM one of the Marshall Islands' most significant—and most unusual—visitor experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canoesmarshallislands.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canoesmarshallislands.com">[canoesmarshallislands.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canoesmarshallislands.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canoes of the Marshall Islands – Waan Aelõñ in Majel</span></span></span>
