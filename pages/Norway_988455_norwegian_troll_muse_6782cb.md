## Introduction

[Norway]({{ 'where-norway-preserves-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s troll museums are not museums in the traditional sense of preserving archaeological finds or historical artefacts. Instead, they are immersive attractions that transform one of the country's best-known folklore traditions into interactive cultural experiences. Located in Tromsø and Bergen, they sit at an intriguing meeting point between folklore, art, family entertainment and commercial tourism. Visitors encounter augmented reality creatures, theatrical storytelling, illustrations inspired by classic Norwegian artists and opportunities to step inside fairy tales rather than observe them from behind glass. This combination makes them unusual within Norway's museum landscape: they preserve cultural imagination more than physical history, while demonstrating how a centuries-old folklore tradition continues to evolve for modern audiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trollmuseum.no/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trollmuseum.no">[Troll Museum Tromsø]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trollmuseum.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Troll Museum TromsøTroll Museum Tromsø</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Troll Museum TromsøTroll Museum Tromsø</span></span></span>

## What visitors find inside the troll museums

The museums in Tromsø and Bergen deliberately avoid presenting trolls as creatures that once literally existed. Instead, they explore how generations of Norwegians imagined them through folk tales, legends and visual art, using digital technology to make those stories accessible to contemporary visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trollmuseum.no/museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trollmuseum.no">[Troll Museum Tromsø]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trollmuseum.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Troll Museum TromsøMuseum – Troll Museum Tromsø</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Troll Museum TromsøMuseum – Troll Museum Tromsø</span></span></span>

## From Kittelsen's monsters to souvenir mascots

One of the most striking aspects of these museums is the way they bridge two very different images of the Norwegian troll.

The first comes from nineteenth-century folklore and art, particularly the work of illustrator Theodor Kittelsen. His trolls were mysterious, often frightening beings that seemed almost inseparable from forests, mountains and dark Norwegian landscapes. Many of his images deliberately blurred the line between rock formations and living creatures, reinforcing the idea that nature itself might conceal hidden life.

The museums acknowledge this artistic tradition through references to classic fairy tales and visual motifs, but they also demonstrate how the image of the troll has softened over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bergen.trollmuseum.no/museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bergen.trollmuseum.no">[Troll Museum Bergen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bergen.trollmuseum.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Troll Museum Bergen Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Troll Museum BergenMuseum - Troll Museum Bergen...</span></span></span>

This evolution mirrors Norway's wider tourism culture. Trolls have become one of the country's most recognisable visual symbols, appearing on roadside souvenirs, Christmas ornaments, children's books and travel advertising. The museums therefore document not only folklore itself but also the continuing reinvention of that folklore for commercial audiences.

## Folklore interpretation versus commercial spectacle

The museums occupy an interesting middle ground between cultural interpretation and entertainment.

Unlike historical museums that preserve original manuscripts or archaeological finds, these attractions primarily interpret intangible cultural heritage. Their main exhibit is storytelling itself. Visitors learn recurring features from Norwegian legends—such as trolls' enormous size, their association with mountains, and traditional beliefs that church bells or Christian symbols drove them away—but these ideas are presented as elements of folklore rather than historical fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trollmuseum.no/museum/trolls/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trollmuseum.no">[Troll Museum Tromsø]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trollmuseum.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Troll Museum TromsøTrolls – Troll Museum Tromsø</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Troll Museum TromsøTrolls – Troll Museum Tromsø</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because Norwegian troll traditions have never existed as a single, uniform belief system. Stories varied widely between regions, with different descriptions, personalities and landscapes attached to local trolls. The museums simplify these traditions into coherent exhibitions that are easier for international visitors to understand. That inevitably sacrifices some regional complexity, but it also makes the folklore more approachable.

Commercial elements are equally visible. Gift shops, branded characters and augmented reality souvenirs extend the experience beyond the galleries, illustrating how folklore has become part of Norway's visitor economy rather than remaining solely within oral tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trollmuseum.no/museum/augmented-reality/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trollmuseum.no">[Troll Museum Tromsø]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trollmuseum.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Troll Museum TromsøAugmented Reality – Troll Museum Tromsø</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Troll Museum TromsøAugmented Reality – Troll Museum Tromsø</span></span></span>

## Why these attractions matter in Norway's curiosity culture

Norway's stranger museums often divide into two broad categories.

One group preserves documented history, such as witch-trial records, medical collections or criminal evidence. Their authority rests on original objects and archival documentation.

The troll museums belong to a different tradition. Their purpose is not to authenticate legends but to interpret how folklore has shaped Norwegian identity. They demonstrate that stories can become cultural artefacts in their own right, worthy of exhibition even when the creatures themselves belong entirely to myth.

That makes them valuable within Norway's wider landscape of unusual museums. Rather than asking visitors to believe in trolls, they invite them to understand why trolls have remained culturally important for centuries—appearing in literature, art, tourism, children's culture and national symbolism long after literal belief disappeared. The attractions succeed because they recognise that folklore is not frozen in the past.     if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
