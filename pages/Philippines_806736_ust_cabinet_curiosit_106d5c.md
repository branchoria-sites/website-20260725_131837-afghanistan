## Introduction

The University of Santo Tomas (UST) Museum in Manila is one of the clearest surviving examples of a nineteenth-century university cabinet of curiosities in the [Philippines]({{ 'where-the-philippines-keeps-its/' | relative_url }}). Long before museums specialised in a single subject, academic collections often brought together natural history specimens, archaeological finds, religious art, coins, shells and ethnographic objects under one roof. The result was not a random assortment of oddities but a physical map of how knowledge was organised during the Spanish colonial period. Today, the UST Museum remains unusual because visitors can still experience this encyclopaedic approach to collecting, where science, religion and empire appear side by side rather than in separate galleries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph">[edu.ph]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UST Museum Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UST Museum Home</span></span></span>

## From teaching collection to public museum

The museum traces its origins to the late nineteenth century, when Spanish educational regulations required leading colleges to maintain natural history collections for teaching. The University of Santo Tomas formally established its museum in 1869–1871, building on earlier teaching cabinets that had supported courses in medicine, pharmacy and the natural sciences. Rather than displaying objects purely for public entertainment, the collection functioned as an educational laboratory where students learned through direct observation of specimens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph">[edu.ph]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UST Museum Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UST Museum Home</span></span></span>

Dominican scholars, particularly the naturalist Fr Casto de Elera, expanded and systematically catalogued the collections. His work transformed what had begun as a teaching cabinet into one of the country's earliest scientific repositories. De Elera's catalogues of Philippine fauna became important references in the development of zoological research, illustrating how university collections could contribute to scholarship as well as classroom instruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ust.edu.ph/research/research-center-for-the-natural-and-applied-sciences/ust-herbarium/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ust.edu.ph">[University of Santo Tomas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Santo Tomas UST-RCNAS Herbarium</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of Santo TomasUST-RCNAS Herbarium - University of Santo Tomas...</span></span></span>

Although the museum moved from the university's original Intramuros campus to Sampaloc before the Second World War, much of its material survived. Many specimens and artefacts displayed today still originate from the nineteenth century, preserving not only the objects themselves but also the intellectual structure in which they were originally assembled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph">[UST Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UST Museum Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UST Museum Home</span></span></span>

## What visitors find in the mixed displays

Unlike a modern natural history museum, the UST Museum still reflects the older idea that all branches of knowledge could coexist within a single institution. Visitors encounter remarkably diverse collections that would now usually be separated into entirely different museums.

Among the most distinctive displays are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* extensive zoological and botanical specimens gathered for scientific teaching;
* one of the Philippines' oldest natural history collections, including internationally significant mollusc collections;
* archaeological and ethnographic artefacts from across the archipelago;
* religious paintings, sculpture and devotional art spanning several centuries;
* coins, medals and historical objects illustrating political, commercial and cultural history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph/collections" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph">[edu.ph]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UST Museum Collections</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UST Museum Collections</span></span></span>

</div>

This mixture creates the atmosphere of a historical cabinet rather than a conventional gallery. Instead of following modern disciplinary boundaries, visitors move between shells, preserved animals, sacred images and cultural artefacts with relatively little separation. The experience provides a rare glimpse of how nineteenth-century scholars imagined the world's order.

The museum has also evolved beyond its original teaching function. While preserving its historic collections, it now hosts exhibitions, supports university research and welcomes public visitors, balancing conservation with education.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ust.edu.ph/administrative-offices/ust-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ust.edu.ph">[University of Santo Tomas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Santo Tomas UST Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of Santo TomasUST Museum - University of Santo Tomas...</span></span></span>

## How colonial classification shaped the collection

The museum's greatest historical value lies not simply in the objects it preserves but in the way they were organised.

European universities of the colonial era generally sought to collect representative examples of nature, religion and human societies. Classification was central to this project. Plants were sorted into taxonomic systems, animals grouped according to scientific relationships, coins arranged chronologically, and ethnographic objects treated as evidence for studying the peoples of the colony. The cabinet therefore reflected a belief that the world could be understood through careful collecting, naming and ordering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ust.edu.ph/research/research-center-for-the-natural-and-applied-sciences/ust-herbarium/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ust.edu.ph">[University of Santo Tomas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Santo Tomas UST-RCNAS Herbarium</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of Santo TomasUST-RCNAS Herbarium - University of Santo Tomas...</span></span></span>

In the Philippine context, this approach carried both scientific and colonial meanings. Dominican scholars documented local biodiversity with remarkable dedication, preserving specimens that remain valuable for historical research. At the same time, Indigenous cultures and material traditions were frequently interpreted through European academic frameworks. Objects gathered from different communities entered institutional collections where they became part of broader narratives about the colony rather than remaining within their original cultural settings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Santo Tomas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of Santo Tomas</span></span></span>

Modern museum practice increasingly recognises these complexities. Rather than celebrating colonial collecting uncritically, contemporary interpretation encourages visitors to consider both the scientific achievements and the unequal power relationships that shaped many historical collections. The UST Museum therefore functions not only as a repository of objects but also as evidence of changing ideas about knowledge, authority and representation.

## Why the museum matters within the Philippines' curiosity landscape

Among the Philippines' unusual collections, the UST Museum occupies a distinctive position because its strangeness comes from intellectual history rather than sensational exhibits.

Where burial caves preserve ancestral traditions and private museums reflect individual passions, the UST collections reveal how an academic institution attempted to gather the natural and cultural world into a single system of knowledge. Walking through its galleries is less like entering a modern specialist museum than stepping into an earlier way of thinking, when a shell, a crucifix, an ethnographic artefact and a preserved animal could all be displayed as interconnected pieces of a universal catalogue.

For visitors interested in cabinets of curiosity, colonial science or the history of museums, it remains one of the Philippines' most significant surviving academic collections. Its enduring mixture of natural history, sacred art and ethnographic material makes it an unusually complete example of how colonial universities organised knowledge before museums became specialised institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph">[UST Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ustmuseum.ust.edu.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UST Museum Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UST Museum Home</span></span></span>
