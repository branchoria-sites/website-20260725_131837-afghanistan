## Where Trinidad and Tobago’s strange collections are found

### Folklore is preserved more often in archives than in haunted museums

Trinidad and Tobago’s most unusual collection is arguably its folklore itself. Rather than being concentrated in a single attraction, stories of supernatural beings and mysterious encounters survive through books, oral traditions, cultural festivals, educational resources and archival collections.

The University of the West Indies’ archival holdings include material on Trinidad and Tobago folklore featuring figures such as Mama D’Leau, Soucouyants, La Diablesse, Douens, Lagahoo, Pap Bois and Jumbies. These records show that folklore has been treated as cultural heritage rather than simply as entertainment or superstition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archivespace.sta.uwi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/6660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivespace.sta.uwi.edu">[ArchivesSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivespace.sta.uwi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchivesSpaceTrinidad and Tobago Folklore., 2008 &#124; ArchivesSpace Public Interface...</span></span></span>

## The National Museum and Art Gallery: a traditional museum with unusual corners

The National Museum and Art Gallery of Trinidad and Tobago is not a “weird museum” in the commercial sense, but it is one of the most important places for understanding Trinidad and Tobago’s unusual heritage. Founded in 1892 as the Royal Victoria Institute, the institution developed from a colonial-era science and art museum into the country’s national museum. Its collections include art, natural history, geology, petroleum displays, Carnival material and historical objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationalmuseum.gallery/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalmuseum.gallery">[NMAG- The RVI Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalmuseum.gallery</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NMAG- The RVI Blog About – NMAG- The RVI Blog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NMAG- The RVI BlogAbout – NMAG- The RVI BlogAugust 19, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 19, 2023</span></span></span>

Its relevance to curiosity culture comes from the way it preserves things that might otherwise disappear: objects connected with landscapes, rituals, industries and changing ideas about the nation. Carnival traditions, geological specimens and historical collections all sit alongside artistic interpretations of identity and belief. Exhibitions on festivals and rituals have also highlighted the relationship between cultural practices and national memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mccd.gov.tt/tts-festivals-and-rituals-celebrated-in-nmags-exhibition/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mccd.gov.tt">[Culture and Community]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mccd.gov.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Culture and CommunityT&amp;T’s Festivals and Rituals celebrated in NMAG’s Exhibition - Ministry of Culture and Community DevelopmentApril 18...</span></span></span>

The museum therefore represents a different kind of cabinet of curiosity: not a collection of unexplained objects, but a collection of things that explain why certain places, traditions and stories became meaningful.

## The Pitch Lake: a natural curiosity that became a cultural object

La Brea Pitch Lake is one of Trinidad and Tobago’s most unusual visitor experiences. Located in south-west Trinidad, it is a vast natural deposit of asphalt where bitumen reaches the surface, creating a landscape that looks almost artificial. The site is promoted as one of the world’s largest natural asphalt deposits and is visited through guided experiences explaining its geology and history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visittrinidad.tt/attractions/la-brea-pitch-lake/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visittrinidad.tt">[visitTrinbago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visittrinidad.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">visit Trinbago La Brea Pitch Lake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">visitTrinbagoLa Brea Pitch Lake - visitTrinbago...</span></span></span>

The lake is unusual because it sits between science and legend. Geological research explains it as a natural hydrocarbon feature, while Indigenous traditions preserved stories about its origin. Historical accounts recorded by NALIS describe First Peoples legends connected with the lake, including stories of punishment and transformation that explain why the ground opened and became a place of pitch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nalis.gov.tt/blog/winer-postcard-collection-the-la-brea-pitch-lake/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nalis.gov.tt">[NALIS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nalis.gov.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 29, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 29, 2021</span></span></span>

This makes Pitch Lake particularly important within Trinidad and Tobago’s curiosity landscape: it is a real physical anomaly surrounded by layers of human interpretation. Visitors encounter both a scientific landscape and a place shaped by centuries of storytelling.

## Chacachacare: quarantine history, ruins and uncomfortable heritage

For those interested in abandoned places, medical history and darker heritage, Chacachacare Island is one of the country’s most evocative locations. The island was used as a leprosy settlement from the colonial period, with patients isolated there until the site closed in the late twentieth century. Remaining structures, including medical buildings, religious spaces and other ruins, have made it a place of historical interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span>

Chacachacare is sometimes discussed through a ghost-story lens because abandoned hospitals, cemeteries and isolated islands often attract supernatural interpretations. However, the strongest historical interest lies elsewhere: the human stories of illness, stigma, religious care and forced separation. The National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago has documented the island’s quarantine history and the role of Dominican sisters who cared for patients there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span>

Rather than a haunted attraction, Chacachacare is better understood as a preserved landscape of memory — a place where medical history, colonial policy and local imagination overlap.

## Police, crime and institutional curiosities

The Museum of the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service offers a different kind of unusual collection. Instead of folklore or natural mysteries, it preserves the history of policing, including artefacts and stories connected with crime, investigations and public safety. Established in 2004, the museum developed from a small internal collection into a public educational resource.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ttps.gov.tt/museum/about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ttps.gov.tt">[Trinidad and Tobago Police Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ttps.gov.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Trinidad and Tobago Police Service About the Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Trinidad and Tobago Police Service About the Museum</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in crime museums and forensic history, this type of collection provides a window into how societies record disorder and attempt to control it. It also connects with broader curiosity traditions: old uniforms, investigative tools, institutional records and unusual cases often become historical objects once their immediate purpose has passed.

## What makes Trinidad and Tobago’s curiosity culture distinctive?

The country’s unusual heritage is not built around a single attraction dedicated to mysteries. Instead, its curiosities appear across several categories:

* **Folklore collections** preserve supernatural figures as cultural memory rather than physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archivespace.sta.uwi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/6660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivespace.sta.uwi.edu">[ArchivesSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivespace.sta.uwi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchivesSpaceTrinidad and Tobago Folklore., 2008 &#124; ArchivesSpace Public Interface...</span></span></span>
* **Natural wonders** such as Pitch Lake combine scientific explanation with traditional stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visittrinidad.tt/attractions/la-brea-pitch-lake/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visittrinidad.tt">[visitTrinbago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visittrinidad.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">visit Trinbago La Brea Pitch Lake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">visitTrinbagoLa Brea Pitch Lake - visitTrinbago...</span></span></span>
* **Medical heritage sites** such as Chacachacare preserve difficult histories of isolation and care.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span>
* **National museums and specialist collections** preserve objects linked to science, art, policing and everyday life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationalmuseum.gallery/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalmuseum.gallery">[NMAG- The RVI Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalmuseum.gallery</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NMAG- The RVI Blog About – NMAG- The RVI Blog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NMAG- The RVI BlogAbout – NMAG- The RVI BlogAugust 19, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 19, 2023</span></span></span>

The result is a curiosity landscape where the “strange” is rarely separated from ordinary life. A ghost story may preserve a warning about the forest, a geological site may carry ancestral explanations, and an abandoned medical settlement may reveal forgotten human experiences. Trinidad and Tobago’s unusual collections are therefore less about proving mysteries and more about showing how communities create meaning around the unfamiliar.
