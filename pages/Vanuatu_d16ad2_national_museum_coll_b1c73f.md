## Introduction

The National Museum of [Vanuatu]({{ 'where-vanuatu-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}) is unusual because it does not treat culture as a collection of disconnected artefacts locked behind glass. Instead, it combines archaeological discoveries, ceremonial objects, natural history specimens and living cultural practices within a single institution that continues to work closely with communities across the archipelago. Housed within the Vanuatu Cultural Centre (Vanuatu Kaljoral Senta) in Port Vila, the museum serves not only as a public exhibition space but also as the country's principal centre for documenting, protecting and transmitting traditional knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanuatu.travel/en/things-to-do/culture/vanuatu-cultural-center" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanuatu.travel">[Vanuatu Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanuatu.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel Vanuatu Tourism Office</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vanuatu Tourism Office - Vanuatu Cultural Center - Cultural Heritage Guide...</span></span></span>

For visitors expecting a conventional ethnographic museum, this approach can be surprising. Many of the most remarkable exhibits are important precisely because they remain part of living cultural traditions. Demonstrations, storytelling and knowledge-sharing are often as significant as the physical objects themselves, making the museum an example of how collections can preserve culture without freezing it in the past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanuatu.travel/en/things-to-do/culture/vanuatu-cultural-center" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanuatu.travel">[Vanuatu Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanuatu.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel Vanuatu Tourism Office</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vanuatu Tourism Office - Vanuatu Cultural Center - Cultural Heritage Guide...</span></span></span>

## What makes the National Museum so different?

Unlike museums that separate archaeology, natural history and anthropology into different departments, Vanuatu's National Museum presents them as interconnected evidence of how people have lived across the islands for more than three millennia. The collections include ceremonial masks, slit gongs, model canoes, pottery, woven objects, shell artefacts, biological specimens, geological material and archaeological finds recovered from important sites throughout the country.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Vanuatu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Museum of Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Museum of Vanuatu</span></span></span>

This reflects the philosophy of the wider Vanuatu Cultural Centre, whose responsibilities extend well beyond exhibition galleries. The institution also manages the National Archives, National Library, Film and Sound Unit, cultural site surveys and an extensive network of local cultural fieldworkers who document traditions within their own communities. Rather than viewing museum collections as isolated historical objects, the institution treats them as part of continuing relationships between places, families and customary authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanuatu.travel/en/things-to-do/culture/vanuatu-cultural-center" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanuatu.travel">[Vanuatu Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanuatu.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel Vanuatu Tourism Office</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vanuatu Tourism Office - Vanuatu Cultural Center - Cultural Heritage Guide...</span></span></span>

For travellers interested in unusual collections, this means the museum's appeal lies less in spectacular curiosities than in understanding how apparently ordinary objects can carry complex ceremonial, historical and social meanings.

## Masks, canoes, fossils and archaeological finds

The museum's galleries bring together objects that illustrate the remarkable diversity of Vanuatu's islands.

Among the most visually striking are ceremonial masks and ritual carvings originating from different islands, many of which were traditionally associated with initiation ceremonies, rank-taking systems or ancestral commemorations. Their unusual forms can appear mysterious to outsiders, but museum interpretation places them within their cultural context rather than presenting them as exotic oddities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Vanuatu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Museum of Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Museum of Vanuatu</span></span></span>

Equally important are model canoes, traditional navigation equipment and objects connected with everyday island life. These displays demonstrate how maritime knowledge shaped settlement across an archipelago of more than eighty islands, linking practical technology with ceremonial traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanuatu.travel/en/things-to-do/culture/vanuatu-cultural-center" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanuatu.travel">[Vanuatu Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanuatu.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel Vanuatu Tourism Office</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vanuatu Tourism Office - Vanuatu Cultural Center - Cultural Heritage Guide...</span></span></span>

The archaeological collections extend the story much further back in time. Pottery from the Lapita culture documents the earliest known settlement of Vanuatu over 3,000 years ago, while later ceramics and excavation archives help trace cultural change across successive generations. The museum also preserves photographs and excavation records from nationally significant archaeological projects, including Chief Roi Mata's burial site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Vanuatu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Museum of Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Museum of Vanuatu</span></span></span>

One of the museum's less expected collections comes from natural history. Geological holdings include fossils and bones of extinct horned turtles belonging to the meiolaniid family, excavated at the important Lapita site of Teouma. These remains provide rare evidence of interactions between Vanuatu's first settlers and now-extinct island megafauna, connecting natural history directly with human history rather than displaying it separately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Vanuatu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Museum of Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Museum of Vanuatu</span></span></span>

## Why sand drawings are living museum objects

Perhaps the museum's most distinctive feature is its treatment of Vanuatu's famous sand drawings.

These intricate geometric designs are not simply decorative patterns. Created by tracing a single continuous line across sand, ash or fine earth, they function as a sophisticated visual language used to communicate stories, genealogies, rituals, navigation, farming knowledge, architecture, choreography and oral history. A completed drawing is only one part of the tradition; the accompanying explanation, performance and cultural knowledge are equally important.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vanuatu-sand-drawings-00073?RL=00073" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICH UNESCOVanuatu sand drawings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

Because the drawings disappear almost immediately after they are made, the museum cannot preserve them in the conventional sense. Instead, it preserves the practice by inviting skilled practitioners to demonstrate the tradition, explain its meanings and teach visitors. In effect, the cultural performance becomes part of the collection.

This approach has international recognition. UNESCO inscribed Vanuatu sand drawings on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008, emphasising that the tradition is a multifunctional form of communication rather than merely an artistic style. UNESCO has also warned that reducing sand drawings to attractive tourist decoration risks stripping away their deeper symbolic and educational roles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vanuatu-sand-drawings-00073?RL=00073" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICH UNESCOVanuatu sand drawings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

The museum has actively embraced this philosophy. UNESCO-supported safeguarding projects included plans for permanent interactive displays and educational programmes that allow the tradition to remain a living practice rather than becoming a static historical exhibit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/projects/safeguarding-of-vanuatu-sand-drawings-00022" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICH UNESCOSafeguarding of Vanuatu Sand Drawings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

International collaborations reinforce this model. Recent exhibitions developed jointly with the Museum of International Folk Art in New Mexico have centred on contemporary practitioners, with museum educator Edgar Hinge creating sand drawings live for audiences rather than relying solely on photographs or preserved diagrams. The exhibitions also explored the relationship between historical collections and the communities from which they originated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nmculture.org/exhibit/6452/2/museum-resources/museums/libraries/preservation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nmculture.org">[New Mexico Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nmculture.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Mexico Culture Exhibit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Mexico Culture Exhibit</span></span></span>

## Earthquake recovery and changing visitor access

## Why the museum matters in Vanuatu's landscape of unusual collections


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

For readers seeking strange museums or unusual collections, the National Museum of Vanuatu stands apart because its most remarkable exhibits are not necessarily the rarest artefacts. Its real distinction lies in refusing to separate objects from the communities that created them.

Masks, fossils, archaeological discoveries and ceremonial objects certainly attract attention, but they gain their significance through interpretation alongside oral traditions, demonstrations and the continuing authority of cultural knowledge holders.       button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
