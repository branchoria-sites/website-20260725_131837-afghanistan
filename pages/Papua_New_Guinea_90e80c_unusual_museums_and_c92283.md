## Introduction

[Papua New Guinea]({{ 'where-papua-new-guinea-keeps-its/' | relative_url }}) does not have a dedicated museum of the bizarre in the style found in some other countries. Instead, its most unusual visitor experiences are spread across national museums, community collections and open-air relic sites where ceremonial objects, archaeological finds and wartime remains are displayed alongside more conventional historical material. The result is a distinctive curiosity trail in which sacred masks, carved ancestor figures, ancient obsidian tools, volcanic relics and rusting Second World War machinery all appear within the same wider heritage landscape.

For travellers interested in unusual collections rather than mainstream art galleries, the country's appeal lies in authenticity rather than spectacle. Many displays preserve objects that were once active parts of ritual life or were recovered directly from battlefields, villages and archaeological excavations. Others remain small, locally managed collections with limited conservation resources, making them fascinating but sometimes unpredictable to visit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenational.com.pg/hidden-treasures-of-the-national-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenational.com.pg">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenational.com.pg</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Hidden treasures of the National Museum &#124; The National</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The NationalHidden treasures of the National Museum &#124; The NationalOctober 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span>

## Where Can You See Papua New Guinea's Curiosities?

### National and regional museum collections

The National Museum and Art Gallery in Port Moresby is the country's most important destination for unusual historical collections. Although it covers archaeology, anthropology, natural history and art, many visitors are drawn to the ceremonial objects that would appear extraordinary almost anywhere else. Displays include towering spirit figures, elaborate masks, carved canoes, ritual drums, decorated shields and body ornaments originating from many different cultural traditions across Papua New Guinea.

What makes the museum especially interesting is that the public galleries represent only a small proportion of its holdings. Behind the scenes are extensive collections of fragile Malagan masks from New Ireland, ancient stone axes, obsidian tools several thousand years old, pottery, musical instruments and colonial-era manuscripts that are rarely exhibited because of conservation and space limitations. Rather than presenting these objects as curiosities alone, the museum increasingly interprets them as evidence of trade, belief systems and regional identities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenational.com.pg/hidden-treasures-of-the-national-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenational.com.pg">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenational.com.pg</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Hidden treasures of the National Museum &#124; The National</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The NationalHidden treasures of the National Museum &#124; The NationalOctober 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span>

The J. K. McCarthy Museum in Goroka offers a more intimate experience and is often regarded as one of the country's most distinctive regional museums. Its collections combine Highlands material culture with archaeological finds, colonial photographs and wartime artefacts. Visitors may encounter ceremonial weapons, traditional musical instruments, pottery and mourning objects that can appear unusual to outsiders but are presented within their cultural context rather than as sensational exhibits.

The museum also includes aviation relics and Second World War material recovered from crash sites, creating an unusual combination of ethnographic collections and military archaeology under one roof. Compared with larger national institutions, its displays often retain the character of a regional historical collection assembled over decades rather than a highly curated modern exhibition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pacificwrecks.com/restore/png/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pacificwrecks.com">[Pacific Wrecks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pacificwrecks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pacific WrecksPacific Wrecks - Papua New Guinea (PNG) Museums, Memorials &amp; Displays...</span></span></span>

The Massim Museum in Alotau represents another variation on Papua New Guinea's curiosity culture. Rather than focusing on dramatic individual artefacts, it introduces visitors to the ceremonial traditions of Milne Bay Province through carvings, storyboards and locally significant objects connected with the famous Kula exchange network. Although modest in size, it provides valuable context for ceremonial shell valuables and ritual items that are otherwise scattered across major museums worldwide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tripadvisor.co.uk/Attractions-g294115-Activities-c49-Papua_New_Guinea.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tripadvisor.co.uk">[Tripadvisor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tripadvisor.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Attractions g294115 Activities c49 Papua New Guinea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE 5 BEST Museums in Papua New Guinea (2026) - Tripadvisor...</span></span></span>

## Wartime relics around Kokopo and Rabaul

East New Britain offers one of the country's most unusual museum landscapes because the collections extend well beyond museum buildings.

The Kokopo War Museum preserves aircraft parts, artillery pieces, military vehicles, weapons and equipment recovered from the surrounding battlefields. Much of the appeal comes from seeing genuine objects recovered from the tropical landscape rather than polished military displays. Weathering, corrosion and improvised preservation techniques are obvious, giving the collection an atmosphere closer to an archaeological salvage yard than a conventional museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://papuanewguinea.travel/stories/best-places-to-visit-in-papua-new-guinea-for-remembrance-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: papuanewguinea.travel">[Papua New Guinea]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">papuanewguinea.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on papuanewguinea.travel.</span></span></span>

Nearby, the New Guinea Club and Rabaul Museum combines military history with volcanic history and colonial-era collections. Exhibits cover the Japanese occupation, the sinking of the *Montevideo Maru*, the 1994 volcanic eruption and earlier colonial administration. Visitors frequently note that many photographs, documents and relics are historically significant despite limited conservation resources, reflecting the practical difficulties of preserving artefacts in a humid tropical environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tripadvisor.co.uk/Attractions-g294115-Activities-c49-Papua_New_Guinea.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tripadvisor.co.uk">[Tripadvisor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tripadvisor.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Attractions g294115 Activities c49 Papua New Guinea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE 5 BEST Museums in Papua New Guinea (2026) - Tripadvisor...</span></span></span>

Beyond the museum buildings themselves, Rabaul contains numerous visitor-accessible relic sites, including Japanese tunnels, defensive bunkers and abandoned military positions. These are effectively open-air collections where the surrounding landscape forms part of the historical display. Some locations remain only lightly interpreted, making local guides especially valuable for understanding their significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pacificwrecks.com/restore/png/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pacificwrecks.com">[Pacific Wrecks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pacificwrecks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pacific WrecksPacific Wrecks - Papua New Guinea (PNG) Museums, Memorials &amp; Displays...</span></span></span>

## Community museums, access and conservation

One characteristic of Papua New Guinea's unusual collections is that many are managed by small institutions, provincial organisations or community groups rather than large national museum networks.

This creates both opportunities and challenges. Smaller museums often preserve objects closely connected to local communities and may present traditions that receive little attention elsewhere. Displays can feel personal and locally grounded, with guides explaining how ceremonial items, carvings or historical relics relate to living cultural practices rather than distant history.

However, visitors should also expect practical limitations. Opening hours may vary, photography policies differ between institutions and some collections have only minimal conservation facilities. Fragile wooden carvings, fibre masks and wartime metalwork all deteriorate rapidly in Papua New Guinea's tropical climate, and funding for preservation remains uneven. ## What makes these collections unusual?

Papua New Guinea's curiosity landscape differs from countries that specialise in museums devoted to ghosts, witchcraft or medical oddities. Its most remarkable collections are unusual because they preserve objects that originally had powerful ceremonial, social or historical functions rather than being created as curiosities.

Visitors are therefore more likely to encounter:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Sacred ceremonial masks and ancestor figures once used in ritual performances.
* Ancient archaeological artefacts illustrating some of the Pacific's earliest long-distance trade networks.
* Traditional weapons, ornaments and ceremonial instruments displayed alongside explanations of their cultural roles.
* Extensive Second World War relics preserved close to the battlefields where they were abandoned.
* Community collections that remain closely linked to living cultural traditions rather than detached museum narratives.

</div>

This combination of anthropology, archaeology and wartime history gives Papua New Guinea a distinctive form of museum tourism. Rather than concentrating unusual material in a single attraction, the country's curiosities are dispersed across national institutions, regional museums and historic landscapes, rewarding visitors who explore beyond the capital and approach each collection as part of a wider cultural story rather than a catalogue of oddities.
