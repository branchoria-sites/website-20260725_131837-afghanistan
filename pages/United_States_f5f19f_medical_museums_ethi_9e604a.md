## Introduction

Medical museums occupy an unusual place in the [United States]({{ 'united-states/' | relative_url }})' landscape of curious collections. They preserve genuine anatomical specimens, preserved organs, skeletons, pathological preparations and surgical artefacts that have transformed medical education, while also raising difficult questions about whether the dead should become museum exhibits. Today, the debate is no longer simply whether these collections are scientifically valuable. Instead, it centres on consent, provenance, cultural respect and the responsibilities museums owe to the individuals whose bodies they preserve. Recent policy changes at major American institutions show that the question is no longer whether ethics matter, but how museums can balance education, research and public access with dignity and accountability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muttermuseum.org/our-policy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muttermuseum.org">[Mutter Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muttermuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span></span></span>

Rather than abandoning human remains altogether, many American institutions are rethinking why they are displayed, how they are interpreted and whether they should remain on view at all. This shift has made medical museums an important case study in how curiosity, science and ethics intersect.

## From teaching collections to public attractions

Most American medical collections began as teaching resources rather than tourist destinations. During the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, medical schools and hospitals assembled skeletons, preserved organs, foetal specimens and pathological preparations to train surgeons and physicians. At a time when photography and advanced imaging did not exist, studying real human anatomy was considered essential.

As medical history became a subject of public interest, some teaching collections gradually opened to visitors. The Mütter Museum in Philadelphia is the best-known example. Founded around the teaching collection donated by surgeon Thomas Dent Mütter, it presents thousands of authentic medical specimens illustrating disease, injury, congenital conditions and the history of surgery. Unlike horror attractions or fictional "cabinet of curiosities", the museum has always presented itself as an educational institution connected to the College of Physicians of Philadelphia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muttermuseum.org/our-policy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muttermuseum.org">[Mutter Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muttermuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span></span></span>

That transition from classroom to public gallery fundamentally changes the ethical questions involved. Material originally collected for medical instruction may now be viewed by tourists, photographed for social media and interpreted outside its original educational setting.

## What authentic specimens can reveal

Medical museums argue that genuine human remains can communicate ideas that models or digital reconstructions cannot.

Authentic specimens can help visitors understand:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the physical effects of disease before modern treatment;
* congenital conditions and anatomical variation;
* the history of surgery and medical innovation;
* changing ideas about disability and bodily difference;
* the realities of epidemics and occupational disease.

</div>

Seeing genuine pathological material often reminds visitors that medicine is ultimately concerned with real human lives rather than abstract scientific concepts. Many educators argue that authentic specimens encourage empathy when carefully interpreted, particularly when exhibitions explain who an individual was rather than reducing them to an anonymous body part.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muttermuseum.org/our-policy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muttermuseum.org">[Mutter Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muttermuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span></span></span>

Modern museums increasingly attempt to move beyond presenting specimens simply as medical anomalies. Labels are more likely to discuss patients' biographies, historical context and the limitations of medical practice during their lifetimes.

## Why consent has become the central ethical question

The greatest challenge facing American medical museums is that many historical collections were assembled before modern standards of informed consent existed.

Numerous nineteenth-century anatomical collections drew material from:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* hospital post-mortems;
* unclaimed bodies;
* poorhouses and prisons;
* dissecting rooms supplied under historical anatomy laws;
* donations made with limited documentation.

</div>

Practices that were legal at the time may not meet present-day ethical expectations. In many cases museums simply cannot establish whether individuals agreed to public display, scientific study or permanent preservation.

This uncertainty has shifted museum ethics away from asking, "Is this legal?" towards asking, "Is continued display justified?" Provenance—the documented history of how a specimen entered a collection—has therefore become almost as important as the specimen itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muttermuseum.org/our-policy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muttermuseum.org">[muttermuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muttermuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span></span></span>

## The Mütter Museum's reassessment

The Mütter Museum illustrates how American medical museums are changing rather than simply closing displays.

Following an extensive review involving museum staff, outside bioethics specialists and community consultation, the College of Physicians introduced comprehensive human remains policies in 2025. These policies emphasise:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* careful documentation of provenance;
* respectful interpretation;
* stricter rules governing photography;
* significant restrictions on acquiring additional human remains;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[britishmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* exhibition decisions guided by ethical review rather than historical custom.

</div>

The museum has also stated that future acquisitions of human remains will generally require informed consent through living donation or documented bequest, replacing older collecting practices that would no longer be considered acceptable. Rather than removing all specimens, the institution intends to provide fuller historical context, including acknowledging cases where consent is uncertain or impossible to verify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muttermuseum.org/our-policy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muttermuseum.org">[Mutter Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muttermuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span></span></span>

This reflects a broader trend across American museums: retaining educational value while recognising that collections themselves are products of historical inequalities and changing medical ethics.

## Provenance matters as much as preservation

Modern museum practice increasingly treats provenance research as an ethical responsibility rather than simply a cataloguing exercise.

Questions now asked about individual specimens include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Who was this person?
* How did the remains enter the collection?
* Was consent documented?
* Are descendants or descendant communities identifiable?
* Does continued display serve a genuine educational purpose?

</div>

Sometimes provenance research reveals unexpected stories that reshape exhibitions. Anonymous specimens may become identifiable individuals with documented biographies. In other cases, investigations reveal that records are incomplete or that earlier collecting reflected discriminatory scientific practices.

Rather than diminishing scientific value, this additional context often makes exhibitions more historically honest by explaining how medicine itself has evolved.

## Indigenous remains and repatriation

The strongest legal obligations concern Native American remains.

The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA) requires many federally funded museums and institutions to identify Native American human remains and, where appropriate, return them to affiliated tribes or descendants. The Smithsonian operates under separate legislation—the National Museum of the American Indian Act—but has pursued extensive repatriation alongside broader ethical reforms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.si.edu/collections/human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: si.edu">[Smithsonian Institution]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on si.edu.</span></span></span>

The Smithsonian has acknowledged that parts of its historical collections were assembled during periods associated with scientific racism and colonial collecting. Recent institutional policy now emphasises:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* consultation with descendant communities;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.penn.museum/about/statements-and-policies/statement-on-human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: penn.museum">[penn.museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">penn.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* ethical return alongside legal repatriation;
* restrictions on research;
* higher approval standards for display;
* documented informed consent for any future acquisition of human remains except in exceptional circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naturalhistory.si.edu/research/anthropology/programs/irdp/resources/sd-600-chapter" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naturalhistory.si.edu">[Smithsonian Natural History Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naturalhistory.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Natural History MuseumSD 600 Chapter 16.3: Human Remains Not Subject to the NMAI Act &#124; Smithsonian National Museum of Natural...</span></span></span>

</div>

This distinction between legal repatriation and broader ethical return has become increasingly important. Museums may choose to return remains even when no legal requirement exists if continued retention is considered ethically unjustified.

## Should museums still display human remains?

There is no universal answer, even among museum professionals.

Arguments supporting continued display include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* preserving important medical evidence;
* educating healthcare professionals and the public;
* documenting diseases that are now rare;
* encouraging reflection on mortality and medical history.

</div>

Arguments for limiting or removing displays include:

* absence of meaningful consent;
* reducing people to anonymous objects;
* historical links to exploitation and discrimination;
* changing public expectations regarding dignity and respect.

Many American institutions are therefore adopting a middle position. Instead of displaying remains primarily because they are visually striking, museums increasingly require that each display have a clear educational purpose, careful interpretation and ethical justification that outweighs its potential to sensationalise the dead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muttermuseum.org/our-policy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muttermuseum.org">[Mutter Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muttermuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutter Museum Human Remains and Image Policy &#124; Mütter Museum</span></span></span>

## A changing model for America's medical museums

The debate surrounding human remains has altered how visitors experience America's most unusual medical collections. What once appeared to be straightforward displays of anatomy are increasingly accompanied by discussions of consent, historical injustice, record-keeping and museum responsibility.

For visitors interested in unusual museums, this shift is significant. The fascination no longer lies solely in seeing rare pathological specimens, but in understanding how museums themselves are confronting uncomfortable aspects of their own history. In the United States, medical museums remain places where remarkable scientific collections survive, yet they are also becoming spaces in which institutions publicly examine the ethics of collecting, displaying and interpreting the human body.

    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
