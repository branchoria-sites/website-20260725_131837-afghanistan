## Introduction

The Lusaka National Museum’s witchcraft exhibit is [Zambia]({{ 'where-zambias-strangest-collections/' | relative_url }})’s best-known collection devoted to objects associated with magic, protection and supernatural beliefs. Rather than presenting proof that witchcraft works, the display functions as a cultural record of how Zambian communities have understood danger, misfortune, power and protection. It is unusual because it places objects connected with feared or respected spiritual practices inside a national museum, where they can be examined as part of social history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/lusaka-national-museum-s-witchcraft-exhibit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Lusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in Lusaka</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraLusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in LusakaMarch 13, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2014</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in strange collections, the attraction is not a “museum of spells” in the fantasy sense. The exhibit is better understood as a collection about belief systems: the meanings people attach to charms, ritual objects and accusations of supernatural influence. It sits at the boundary between folklore, anthropology, religion, law and modern debates about how societies interpret unexplained events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zm.chm-cbd.net/lusaka-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zm.chm-cbd.net">[zm.chm-cbd.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zm.chm-cbd.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span></span></span>

## Inside Zambia’s Witchcraft Collection: Belief and Reality

The witchcraft section forms one part of the Lusaka National Museum’s wider cultural history displays, alongside ethnography, history and contemporary art. Its importance comes from documenting a subject that is often discussed privately but has played a visible role in social life, politics and legal debates in Zambia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zm.chm-cbd.net/lusaka-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zm.chm-cbd.net">[zm.chm-cbd.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zm.chm-cbd.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span></span></span>

The collection presents objects that have been associated with witchcraft beliefs, including charms and items said to have protective or harmful powers. Descriptions of these objects reflect the beliefs surrounding them: they show what people have claimed objects could do, not scientific confirmation that supernatural effects occurred. This distinction is central to understanding the exhibit. A museum can preserve evidence of a belief without endorsing the belief itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/lusaka-national-museum-s-witchcraft-exhibit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Lusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in Lusaka</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraLusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in LusakaMarch 13, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2014</span></span></span>

### Objects linked with magic and protection

Among the better-known items associated with the exhibit are objects described through traditional stories of magical power. Visitors have encountered displays involving dolls linked with curses, protective or harmful charms, and unusual ritual objects whose meanings depend on cultural interpretation rather than physical appearance alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/lusaka-national-museum-s-witchcraft-exhibit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Lusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in Lusaka</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraLusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in LusakaMarch 13, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2014</span></span></span>

A particularly striking feature of such collections is that ordinary materials can become powerful symbols. A small figure, animal part or handmade object may be interpreted as a source of protection, a tool of harm or a sign of hidden intentions depending on the social setting in which it appears. Researchers studying Zambian witchcraft-related collections have noted that objects associated with these beliefs have changed over time, sometimes incorporating modern manufactured items alongside older materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scholarship.law.cornell.edu/zssj/vol5/iss1/5/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scholarship.law.cornell.edu">[Scholarship@Cornell Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scholarship.law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cornell.edu.</span></span></span>

This changing material culture is one reason the Lusaka collection is significant. It does not simply preserve an image of a distant past; it reflects how ideas about supernatural power adapt alongside economic change, technology and new forms of authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scholarship.law.cornell.edu/zssj/vol5/iss1/5/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scholarship.law.cornell.edu">[Scholarship@Cornell Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scholarship.law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cornell.edu.</span></span></span>

## How Museums Interpret Witchcraft Beliefs

The challenge for a museum displaying witchcraft-related material is interpretation. The objects may be historically important because people believed in their power, but museums must also avoid presenting supernatural claims as established fact. The Lusaka exhibit’s value lies in showing how communities have explained events such as illness, failed harvests, personal conflicts and sudden misfortune.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zm.chm-cbd.net/lusaka-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zm.chm-cbd.net">[zm.chm-cbd.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zm.chm-cbd.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span></span></span>

Anthropologists often study witchcraft beliefs as systems for explaining uncertainty and social tensions. In the Zambian context, research on witchcraft objects has highlighted links between accusations of supernatural harm and concerns about family relationships, wealth, health, success and vulnerability. Objects connected with witchcraft can therefore reveal ideas about power and social responsibility as much as ideas about magic itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scholarship.law.cornell.edu/zssj/vol5/iss1/5/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scholarship.law.cornell.edu">[Scholarship@Cornell Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scholarship.law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cornell.edu.</span></span></span>

For a visitor, the most useful way to approach the collection is to ask: “What does this object tell us about the people who made, used or feared it?” rather than simply asking whether the object has supernatural abilities. That approach turns the exhibit from a curiosity display into a window on cultural memory.

## Why Witchcraft Remains a Living Debate in Zambia

The Lusaka collection remains relevant because beliefs surrounding witchcraft have not disappeared from modern Zambian life. Traditional explanations of misfortune continue to exist alongside Christianity, scientific medicine and state institutions. Public debates about witchcraft accusations show that these ideas remain socially powerful, even among people who disagree about their meaning or truth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/20aead81a370584eb4373474212206fe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The case spotlights traditional African beliefs and criticisms of a colonial-era law that criminalizes witchcraft. One example of this continuing tension is Zambia’s legal treatment of witchcraft. The country’s Witchcraft Act, originally introduced in 1914 and later amended, addresses activities connected with claims of witchcraft, including accusations against others and the use of supposed supernatural abilities to deceive or cause harm. The law reflects an attempt to manage the social consequences of witchcraft accusations while also revealing the difficulty of legislating around beliefs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zambialaws.com/w/385-chapter-90witchcraft-act" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zambialaws.com">[Zambia Laws]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zambialaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">385 chapter 90witchcraft act</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zambia LawsWITCHCRAFT ACT: CHAPTER 90May 9, 1914...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 9, 1914</span></span></span>

Recent high-profile cases have brought these questions into public discussion. A case involving allegations that individuals attempted to harm President Hakainde Hichilema through witchcraft drew attention to the continued intersection of traditional beliefs, criminal law and political life in Zambia. ## A Strange Collection Because It Records a Human Question

The fascination of the Lusaka witchcraft exhibit is not that it offers a mysterious answer. Its importance comes from preserving objects connected to one of humanity’s oldest questions: why do bad things happen, and how do people protect themselves?

In that sense, the collection belongs within Zambia’s unusual heritage landscape because it preserves a category of object that is difficult to classify. A charm may be viewed as a spiritual tool by one person, a cultural artefact by a museum curator, and a historical record by a researcher. The tension between these interpretations is exactly what makes the collection unusual.

The exhibit also demonstrates why museums dealing with belief systems require careful explanation. A witchcraft collection can document fear, faith, social conflict and creativity without reducing those experiences to either simple superstition or unquestioned truth.
