## Introduction

The Uri Geller Museum in Old Jaffa is one of [Israel]({{ 'where-israels-strangest-collections/' | relative_url }})’s most unusual modern curiosity collections: a private archive built around a global celebrity, a famous performance style, and decades of arguments about whether extraordinary claims should be believed or tested. Rather than preserving ancient relics or folklore, it preserves the objects, stories and publicity surrounding Uri Geller’s career as a spoon-bending performer who presented his abilities as psychic phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://urigellermuseum.com/about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: urigellermuseum.com">[Urigeller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">urigellermuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Urigeller Museum About Us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Urigeller MuseumAbout Us - Urigeller Museum...</span></span></span>

The museum matters because it sits at the meeting point between entertainment, belief and scepticism. For visitors, the attraction is not only the spectacle of thousands of spoons and celebrity memorabilia, but the larger question behind them: why have claims of telepathy, psychokinesis and unexplained abilities fascinated audiences for so long? Geller’s collection offers a rare physical record of a cultural moment when television, science, magic and paranormal belief became closely intertwined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/uri-geller-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jewishvirtuallibrary.org">[Jewish Virtual Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jewishvirtuallibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jewish Virtual Library Uri Geller Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jewish Virtual LibraryUri Geller MuseumNovember 10, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 10, 2021</span></span></span>

## The story behind the spoon collection

The centrepiece of the museum is Uri Geller’s relationship with the spoon. During the 1970s, Geller became internationally famous for demonstrations in which he appeared to bend spoons, keys and other metal objects using what he described as the power of the mind. The performances turned a simple household object into a symbol of psychic possibility and made spoon bending one of the best-known images associated with modern paranormal culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Geller" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Uri Geller</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uri Geller</span></span></span>

The museum in Old Jaffa transforms that personal symbol into a full collection. It displays the so-called Uri Geller Peace Cadillac, a vehicle decorated with more than 2,000 bent spoons, alongside other objects connected with Geller’s career and his relationships with famous figures. The collection includes items associated with people such as John Lennon, Salvador Dalí, Michael Jackson, Andy Warhol and Albert Einstein, as well as objects linked to Geller’s own life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://urigellermuseum.com/about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: urigellermuseum.com">[Urigeller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">urigellermuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Urigeller Museum About Us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Urigeller MuseumAbout Us - Urigeller Museum...</span></span></span>

The setting adds to the unusual character of the attraction. The museum occupies a restored Ottoman-era building in Old Jaffa, one of Israel’s oldest urban areas, creating an unusual contrast between historic surroundings and a collection devoted to a modern media personality. Outside stands a giant bent spoon sculpture weighing around 11 tonnes, promoted by the museum as the world’s largest spoon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://urigellermuseum.com/about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: urigellermuseum.com">[Urigeller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">urigellermuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Urigeller Museum About Us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Urigeller MuseumAbout Us - Urigeller Museum...</span></span></span>

For a visitor, the collection is best understood less as a traditional scientific museum and more as a personal archive of fame. Its objects preserve the story of how a performer became a cultural figure: the gifts from admirers, the celebrity connections, the publicity moments and the physical symbols that allowed Geller’s image to travel around the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/uri-geller-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jewishvirtuallibrary.org">[Jewish Virtual Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jewishvirtuallibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jewish Virtual Library Uri Geller Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jewish Virtual LibraryUri Geller MuseumNovember 10, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 10, 2021</span></span></span>

## Performance, belief and public debate

Uri Geller’s career became part of a much wider debate about psychic claims. His supporters viewed his performances as evidence that human abilities might extend beyond accepted scientific explanations. Geller has continued to maintain that his abilities are genuine and has pointed to experiments and investigations as part of his defence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.urigeller.com/content/research/twgm.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: urigeller.com">[Uri Geller]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">urigeller.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uri Geller</span></span></span>

One important chapter came in the 1970s, when researchers at the Stanford Research Institute in California studied Geller as part of wider interest in so-called extrasensory perception and psychokinesis. These experiments, associated with researchers including Harold Puthoff and Russell Targ, became controversial because supporters argued they showed unusual results, while critics questioned the methods and whether ordinary explanations had been fully excluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.urigeller.com/content/research/twgm.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: urigeller.com">[Uri Geller]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">urigeller.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uri Geller</span></span></span>

The strongest challenge to Geller’s public reputation came from the world of stage magic. Magician and sceptic James Randi argued that Geller’s demonstrations could be reproduced through conventional techniques used by performers, including misdirection and preparation of objects. The famous 1973 appearance on *The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson* became a defining moment in the debate. Before the broadcast, precautions were taken to ensure that props were supplied by the programme rather than by Geller, and his inability to reproduce some of his usual demonstrations intensified arguments about whether the performances involved psychic powers or stage techniques.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2020/oct/25/james-randi-obituary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian James Randi obituary &#124; Magic &#124; The Guardian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianJames Randi obituary &#124; Magic &#124; The GuardianOctober 25, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 25, 2020</span></span></span>

This dispute is what makes the museum culturally interesting. The collection does not simply represent a belief in the paranormal; it also represents the history of public scepticism. Geller became famous partly because people argued about him. His career shows how extraordinary claims can gain attention not only through acceptance, but through controversy and attempts at verification.

## A personal archive turned attraction

The Uri Geller Museum is unusual among Israel’s curiosity collections because it is centred on one living individual’s story rather than a historical period, archaeological discovery or traditional folklore. It functions as a celebrity archive, a performance space and a tourist attraction at the same time. Tours are presented as personal experiences, with the museum promoting guided visits led by Geller himself and displays of objects from across his career.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.urigellermuseum.com/home-test/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: urigellermuseum.com">[Urigeller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">urigellermuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on urigellermuseum.com.</span></span></span>

The collection also shows how modern mysteries are created and preserved. Earlier curiosity cabinets often displayed unusual natural specimens, relics or objects believed to have special powers. Geller’s museum instead preserves the material culture of a modern mystery: the spoon as an icon, the celebrity gift as evidence of influence, and the media appearance as a turning point in public memory.

This makes the museum part of a broader tradition of collections built around contested ideas. Like displays connected with UFO stories, unexplained phenomena or famous hoaxes, its importance lies partly in the questions it raises. A visitor does not need to accept psychic claims to find the collection significant. The museum reveals how belief, entertainment and criticism interact when a mysterious claim becomes part of popular culture.

Within Israel’s landscape of unusual collections, the Uri Geller Museum occupies a distinctive place. It is not a museum of ancient mystery but a museum of a modern one: the story of how a performer, a spoon and a debate about the limits of human ability became an international cultural phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://urigellermuseum.com/about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: urigellermuseum.com">[Urigeller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">urigellermuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Urigeller Museum About Us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Urigeller MuseumAbout Us - Urigeller Museum...</span></span></span>
