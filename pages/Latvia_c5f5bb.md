## Introduction

For visitors, the best unusual collections fall into three broad groups. Riga contains the strongest medically curious and privately collected material. ## Where Latvia’s strangest collections are found

Latvia does not appear to have a major permanent public museum devoted entirely to witchcraft, cryptids or UFOs. Instead, uncanny material tends to be embedded within broader collections. Folk medicine appears inside the Pharmacy Museum; bodily anomalies belong to an academic anatomy collection; ghost stories have attached themselves to a genuine military prison; and legendary exaggeration is interpreted through Baron Munchausen’s documented connection with Latvia.

This gives the country’s curiosity culture an unusually strong tension between evidence and storytelling. The visitor is often standing in an authentic place or looking at a real object, while the interpretation around it may range from medical teaching and political remembrance to folklore, theatrical reconstruction and commercial entertainment.

## The body as a museum object

### RSU Anatomy Museum

The RSU Anatomy Museum is Latvia’s most direct answer to the classic [medical-oddities]({{ 'medical-oddities-e845f3/' | relative_url }}) museum. Its collection contains several thousand objects associated with normal and pathological human anatomy, embryology, congenital anomalies, comparative anatomy and physical anthropology. Much of the historical material was collected during the 1920s and 1930s for medical teaching rather than public spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/en/sight/rsu-anatomy-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[Latvia Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">20-30 years</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The RSU Anatomy Museum &#124; latvia.travelThe RSU Anatomy Museum&#x27;s collection consists of several thousand different and unusual objects - pr...</span></span></span>

Visitors should expect preserved anatomical preparations rather than horror-themed decoration. The unusual force of the museum comes from seeing what is ordinarily restricted to dissecting rooms, operating theatres and specialist textbooks. Specimens illustrating disease or unusual development can be emotionally difficult, but the institutional setting matters: this is a university collection whose purpose is to explain bodily variation and medical knowledge, not to present anonymous human remains as freak-show entertainment.

That distinction makes the museum particularly important within Latvia’s curiosity landscape. It represents the transformation of a closed teaching collection into a modern public museum, while retaining the ethical questions that accompany all displays of human tissue. The objects are scientifically credible; the unease comes from their reality.

### Medicine, pharmacy and folk healing

The Pauls Stradiņš Medicine History Museum grew from the personal collecting and educational work of surgeon and oncologist Pauls Stradiņš. Its institutional history includes teaching collections, pharmaceutical material and elaborate historical dioramas. The museum now also interprets its own development, including the ways earlier exhibitions reflected the priorities and aesthetics of different periods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mvm.lv/en/about-the-museum/museum-history" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mvm.lv">[mvm.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mvm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mvm.lv.</span></span></span>

That self-awareness is useful because old medical museums can blur the line between scholarship and theatre. Reconstructions of ancient healing, dramatic disease models and obsolete instruments are compelling, but they also reveal how previous generations imagined medical progress. The museum is therefore not simply a cabinet of grim instruments. It is a record of how medicine has explained itself to the public.

The associated Pharmacy Museum is smaller and more immediately atmospheric. Its rooms include pharmaceutical vessels, prescriptions, moulds and equipment for preparing medicines, alongside an eighteenth- and nineteenth-century laboratory, historical Latvian products and a reconstruction connected with traditional healing. The collection traces its roots to private pharmaceutical collections assembled before the Second World War and later incorporated into the national medical museum system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.farmacijasmuzejs.lv/muzeja-vesture/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: farmacijasmuzejs.lv">[farmacijasmuzejs.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">farmacijasmuzejs.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">muzeja vesture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">muzeja vesture</span></span></span>

For readers interested in alchemy or witchcraft, the Pharmacy Museum is one of Latvia’s most relevant public collections, but it should not be mislabelled as an occult museum. Its alchemist’s laboratory and folk-healer’s setting explain the overlap between household remedies, learned pharmacy and older medical belief. They do not present supernatural claims as established fact.

## Soviet spaces that survived almost intact

### The bunker beneath Līgatne

The secret Soviet bunker at Līgatne is among Latvia’s strongest preserved Cold War curiosities. Hidden beneath a rehabilitation centre and built during the 1980s, the roughly 2,000-square-metre complex lies about nine metres underground. It was intended as a protected command centre for the political and administrative leadership of Soviet Latvia in the event of nuclear or chemical war.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rehcentrsligatne.lv/en/pakalpojumi/turisms/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rehcentrsligatne.lv">[rehcentrsligatne.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rehcentrsligatne.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rehcentrsligatne.lv.</span></span></span>

Its strangeness comes from the clash between secrecy and bureaucracy. Rather than a ruined battlefield structure, visitors encounter the practical machinery of planned survival: communications rooms, maps, ventilation and power systems, meeting spaces and period furnishings. Much of the atmosphere derives from the survival of the complex as a coherent environment rather than from a collection of isolated objects.

The bunker is now visitor-facing and generally experienced on a guided tour. Some offerings deliberately add period flavour, including Soviet-style food. That commercial layer does not make the bunker inauthentic, but it can soften the political reality of the site. The original purpose was not entertainment; it was the continuation of government during a catastrophe in which most of the surrounding population would have had no comparable protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rehcentrsligatne.lv/en/cenas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rehcentrsligatne.lv">[Līgatnes Rehabilitācijas Centrs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rehcentrsligatne.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rehcentrsligatne.lv.</span></span></span>

### The Corner House

The Corner House in central Riga served as the headquarters of the Soviet security police in Latvia. Today the Museum of the Occupation of Latvia maintains an exhibition on the institution’s activities and conducts guided visits through areas including the basement, courtyard and detention cells.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muzeji.lv/en/museum-catalogue/museum/the-kgb-building-the-corner-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muzeji.lv">[muzeji.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muzeji.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title"># The KGB Building – The &quot;Corner House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">61 Brīvības Street, Riga, LV 1010... The Museum of the Occupation of Latvia has created an exhibition “Cheka’s Operation in Latvia” in t...</span></span></span>

This is a dark-history site rather than a conventional object museum. The building itself is the principal exhibit. Corridors, interrogation spaces and cells make political repression physical and immediate in a way that documents displayed in a neutral gallery cannot.

It should not be approached as a “scary attraction” even though it is unsettling. Its authority comes from institutional research, survivor history and the material fabric of an actual security-police building. The guided element is especially important because empty rooms alone cannot explain who was imprisoned, how the system operated or why the site occupies such a powerful place in Latvian memory.

## Karosta Prison: history, theatre and ghosts

Karosta Prison in Liepāja began as a military detention facility within the fortified naval district developed by the Russian Empire. The building continued to serve successive military and political systems, and it is now open to visitors through guided tours. Its preserved cells and punishment spaces make it one of Latvia’s most severe visitor experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/sites/default/files/download/2016/pdfs/Kurzeme_EN_Y16.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[latvia.travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kurzeme EN Y16</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kurzeme EN Y16</span></span></span>

Unlike the Corner House, Karosta openly combines historical interpretation with performance tourism. Depending on the programme, visitors may encounter costumed role-play, escape activities, a Soviet-style canteen or more intense experiences based on prison discipline. The site has also offered overnight stays and staged “behind bars” programmes. These activities can make history memorable, but they turn incarceration into a product, creating an unavoidable tension between education and spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/sites/default/files/download/2016/pdfs/Kurzeme_EN_Y16.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[Latvia Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kurzeme EN Y16</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kurzeme EN Y16</span></span></span>

Karosta is also marketed through ghost stories, particularly the legend of a “White Lady”. Paranormal television attention helped amplify its reputation, and the site itself repeats claims that investigators described it as exceptionally haunted. Reports of apparitions remain folklore and visitor testimony, not historical or scientific proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.karosta.lv/sakums-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karosta.lv">[karosta.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karosta.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on karosta.lv.</span></span></span>

The prison is therefore a useful test of credibility. Its military history and surviving architecture are real. Its theatrical tours are openly staged. Its ghost narrative belongs to legend and tourism marketing. Keeping those three layers separate makes the site more interesting, not less.

## A museum devoted to magnificent lies

The Munchausen Museum at Dunte occupies an unusual position between biography, literary tourism and deliberate fantasy. Hieronymus Karl Friedrich von Munchausen was a real eighteenth-century German nobleman who served in the Russian military and spent part of his life in the Latvian region of Vidzeme with his wife. Stories loosely connected with him were later transformed into increasingly impossible adventures published by other writers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.minhauzens.lv/en/muzejs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: minhauzens.lv">[Minhauzens]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">minhauzens.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on minhauzens.lv.</span></span></span>

The museum does not function as a sober documentary archive alone. It invites visitors into the fictional world of impossible journeys, extravagant boasts and comic deception. Wax figures and playful displays extend the experience beyond the surviving biographical evidence.

That approach is appropriate because Munchausen’s cultural importance lies precisely in the collapse of the boundary between person and invented persona. The museum’s objects and reconstructions are not evidence that the Baron flew on a cannonball or escaped impossible dangers. They show how a small connection to a real place can sustain a much larger mythology.

Within Latvia’s unusual-museum landscape, Dunte is the clearest museum of hoax-like storytelling. It celebrates lies without trying to pass them off as truth. Its subject is the cultural pleasure of exaggeration.

## Ritual hats and private collecting

The World of Hat in Riga is a privately founded ethnographic museum centred on traditional and ceremonial headwear from different regions and communities. Its collection includes hats and related objects associated with ritual, social status, occupation, gender and local identity. The museum advertises guided tours and public opening hours, although smaller private institutions are always worth checking directly before travelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://worldhat.lv/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: worldhat.lv">[SIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">worldhat.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on worldhat.lv.</span></span></span>

What makes it more than a costume display is the way headwear can condense an entire social system into one object. Materials, height, decoration and method of wearing may indicate marital status, religious role, profession or ceremonial authority. Some pieces look fantastical to outsiders, but they were not created as curiosities; they belonged to functioning cultural traditions.

This raises a curatorial question common to private ethnographic collections. A spectacular headdress can easily be detached from the people and ceremonies that gave it meaning. Guided interpretation therefore matters. The strongest visit is not simply a parade of bizarre shapes, but an explanation of why apparently eccentric forms made sense within their original communities.

## Folklore without a witchcraft museum

Latvia’s supernatural heritage is more visible in folklore landscapes and local historical interpretation than in a single national museum of witches or monsters. Turaida Museum Reserve, near Sigulda, is the most substantial example. The protected cultural site combines a medieval castle, archaeology, a historic church, manor heritage, material connected with the Liv people of the Gauja region and Folksong Hill, a landscape devoted to Latvia’s traditional songs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ehl-bureau.eu/en/project/turaida-historical-centre/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ehl-bureau.eu">[EHL BUREAU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ehl-bureau.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EHL BUREAUTuraida Historical Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EHL BUREAUTuraida Historical Centre</span></span></span>

Turaida’s legends should be understood as cultural memory rather than documentary reporting. The best-known romantic stories associated with the area have been repeatedly retold, adapted and attached to physical sites. Archaeological collections and architectural remains can be assessed as historical evidence; legends reveal how later communities gave those remains emotional meaning.

This pattern appears across Latvia. Folklore is rarely isolated in a glass case labelled “the supernatural”. It is woven into castles, caves, sacred landscapes, seasonal customs and regional museums. For strange-travel readers, this can be more rewarding than a theatrical witch museum, but it requires attention to categories: an archaeological object is not proof of a legend, and a legend does not need to be literally true to matter.

## What is academic, commercial or contested?

Latvia’s unusual collections become easier to navigate when their different kinds of authority are recognised.

**Academic and scientific collections** include the RSU Anatomy Museum. Its specimens originate in medical teaching and research traditions, and its claims about the human body sit within a professional university framework. The ethical issue is not whether the anatomy is real, but how human remains are acquired, contextualised and displayed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/en/sight/rsu-anatomy-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[Latvia Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">20-30 years</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The RSU Anatomy Museum &#124; latvia.travelThe RSU Anatomy Museum&#x27;s collection consists of several thousand different and unusual objects - pr...</span></span></span>

**State or institutionally curated history** includes the Corner House, the Medicine History Museum and much of Turaida. These sites use collections, archives and preserved places to support historical interpretation. Their exhibitions still reflect curatorial choices, but they are not simply entertainment businesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mvm.lv/en/about-the-museum/exhibition-history" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mvm.lv">[mvm.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mvm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mvm.lv.</span></span></span>

**Authentic sites with commercial performance** include Karosta Prison and the Līgatne bunker. Both preserve genuine environments, but tourism programmes may add role-play, themed meals or deliberately dramatic language. Visitors should distinguish the surviving structure from the experience designed around it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://liepaja.travel/en/see-and-do/karosta-prison/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liepaja.travel">[liepaja.travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liepaja.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on liepaja.travel.</span></span></span>

**Private and collector-led museums** include the World of Hat. Their appeal often comes from the collector’s singular vision, although interpretation may be more personal and less fully documented than in a national institution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://worldhat.lv/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: worldhat.lv">[SIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">worldhat.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on worldhat.lv.</span></span></span>

**Legend-led attractions** include the Munchausen Museum and the ghost culture surrounding Karosta. In these places, fantasy is part of the subject. Credibility depends less on eliminating legend than on making clear where biography or documented history ends and imaginative tradition begins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.minhauzens.lv/en/muzejs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: minhauzens.lv">[Minhauzens]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">minhauzens.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on minhauzens.lv.</span></span></span>

## Planning an unusual-museum route

For a compact Riga itinerary, the strongest pairing is the RSU Anatomy Museum with the Pharmacy Museum. Together they cover human anatomy, disease, old remedies, alchemical imagery and the history of pharmaceutical practice. The Corner House then adds a very different kind of unease: not bodily curiosity, but the preserved architecture of state repression.

Travellers with more time can build two contrasting day trips. Līgatne combines Cold War machinery with the strange normality of an underground government office prepared for nuclear disaster. Dunte offers the opposite mood: a museum where biography dissolves into comic impossibility. Turaida can be linked naturally with either route for folklore, archaeology and the landscape of national memory.

Liepāja deserves more than a hurried visit because Karosta Prison makes most sense within the wider former naval district. The prison’s cells, nearby fortifications and military architecture collectively explain why the building exists. Booking ahead is prudent because access to the prison is guided rather than simply open-room wandering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://liepaja.travel/en/see-and-do/karosta-prison/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liepaja.travel">[liepaja.travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liepaja.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on liepaja.travel.</span></span></span>

Opening arrangements can change, particularly at small private museums and sites dependent on scheduled tours. The Sun Museum, once frequently listed as a Riga curiosity containing hundreds of sun images and objects, is now reported as permanently closed by current museum directories; older tourism pages and reviews remain online and can easily mislead trip planning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whichmuseum.com/museum/sun-museum-riga-7641/opening-hours" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whichmuseum.com">[WhichMuseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whichmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Which Museum Opening Hours</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Which Museum Opening Hours</span></span></span>

## Why Latvia’s curiosity culture matters


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Latvia’s most memorable unusual museums are rarely collections of random oddities. They preserve systems that once controlled, healed, classified, frightened or entertained people. The anatomy museum records how bodies became teaching material. The pharmacy collections show the uncertain border between remedy, craft and science. The bunker and prison sites expose the physical infrastructure of authoritarian power. Turaida demonstrates how landscapes accumulate stories, while Dunte shows how a historical person can disappear inside an endlessly retold fiction.

</div>

The result is a museum culture in which the strange is often inseparable from the serious. Even the most theatrical attractions rest on questions about evidence: whether an object is human tissue, a genuine prison fixture, a reconstructed laboratory, a ritual artefact, a literary prop or a marketing claim about ghosts. Latvia rewards visitors who enjoy the uncanny but also want to know what, precisely, they are looking at—and who created the story around it.

    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
