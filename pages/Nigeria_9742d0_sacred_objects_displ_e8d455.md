## Introduction

Sacred objects often change meaning when they leave the communities that created and used them. In [Nigeria]({{ 'where-nigeria-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}), charms, ancestral figures and ritual objects have been collected by churches, universities and museums that interpret them in very different ways from their original custodians. Rather than serving as active religious objects, they may become evidence of history, examples of craftsmanship, educational exhibits or symbols of cultural identity. That shift is not simply about moving an artefact into a display case. It raises questions about who has the authority to explain the object, whether sacred power is believed to remain within it, and how museums should present objects that some visitors regard as heritage while others see them as religiously dangerous or spiritually significant.

Nigeria offers particularly revealing examples because traditional religions, Christianity and Islam continue to coexist alongside one another. Museums therefore do not merely preserve the past; they become places where competing understandings of sacred objects are negotiated.

## How sacred objects acquire new meanings

When a ritual object enters a museum, three different interpretations often exist at the same time.

## Charms at Madonna University's Elele museum

One of Nigeria's most unusual religious collections is associated with the International Sanctuary of Jesus the Saviour and Mother Mary at Elele in Rivers State, closely linked with Madonna University.

Conference information published by the university describes a **Museum of Charms and Fetish Objects** containing ritual objects surrendered by people who converted from traditional religious practice to Christianity. According to the university, the collection has also been used for academic research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.madonnauniversity.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Conference-Practical-Information.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: madonnauniversity.edu.ng">[Madonna University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">madonnauniversity.edu.ng</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Madonna University International Conference</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Madonna UniversityInternational ConferenceDecember 5, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2023</span></span></span>

This makes the collection distinctive because it occupies two roles simultaneously.

First, it functions as a Christian testimony. Visitors are encouraged to understand the charms as objects abandoned after religious conversion rather than as continuing ritual instruments. Their presence supports a narrative of spiritual transformation.

Secondly, the objects are also treated as ethnographic material. Once catalogued and displayed, they become evidence for researchers studying local religious practice, material culture and changing beliefs. The physical artefacts remain, but their institutional meaning shifts from ritual effectiveness to historical documentation.

That transformation is significant because it demonstrates how museums connected to religious organisations may preserve objects that they simultaneously reject as active religious instruments. Instead of celebrating the original ritual system, the display reframes the objects as records of a previous religious life.

## Ancestral figures and memory at Oron

The National Museum at Oron presents a very different example of reframing sacred objects.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oron_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Oron Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oron Museum</span></span></span>

The museum was established largely to preserve hundreds of **Ekpu** ancestral figures carved by the Oron people. These wooden sculptures originally commemorated deceased ancestors and were closely connected with lineage, remembrance and community identity rather than being decorative artworks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oron_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Oron Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oron Museum</span></span></span>

By the mid-twentieth century, many Ekpu figures had already ceased to be used in their original ritual context. Colonial-era preservation efforts sought to document and rescue surviving carvings before they disappeared completely. Later, theft and extensive losses during the Nigerian Civil War further altered the collection's history, making the museum itself part of the story of preservation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.si.edu/object/epic-ekpu-ancestor-figures-oron-south-east-nigeria%3Asiris_sil_737146" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: si.edu">[Smithsonian Institution]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on si.edu.</span></span></span>

Visitors therefore encounter the sculptures through several overlapping narratives:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* as masterpieces of historic wood carving;
* as records of Oron ancestry and social organisation;
* as survivors of war, looting and dispersal;
* as museum objects whose present identity differs from their original ceremonial role.

</div>

Unlike the Elele collection, the Oron Museum does not primarily reinterpret the figures through Christian conversion. Instead, it presents them as cultural heritage whose importance extends beyond active religious practice. The sacred dimension is acknowledged as part of historical context rather than as an ongoing ritual function.

## Who controls meaning, ownership and display?

These collections raise wider questions extending beyond Nigeria.

### Is preservation the same as respect?

Removing sacred objects from ritual use can protect them from deterioration, theft or destruction. Museums provide climate control, conservation and public access that communities may find difficult to sustain.

Yet preservation also changes the relationship between people and objects. An ancestor figure behind glass is no longer approached in the same way as one standing within a lineage shrine. The object survives physically while its original social role may disappear.

### Who has the authority to interpret?

Museum labels often explain objects using archaeology, anthropology or art history. Community members may instead emphasise genealogy, oral history or continuing spiritual significance.

Religious institutions introduce another perspective, interpreting surrendered ritual objects through narratives of conversion and faith. None of these viewpoints is entirely neutral. Each reflects the institution displaying the collection.

### Should sacred objects be displayed at all?

Some communities welcome museum display because it safeguards endangered heritage and introduces younger generations to local history.

Others argue that ritual objects lose important aspects of their meaning when removed from ceremonies, sacred landscapes or family custodianship. For them, documentation cannot fully replace lived tradition.

These debates increasingly influence museum practice worldwide, encouraging consultation with source communities and greater transparency about how collections were acquired and interpreted.

## A broader Nigerian pattern

The issues visible at Elele and Oron also appear in wider discussions about Nigerian cultural heritage. National museums increasingly present artefacts not simply as artistic masterpieces but as expressions of living cultures whose meanings continue to evolve.

Recent debates surrounding the return of the Benin Bronzes illustrate similar questions. ## Why these collections matter

Nigeria's unusual religious collections reveal that sacred objects rarely remain fixed in meaning. A charm surrendered after conversion, an ancestral carving rescued from neglect or a ritual object preserved by a national museum all continue to exist physically, but they speak differently depending on who is interpreting them.

For visitors interested in Nigeria's stranger collections, this is one of the country's most distinctive themes. The fascination lies not only in the objects themselves but in watching their identities change—from ritual tools to historical artefacts, from family memorials to museum exhibits, and from living religious practice to subjects of scholarship and public debate.

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
