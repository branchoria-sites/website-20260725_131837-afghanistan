## Introduction

[South Korea]({{ 'what-makes-south-koreas-museums-so/' | relative_url }})’s shamanic collections are unusual because many of the objects on display were never created as art in the conventional sense. They were working ritual tools: paintings that invited particular spirits, bells and drums used during ceremonies, costumes worn while mediating between the human and spirit worlds, and paper offerings designed to be burned or discarded after use. Museums preserve these objects because they are culturally significant, but the traditions that gave them meaning are still practised in parts of Korea today. That creates an unusual challenge: how can a museum conserve something whose real significance lies in movement, sound, performance and living belief rather than in the physical object alone?

Rather than presenting Korean shamanism as an extinct religion or an exotic curiosity, the strongest collections place ritual objects within everyday concerns such as illness, family welfare, protection, prosperity and remembrance. The result is a distinctive form of museum experience in which ethnography, art history, folklore and living religious practice overlap rather than sitting in separate categories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nfm.go.kr/english/subIndex/436.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nfm.go.kr">[NFM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nfm.go.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NFMNational Folk Museum of Korea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">영어 &gt; Introduction &gt; Introduction...</span></span></span>

## Can a Museum Preserve a Living Ritual?

The central tension running through Korea's shamanic collections is that museums are designed to preserve stable objects, while shamanic practice is centred on changing relationships between people, spirits, communities and places.

A ceremonial knife displayed behind glass once formed part of an active ritual. A painted spirit image originally functioned as a point of contact during ceremonies rather than as a decorative artwork. Costumes were intended to move through dance, music and dramatic performance. Even paper decorations and offerings were often deliberately temporary, created to be cut, folded, displayed briefly and then destroyed as part of the ceremony itself. Removing such objects from ritual protects them physically but inevitably changes their purpose.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shamanismmuseum.org/sub07/sub01.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shamanismmuseum.org">[Shamanism Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shamanismmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span></span></span>

For this reason, Korean museums increasingly combine physical collections with photographs, field notes, film, sound recordings and oral testimony. These records acknowledge that the ritual cannot be fully captured by preserving objects alone.

## What Korean Shamanic Objects Were Made to Do

Visitors expecting mysterious relics often discover that Korean shamanic collections are remarkably practical. Most objects existed to solve problems rather than simply express belief.

Common collections include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Spirit paintings** used to represent particular divine figures invited during ceremonies.
* **Ritual costumes** whose colours, accessories and changing garments formed part of ceremonial performance.
* **Hand bells, drums and gongs** used to establish rhythm and communication during rituals.
* **Mirrors** that symbolised spiritual authority and were handled during ceremonies.
* **Fans, knives and staffs** carried as ritual implements rather than weapons.
* **Paper flowers and paper decorations**, many intended for temporary ceremonial use.
* **Divination equipment and protective amulets** associated with fortune, health and household wellbeing.

</div>

Unlike many religious museums, these collections emphasise function alongside appearance. Labels frequently explain not simply what an object is, but how it was handled, when it appeared during a ceremony and which kinds of practical concerns—illness, dangerous journeys, business success or family conflict—it addressed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shamanismmuseum.org/sub07/sub01.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shamanismmuseum.org">[Shamanism Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shamanismmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span></span></span>

## Specialist Archives, National Collections and Private Museums

South Korea preserves shamanic heritage through several complementary approaches rather than one definitive institution.

The **Museum of Shamanism** in Seoul was established in 2013 specifically for education and research and later relocated beside the historic Geumseongdang Shamanic Shrine. Its collection numbers roughly 30,000 objects, including ritual paintings, costumes, mirrors, bells, incense burners, paper decorations, ritual tools, divination equipment and extensive photographic and video archives. Its founder, folklorist Yang Jongsung, deliberately designed the museum as both a research centre and a place where visitors could encounter a living cultural tradition rather than a collection of supernatural curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shamanismmuseum.org/sub07/sub01.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shamanismmuseum.org">[Shamanism Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shamanismmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span></span></span>

The **National Folk Museum of Korea** approaches the subject differently. Instead of isolating shamanism, it places ritual life alongside domestic customs, seasonal festivals, agriculture and everyday Korean society. The museum also maintains extensive folklore archives, recognising that recordings, photographs and interviews can be as historically valuable as ritual artefacts themselves. Modernisation has transformed many local traditions, making documentation an important form of preservation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nfm.go.kr/english/subIndex/436.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nfm.go.kr">[NFM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nfm.go.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NFMNational Folk Museum of Korea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">영어 &gt; Introduction &gt; Introduction...</span></span></span>

One of the museum's most significant acquisitions came through folklorist **Kim Taegon**, whose family donated more than 31,700 items in 2012 after decades of nationwide fieldwork. The donation included spirit paintings, ceremonial clothing, ritual equipment, notebooks, photographs and films documenting ceremonies that had already disappeared or were rapidly changing. In several cases, these recordings later helped reconstruct rituals that would otherwise have been lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nfm.go.kr/user/bbs/english/17/469/bbsDataView/7560.do?page=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nfm.go.kr">[NFM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nfm.go.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NFMNational Folk Museum of Korea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">영어 &gt; Exhibitions &gt; Special Exhibition 상세정보 페이지April 22, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2015</span></span></span>

## Why Field Records Matter as Much as Ritual Objects

Kim Taegon's archive illustrates why Korean shamanic collections extend far beyond display cases.

Beginning in the 1960s, he travelled widely to record ceremonies before urbanisation and social change altered local traditions. His notebooks documented ritual sequences, while photographs captured shrine arrangements and costumes. Film preserved music, dance and performance that static objects cannot communicate.

One frequently cited example concerns the recording of a Seoul village ritual that had ceased by the early 1970s. Because detailed film documentation survived, researchers and practitioners later had sufficient evidence to reconstruct elements of the ceremony. Without those audiovisual records, the surviving ritual objects would have revealed only part of the tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nfm.go.kr/user/bbs/english/17/469/bbsDataView/7560.do?page=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nfm.go.kr">[NFM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nfm.go.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NFMNational Folk Museum of Korea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">영어 &gt; Exhibitions &gt; Special Exhibition 상세정보 페이지April 22, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2015</span></span></span>

This emphasis on documentation reflects a broader shift within folklore museums: preserving intangible cultural heritage increasingly requires preserving knowledge, performance and memory alongside physical artefacts.

## What Display Cases Preserve — and What They Leave Out

Museum collections successfully preserve craftsmanship, regional artistic styles and historical evidence, but several essential aspects of Korean shamanism inevitably remain outside the gallery.

A visitor cannot experience:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the soundscape created by chanting, percussion and singing;
* the interaction between the ritual specialist and participants;
* changing emotional states during ceremonies;
* offerings that were intended to be consumed or destroyed;
* the social role of rituals within families or neighbourhoods.

</div>

Many exhibitions therefore avoid presenting shamanic objects as magical curiosities in their own right. Instead, they explain that these objects gained significance through relationships and performance. A beautifully painted spirit image may have artistic value, but within ritual it functioned primarily as part of communication between visible and invisible worlds rather than as an independent artwork.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nfm.go.kr/user/bbs/english/17/469/bbsDataView/7540.do?bbsDataCategory=&amp;column=&amp;page=7&amp;search=&amp;searchEDate=&amp;searchSDate=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nfm.go.kr">[NFM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nfm.go.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NFMNational Folk Museum of Korea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">영어 &gt; Exhibitions &gt; Special Exhibition 상세정보 페이지...</span></span></span>

## A Living Tradition Rather Than a Fossil

Perhaps the most distinctive feature of Korea's shamanic collections is that they document a tradition that continues to evolve.

Museums acknowledge that ceremonies are still performed in parts of South Korea, although practice varies widely by region, family tradition and individual ritual specialists. This makes curatorial decisions unusually sensitive. Displaying ritual equipment solely as historical artefacts risks implying that the beliefs belong entirely to the past, while presenting them only as contemporary religion overlooks their importance as historical, artistic and ethnographic material.

The strongest Korean collections navigate this balance by treating shamanic objects simultaneously as works of craftsmanship, records of community life and evidence of continuing cultural practice. Rather than asking visitors to believe in the spiritual power of the objects, they explain why generations of Koreans created, used and preserved them in response to everyday hopes, fears and uncertainties. That approach makes these collections distinctive within South Korea's wider landscape of unusual museums: they preserve not merely strange objects, but material traces of a living relationship between ritual, memory and community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.shamanismmuseum.org/sub07/sub01.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shamanismmuseum.org">[shamanismmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shamanismmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shamanism Museum샤머니즘박물관</span></span></span>

    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
