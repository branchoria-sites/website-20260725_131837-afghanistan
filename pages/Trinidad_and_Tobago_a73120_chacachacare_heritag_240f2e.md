## Introduction

Chacachacare is one of [Trinidad and Tobago]({{ 'trinidad-and-tobago/' | relative_url }})’s most unusual heritage landscapes: an abandoned island where medical history, colonial isolation policies and overgrown ruins meet. Rather than a conventional museum, it functions as a preserved historic site — a place where visitors encounter the remains of a former leprosy settlement, including religious buildings, medical facilities and living spaces left behind after the island’s final residents departed. Its importance lies not in ghost stories, but in the physical evidence of how societies once responded to feared diseases and how those decisions shaped the lives of isolated communities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visittrinidad.tt/attractions/chacachacare-island-historic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visittrinidad.tt">[visitTrinbago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visittrinidad.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">visit Trinbago Chacachacare Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">visitTrinbagoChacachacare Island - visitTrinbagoJune 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2026</span></span></span>

For travellers interested in unusual collections, forgotten places and difficult heritage, Chacachacare represents a different kind of “cabinet of curiosity”. Its collection is not a room of objects behind glass; it is a landscape of ruins, memories and contested interpretations. The island shows how medical history can become cultural heritage, while also raising questions about preservation, access and the risk of reducing real human suffering to an atmosphere of mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago...</span></span></span>

## Leprosy settlement history

### Why was Chacachacare chosen for isolation?

Before becoming associated with leprosy care, Chacachacare had several different chapters, including plantation activity, whaling and military use. Its most significant heritage identity developed in the twentieth century when Trinidad’s colonial government established a leprosy settlement there. The move followed earlier attempts to manage the disease on the mainland, where existing facilities struggled with overcrowding and public-health concerns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago...</span></span></span>

The island’s distance from Port of Spain made it attractive to authorities following the medical thinking of the period, when many governments believed strict separation was necessary to control leprosy. Modern understanding recognises that historical responses to leprosy were often shaped as much by fear and stigma as by medical evidence. Chacachacare therefore represents both a chapter in healthcare history and a reminder of the social costs of forced isolation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago...</span></span></span>

By the early twentieth century, patients were transferred from earlier facilities to the island settlement. The National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago records that Dominican Sisters of Etrépagny played a major role in caring for residents, continuing their work from the nineteenth century into the twentieth century. The settlement included spaces for treatment, worship, accommodation and daily life, creating a small but highly controlled community separated from the wider population.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago...</span></span></span>

The settlement was not simply a prison-like site, although isolation was central to its purpose. It was also a place where patients built routines, relationships and forms of community. Records of the settlement describe religious facilities, different residential areas, a hospital and spaces connected to the work of the Dominican sisters. This human dimension is important because abandoned medical sites can easily become interpreted only through fear, while the people who lived there disappear from the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citizensforconservationtt.org/home/sites/chacachacare/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citizensforconservationtt.org">[Citizens for Conservation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citizensforconservationtt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on citizensforconservationtt.org.</span></span></span>

## Ruins, care and isolation

### What can visitors see on the island?

Today, Chacachacare’s appeal comes from the physical remains of the former settlement. Visitors may encounter the ruins of the leprosarium, the nuns’ convent, chapels, cemetery areas and other structures associated with the community that once existed there. The National Trust has highlighted locations such as the nuns’ convent, chapel, cemetery and Salt Pond as heritage features explored during organised visits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/chacachacare-heritage-quest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoChacachacare Heritage Quest — National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoJuly 11, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2018</span></span></span>

The buildings are gradually being reclaimed by vegetation and the tropical environment. This creates the atmosphere that has made Chacachacare popular among visitors interested in abandoned places, but the decay also creates a preservation challenge. The island’s significance comes from fragile remains rather than monumental architecture, meaning every damaged wall, neglected structure or lost feature reduces the ability to understand the settlement’s history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citizensforconservationtt.org/home/sites/chacachacare/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citizensforconservationtt.org">[Citizens for Conservation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citizensforconservationtt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on citizensforconservationtt.org.</span></span></span>

Chacachacare is also not a conventional museum with permanent exhibitions, labels and controlled galleries. It is better understood as an open-air heritage site. Some official tourism material presents the island as a destination for guided visits, hiking and exploration of the leprosarium ruins, while heritage organisations have used tours to interpret its buildings and historical layers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visittrinidad.tt/attractions/chacachacare-island-historic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visittrinidad.tt">[visitTrinbago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visittrinidad.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">visit Trinbago Chacachacare Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">visitTrinbagoChacachacare Island - visitTrinbagoJune 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2026</span></span></span>

The practical experience is therefore closer to visiting an archaeological or industrial heritage landscape than entering a museum. Conditions can be uneven, and visitors need to treat the site with care because abandoned structures, vegetation and coastal conditions affect safety. The attraction is not a staged recreation of the past but an encounter with what remains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/chacachacare-heritage-quest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoChacachacare Heritage Quest — National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoJuly 11, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2018</span></span></span>

### A medical history site rather than a “haunted island”

Chacachacare is frequently described online as eerie because of its abandoned buildings, empty rooms and association with a feared disease. Popular discussions often frame it as a ghostly location, but the strongest historical interpretation is more grounded: it is a site of illness, caregiving, separation and memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago...</span></span></span>

The emotional power of the island comes from its real history. The ruins represent people who were removed from ordinary society, medical workers who served in difficult conditions, and families affected by the stigma surrounding leprosy. Treating the island only as an abandoned curiosity risks turning a complex human story into a backdrop for exploration videos and ghost narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationaltrust.tt/home/quarantine-islands-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.tt">[National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago Islands of Quarantine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Trust of Trinidad and TobagoIslands of Quarantine - Part 1 — National Trust of Trinidad and Tobago...</span></span></span>
