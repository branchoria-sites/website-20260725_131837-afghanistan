## Introduction

The clay tablets behind the Gilgamesh legend are among Iraq’s most remarkable links between myth and archaeology. Written in cuneiform on baked clay, these fragments preserve the story of Gilgamesh, the legendary king of Uruk, whose adventures became one of the earliest surviving works of world literature. They are not historical records in the modern sense, but they are real archaeological evidence showing how ancient Mesopotamians remembered kings, imagined heroes, explained disasters and explored human fears about death and immortality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseumshoponline.org/trade/flood-tablet-replica.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseumshoponline.org">[britishmuseumshoponline.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseumshoponline.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flood Tablet Replica</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assyrian Sculpture - The British Museum...</span></span></span>

For Iraq’s unusual heritage landscape, the Gilgamesh tablets are especially important because they sit at the boundary between a museum object and a surviving piece of ancient imagination. A visitor looking at a small piece of clay covered with wedge-shaped marks is seeing both a literary masterpiece and the physical remains of a civilisation that used writing to preserve its myths, knowledge and identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/celebrated-gilgamesh-tablet-handed-back-iraq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">celebrated gilgamesh tablet handed back iraq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The celebrated ‘Gilgamesh Tablet’ handed back to Iraq &#124; UNESCOApril 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2022</span></span></span>

## The epic preserved on clay

The Epic of Gilgamesh was not written once in a single final form. It developed over centuries through different versions written by scribes in ancient Mesopotamia. Earlier Sumerian stories about Gilgamesh were later combined and reshaped into longer Akkadian versions, including the Standard Babylonian edition that became the best-known form. The surviving manuscripts are incomplete, with different tablets preserving different episodes rather than one perfect original copy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumtablet &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumtablet &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

The best-known version is arranged around a series of clay tablets telling the adventures of Gilgamesh, ruler of Uruk, and his companion Enkidu. The story includes their struggle against Humbaba, guardian of the Cedar Forest, the death of Enkidu, and Gilgamesh’s search for a way to escape mortality. These themes explain why the epic still feels surprisingly modern: it deals with friendship, grief, power, fear of death and the limits of human achievement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2019/10/14/how-to-read-gilgamesh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newyorker.com">[The New Yorker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newyorker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scholars like George Smith, sparked by artifacts from ancient Mesopotamia, played pivotal roles in this pursuit. The tablets are unusual because they preserve mythology through a material process that was practical and everyday. Ancient scribes pressed a reed stylus into soft clay to create cuneiform signs, then dried or fired the tablets so they could survive for thousands of years. Unlike fragile manuscripts written on plant fibres or animal skins, clay could endure burial, collapse and abandonment, allowing stories to reappear from archaeological sites long after the societies that produced them had disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/celebrated-gilgamesh-tablet-handed-back-iraq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">celebrated gilgamesh tablet handed back iraq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The celebrated ‘Gilgamesh Tablet’ handed back to Iraq &#124; UNESCOApril 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2022</span></span></span>

## Nineveh’s discoveries revealed a lost literary world

One of the most important discoveries connected with Gilgamesh came from Nineveh, the ancient Assyrian capital near modern Mosul in northern Iraq. Archaeologists excavating the library of King Ashurbanipal uncovered thousands of cuneiform tablets, including major portions of the Gilgamesh epic. The collection showed that ancient Assyrian scholars were preserving and studying older Mesopotamian literature rather than simply creating new royal propaganda.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseumshoponline.org/trade/flood-tablet-replica.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseumshoponline.org">[britishmuseumshoponline.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseumshoponline.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flood Tablet Replica</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assyrian Sculpture - The British Museum...</span></span></span>

Among the most famous pieces is the so-called Flood Tablet, the eleventh tablet of the Standard Babylonian Epic of Gilgamesh. It contains the episode in which Gilgamesh meets Utnapishtim, a survivor of a great flood who tells how he built a boat and saved life from destruction. The discovery became famous in the nineteenth century because scholars noticed similarities between this Babylonian flood account and later flood traditions, including the biblical story of Noah.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_SM-1881" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumtablet &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumtablet &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

The importance of the Flood Tablet is not that it proves one tradition copied another in a simple way. Instead, it demonstrates that flood narratives were part of a much older Near Eastern storytelling environment. The tablet gives historians evidence for how ancient communities used stories to think about catastrophe, divine judgement and survival.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_SM-1881" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumtablet &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumtablet &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

## Sulaymaniyah’s cuneiform discoveries added new evidence

A more recent chapter in the story of Gilgamesh tablets comes from the Sulaymaniyah Museum in Iraq’s Kurdistan Region. A clay tablet acquired by the museum was later identified as part of Tablet V of the Epic of Gilgamesh, adding new information about the episode in which Gilgamesh and Enkidu travel to the Cedar Forest and confront Humbaba.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://etc.worldhistory.org/exhibitions/giglamesh-enkidu-humbaba-cedar-forest-newest-discovered-tablet-v-epic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: etc.worldhistory.org">[etc.worldhistory.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">etc.worldhistory.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 24, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 24, 2015</span></span></span>

The discovery showed that even a text studied for more than a century could still change. The fragment helped scholars compare different manuscript traditions and refine their understanding of how the epic developed over time. Rather than revealing a completely unknown story, the tablet filled gaps in an already known tradition and showed that ancient literature survived through many imperfect copies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/ancient-cultures/ancient-near-eastern-world/ancient-clay-tablet-offers-new-insights-into-the-gilgamesh-epic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblicalarchaeology.org">[Biblical Archaeology Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblical Archaeology SocietyAncient Clay Tablet Offers Insights into the Gilgamesh Epic - Biblical Archaeology Society...</span></span></span>

This is one of the most fascinating aspects of the Gilgamesh tablets as archaeological objects: they are not just containers of an old story. Each fragment has its own history of writing, copying, breaking, rediscovery and interpretation. A damaged piece of clay can alter modern understanding of a work that has travelled across thousands of years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://etc.worldhistory.org/exhibitions/giglamesh-enkidu-humbaba-cedar-forest-newest-discovered-tablet-v-epic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: etc.worldhistory.org">[etc.worldhistory.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">etc.worldhistory.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 24, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 24, 2015</span></span></span>

## Myth compared with archaeological evidence

The Gilgamesh tablets show the difference between a legendary figure and a historically proven individual. The epic presents Gilgamesh as a powerful king of Uruk, but scholars do not have archaeological evidence confirming that the hero existed exactly as described. Some researchers have suggested that the character may preserve memories of a real early ruler, but the surviving evidence does not allow the legendary king and a historical person to be confidently identified as the same figure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2019/10/14/how-to-read-gilgamesh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newyorker.com">[The New Yorker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newyorker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scholars like George Smith, sparked by artifacts from ancient Mesopotamia, played pivotal roles in this pursuit. Smith, an enthusiast of...</span></span></span>

What archaeology can confirm is the world around the story. Ancient Uruk was a real and influential city in southern Mesopotamia, cuneiform writing was a real system used across Iraq for thousands of years, and scribes genuinely copied literary works about gods, heroes and kings. The tablets therefore preserve history in a different way: they document what people believed, valued and imagined rather than providing a straightforward biography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/celebrated-gilgamesh-tablet-handed-back-iraq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">celebrated gilgamesh tablet handed back iraq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The celebrated ‘Gilgamesh Tablet’ handed back to Iraq &#124; UNESCOApril 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2022</span></span></span>

This distinction is what makes the tablets important within Iraq’s unusual collections. They are not mysterious because they reveal a lost civilisation of fantasy. They are remarkable because they show a real civilisation recording its own dreams, fears and questions about existence.

## The tablets’ journey through modern collections

The survival of Gilgamesh tablets also reflects the complicated modern history of Iraqi antiquities. Many pieces from Mesopotamian sites entered international collections during nineteenth- and twentieth-century excavations, while others were affected by later looting and illegal trade. The return of the so-called Gilgamesh Dream Tablet to Iraq in 2021 highlighted continuing debates over cultural heritage and ownership.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/celebrated-gilgamesh-tablet-handed-back-iraq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">celebrated gilgamesh tablet handed back iraq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The celebrated ‘Gilgamesh Tablet’ handed back to Iraq &#124; UNESCOApril 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2022</span></span></span>

The Dream Tablet, a fragment containing part of the Gilgamesh story, had been removed from Iraq and later appeared on the international market before being seized and returned. Its repatriation brought attention not only to the object itself but also to the wider question of how ancient Iraqi heritage should be preserved and displayed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/celebrated-gilgamesh-tablet-handed-back-iraq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">celebrated gilgamesh tablet handed back iraq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The celebrated ‘Gilgamesh Tablet’ handed back to Iraq &#124; UNESCOApril 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2022</span></span></span>

Today, Gilgamesh tablets are studied in museums, universities and digital archives around the world. Some fragments remain in Iraq, including important material connected with the [Iraq Museum]({{ 'iraq-museum/' | relative_url }}) and Sulaymaniyah Museum, while others are held internationally. Together they form a scattered but connected record of one of humanity’s oldest surviving literary traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/celebrated-gilgamesh-tablet-handed-back-iraq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">celebrated gilgamesh tablet handed back iraq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The celebrated ‘Gilgamesh Tablet’ handed back to Iraq &#124; UNESCOApril 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2022</span></span></span>

The clay tablets behind the Gilgamesh legend matter because they preserve more than an ancient adventure. They preserve evidence of how people in ancient Iraq used writing to make sense of friendship, loss, nature, power and mortality. In that sense, these small pieces of clay are both archaeological discoveries and surviving fragments of human memory.

    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);

        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',

      });

      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
