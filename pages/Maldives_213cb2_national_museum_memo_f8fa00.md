## Introduction

The [Maldives]({{ 'maldives/' | relative_url }}) National Museum is remarkable not only for what it preserves but also for what it has lost. Housed within the former royal precinct at Sultan Park in Malé, it brings together royal regalia, manuscripts, weapons, lacquerwork, coral-stone carvings and archaeological discoveries from across the archipelago. Yet the museum has also become internationally known because much of its most important pre-Islamic sculpture was deliberately destroyed during an attack in 2012. That event transformed the museum from a repository of national heritage into a symbol of the tensions surrounding history, religion and cultural memory in the Maldives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/collection/maldives-national-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Maritime Asia Heritage Survey Maldives National Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyMaldives National Museum - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

Rather than presenting a simple chronological history of the islands, the museum now tells a more complicated story. Visitors encounter surviving royal and Islamic collections alongside the absence left by destroyed Buddhist artefacts, making the galleries as much about contested memory as about preserved objects.

## What Survived Inside the Maldives National Museum?

The surviving collections demonstrate how the Maldives sat at the crossroads of the Indian Ocean for centuries. Instead of focusing on a single civilisation, the museum preserves material from the sultanate, religious institutions and everyday island life, allowing visitors to trace political, artistic and commercial connections across the region.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/collection/maldives-national-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Maritime Asia Heritage Survey Maldives National Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyMaldives National Museum - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

### Royal objects, manuscripts and coral carvings

Among the museum's most significant surviving holdings are:

* Ceremonial furniture, royal thrones, parasols and clothing associated with the Maldivian sultanate.
* Historic weapons, armour and ceremonial objects reflecting the islands' royal administration.
* Finely carved lacquerware and wooden architectural elements demonstrating distinctive Maldivian craftsmanship.
* Copper-plate inscriptions, legal documents and rare manuscripts in Dhivehi and Arabic that record royal decrees, religious endowments and administration over many centuries.
* Coral-stone carvings recovered from archaeological sites, including architectural fragments and sculptural pieces illustrating the importance of coral as a traditional building material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/collection/maldives-national-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Maritime Asia Heritage Survey Maldives National Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyMaldives National Museum - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

Many of these manuscripts and inscriptions have been digitally photographed and catalogued through the Maritime Asia Heritage Survey. High-resolution images and searchable records now provide researchers with access that extends beyond the museum's display cases, reducing the risk that fragile originals become inaccessible through deterioration or future loss.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/manuscript-viewer/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Maritime Asia Heritage Survey Manuscript viewer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyManuscript viewer - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

The collection also reflects the Maldives' position within wider Indian Ocean networks. Copper plates, religious texts and court documents reveal exchanges of ideas as well as trade, while archaeological finds help reconstruct periods for which written sources remain scarce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maldives-heritage-survey/15F61521DF052F7FEA4CA8AEEEA554FC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Maldives Heritage Survey &#124; Antiquity &#124; Cambridge Core...</span></span></span>

## The destruction of the pre-Islamic collection

The museum's most painful chapter unfolded in February 2012, when attackers entered the galleries and deliberately smashed much of the collection of Buddhist sculpture. The damaged artefacts included coral and limestone statues dating from the islands' Buddhist period before the conversion to Islam during the twelfth century. Contemporary museum officials described many pieces as beyond restoration, representing an irreplaceable loss to Maldivian archaeology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maldives-heritage-survey/15F61521DF052F7FEA4CA8AEEEA554FC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Maldives Heritage Survey &#124; Antiquity &#124; Cambridge Core...</span></span></span>

The attack mattered because relatively little archaeological material from the Maldives' pre-Islamic past had survived into the modern era. Earlier discoveries had already been lost through natural decay, redevelopment or earlier destruction. The museum therefore contained one of the country's most important concentrations of Buddhist sculpture, making the losses especially significant for understanding the early history of the archipelago.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maldives-heritage-survey/15F61521DF052F7FEA4CA8AEEEA554FC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Maldives Heritage Survey &#124; Antiquity &#124; Cambridge Core...</span></span></span>

The destruction has often been compared with other high-profile attacks on cultural heritage elsewhere, but the Maldivian case also raises more local questions. It highlights how archaeological objects can become symbols within contemporary debates about religion, national identity and the interpretation of history. The damaged sculptures were not simply museum exhibits; they represented evidence of a historical period that remains politically and culturally sensitive for some observers.

## Why the museum remains important despite the losses


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Although the destroyed Buddhist sculptures receive considerable international attention, they do not define the museum's entire significance. The institution continues to preserve extensive collections documenting the Islamic sultanate, royal government, craftsmanship and everyday life across the islands.

</div>

Equally important is the growing effort to document surviving heritage digitally. The Maldives Heritage Survey and its successor, the Maritime Asia Heritage Survey, have recorded museum collections, archaeological sites, inscriptions and manuscripts using photography, photogrammetry and three-dimensional modelling. These projects cannot replace destroyed originals, but they create permanent digital records that support research, conservation and public access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maldives-heritage-survey/15F61521DF052F7FEA4CA8AEEEA554FC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[cambridge.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Maldives Heritage Survey &#124; Antiquity &#124; Cambridge Core...</span></span></span>

Recent documentation has even incorporated newly recovered archaeological objects, including carved coral pieces from Buddhist sites that have been recorded in detail before entering museum collections. This approach reflects a broader shift towards preserving information as well as physical artefacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/mahs-maldives-field-team-at-work/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Maritime Asia Heritage Survey MAHS MALDIVES FIELD TEAM AT WORK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyMAHS MALDIVES FIELD TEAM AT WORK - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

## What visitors can expect today

Visitors should not expect a museum dominated by dramatic archaeological displays. Instead, the galleries are relatively compact and present a broad overview of Maldivian history through royal, religious and cultural collections.

Those interested in the country's unusual heritage will find several themes especially rewarding:

* Royal ceremonial objects that illustrate the authority and symbolism of the former sultanate.
* Historic manuscripts and copper-plate inscriptions offering direct links to medieval administration and religious life.
* Surviving coral carvings that demonstrate traditional artistic techniques.
* Archaeological objects whose significance is heightened by the loss of many comparable pieces in 2012.
