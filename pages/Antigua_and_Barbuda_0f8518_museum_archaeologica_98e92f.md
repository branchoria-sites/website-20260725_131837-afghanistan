## Introduction

Antigua's most revealing museum collection is not built around spectacular treasures but around thousands of ordinary archaeological fragments. The Museum of [Antigua and Barbuda]({{ 'antigua-and-barbuda/' | relative_url }}) serves as the country's principal archaeological repository, preserving ceramics, shell tools, stone implements, tobacco pipes, glass, animal bones, metal objects and countless other excavated finds from across the islands. Individually these objects often appear unremarkable. Together they allow archaeologists to reconstruct trade networks, Indigenous settlement, colonial expansion, foodways, migration and everyday life over many centuries. Rather than functioning as a cabinet of curiosities filled with rare masterpieces, the collection demonstrates how careful accumulation, cataloguing and comparison can transform broken objects into evidence about an entire society.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antiguamuseums.net/portfolios/research/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguamuseums.net">[antiguamuseums.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguamuseums.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span></span></span>

## What Do Antigua's Archaeological Fragments Reveal?

Unlike countries known for monumental ruins or lavish royal collections, Antigua's archaeological record survives largely in dispersed fragments recovered from settlements, plantations, coastal sites and Indigenous villages. The importance of the national museum lies in bringing these scattered discoveries together under one institution, where they can be studied as connected evidence rather than isolated finds. The museum reports that its archaeological collections contain tens of thousands of artefacts from Antigua's historical and archaeological sites, supplemented by comparative material from elsewhere in the Caribbean. Its research library adds excavation reports, maps, photographs and unpublished studies that help place the physical objects into historical context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antiguamuseums.net/portfolios/research/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguamuseums.net">[antiguamuseums.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguamuseums.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span></span></span>

This cumulative approach allows researchers to ask questions that no single artefact could answer. Hundreds of pottery fragments can reveal changing cooking traditions. Imported ceramics indicate commercial links with Europe or neighbouring Caribbean islands. Shell remains and animal bones help reconstruct diet, while stone tools and worked shell illustrate technologies used before European colonisation. Even broken clay tobacco pipes become chronological markers because manufacturing styles changed over time. Together these modest objects build a layered picture of Antigua's past.

## Inside the National Archaeological Collection

The museum's archaeological holdings cover both the pre-Columbian and historical periods, reflecting the islands' long sequence of Indigenous occupation, colonial settlement and plantation society. Public displays showcase representative examples, but the majority of the collection is stored for research, conservation and future exhibitions rather than permanent display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antiguamuseums.net/portfolios/research/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguamuseums.net">[antiguamuseums.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguamuseums.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span></span></span>

## How Ordinary Objects Become Historical Evidence

Archaeology depends less on spectacular discoveries than on patterns. A single pottery sherd says little by itself, but hundreds recovered from carefully recorded excavations reveal changes in technology, trade and daily behaviour.

Researchers working with Antigua's collections examine several kinds of evidence simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Distribution:** Where objects are found can identify settlements, specialised activity areas or trading centres.
* **Material:** Imported stone, glass or ceramics reveal overseas connections and commercial exchange.
* **Association:** Objects discovered together can reconstruct households, workshops or plantation buildings.
* **Dating:** Manufacturing techniques and decoration help establish when deposits were created.
* **Environmental remains:** Animal bones, shells and plant remains contribute evidence about diet, farming and changing landscapes.

</div>

Because the museum preserves material from numerous excavations rather than only famous sites, it supports broad comparisons across different parts of the island instead of isolated case studies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antiguamuseums.net/portfolios/research/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguamuseums.net">[antiguamuseums.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguamuseums.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span></span></span>

## From Excavation to Research Collection

The museum's archaeological collection is closely connected with Antigua's wider research infrastructure. The Field Research Centre, established through collaboration between the Historical and Archaeological Society and Antigua's National Parks, provides facilities for curating excavated collections, supporting archaeological projects and encouraging publication of research. It works with university researchers on excavation, mapping, climate studies and forensic archaeology while helping ensure that newly recovered material is properly documented and preserved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/current-research/field-research-centre" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[archaeologyantigua.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Field Research Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Field Research Centre</span></span></span>

This relationship means that the museum is not simply a display space. It is part of an active system in which artefacts move from excavation, through conservation and cataloguing, into long-term research collections that remain available for future study as new techniques and new historical questions emerge.

## What Visitors Can See and What Researchers Can Access

For most visitors, the museum offers a concise introduction to Antigua's long history through carefully selected objects that illustrate major themes rather than attempting to display every artefact. The galleries explain geology, Indigenous occupation, European colonisation, slavery, plantation agriculture and modern cultural life using representative material from the much larger collections behind the scenes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antiguamuseums.net/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguamuseums.net">[antiguamuseums.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguamuseums.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span></span></span>

Researchers have broader access. According to the museum, its public research library includes books, monographs, maps, photographs and historical resources, while staff assist visitors interested in archaeological or historical research. The archaeological collections themselves support specialist study alongside the documentary resources that help interpret them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antiguamuseums.net/portfolios/research/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguamuseums.net">[antiguamuseums.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguamuseums.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span></span></span>

This combination of public exhibition and research access makes the institution unusual within Antigua and Barbuda's heritage landscape. It functions simultaneously as a local history museum, archaeological archive and research centre.

## Why the Collection Matters

The museum's archaeological holdings are unusual not because they contain famous relics but because they preserve the physical evidence from which Antigua's history is continually reconstructed. Coastal erosion, urban development and agricultural change threaten archaeological sites across small Caribbean islands, making curated collections increasingly valuable as permanent records of places that may later be altered or lost.

Within Antigua and Barbuda's wider landscape of heritage attractions, the archaeological collection occupies a distinctive place. Nearby plantation sites, dockyards and historic buildings provide the landscapes where history unfolded, while the museum preserves the portable evidence recovered from those landscapes. Its thousands of fragments demonstrate that understanding the past often depends less on extraordinary discoveries than on the patient collection of ordinary objects whose combined evidence reveals how generations of people lived, traded, worked and adapted across the islands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antiguamuseums.net/portfolios/research/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguamuseums.net">[antiguamuseums.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguamuseums.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research ‹ Museum of Antigua and Barbuda</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);

        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',

      });

      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');

      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });

    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';

        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });

        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
