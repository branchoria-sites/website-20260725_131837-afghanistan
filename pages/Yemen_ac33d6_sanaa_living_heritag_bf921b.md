## Introduction

Sana’a’s Old City is unusual because it does not place heritage behind glass. Its streets, homes, workshops and markets form a living collection in which architecture itself becomes the exhibit. Instead of a conventional museum of curiosities, visitors encounter a functioning historic city: tall decorated houses, craft traditions, old trading streets, religious buildings and domestic spaces that still carry traces of centuries of urban life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreOld City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

The city’s fame comes from its distinctive tower houses, built from local earth, brick and stone, then decorated with geometric plasterwork and coloured window designs. Yet Sana’a’s importance is not only visual. The unusual feature is that the architecture remains tied to everyday practices: builders still understand traditional materials, craftsmen maintain decorative skills, and neighbourhoods preserve a relationship between homes, markets and communal spaces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreOld City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

For those interested in unusual collections and curiosity culture, Sana’a offers a different kind of attraction. It is a city-sized archive where the “objects” are buildings, techniques and habits rather than isolated artefacts. Its survival also raises difficult questions: how can a living city be preserved without turning it into a frozen monument?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/soc/4558/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre UNESCO World Heritage Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreUNESCO World Heritage Centre - State of Conservation (SOC 2024) Old City of Sana&#x27;a (Yemen)January 30, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 30, 2024</span></span></span>

## Historic tower houses and decorated facades

The most recognisable curiosity of Sana’a is its vertical domestic architecture. Within the Old City, multi-storey houses rise above narrow streets, creating a skyline unlike most historic cities in the region. UNESCO describes the Old City as an exceptional ensemble of rammed-earth and burnt-brick towers, often several storeys high, with decorated façades using fired brick patterns and white gypsum details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/old-city-sanaa?hub=195868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Old City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

These buildings are sometimes compared informally to ancient skyscrapers, but their purpose was not simply to build upwards. Their height allowed families to create generous living spaces inside a restricted, defensible urban area. Lower levels traditionally served practical functions such as storage and animals, while upper floors contained family rooms and reception spaces. The design reflects adaptation to Sana’a’s environment, society and history rather than an attempt to imitate modern high-rise buildings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreOld City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

One of the most striking features is the contrast between plain structural materials and elaborate decoration. Earth and brick walls are transformed by white plaster patterns, carved frames and distinctive window designs. These details make individual houses recognisable while also creating a shared visual language across neighbourhoods. UNESCO considers the decorated façades, traditional materials and urban spaces among the elements that carry the city’s heritage value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreOld City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

### Windows that turn houses into artworks

Sana’a’s windows are among its most memorable architectural details. Traditional houses often feature decorative glazed openings that filter sunlight and add colour to interiors. These features demonstrate that domestic architecture was also an artistic practice: a family home could function as a display of craftsmanship, status and local identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folkculturebh.org/en/?id=205&amp;issue=22&amp;page=article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folkculturebh.org">[Folk Culture Bahrain]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folkculturebh.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Folk Culture Bahrain The Material Folk Heritage of the Old City of Sanaa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folk Culture BahrainThe Material Folk Heritage of the Old City of Sanaa - Folk Culture...</span></span></span>

The interiors also reveal that the city was designed around social life rather than only appearance. Reception rooms, private family areas and rooftop spaces reflect long-established patterns of hospitality and community. A visitor walking through the Old City therefore encounters not just historic façades, but the remains of a social system built into architecture itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/old-city-sanaa?hub=195868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Old City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

## Craft traditions and urban memory

Sana’a’s heritage is preserved not only by buildings but by the skills needed to maintain them. Traditional construction depends on knowledge of local materials, decorative methods and repair techniques passed between generations. Conservation work has therefore focused not only on saving structures but also on documenting and supporting the expertise behind them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/activities/569" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreWorld Heritage Centre - The Inventory of the historic city of Sana&#x27;a: a tool for urban conservationDecember 1...</span></span></span>

The city’s markets add another layer to this living collection. Historic souqs are places where craft, trade and architecture overlap. Narrow streets, specialist market areas and workshops preserve the relationship between making and selling that shaped Sana’a’s urban identity for centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folkculturebh.org/en/?id=205&amp;issue=22&amp;page=article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folkculturebh.org">[Folk Culture Bahrain]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folkculturebh.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Folk Culture Bahrain The Material Folk Heritage of the Old City of Sanaa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folk Culture BahrainThe Material Folk Heritage of the Old City of Sanaa - Folk Culture...</span></span></span>

This connection between architecture and craftsmanship is particularly important because many of Sana’a’s “museum-like” qualities come from continuity rather than display. A restored façade, a repaired traditional house or a craft workshop represents knowledge still in use. In this sense, Sana’a resembles a living archive: the evidence is not only what survives from the past, but what people continue to practise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/state/yemen-YE" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICH UNESCOYemen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

The city’s cultural life also includes traditions beyond architecture. The UNESCO-listed Song of Sana’a, a musical tradition associated with social gatherings, shows how the city’s heritage extends into performance, memory and everyday customs. This broader view helps explain why Sana’a cannot be understood simply as an architectural attraction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/song-of-sana-a-00077" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICH UNESCOSong of Sana’a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Threats facing a living heritage site

Sana’a’s greatest challenge is that a living city cannot be preserved like a sealed archaeological site. Residents need functioning homes, services and repairs, while conservation authorities aim to protect historic character. The tension between daily life and heritage protection is central to the city’s future.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/soc/4558/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre UNESCO World Heritage Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreUNESCO World Heritage Centre - State of Conservation (SOC 2024) Old City of Sana&#x27;a (Yemen)January 30, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 30, 2024</span></span></span>

UNESCO reported damage to areas including the Al-Qasimi neighbourhood, where traditional houses and historic structures were affected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/yemens-old-city-sanaa-and-old-walled-city-shibam-added-list-world-heritage-danger" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Yemen’s Old City of Sana’a and Old Walled City of Shibam added to List</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Yemen’s Old City of Sana’a and Old Walled City of Shibam added to ListJuly 2, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2015</span></span></span>

## Why Sana’a matters in Yemen’s curiosity landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Sana’a represents a different kind of unusual heritage from the rare objects normally associated with museums. Its curiosity lies in scale: an entire historic environment has become a collection. The visitor is not looking at a single mysterious artefact but walking through layers of human adaptation, craftsmanship and memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/old-city-sanaa?hub=195868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Old City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

</div>

Within [Yemen]({{ 'yemens-hidden-world-of-strange-heritage/' | relative_url }})’s wider heritage landscape, Sana’a is especially important because it shows how architecture can preserve stories normally found in archives. A tower house records building techniques; a market street records economic history; a decorative façade records artistic traditions; a neighbourhood records patterns of family and community life.

The result is a rare example of a place where the boundary between museum and everyday life disappears. Sana’a’s streets are not a reconstructed display of the past. They are a surviving cultural environment where history remains visible, inhabited and constantly negotiated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/old-city-sanaa?hub=195868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Old City of Sana&#x27;a</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Old City of Sana&#x27;a - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
