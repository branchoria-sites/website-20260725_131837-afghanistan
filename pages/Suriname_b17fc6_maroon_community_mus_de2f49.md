## Introduction

Maroon community museums in [Suriname]({{ 'where-suriname-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}) are unusual because they are not simply places where outsiders look at preserved objects. They are places where communities explain their own histories, skills and values through objects, stories and living practices. The Saamaka Marron Museum in Pikin Slee and Diitabiki Museum Fositen Gudu in the Aukan/Ndyuka village of Diitabiki represent a different model of heritage: the museum as a community-controlled space where carving, language, clothing, architecture and oral knowledge remain connected to everyday life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://saamakamuseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saamakamuseum.com">[Saamaka Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saamakamuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on saamakamuseum.com.</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in unusual collections, these museums offer something different from conventional displays of rare artefacts. Their importance lies less in mysterious objects behind glass and more in the question of who has the authority to explain them. Maroon communities in Suriname were historically studied and collected by outsiders, including colonial researchers and museum institutions abroad. Community museums reverse that relationship by allowing descendants of those communities to shape interpretation, decide what knowledge is shared and connect objects with the social systems that produced them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/801080/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.naturalis.nl">[Naturalis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.naturalis.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naturalis Institutional Repository: Surinamese Maroon and Indigenous Knowledge in the Creation of Natural and Ethnographic Colle...</span></span></span>

## Why these museums tell a different kind of story

Maroon heritage in Suriname developed from communities founded by Africans who escaped slavery and created independent societies in the interior. Over generations, these communities developed distinctive artistic traditions, political structures, languages and relationships with the forest environment. Museums in Pikin Slee and Diitabiki do not present this history as a closed chapter; they show how historical knowledge continues through families, craftspeople, elders and community institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mpm.org/research-collections/anthropology/online-collections-research/ndyuka" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mpm.org">[MPM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mpm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ndyuka Collection &#124; Milwaukee Public Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ndyuka Collection &#124; Milwaukee Public MuseumJanuary 1, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2008</span></span></span>

This approach challenges an older ethnographic tradition in which Indigenous and Maroon objects were often removed from their original settings and displayed mainly as examples of another culture. Research into historical collections in the Netherlands has shown that Maroon and Indigenous knowledge holders were essential contributors to colonial-era collecting expeditions, although their roles were often overlooked in official accounts. Community museums provide a contrasting model where cultural knowledge is not separated from the people who maintain it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/801080/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.naturalis.nl">[Naturalis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.naturalis.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naturalis Institutional Repository: Surinamese Maroon and Indigenous Knowledge in the Creation of Natural and Ethnographic Colle...</span></span></span>

The result is a type of museum that sits between archive, cultural centre, craft workshop and village memory space. The collection is important, but the interpretation around it is equally significant.

## Saamaka memory, carving and architecture at Pikin Slee

The Saamaka Marron Museum at Pikin Slee, a Saamaka village on the Upper Suriname River, focuses on the history and cultural heritage of the Saramaka people. The museum presents the journey from forced enslavement and escape to the creation of autonomous communities in the rainforest, while also showing contemporary Saamaka life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://saamakamuseum.com/het-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saamakamuseum.com">[Saamaka Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saamakamuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Saamaka Museum Het museum – Saamaka Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saamaka Museum Het museum – Saamaka Museum</span></span></span>

One of the most distinctive parts of the museum experience is the way everyday objects become carriers of memory. Saamaka woodcarving, decorated houses, textiles and household objects are not treated as isolated artworks. They are linked to skills, family histories and social meanings. The museum highlights how craft traditions remain active rather than frozen remnants of the past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://saamakamuseum.com/het-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saamakamuseum.com">[Saamaka Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saamakamuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Saamaka Museum Het museum – Saamaka Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saamaka Museum Het museum – Saamaka Museum</span></span></span>

Wood carving is especially important in Maroon cultural expression. Across Maroon communities, carved objects can combine practical use with artistic design. Collections of Maroon material culture elsewhere include paddles, benches, tools, household objects and musical instruments, showing how functional items can also preserve artistic traditions and community identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://surinaamsmuseum.org/collectie/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: surinaamsmuseum.org">[Surinaams Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">surinaamsmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Surinaams Museum Collectie</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Surinaams MuseumCollectie - surinaamsmuseum.org...</span></span></span>

Pikin Slee also demonstrates how museums can support living cultural economies. The museum grew alongside local artistic initiatives, including the Totomboti collective, which promotes Saamaka carving and crafts. This connection between museum activity, education and local creative work means the collection is not only about looking backwards; it is also part of maintaining cultural skills for future generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thami-mnyele.nl/artists-collection/totomboti-collective" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thami-mnyele.nl">[Thami Mnyele Foundat]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thami-mnyele.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Thami Mnyele Foundat Totomboti collective</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thami Mnyele Foundat Totomboti collective</span></span></span>

For visitors, the unusual aspect is that the museum experience extends beyond exhibition rooms. The surrounding village, river landscape, buildings and craft traditions form part of the interpretation. A carved object makes more sense when seen as part of a living community rather than as a detached curiosity.

## Aukan heritage and local authority at Diitabiki

Diitabiki Museum Fositen Gudu offers a related but distinct perspective through Aukan/Ndyuka heritage. Its collection presents cultural knowledge connected to textiles, wood carving, stories, traditional governance and the Afaka script, a writing system created in the early twentieth century by Aukan man Afaka Atumisi.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fositengudu.org/en/collection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fositengudu.org">[Diitabiki Museum Fositen Gudu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fositengudu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on fositengudu.org.</span></span></span>

The museum is particularly interesting because it preserves forms of knowledge that do not fit easily into ordinary museum categories. A written script, a carved object, a ceremonial item or a story may each represent more than a historical artefact: they can represent identity, authority and continuity. The museum’s collection structure reflects this broader understanding by grouping material around themes such as wood carving, textiles, stories and community history rather than simply arranging objects by age or rarity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fositengudu.org/en/collection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fositengudu.org">[Diitabiki Museum Fositen Gudu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fositengudu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on fositengudu.org.</span></span></span>

The Afaka script is an example of knowledge preservation with a complicated history. Afaka developed a syllabic writing system for the Aukan language, but it did not become widely adopted after his death. Today, the script remains an important cultural symbol, and artists and cultural workers have used it as a way of reconnecting with Aukan heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fositengudu.org/en/afaka/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fositengudu.org">[Diitabiki Museum Fositen Gudu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fositengudu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Diitabiki Museum Fositen Gudu See Afaka in our collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Diitabiki Museum Fositen GuduSee Afaka in our collection - Diitabiki Museum Fositen Gudu...</span></span></span>

This makes Diitabiki especially relevant to the idea of “living knowledge”. The museum is not only preserving things that survived from the past; it is helping communities discuss how older knowledge can continue in a changing world.

## Planning respectful visits to remote community museums

Visiting Maroon museums in Suriname requires a different mindset from visiting a city museum. These are remote community institutions, and the journey itself is part of understanding the relationship between landscape, history and culture. Visitors should approach them as places where people live, work and maintain traditions, not as staged cultural performances.

Useful expectations include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Allow time for interpretation.** Guided explanations and conversations often provide the meaning behind objects that cannot be understood from labels alone.
* **Respect community authority.** Some knowledge may be shared publicly, while other cultural information may belong within family, spiritual or community contexts.
* **See objects as connected to life.** A carving, garment or building style may represent skills, relationships and memories rather than simply artistic decoration.
* **Recognise that remoteness is part of the experience.** The location along rivers and within the interior landscape is central to why these museums exist in the first place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://saamakamuseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saamakamuseum.com">[Saamaka Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saamakamuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on saamakamuseum.com.</span></span></span>

</div>

These museums are also a reminder that heritage preservation is not always about keeping the past untouched. In Pikin Slee and Diitabiki, preservation means allowing communities to continue making, teaching and interpreting their own traditions.

## Why Maroon museums matter in Suriname’s unusual heritage landscape

Among Suriname’s distinctive collections, Maroon community museums stand out because their unusual quality comes from control of meaning rather than from rarity or spectacle. They do not display “exotic” objects separated from their origins. Instead, they show how objects, stories and landscapes remain connected to communities that still use and interpret them.

For travellers interested in strange museums and forgotten histories, these museums offer a different kind of curiosity: not the curiosity of a cabinet of unusual things, but the curiosity of discovering how a society preserved freedom, identity and knowledge through generations. Their importance lies in showing that a museum can be not only a place where culture is stored, but also a place where culture continues.

    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
