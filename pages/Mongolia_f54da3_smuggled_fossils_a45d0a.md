## Introduction

[Mongolia]({{ 'why-mongolias-strangest-collections/' | relative_url }})’s dinosaur fossils are among the world's greatest palaeontological treasures, but many of the country's most spectacular specimens have lived two lives: first buried beneath the Gobi Desert, then smuggled into the international fossil market. Over the past decade, Mongolia has become a leading example of fossil restitution, using courts, diplomatic pressure and scientific cooperation to recover illegally exported dinosaurs and return them to public collections. The result is more than a legal success story. It has reshaped how visitors experience Mongolia's dinosaur museums, where many famous skeletons are now displayed not only as prehistoric animals but also as recovered pieces of national heritage. The story demonstrates how scientific discovery, museum collections and international law have become tightly connected whenever fossils are treated as commercial commodities rather than part of the archaeological record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dinosaur-auctions-is-it-right-to-have-fossils-for-sale.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhm.ac.uk">[Natural History Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhm.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhm.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

## How the *Tarbosaurus* Auction Changed Everything

The turning point came in 2012 when a nearly complete skeleton of *Tarbosaurus bataar*—a giant predator closely related to *Tyrannosaurus rex*—appeared at a high-profile auction in New York. Mongolia immediately objected, arguing that the fossil had been removed illegally because Mongolian law treats vertebrate fossils as state property and prohibits their unauthorised export.

The auction attracted worldwide attention because the skeleton was not simply another commercial fossil. Experts quickly recognised it as almost certainly originating from Mongolia's Late Cretaceous rocks in the Gobi Desert. The United States Department of Justice intervened, courts halted the sale, and an investigation traced the specimen through false customs declarations and complex international transactions. Eventually the skeleton was ordered to be returned to Mongolia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dinosaur-auctions-is-it-right-to-have-fossils-for-sale.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhm.ac.uk">[nhm.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhm.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhm.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

The case led to the conviction of commercial fossil dealer Eric Prokopi, whose activities revealed how illegally collected Mongolian fossils could be disguised, exported and sold through apparently legitimate channels. Rather than representing a single isolated crime, prosecutors described a broader trafficking network that had moved numerous specimens out of Mongolia over many years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dinosaur-auctions-is-it-right-to-have-fossils-for-sale.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhm.ac.uk">[Natural History Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhm.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhm.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

The *Tarbosaurus* case became an international precedent because it demonstrated that fossil trafficking could be challenged using customs law, property law and international cooperation rather than being treated merely as a dispute between collectors.

## Why Fossil Smuggling Does More Than Remove Bones

The value of returned fossils lies not only in recovering impressive skeletons but also in protecting scientific knowledge.

A dinosaur skeleton found in its original position preserves information about:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* the precise geological layer in which it was buried;
* nearby plants, eggs or other animals;
* evidence of behaviour, injury or environment;
* the exact location needed to compare future discoveries.

</div>

Illegal excavation usually destroys much of this context. Smugglers often prioritise visually impressive bones that can command high auction prices while discarding smaller fossils and geological evidence that researchers consider equally valuable. Once that information has been lost, it can rarely be reconstructed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dinosaur-auctions-is-it-right-to-have-fossils-for-sale.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhm.ac.uk">[Natural History Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhm.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhm.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

This explains why palaeontologists frequently describe fossil trafficking as a scientific loss rather than simply theft of museum objects. Even when a skeleton is eventually recovered, researchers may never know precisely where it came from or what associated evidence disappeared during illicit excavation.

## A Growing Programme of Fossil Restitution

The famous *Tarbosaurus* was only the beginning.

Since 2012, Mongolia has worked with foreign governments, customs agencies, museums and scientists to identify illegally exported fossils and return them home. Numerous dinosaur skeletons, skulls, eggs and other specimens have been repatriated from private collections and commercial dealers. The Institute for the Study of Mongolian Dinosaurs has documented assistance in returning more than thirty scientifically important specimens through cooperation with Mongolian and United States authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mongoliandinosaurs.org/project/fossil-repatriation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mongoliandinosaurs.org">[mongoliandinosaurs.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mongoliandinosaurs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fossil Repatriation – Institute for the Study of Mongolian Dinosaurs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fossil Repatriation – Institute for the Study of Mongolian Dinosaurs</span></span></span>

Recoveries have continued well beyond the original court case. More recent returns have included dozens of additional fossils representing multiple dinosaur species, some removed from Mongolia decades earlier before being identified and voluntarily surrendered or seized abroad.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popsci.com/science/stolen-fossils-returned-mongolia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popsci.com">[Popular Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popsci.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Popular ScienceRare dinosaur fossils finally returned to Mongolia 20 years after theft &#124; Popular ScienceJune 12, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 12, 2026</span></span></span>

Not every recovered fossil makes international headlines. Many are fragmentary or scientifically specialised rather than spectacular exhibition pieces. Collectively, however, they restore important research collections that help scientists understand Mongolia's Late Cretaceous ecosystems.

## What Visitors See in Ulaanbaatar Today

For museum visitors, this legal history is visible inside Mongolia's principal natural history collections.

The former Central Museum of Dinosaurs became internationally famous because many of its centrepiece displays consisted of repatriated fossils rather than newly excavated specimens. Following the 2019 merger of the dinosaur museum with the Natural History Museum, these collections continue to form the heart of Mongolia's public dinosaur exhibitions while plans progress for a larger modern museum complex.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Museum_of_Dinosaurs_of_Mongolia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Central Museum of Dinosaurs of Mongolia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Central Museum of Dinosaurs of Mongolia</span></span></span>

Visitors therefore encounter exhibits with unusually rich biographies. Labels may describe the animal's anatomy and age, but the specimen may also have travelled through:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* illegal excavation in the Gobi Desert;
* international smuggling routes;
* commercial dealers;
* auction houses or private collections;
* court proceedings;
* diplomatic negotiations;
* eventual return to Mongolia.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/us-attorney-announces-return-mongolia-looted-dinosaur-fossils" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[justice.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

Few natural history museums elsewhere can present dinosaurs whose histories include both Cretaceous ecosystems and modern international litigation.

## Museums as Evidence of National Ownership

The restitution campaign has altered the role of Mongolia's dinosaur museums.

Rather than functioning only as scientific institutions, they also serve as public demonstrations that fossils belong within their geological and cultural landscape. Returned specimens reinforce the principle that palaeontological heritage is comparable to archaeological heritage: objects removed illegally are expected to come home.

This approach differs from countries where fossils discovered on private land can often be bought and sold legally. Mongolia's legal framework instead treats vertebrate fossils as part of the national heritage, making museum display the preferred public destination for significant discoveries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dinosaur-auctions-is-it-right-to-have-fossils-for-sale.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhm.ac.uk">[Natural History Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhm.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhm.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

For visitors, this gives the collections an additional layer of meaning. A mounted *Tarbosaurus* is simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* an extraordinary dinosaur;
* a scientific specimen;
* evidence in a criminal investigation;
* a symbol of cultural restitution;
* an example of international cooperation between museums, scientists and law enforcement.

</div>

## An Ongoing Challenge

Despite major successes, fossil trafficking has not disappeared.

The Gobi Desert remains one of the richest dinosaur-producing regions in the world, and the international market for exceptional fossils has continued to expand as wealthy private buyers compete for increasingly expensive specimens. Scientists argue that soaring auction prices create incentives for illegal excavation in countries with strict export laws, including Mongolia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thetimes.com/uk/science/article/t-rex-fossil-dinosaur-auction-rzmhcg86b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thetimes.com">[thetimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thetimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The sale has sparked alarm among scientists and museum professionals, who fear that the soaring commercial value of significant dinosaur...</span></span></span>

Mongolia therefore continues to combine enforcement with education. Public exhibitions emphasise that fossils are not merely spectacular curiosities but irreplaceable scientific records whose value depends upon their documented origin.     if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
