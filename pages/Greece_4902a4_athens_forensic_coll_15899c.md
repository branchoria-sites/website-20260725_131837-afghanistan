## Introduction

Athens is home to some of [Greece]({{ 'where-greece-keeps-its-strangest-stories/' | relative_url }})'s most unusual scientific collections, but they are very different from commercial "museum of horrors" attractions. Hidden within the Medical School of the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens are teaching collections that preserve human remains, forensic evidence, pathological specimens and historical criminal artefacts for education and research rather than entertainment. Many visitors never see them because access is carefully controlled, reflecting both ethical responsibilities and their continuing role in medical training.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/criminology_museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[NKUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span></span></span>

These collections occupy an unusual position between museum, laboratory and archive. They preserve the history of forensic medicine in Greece while continuing to support students of medicine, law, forensic anthropology and toxicology. For anyone interested in Greece's stranger heritage, they represent one of the country's most authentic—but least accessible—collections of medical and criminal history.

## What the criminology and anatomy collections contain

The best-known institution is the Criminology Museum at the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens. Founded in 1932 by forensic medicine professor Ioannis Georgiadis, it developed from decades of collecting evidence used in real criminal investigations. Rather than recreating famous crimes, the museum preserves the physical material that helped investigators understand them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/criminology_museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[NKUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span></span></span>

Its approximately 1,500 objects are organised into several teaching collections, including:

* Human skeletal and mummified remains.
* Formalin-preserved pathological and medico-legal specimens.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Embalmed heads of notorious early twentieth-century Greek outlaws.
* Historic tattooed skin preparations collected during autopsies.
* Wax models demonstrating injuries from homicide, suicide and accidents.
* Crime scene photographs, police records and early forensic identification documents.
* Historic firearms, knives, ammunition and bullets recovered during autopsies.
* Toxicology collections containing historical poisoning evidence, drug samples and laboratory reagents.
* Objects associated with folk magic and ritual practices, preserved as evidence rather than as occult artefacts.
* Greece's only surviving judicial guillotine, retained because of its importance in the country's legal history rather than for sensational display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/criminology_museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[NKUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span></span></span>

</div>

Nearby, the university's Anatomical Museum "Georgios Papanikolaou" presents a different type of restricted collection. Established in the nineteenth century, it contains more than 800 anatomical preparations assembled for teaching generations of medical students. These include normal and abnormal skeletons, preserved organs, injected vascular specimens, comparative animal anatomy and historic teaching models made from wax, plaster and wood. Particularly notable are skeletons showing rare skeletal disorders, extensive brain collections and preserved specimens illustrating congenital abnormalities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/anatomical_museum_/_collection_georgios_papanikolaou" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[NKUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uoa.gr.</span></span></span>

Closely related is the university's Pathology Museum, centred on the nineteenth-century Rokitansky Collection. Its holdings include over 2,500 pathological specimens, with around 1,000 on display for specialist audiences. The collection preserves surgically removed organs, autopsy material and more than one hundred foetal specimens demonstrating congenital conditions, making it one of Greece's most significant historical pathology collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/pathology_museum_rokitanski_collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[NKUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUAPathology Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rokitanski Collection &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens...</span></span></span>

## Why these collections exist for teaching and research

Although their contents can appear macabre, these collections were never assembled simply because they were unusual. Their primary purpose has always been practical education.

Forensic medicine depends heavily on recognising injury patterns, disease processes and the physical effects of violence. Before widespread digital imaging, preserved specimens allowed students to study conditions they might never otherwise encounter. Wax models reproduced complex injuries with remarkable accuracy, while preserved tissues demonstrated the real appearance of disease and trauma. Even today, many of these historical preparations remain valuable because they document conditions that are now uncommon or illustrate the development of forensic practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/criminology_museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[NKUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span></span></span>

The criminology collections also chart the evolution of criminal investigation in Greece. Historic police files, anthropometric identification cards based on the Bertillon system, toxicological evidence and recovered weapons reveal how forensic science gradually replaced confession, witness testimony and circumstantial evidence as the foundation of criminal investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/criminology_museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[NKUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span></span></span>

The museums therefore function as historical archives as much as scientific collections. Researchers studying legal history, forensic anthropology, toxicology, pathology or the history of medicine continue to use them as reference material rather than simply as preserved curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/criminology_museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[uoa.gr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span></span></span>

## How public access differs from ordinary museums

One of the most distinctive features of Athens's forensic collections is that they are not designed as conventional visitor attractions.

The Criminology Museum explicitly states that, because of the nature of its exhibits, access is controlled. The collection primarily serves medical students, law students, police academy trainees and researchers. Public visits are limited and generally require advance arrangement or special permission, reflecting both ethical considerations surrounding human remains and the museum's continuing academic role.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.uoa.gr/about_us/museums/criminology_museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.uoa.gr">[uoa.gr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NKUACriminology Museum &#124; National and Kapodistrian University of Athens</span></span></span>

The Anatomical Museum follows a similar model. Although it has broadened educational outreach and can accommodate some organised visits, entry normally requires prior permission through the university rather than the purchase of a standard museum ticket.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://estories.uoa.gr/en/cultural_heritage/museums/anatomical-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: estories.uoa.gr">[eStories]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">estories.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">e Stories Anatomical Museum &#124; e Stories.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">e Stories Anatomical Museum &#124; e Stories.uoa.gr</span></span></span>

This restricted access differs sharply from commercial medical museums found elsewhere in Europe. Athens's collections remain embedded within working university departments, where teaching, research and ethical stewardship take priority over tourism. Their presentation is correspondingly clinical, with specimens interpreted as educational material rather than spectacles.

## Why these museums matter in Greece's curiosity landscape

Within Greece's wider landscape of unusual collections, the Athens university museums demonstrate that some of the country's strangest heritage survives not in purpose-built curiosity museums but inside scientific institutions.

Their significance lies in the combination of authenticity and continuity. The specimens were collected through genuine medical practice, criminal investigation and university teaching over more than a century. Unlike reconstructed displays or theatrical exhibitions, they preserve the material history of forensic science itself.

For readers exploring Greece's stranger cultural heritage, these collections illustrate an important distinction. The country's most unsettling museums are often not designed to shock. Instead, they reveal how universities preserved difficult evidence—human remains, pathological specimens and criminal artefacts—as tools for understanding anatomy, disease, violence and justice. That educational purpose continues to shape both their interpretation and the carefully restricted way in which they are made available today.

    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
