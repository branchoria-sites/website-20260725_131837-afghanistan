## Introduction

Caracas’ Museum of Sciences is one of [Venezuela]({{ 'venezuelas-strangest-collections-beyond/' | relative_url }})’s most important places for understanding how science has historically collected, classified and displayed human bodies, cultural objects and the natural world. Among its most unusual holdings are anthropology collections containing human skeletal material, preserved bodies, funerary objects and reduced heads associated with Shuar and Achuar communities of Ecuador and Peru.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rnv.gob.ve/museo-de-ciencias-expone-sus-colecciones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rnv.gob.ve">[RNV]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rnv.gob.ve</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RNVMuseo de Ciencias expone sus colecciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Emisora Oficial del Estado venezolanoSeptember 26, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2016</span></span></span>

These collections make the museum unusual not because they are simply “macabre”, but because they reveal a complicated history of scientific curiosity. The same objects that can help researchers study health, migration, burial traditions and past societies also raise difficult questions about colonial-era collecting, consent, cultural ownership and the respectful treatment of human remains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://peabody.yale.edu/explore/collections/anthropology/stewardship-human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: peabody.yale.edu">[Yale Peabody Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">peabody.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Peabody Museum Stewardship of Human Remains &#124; Yale Peabody Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Yale Peabody MuseumStewardship of Human Remains &#124; Yale Peabody Museum...</span></span></span>

The Museum of Sciences therefore belongs to Venezuela’s strange-history landscape as a place where curiosity meets ethics: a scientific institution preserving evidence of human lives, while also preserving the debates created by earlier generations of collectors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inparques.gob.ve/cms/main/verCalendarioV/1634" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inparques.gob.ve">[Inparques]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inparques.gob.ve</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inparques &#124; Calendario verdeSeptember 1, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 2022</span></span></span>

## Anthropology collections and unusual specimens

The Museum of Sciences in Caracas began as Venezuela’s national scientific museum tradition in the nineteenth century. Today it holds a broad collection covering fields such as zoology, palaeontology, archaeology, ethnography and anthropology. Institutional descriptions place its overall holdings at more than 150,000 objects and specimens, although only a portion can be displayed at any given time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inparques.gob.ve/cms/main/verCalendarioV/1634" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inparques.gob.ve">[Inparques]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inparques.gob.ve</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inparques &#124; Calendario verdeSeptember 1, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 2022</span></span></span>

The anthropology holdings are among the most striking because they include direct evidence of human existence rather than only objects made by humans. The physical anthropology collection includes skulls and other skeletal remains, preserved human bodies, funerary materials and comparative anatomical material such as casts of hominin and primate skulls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_de_Ciencias_%28Venezuela%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo de Ciencias (Venezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo de Ciencias (Venezuela</span></span></span>

A particularly unusual part of the collection is the presence of reduced heads, known as tsantsa, linked to Shuar and Achuar peoples of the Ecuadorian and Peruvian Amazon. These objects are often presented in popular culture as mysterious curiosities, but their real significance lies in their connection to Indigenous ritual practices and the later international trade in ethnographic objects. The Museum of Sciences’ collection is therefore part of a much wider South American and global history of collecting Indigenous cultural materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_de_Ciencias_%28Venezuela%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo de Ciencias (Venezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo de Ciencias (Venezuela</span></span></span>

The history of these objects is important because museums once often treated such items as examples of “exotic” customs rather than as objects connected to living communities. Modern museum practice increasingly tries to explain the people, beliefs and historical circumstances behind collections rather than presenting them only as unusual spectacles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.prm.ox.ac.uk/shrunken-heads" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prm.ox.ac.uk">[PRM Oxford]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prm.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PRM Oxford Shrunken heads &#124; Pitt Rivers Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PRM Oxford Shrunken heads &#124; Pitt Rivers Museum</span></span></span>

Other human remains in the collection have a more direct connection to Venezuela’s own archaeological and anthropological record. The museum’s physical anthropology holdings include pre-Hispanic Venezuelan skulls, including examples showing intentional cranial modification, as well as remains showing evidence of disease or physical conditions experienced during life. These materials allow researchers to study past populations, health and cultural practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_de_Ciencias_%28Venezuela%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo de Ciencias (Venezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo de Ciencias (Venezuela</span></span></span>

## Collecting histories and ethical questions

The most controversial aspect of the Museum of Sciences’ human remains collections is not whether the objects are scientifically interesting, but how they entered museum collections and how they should be treated today.

The museum’s anthropology collection developed during a period when scientific institutions around the world were actively gathering human remains and cultural objects. In Venezuela, early collecting included a reduced head donated by writer Arístides Rojas in 1879, after which the museum accumulated further anthropological material through research and collecting activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inparques.gob.ve/cms/main/verCalendarioV/1634" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inparques.gob.ve">[Inparques]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inparques.gob.ve</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inparques &#124; Calendario verdeSeptember 1, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 2022</span></span></span>

This history reflects a broader nineteenth- and early twentieth-century museum tradition in which human remains were often treated as scientific specimens. Contemporary approaches have changed considerably. Many museums now recognise that human remains are not ordinary objects but the remains of individuals, often connected to communities with their own views about ancestors, burial and memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://peabody.yale.edu/explore/collections/anthropology/stewardship-human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: peabody.yale.edu">[Yale Peabody Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">peabody.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Peabody Museum Stewardship of Human Remains &#124; Yale Peabody Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Yale Peabody MuseumStewardship of Human Remains &#124; Yale Peabody Museum...</span></span></span>

Reduced heads provide a clear example of this shift. International museum debates have shown that displaying tsantsa without context can reinforce outdated stereotypes about Indigenous societies. The Pitt Rivers Museum in Oxford, for example, removed its Shuar/Achuar reduced heads from public display after an ethical review, arguing that earlier presentation methods had encouraged visitors to view them through sensational or colonial ideas rather than cultural understanding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2020/09/pitt-rivers-museum-removes-shrunken-heads-from-display-after-ethical-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museumsassociation.org">[Museums Association]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museumsassociation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museums AssociationPitt Rivers Museum removes shrunken heads from display after ethical review - Museums Association...</span></span></span>

The issue for institutions such as the Museum of Sciences is therefore not simply whether human remains are “real” or scientifically valuable. The deeper questions are:

* **Who collected them and under what circumstances?** Older collections often contain incomplete records about acquisition.
* **How should descendants and source communities be involved?** Many museums now seek dialogue with communities connected to human remains and sacred objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://peabody.yale.edu/explore/collections/anthropology/stewardship-human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: peabody.yale.edu">[Yale Peabody Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">peabody.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Peabody Museum Stewardship of Human Remains &#124; Yale Peabody Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Yale Peabody MuseumStewardship of Human Remains &#124; Yale Peabody Museum...</span></span></span>
* **Should remains be displayed, stored, researched or returned?** Different museums and communities answer this differently depending on cultural, legal and historical circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencemuseum.org.uk/human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencemuseum.org.uk">[Science Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencemuseum.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Museum Human Remains &#124; Science Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science Museum Human Remains &#124; Science Museum</span></span></span>

These questions make the Caracas collection historically significant. It is not only a collection of unusual specimens; it is also a record of changing ideas about science, anthropology and the relationship between museums and the people whose histories they preserve.

For visitors, the Museum of Sciences is not simply a “human remains museum”. Its public identity remains that of a broad science institution with collections ranging from animals and fossils to archaeology and ethnography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rnv.gob.ve/museo-de-ciencias-expone-sus-colecciones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rnv.gob.ve">[RNV]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rnv.gob.ve</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RNVMuseo de Ciencias expone sus colecciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Emisora Oficial del Estado venezolanoSeptember 26, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2016</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }

    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');

        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();

        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
