## Introduction

[Mauritius]({{ 'what-makes-mauritiuss-museums-so-unusual/' | relative_url }}) has relatively few traditional “cabinets of curiosity”, yet it has two striking visitor attractions built around private natural history collections: the World of Seashells in Bel Ombre and the insectarium at La Vanille Nature Park. Rather than focusing on rare artefacts or supernatural legends, these collections transform decades of personal collecting into carefully interpreted public displays. Together they show how individual enthusiasm can evolve into museums that are visually spectacular while also encouraging visitors to think about biodiversity, conservation and the history of scientific collecting.

These attractions are unusual because they sit on the boundary between private passion, museum display and environmental education. They preserve thousands of carefully classified specimens that might once have remained in family collections, but are now presented as accessible exhibitions for tourists, school groups and amateur naturalists. ## The World of Seashells collection

The World of Seashells at Bel Ombre is built around the lifelong collection of Mauritian conchologist Eric Le Court de Billot, continuing a collecting tradition inherited from his parents. The museum displays around 8,000 shells representing roughly 1,500 species, making it one of the largest seashell collections in Africa and the only museum of its kind in Mauritius.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.worldofseashells.mu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: worldofseashells.mu">[World of Seashells]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">worldofseashells.mu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on worldofseashells.mu.</span></span></span>

Rather than presenting shells as decorative souvenirs, the exhibition treats them as objects that connect science, mathematics, history and culture. Displays explain:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* shell anatomy and growth;
* defensive adaptations;
* colour and pattern formation;
* the mathematical spirals found in shell shapes;
* myths and legends associated with shells across different cultures;
* historical human uses of shells as ornaments, currency and symbols.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.worldofseashells.mu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: worldofseashells.mu">[World of Seashells]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">worldofseashells.mu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on worldofseashells.mu.</span></span></span>

</div>

This wider interpretation makes the gallery feel closer to a modern cabinet of curiosity than a simple display of marine specimens. Visitors move from cases filled with spectacular giant shells and delicate miniature species to interactive exhibits explaining geometry, ecology and cultural symbolism.

The museum also deliberately places collecting within a conservation framework. Its visitor information explains that shell collecting on Mauritian beaches is legally restricted and describes the ecological role that empty shells play in stabilising beaches and providing habitat for wildlife. By doing so, it distances itself from the older tradition of indiscriminate collecting that supplied many nineteenth-century natural history cabinets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.worldofseashells.mu/frequently-asked-questions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: worldofseashells.mu">[World of Seashells]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">worldofseashells.mu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World of Seashells FAQ &#124; World of Seashells</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World of Seashells FAQ &#124; World of Seashells</span></span></span>

Located at Place du Moulin in Bel Ombre, the gallery also sits within a restored heritage site associated with the area's former sugar industry, allowing visitors to move between industrial history and natural history during the same visit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.worldofseashells.mu/plan-your-visit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: worldofseashells.mu">[World of Seashells]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">worldofseashells.mu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World of Seashells PLAN YOUR VISIT &#124; World of Seashells</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World of Seashells PLAN YOUR VISIT &#124; World of Seashells</span></span></span>

## La Vanille's enormous insectarium

If the shell museum demonstrates the visual beauty of marine life, La Vanille Nature Park showcases the astonishing diversity of insects.

Its insectarium is based on the work of Jacques Siedlecki, whose collection was assembled over roughly three decades. According to the park, the displays include approximately 30,000 preserved specimens representing more than 26,000 species collected across five continents, making it one of the largest and most diverse insect collections available to the public.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavanillenaturepark.com/index.php/en/insectarium-24" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavanillenaturepark.com">[La Vanille Nature Park]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavanillenaturepark.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lavanillenaturepark.com.</span></span></span>

The exhibition's impact comes from scale as much as rarity. Cabinet after cabinet presents butterflies, beetles, stick insects, cicadas and countless other groups arranged by scientific classification. Brilliant metallic beetles, giant tropical butterflies and intricately camouflaged insects reveal extraordinary variation in colour, body shape and survival strategies.

Unlike many historical insect collections that remained hidden in university stores or private homes, La Vanille actively explains how specimens are prepared, preserved and classified. The park highlights the painstaking work required to collect, mount and identify insects, presenting collecting as a disciplined scientific activity rather than merely an eccentric hobby. Visitors can even meet the collector behind the exhibition and hear stories about the collection's development.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavanillenaturepark.com/index.php/en/insectarium-24" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavanillenaturepark.com">[La Vanille Nature Park]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavanillenaturepark.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lavanillenaturepark.com.</span></span></span>

Because the insectarium sits inside a broader wildlife park known for giant tortoises, crocodiles and conservation programmes, preserved specimens are constantly contrasted with living animals. This combination helps visitors understand insects not simply as pinned curiosities but as part of wider ecosystems.

## When private collecting becomes public science

Both attractions belong to a long international tradition in which enthusiastic private collectors accumulated extraordinary natural objects before eventually opening them to wider audiences. In earlier centuries such collections often served mainly as displays of rarity and wealth. Mauritius's modern examples take a noticeably different approach.

Instead of celebrating ownership, both institutions increasingly emphasise:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* biodiversity and species identification;
* ecological responsibility;
* conservation awareness;
* scientific classification;
* educational interpretation for families and schools.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.worldofseashells.mu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: worldofseashells.mu">[World of Seashells]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">worldofseashells.mu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on worldofseashells.mu.</span></span></span>

</div>

That shift matters because natural history collecting has become more ethically complex. Historic shell and insect collections were sometimes assembled during periods when environmental regulation was minimal and collecting pressures were poorly understood. Today's exhibitions therefore place much greater emphasis on explaining ecosystems and discouraging destructive collecting, particularly in fragile island environments.

The result is a more reflective form of curiosity. Visitors are invited to marvel at extraordinary natural forms while also considering how specimens were gathered, why biodiversity matters and how scientific collections can contribute to public understanding rather than simple accumulation.

## Why these collections matter within Mauritius's curiosity culture

Mauritius's unusual collections are often rooted in natural history rather than the supernatural. The island's curiosity landscape revolves around extinction, biodiversity and private scientific passion rather than occult museums or eccentric relic collections.

The World of Seashells and La Vanille's insectarium illustrate this distinctive pattern. Both began with individuals who devoted years to assembling remarkably comprehensive collections. Once opened to the public, those collections became spectacles not because they promised mystery, but because they revealed the astonishing diversity of the natural world in ways that ordinary visitors rarely encounter.

They also demonstrate how the traditional cabinet of curiosity has evolved. Instead of presenting nature as something to possess, these Mauritian collections increasingly frame it as something to understand, protect and appreciate. Their appeal lies in combining visual abundance with scientific interpretation, making private collecting serve a broader public purpose while preserving a sense of wonder that remains central to curiosity culture.

    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
