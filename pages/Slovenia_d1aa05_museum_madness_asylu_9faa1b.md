## Introduction

The Museum of Madness at Cmurek Castle is one of [Slovenia]({{ 'where-slovenia-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s most distinctive and ethically ambitious museums. Rather than treating psychiatric history as a collection of medical curiosities, it preserves the physical remains of a former institution while asking difficult questions about confinement, diagnosis, exclusion and human dignity. The result is not a museum that invites visitors to marvel at "madness", but one that challenges the language, systems and assumptions that once justified isolating thousands of vulnerable people from society. It occupies an unusual place within Slovenia's landscape of strange museums because its central exhibit is an institutional past that many communities would prefer to forget.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coe.int/en/web/culture-and-heritage/faro-active-members/-/asset_publisher/E3FT1EVQJST8/content/the-museum-of-madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coe.int">[Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coe.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal The Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PortalThe Museum of Madness - Culture and Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Can an Old Asylum Become an Ethical Museum?

Unlike museums that display unusual artefacts or sensational stories, the Museum of Madness is built around a moral question: how should a society remember places where people lost much of their freedom in the name of care?

The museum occupies Cmurek Castle in Trate, on Slovenia's border with Austria. Although the medieval castle has a long history, its defining modern identity comes from its use as a residential institution for people labelled mentally ill or intellectually disabled during the second half of the twentieth century. After the institution closed in 2004, local activists, heritage specialists and cross-border partners gradually transformed part of the abandoned complex into the Museum of Madness, which opened as an independent initiative in 2014. Rather than restoring the building into a romantic castle attraction, the project deliberately foregrounds its difficult recent history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culture.si/en/Museum_of_Madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culture.si">[Culture.si]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culture.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Culture of SloveniaJuly 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2026</span></span></span>

This approach places the museum within the growing international movement for interpreting "difficult heritage"—places associated with suffering, discrimination or injustice rather than triumph. Its purpose is therefore educational and reflective as much as historical.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coe.int/en/web/culture-and-heritage/faro-active-members/-/asset_publisher/E3FT1EVQJST8/content/the-museum-of-madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coe.int">[Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coe.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal The Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PortalThe Museum of Madness - Culture and Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Cmurek Castle as an Institution

For decades the castle functioned not as a monument but as a closed institution. Residents lived under a regime shaped by the assumptions of large psychiatric and social welfare facilities that were once common across Europe. Hundreds of people could be housed there simultaneously, often separated from ordinary community life for many years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coe.int/en/web/culture-and-heritage/faro-active-members/-/asset_publisher/E3FT1EVQJST8/content/the-museum-of-madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coe.int">[Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coe.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal The Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PortalThe Museum of Madness - Culture and Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

The museum avoids presenting this history as the result of uniquely cruel individuals. Instead, it encourages visitors to examine broader systems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* how psychiatric diagnoses changed over time;
* why institutional care expanded during the twentieth century;
* how disability and mental illness became reasons for social exclusion;
* why large residential institutions persisted long after criticism of them had begun.

</div>

This wider perspective prevents the castle from becoming merely a site of historical blame. Instead, it becomes a place for examining how public policy, medicine, welfare systems and social attitudes interacted.

An important reason the site attracts attention from researchers is that the closure of the institution formed part of Slovenia's wider movement towards deinstitutionalisation. According to the museum and the Council of Europe, Cmurek became the first—and remains the only—large Slovenian "total institution" whose residents were systematically resettled into community homes after closure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coe.int/en/web/culture-and-heritage/faro-active-members/-/asset_publisher/E3FT1EVQJST8/content/the-museum-of-madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coe.int">[Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coe.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal The Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PortalThe Museum of Madness - Culture and Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Objects, Spaces and Memories of Confinement

The museum's collections are unusual because many of their most significant exhibits are not rare artefacts but ordinary institutional remains.

Visitors encounter preserved rooms, architectural spaces and everyday objects that illustrate life inside the institution. Personal belongings, administrative material, photographs and oral testimonies provide context, while the building itself remains one of the most powerful exhibits. Corridors, wards and shared spaces communicate aspects of institutional life that would be difficult to explain through text alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culture.si/en/Museum_of_Madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culture.si">[Culture.si]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culture.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Culture of SloveniaJuly 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2026</span></span></span>

The museum deliberately combines material evidence with personal memory. Former employees, residents and local communities contribute stories that complicate simple narratives of heroes and villains. Some staff recall difficult working conditions within an under-resourced system, while former residents and advocates highlight the loss of autonomy experienced by those who spent years inside institutional care. This combination helps visitors understand both the human consequences of confinement and the structural pressures that shaped it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coe.int/en/web/culture-and-heritage/faro-active-members/-/asset_publisher/E3FT1EVQJST8/content/the-museum-of-madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coe.int">[Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coe.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal The Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PortalThe Museum of Madness - Culture and Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

Rather than separating psychiatric history from the surrounding landscape, the museum also links it with the wider heritage of the Mura River border region, making clear that the institution was embedded within an ordinary rural community rather than existing outside society.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culture.si/en/Museum_of_Madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culture.si">[Culture.si]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culture.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Culture of SloveniaJuly 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2026</span></span></span>

## Ethics, Stigma and Deinstitutionalisation

The museum's most distinctive feature is its refusal to treat psychiatric history as finished.

Its stated mission is to reduce the stigma surrounding mental illness, broaden public understanding of "madness" beyond medical labels and contribute to debates about community-based care. Exhibitions, lectures, educational programmes and collaborations with advocacy groups encourage discussion about how people experiencing mental illness should participate in society today rather than simply documenting the past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.muzejnorosti.eu/about-us" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.muzejnorosti.eu">[Muzejnorosti]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.muzejnorosti.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About us &#124; Muzej norosti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">About us &#124; Muzej norosti...</span></span></span>

This makes the museum unusual among heritage institutions. Instead of presenting a neutral historical narrative, it openly acknowledges its values:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* preserving memories of institutional life;
* supporting human rights and social inclusion;
* encouraging discussion about deinstitutionalisation;
* involving people with lived experience in interpretation whenever possible.

</div>

The Council of Europe recognised the museum within its Faro Convention Network partly because of this participatory approach, describing it as a meeting place connecting researchers, visitors, local communities and people with direct experience of psychiatric institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coe.int/en/web/culture-and-heritage/faro-active-members/-/asset_publisher/E3FT1EVQJST8/content/the-museum-of-madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coe.int">[Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coe.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal The Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PortalThe Museum of Madness - Culture and Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

The museum therefore occupies an interesting position between heritage site, community project, educational centre and social intervention. Critics of advocacy-based museums sometimes argue that such institutions risk privileging present-day values over historical context. The Museum of Madness responds by grounding its interpretation in surviving buildings, archival evidence and personal testimony while making its ethical perspective explicit rather than pretending complete neutrality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coe.int/en/web/culture-and-heritage/faro-active-members/-/asset_publisher/E3FT1EVQJST8/content/the-museum-of-madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coe.int">[Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coe.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal The Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PortalThe Museum of Madness - Culture and Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Why It Matters Within Slovenia's Curiosity Culture

Among Slovenia's unusual museums, the Museum of Madness stands apart because its central curiosity is not an exotic object but the transformation of an institution itself into an exhibit.

Where other offbeat collections preserve [carnival masks]({{ 'carnival-masks/' | relative_url }}), underground creatures or archaeological oddities, Cmurek Castle preserves evidence of changing ideas about normality, illness and citizenship. The building asks visitors to consider how future generations may judge present-day assumptions about disability and mental health.

For travellers interested in unusual museums, the experience is therefore quieter but arguably more unsettling than many collections of medical curiosities. The castle demonstrates that the most extraordinary exhibits are sometimes ordinary rooms whose history forces visitors to rethink accepted social systems. Rather than exploiting the spectacle of psychiatric history, the Museum of Madness seeks to restore dignity to those whose lives were shaped by it, making it one of Slovenia's most thoughtful examples of heritage used as a tool for public reflection rather than nostalgia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culture.si/en/Museum_of_Madness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culture.si">[culture.si]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culture.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Madness</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Culture of SloveniaJuly 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2026</span></span></span>
