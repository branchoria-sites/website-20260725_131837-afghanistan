## Introduction

For visitors interested in strange history and unusual collections, Togo’s strongest attraction is the meeting point between Vodun traditions, traditional medicine, sacred objects and public heritage. The Akodessewa Fetish Market in Lomé is the best-known example: it is not a museum in the formal sense, but a working marketplace where ritual specialists obtain materials used within spiritual and healing practices. Alongside it, institutions such as the [National Museum]({{ 'national-museum-160a3c/' | relative_url }}) of Togo and the [Palais de Lomé]({{ 'palais-de-lomé/' | relative_url }}) show how the country preserves more conventional historical collections while also reinterpreting colonial and cultural heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/inside-togos-thriving-voodoo-fetish-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[atlasobscura.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Inside Togo&#x27;s Thriving Voodoo Fetish Market</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraInside Togo&#x27;s Thriving Voodoo Fetish MarketJuly 30, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2015</span></span></span>

## Why Togo stands out in the world of unusual collections

Togo’s unusual heritage is less about collecting “oddities” for entertainment and more about preserving objects that sit between art, religion, medicine and social memory. Many items that appear mysterious to outsiders — charms, carved figures, animal materials, ritual tools or ancestor-related objects — have meanings within particular belief systems rather than simply being curiosities. This makes Togo different from classic European-style cabinets of curiosity, where rare objects were often removed from their original contexts and displayed as wonders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.chateau-vodou.com/the-collection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: chateau-vodou.com">[Musée Vodou]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">chateau-vodou.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Vodou The collection and the vodou</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée VodouThe collection and the vodou - Musée Vodou the collection vodou museum...</span></span></span>

## Akodessewa: the market that became a global curiosity

### A collection that is alive rather than preserved

The Akodessewa Fetish Market in Lomé is probably the closest thing Togo has to a famous “strange collection”. Unlike a museum, it does not present objects as historical specimens. It functions as a marketplace connected to traditional spiritual practice, where practitioners seek materials used in rituals, remedies and protective practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/inside-togos-thriving-voodoo-fetish-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Inside Togo&#x27;s Thriving Voodoo Fetish Market</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraInside Togo&#x27;s Thriving Voodoo Fetish MarketJuly 30, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2015</span></span></span>

Visitors commonly encounter displays of dried animal materials, carved objects, herbs and ritual items. To an outsider, the visual effect can resemble a museum of the macabre, with unusual bones, skins and preserved remains arranged in stalls. However, practitioners generally understand these objects through systems of belief, healing and spiritual relationships rather than as displays of horror.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/inside-togos-thriving-voodoo-fetish-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Inside Togo&#x27;s Thriving Voodoo Fetish Market</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraInside Togo&#x27;s Thriving Voodoo Fetish MarketJuly 30, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2015</span></span></span>

## Palais de Lomé: a colonial palace transformed into a cultural curiosity

The Palais de Lomé is unusual because the building itself is the collection. Originally built in 1905 as a residence for German colonial governors, it later served French colonial officials and became linked with the government of independent Togo. After years of decline, it was restored and reopened as an arts and cultural centre in 2019.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://togotourisme.tg/site/palais-de-lome/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: togotourisme.tg">[Togo Tourisme]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">togotourisme.tg</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Togo Tourisme Palais de Lomé</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Togo TourismePalais de Lomé - Togo Tourisme...</span></span></span>

## Beyond museums: Togo’s wider curiosity landscape

### Sacred objects, craft and folklore

Many of Togo’s most unusual collections exist outside official institutions. Regional museums contain ethnographic objects, ritual items, musical instruments and craft traditions connected to local communities. The country’s heritage authorities highlight collections in places such as Aného, Kara, Sokodé and Dapaong, where visitors can encounter objects linked to regional history and everyday life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://togotourisme.tg/un-patrimoine-historique/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: togotourisme.tg">[Togo Tourisme]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">togotourisme.tg</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Togo Tourisme- Togo Tourisme</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Togo Tourisme- Togo Tourisme</span></span></span>

Some displays also preserve stories that sit between history and folklore. For example, the regional museum in Dapaong has highlighted objects and traditions connected with local figures and beliefs, including stories attached to objects said to possess special powers. These accounts are valuable as cultural narratives even when their supernatural claims are matters of belief rather than historical proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://togotourisme.tg/un-patrimoine-historique/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: togotourisme.tg">[Togo Tourisme]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">togotourisme.tg</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Togo Tourisme- Togo Tourisme</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Togo Tourisme- Togo Tourisme</span></span></span>

### A country where the “museum” is often the tradition itself

Togo demonstrates that unusual collections do not always require shelves, labels and glass cases. A ritual market, a sacred object, a historic palace or an ethnographic display can all preserve unusual knowledge about how societies understand health, identity, ancestors and the unseen world.

For travellers interested in strange museums and curiosity culture, Togo’s appeal lies in this boundary between collection and practice. The country offers fewer classic oddity museums than some destinations, but it provides something rarer: places where unusual objects are still connected to the communities, beliefs and stories that gave them meaning.
