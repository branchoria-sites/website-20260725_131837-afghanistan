## Introduction

[San Marino]({{ 'why-is-san-marino-packed-with-strange/' | relative_url }}) does not have a deep native tradition of vampire or werewolf folklore comparable with Romania's Dracula associations or the werewolf legends of parts of France and Germany. Instead, it has created a visitor attraction that deliberately stages these figures as part of a broader European horror tradition. The Museum of Vampires and Werewolves uses life-sized models, theatrical scenery and familiar literary and cinematic characters to transform centuries of myth into an entertainment experience rather than an ethnographic study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

The result says as much about modern cultural tourism and Europe's shared fascination with monsters as it does about folklore itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

## Are San Marino's Vampires Folklore or Theatre?

The attraction presents vampires and werewolves as creatures that have travelled through European imagination for roughly a thousand years. Official descriptions emphasise myth, legend, superstition and history together, inviting visitors to move between documented beliefs and fictional reinventions rather than separating them into strict categories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

This approach reflects a common pattern in dark tourism. Instead of reconstructing one local belief system, the museum assembles recognisable figures from across Europe into a single narrative that is immediately familiar to visitors. It functions more like a themed exhibition or horror attraction than a folklore archive. The emphasis falls on atmosphere, dramatic reconstructions and visual storytelling rather than original artefacts or scholarly interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

For readers interested in San Marino's curiosity culture, this is an important distinction. The museum belongs alongside the country's other private attractions—such as the torture museum, wax museum and curiosity museum—which use immersive displays to entertain while drawing on genuine historical themes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sanmarinosite.com/en/things-to-see/museums/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sanmarinosite.com">[sanmarinosite.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sanmarinosite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museums Archivi &#124; San Marino Site Turismo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museums Archivi &#124; San Marino Site Turismo</span></span></span>

## Dracula, Nosferatu and the Modern Horror Canon

Rather than concentrating on obscure regional legends, the museum builds its exhibition around characters already embedded in international popular culture.

Among those highlighted are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Count Dracula**, whose literary origins in Bram Stoker's 1897 novel have largely eclipsed earlier vampire traditions in the public imagination.
* **Nosferatu**, the bald, plague-like vampire from early German cinema, representing the visual language of twentieth-century horror.
* **Elizabeth Báthory**, the Hungarian noblewoman whose historical reputation has become intertwined with vampire mythology despite the absence of evidence that she inspired traditional vampire beliefs directly.
* **The Highgate Vampire**, associated with sensational reports from a London cemetery during the early 1970s, illustrating how modern urban legends continue to reshape vampire folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

</div>

Werewolves receive similar treatment. Rather than focusing on one regional tradition, the exhibition presents lycanthropy as a recurring European belief that has evolved from medieval superstition into modern horror fiction. Displays reportedly combine explanatory panels with life-sized figures, reconstructed scenes and memorabilia that blur the line between historical belief and cinematic monster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sanmarinosite.com/en/things-to-see/museums/museum-of-vampires-and-werewolves/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sanmarinosite.com">[sanmarinosite.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sanmarinosite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Vampires and Werewolves San Marino</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Vampires and Werewolves San Marino</span></span></span>

The effect is to place literary classics, film history and older folklore within a single continuous narrative. Visitors are encouraged to recognise famous characters while also discovering that many horror conventions originated in older European fears about disease, death, transformation and the supernatural.

## Historical Figures and Legends Placed Side by Side

One of the museum's defining characteristics is its willingness to juxtapose documented historical figures with fictional creations and disputed legends.

A visitor may move from a display inspired by Elizabeth Báthory to Dracula, then to stories such as the Highgate Vampire, without sharp distinctions between verified history, literary invention and urban legend. This reflects the way popular horror culture often operates: memorable stories become culturally significant regardless of whether they originated in historical records, novels or newspaper sensationalism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

For general audiences this creates an engaging narrative, but it also means visitors should understand the exhibition as interpretive theatre rather than historical proof. The museum itself frames its subject through myths, legends and imagination, not as evidence that vampires or werewolves literally existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

This blending of categories is common across European horror attractions, where historical personalities, folklore and fictional monsters are deliberately presented together because they share an emotional rather than factual connection.

## Why the Medieval Hill City Strengthens the Illusion

The museum's location contributes significantly to its impact.

San Marino's historic centre consists of steep medieval streets, stone buildings, fortified gates and towers overlooking dramatic mountain scenery. Even before entering the attraction, visitors are already walking through an environment that visually resembles the settings associated with Gothic fiction. The surrounding architecture supplies much of the atmosphere that the exhibition relies upon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/luogo/Divertimenti-e-attrazioni/Museo-dei-Vampiri-e-Licantropi.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsanmarino.com.</span></span></span>

Unlike purpose-built horror parks located on urban fringes, this attraction sits among genuine medieval streets that naturally reinforce ideas of ancient legends, mysterious nights and hidden histories. The contrast between UNESCO-recognised civic heritage and commercial horror spectacle becomes part of the experience.

This explains why San Marino has proved successful at clustering unusual museums together. A visitor can move within minutes from state monuments and historic fortifications to museums devoted to torture, [curiosities]({{ 'curiosities/' | relative_url }}), wax figures and supernatural creatures without leaving the old city. The medieval setting makes these transitions feel surprisingly coherent, even when the exhibitions themselves are largely theatrical.

Its significance lies less in documenting indigenous belief than in demonstrating how European folklore is repackaged for contemporary tourism.
