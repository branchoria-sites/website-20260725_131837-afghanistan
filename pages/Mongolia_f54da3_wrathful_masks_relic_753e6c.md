## Introduction

[Mongolia]({{ 'why-mongolias-strangest-collections/' | relative_url }})'s fierce Buddhist masks and reports of preserved clergy can look unsettling to modern visitors, but they are not intended as objects of horror. Instead, they belong to a ritual tradition in which terrifying faces symbolise the defeat of ignorance, fear and harmful forces rather than the celebration of evil. The country's best-known public collection is preserved at the Choijin Lama Temple Museum in Ulaanbaatar, where elaborate ceremonial masks, ritual costumes and sacred objects survive alongside a much-discussed preserved cleric. Together they illustrate how Mongolia's Buddhist heritage was transformed from a living religious tradition into a museum collection after the anti-religious campaigns of the late 1930s, while still retaining deep devotional significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/museums/choijin/choijin.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: depts.washington.edu">[washington.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">depts.washington.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span></span></span>

## Why Mongolia's Fierce Masks Are Not Demons

To many first-time visitors, the oversized masks with bulging eyes, skull crowns, fangs and grimacing expressions resemble demons from folklore or horror films. Within Mongolian Buddhism, however, these figures represent wrathful protectors rather than evil beings.

Wrathful deities are depicted in frightening forms because their role is to overcome spiritual obstacles forcefully. Their terrifying appearance is understood as compassionate rather than malicious: anger is directed against ignorance, attachment and destructive behaviour, not against ordinary people. This symbolism is common across the Tibetan Buddhist traditions that profoundly influenced Mongolian religious life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/museums/choijin/choijin.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: depts.washington.edu">[UW Departments]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">depts.washington.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span></span></span>

The masks were not everyday religious objects. They formed part of highly choreographed ritual dances performed by trained monks during major ceremonies. Each mask represented a specific protector, deity or symbolic character, with elaborate costumes completing the transformation from performer into sacred figure. The visual impact was intended to communicate religious ideas to large audiences while ritually purifying the surrounding community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/museums/choijin/choijin.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: depts.washington.edu">[UW Departments]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">depts.washington.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span></span></span>

Rather than presenting supernatural monsters, the museum preserves objects that once functioned as active ritual instruments. Display cases therefore freeze what were originally moving performances involving music, chanting, costume and carefully prescribed ceremonial actions.

## The Monumental Masks of Choijin Lama Temple

The Choijin Lama Temple Museum contains one of Mongolia's most important surviving collections of ceremonial Tsam dance masks. Built between 1904 and 1908 for Choijin Lama Luvsanhaidav, brother of the Eighth Bogd Khaan, the temple escaped the widespread destruction that eliminated hundreds of monasteries during the religious purges of the 1930s. It subsequently became a museum rather than returning to full monastic use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://insightmongolia.com/guides/choijin-lama-temple-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: insightmongolia.com">[insightmongolia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">insightmongolia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Choijin Lama Temple Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Insight Mongolia...</span></span></span>

The preserved masks are remarkable both for their size and craftsmanship. Many are constructed from papier-mâché, then richly decorated with paint, textiles, metalwork, coral or other ornamentation. Their exaggerated expressions were designed to be visible across crowded monastery courtyards during ritual performances rather than examined at close range behind museum glass.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/museums/choijin/choijin.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: depts.washington.edu">[UW Departments]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">depts.washington.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span></span></span>

## Preserved Remains, Visitor Claims and Evidence Limits

The museum's most frequently discussed curiosity is not a mask but a preserved cleric associated with the main temple. Guidebooks and museum descriptions commonly note the presence of the embalmed remains of Baldanchoimbol, a senior lama and teacher connected with the Bogd Khaan's court. Some descriptions state that the remains are displayed directly, while others explain that they are preserved within a statue or shrine inside the main temple.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mongolia.gogo.mn/r/jkj06" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mongolia.gogo.mn">[gogo.mn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mongolia.gogo.mn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">to visit: Museums in UBJuly 16, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 2016</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because visitor accounts sometimes exaggerate what can actually be seen. The museum is not organised as a display of human remains comparable to an anatomical museum or crypt. Instead, the preserved cleric forms part of a sacred historical setting in which religious significance outweighs curiosity value.

Published descriptions generally agree that preserved remains exist within the temple complex, but they differ in exactly how visible they are to visitors and how they are incorporated into the display. The available evidence does not support sensational claims about openly exhibited mummies or hidden supernatural relics. Rather, the preserved body survives as an exceptional religious object connected with Mongolia's Buddhist hierarchy and the temple's original function.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mongolia.gogo.mn/r/jkj06" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mongolia.gogo.mn">[gogo.mn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mongolia.gogo.mn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">to visit: Museums in UBJuly 16, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 2016</span></span></span>

The survival of these remains is historically significant for another reason. During the anti-religious campaigns of 1937–38, thousands of monks were killed, monasteries were destroyed and enormous numbers of religious objects disappeared. The Choijin Lama complex became one of the rare sites where important ritual collections and associated sacred objects escaped wholesale destruction, making both the masks and the preserved cleric witnesses to a disrupted religious past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://insightmongolia.com/guides/choijin-lama-temple-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: insightmongolia.com">[insightmongolia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">insightmongolia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Choijin Lama Temple Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Insight Mongolia...</span></span></span>

## Why These Collections Matter Within Mongolia's Curiosity Culture

For travellers seeking unusual collections, the Choijin Lama Temple Museum offers something distinct from conventional ideas of the bizarre. The unsettling appearance of its wrathful masks derives from religious symbolism rather than occult spectacle, while the preserved cleric represents continuity of sacred tradition rather than a museum of death.

This combination makes the collection especially revealing within Mongolia's landscape of unusual heritage. The objects have crossed several identities over the last century: they began as active ritual equipment, became protected museum artefacts during the socialist era, and today serve simultaneously as historical treasures, works of art and reminders of a religious culture that narrowly survived political repression. The sense of mystery comes not from supernatural claims but from encountering objects whose original ceremonial power is still recognisable even after they have entered the museum world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/museums/choijin/choijin.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: depts.washington.edu">[washington.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">depts.washington.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UW Departments Choijin Lama Museum, Ulaanbaatar</span></span></span>

        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
