## Introduction

Switzerlands witch museums do not present witches as a hidden supernatural reality; they preserve the much more unsettling story of how ordinary people came to be accused, investigated and sometimes executed for imagined crimes. The countrys witch-related collections sit between folklore, social history and the history of justice, showing how fears about magic, illness, misfortune and religious danger shaped communities for centuries. The most important visitor-facing examples are the Hexenmuseum Schweiz in Grnichen, which explores witch beliefs and persecution traditions, and the Anna Gldi Museum in Glarus, which focuses on one of Europes last witchcraft executions and its later recognition as a miscarriage of justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/experiences/hexenmuseum-schweiz/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: myswitzerland.com">[Switzerland Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">myswitzerland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span></span></span>

These collections are unusual because they do not simply display witch objects. Their deeper subject is memory: how societies create explanations for unexplained events, how courts turn beliefs into accusations, and how later generations reinterpret painful episodes from the past. Switzerlands witch museums are therefore less about magic itself than about the relationship between fear, evidence, power and justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hexenmuseum.ch/Englisch.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hexenmuseum.ch">[Hexenmuseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hexenmuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Schweiz Englisch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hexenmuseum Schweiz Englisch...</span></span></span>

## Hexenmuseum Schweiz and folk beliefs

The Hexenmuseum Schweiz at Schloss Liebegg near Grnichen in the canton of Aargau is one of Switzerlands few dedicated witchcraft museums. It is a private museum that combines historical material, folklore and interpretations of magical traditions. The exhibition covers witch trials in [Switzerland]({{ 'where-switzerland-preserves-its-weirdest/' | relative_url }}) and Europe alongside folk religion, protective customs, medicinal plants, supernatural figures, healing traditions and beliefs about charms or omens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/experiences/hexenmuseum-schweiz/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: myswitzerland.com">[Switzerland Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">myswitzerland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span></span></span>

What makes the collection distinctive is its position between a historical museum and a folklore attraction. Visitors encounter the ideas that shaped past communities: why people believed certain objects could protect them, why illness or bad harvests were sometimes blamed on hidden forces, and why accusations of witchcraft could become socially accepted. The museums interest is not that these beliefs were scientifically true, but that they reveal how people in earlier periods tried to understand uncertainty and danger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hexenmuseum.ch/Englisch.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hexenmuseum.ch">[Hexenmuseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hexenmuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Schweiz Englisch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hexenmuseum Schweiz Englisch...</span></span></span>

The museum also reflects a wider change in the way witchcraft is remembered. Earlier societies often treated witch accusations as a legal and religious problem. Modern museums usually examine the accusations themselves as historical evidence of social tensions, prejudice and failures of justice. This shift changes the visitors question from Did witches exist? to Why did communities believe they needed witches as explanations?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.graenichen.ch/de/unternehmensverzeichnis/80_hexenmuseum-schweiz" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: graenichen.ch">[Graenichen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">graenichen.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unternehmensverzeichnis: Hexenmuseum Schweiz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unternehmensverzeichnis: Hexenmuseum Schweiz...</span></span></span>

### From accusations to cultural memory

Swiss witch collections are especially interesting because the countrys witch-hunt history was severe by European standards. Historians estimate that thousands of people were executed for witchcraft accusations in the territories of modern Switzerland, with particularly intense persecutions in areas such as Valais, Vaud and Fribourg. Exact numbers remain debated because surviving records are incomplete, but researchers agree that witch trials were a major part of early modern Swiss legal and social history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://60stories.essex.ac.uk/what-is-a-witch/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: 60stories.essex.ac.uk">[Sixty Stories]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">60stories.essex.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sixty Stories What is a witch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sixty Stories What is a witch</span></span></span>

The accusations were rarely based on one simple idea. They combined religious fears, local conflicts, rumours, political authority and legal practices that allowed torture to be used in investigations. A neighbours illness, a failed crop, unusual behaviour or a personal dispute could become part of a larger story of suspicion. Swiss witch museums are valuable because they show this process rather than reducing the past to a collection of frightening legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/culture/no-one-tortured-witches-like-the-swiss/32908" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swissinfo.ch">[SWI swissinfo.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swissinfo.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWI swissinfo.ch No one tortured witches like the Swiss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWI swissinfo.chNo one tortured witches like the Swiss - SWI swissinfo.ch...</span></span></span>

## Anna Gldi and the legacy of witch trials

The most famous Swiss witchcraft memory is the case of Anna Gldi, a domestic servant executed in Glarus in 1782. She is often described as the last person executed for witchcraft in Europe, although historians note that such labels depend on how different regions define their final witchcraft prosecutions. Her case remains one of the clearest examples of how accusations of supernatural crime could continue into the late eighteenth century.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/society/crime-and-punishment_killing-off-the-death-penalty-in-switzerland/41732660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swissinfo.ch">[SWI swissinfo.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swissinfo.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWI swissinfo.ch Killing off the death penalty in Switzerland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWI swissinfo.chKilling off the death penalty in Switzerland - SWI swissinfo.chOctober 22, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 22, 2015</span></span></span>

The Anna Gldi Museum in Ennenda, Glarus, does not present her story as a tale of a genuine witch. Instead, it examines the circumstances of her accusation, the legal system that condemned her and the later reassessment of the case. The canton of Glarus officially rehabilitated Anna Gldi in 2008, recognising the trial as a miscarriage of justice more than two centuries after her execution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annagoeldi.ch/index.php/de/component/content/article/11-deutsch/informationen/14-lageplan-anreise" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annagoeldi.ch">[Anna-Goeldi-Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annagoeldi.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on annagoeldi.ch.</span></span></span>

Her story matters because it changes the meaning of the word witch in a museum setting. Anna Gldi was not remembered because of proven magical acts; she is remembered because a society once accepted an accusation that later generations judged unjust. The museum therefore belongs as much to the history of law, human rights and public memory as to the history of folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://annagoeldimuseum.ch/index.php/de/information/information-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annagoeldimuseum.ch">[Anna-Goeldi-Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annagoeldimuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anna-Goeldi-Museum Informationen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anna-Goeldi-Museum Informationen</span></span></span>

### Why Anna Gldi remains a powerful symbol

Anna Gldis case is unusual because it occurred at the end of the European witch-trial era, when Enlightenment ideas about evidence and rational investigation were becoming more influential. Her execution shows that older beliefs and legal traditions did not disappear suddenly. In some places, accusations connected to witchcraft continued even as other parts of Europe were moving away from such prosecutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/society/crime-and-punishment_killing-off-the-death-penalty-in-switzerland/41732660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swissinfo.ch">[SWI swissinfo.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swissinfo.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWI swissinfo.ch Killing off the death penalty in Switzerland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWI swissinfo.chKilling off the death penalty in Switzerland - SWI swissinfo.chOctober 22, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 22, 2015</span></span></span>

The modern museum interpretation also highlights a broader historical lesson: persecution often depended not only on belief in magic, but on social vulnerability. Accused people could be outsiders, servants, poor neighbours or individuals involved in local disputes. The witch trials reveal how fear can transform ordinary conflicts into accusations with life-changing consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/culture/no-one-tortured-witches-like-the-swiss/32908" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swissinfo.ch">[SWI swissinfo.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swissinfo.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWI swissinfo.ch No one tortured witches like the Swiss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWI swissinfo.chNo one tortured witches like the Swiss - SWI swissinfo.ch...</span></span></span>

## Visiting collections of magic and memory

For travellers interested in Switzerlands unusual museums, witch-related collections offer a different experience from traditional historical displays. They are not museums of confirmed mysteries; they are museums about the human need to explain mysteries. A visitor is likely to encounter a mixture of historical documents, folklore objects, interpretations of magical traditions and stories of people caught up in systems of accusation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/experiences/hexenmuseum-schweiz/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: myswitzerland.com">[Switzerland Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">myswitzerland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span></span></span>

The two main visitor experiences have different emphases:

* **Hexenmuseum Schweiz, Grnichen:** focuses broadly on witch beliefs, folk traditions, magical practices and the history of witch persecution in Switzerland and Europe. It is a folklore-oriented collection with an emphasis on cultural beliefs and customs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/experiences/hexenmuseum-schweiz/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: myswitzerland.com">[Switzerland Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">myswitzerland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Switzerland Tourism Hexenmuseum Schweiz &#124; Switzerland Tourism</span></span></span>
* **Anna Gldi Museum, Ennenda:** focuses on a single historical case and uses it to explore injustice, legal responsibility and the changing interpretation of witch trials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annagoeldi.ch/index.php/de/component/content/article/11-deutsch/informationen/14-lageplan-anreise" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annagoeldi.ch">[Anna-Goeldi-Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annagoeldi.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on annagoeldi.ch.</span></span></span>

Together, these collections show two sides of Switzerlands witch heritage. One preserves the imaginative world of charms, legends and supernatural explanations; the other preserves the human consequences of when those explanations entered courts and became accusations. Their importance lies not in keeping old fears alive, but in showing how societies learn from the fears they once accepted.
