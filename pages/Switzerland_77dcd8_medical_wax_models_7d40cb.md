## Introduction

The Museum of Wax Moulages in Zurich preserves one of [Switzerland]({{ 'where-switzerland-preserves-its-weirdest/' | relative_url }})’s strangest medical collections: hundreds of extraordinarily realistic wax models showing diseased skin, surgical changes and other bodily conditions. These objects can look unsettling to modern visitors, but they were never created as curiosities in the traditional sense. They were scientific teaching tools designed to help doctors recognise illnesses before photography, colour printing and digital imaging could reliably capture clinical appearances. Today, the collection has become both a medical archive and a rare record of how people have historically observed, classified and represented the human body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uzh.ch/en/explore/museums/moulagenmuseum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uzh.ch">[UZH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH...</span></span></span>

The Zurich collection belongs to the wider history of medical wax modelling, a field where craftsmanship, observation and medicine overlapped. Its importance lies in the fact that the models preserve evidence of diseases, treatments and medical ideas from earlier periods, including conditions that have become rare or changed through modern healthcare. Rather than displaying a “cabinet of horrors”, the collection shows how medicine once depended on careful visual memory and physical objects to transfer knowledge between patients, teachers and students.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zuercher-museen.ch/en/museums/museum-of-wax-moulages" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zuercher-museen.ch">[Zürcher Museen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zuercher-museen.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zürcher Museen Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; Zürcher Museen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zürcher Museen Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; Zürcher Museen</span></span></span>

## How wax moulages taught doctors

### A classroom built from wax and observation

A wax moulage is a three-dimensional reproduction of a medical condition, usually made by modelling wax directly from clinical observations. The Zurich museum describes moulages as realistic recreations of pathological changes, especially skin alterations, produced for teaching and documentation from the nineteenth century onwards. Their value came from their ability to preserve details that written descriptions often failed to communicate: the colour, texture, shape and distribution of a disease on the body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uzh.ch/en/explore/museums/moulagenmuseum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uzh.ch">[UZH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH...</span></span></span>

Before modern medical photography became widespread, doctors and students needed reliable ways to study visible symptoms. A textbook might describe a rash or lesion, but a carefully made wax model allowed a student to examine the appearance of a condition repeatedly and compare it with other cases. The models acted as permanent clinical examples at a time when a real patient’s illness could not simply be kept available for future teaching.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uzh.ch/en/explore/museums/moulagenmuseum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uzh.ch">[UZH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH...</span></span></span>

Zurich became an important centre for this practice through its university and hospital environment. The collection developed alongside dermatology and surgery, disciplines where visual recognition was especially important. Some moulages represented common diseases, while others recorded rare or historically significant conditions that modern doctors may encounter less frequently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moulagen.uzh.ch/en/museum/exhibition.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moulagen.uzh.ch">[Moulagen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moulagen.uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Moulagen Exhibition &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Moulagen Exhibition &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span></span></span>

### Why the models look so lifelike

The unusual appearance of moulages comes from the combination of medical accuracy and artistic technique. Makers had to reproduce subtle human details: variations in skin tone, swelling, scars, wounds and surface texture. The goal was not artistic beauty but clinical realism.

This realism is one reason the Zurich collection attracts visitors beyond medical audiences. Many people expect a museum object to be a painting, sculpture or historical artefact, but these wax works occupy an unusual middle ground. They are scientific records created by hand, yet they also reveal the skill of specialist craftspeople who translated human illness into a durable physical form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moulagen.uzh.ch/en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moulagen.uzh.ch">[Moulagen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moulagen.uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Moulagen Moulagenmuseum &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Moulagen Moulagenmuseum &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span></span></span>

## The preservation of rare medical records

### A body archive rather than a collection of oddities

The Museum of Wax Moulages contains almost 2,500 wax moulages, with around 600 displayed to the public. The University of Zurich describes the collection as among the best preserved and most lifelike medical wax collections in the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uzh.ch/en/explore/museums/moulagenmuseum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uzh.ch">[UZH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH...</span></span></span>

What makes the collection unusual within Switzerland’s curiosity landscape is that its “strangeness” comes from historical medicine rather than folklore or entertainment. The objects preserve traces of real patients and real medical encounters, although they are now separated from the original clinical situations in which they were created. They show how doctors once documented the body, what diseases attracted medical attention and which visual signs were considered important for diagnosis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zuercher-museen.ch/en/museums/museum-of-wax-moulages" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zuercher-museen.ch">[Zürcher Museen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zuercher-museen.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zürcher Museen Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; Zürcher Museen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zürcher Museen Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; Zürcher Museen</span></span></span>

The collection also demonstrates how medical knowledge changes. Some conditions represented in wax have become rarer because of antibiotics, vaccination, improved hygiene or different treatment methods. Others remain relevant because they show enduring patterns of disease. A visitor is therefore not simply looking at old medical models; they are seeing a record of changing relationships between illness, technology and society.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moulagen.uzh.ch/en/museum/exhibition.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moulagen.uzh.ch">[Moulagen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moulagen.uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Moulagen Exhibition &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Moulagen Exhibition &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span></span></span>

### Saving objects that were once considered outdated

For much of the twentieth century, wax moulages lost importance as medical photography, slides and digital resources transformed education. Some collections elsewhere were neglected or discarded because the models seemed obsolete. Zurich’s collection survived through periods when its educational role changed, with historical and conservation efforts helping to preserve the objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moulagen.uzh.ch/en/history-technique/zurich.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moulagen.uzh.ch">[Moulagen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moulagen.uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Moulagen Moulage History in Zurich &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Moulagen Moulage History in Zurich &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span></span></span>

Today, the collection has gained renewed attention from historians and medical researchers because the objects are valuable not only as teaching aids but also as historical evidence. The University of Zurich’s involvement with international networks studying medical wax models reflects a wider reassessment of these collections as important material records of medicine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moulagen.uzh.ch/en/IWWM.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moulagen.uzh.ch">[Moulagen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moulagen.uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Moulagen Home &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Moulagen Home &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span></span></span>

## Science behind a strange-looking collection

### Where medicine meets craft

The Museum of Wax Moulages is unusual because it challenges the boundary between science and museum art. A visitor may first notice the unsettling realism of the models, but their deeper significance is methodological: they reveal how doctors once created shared knowledge from physical observation.

The making of a moulage required cooperation between medical specialists and model makers. Doctors provided clinical expertise, while artisans transformed observations into objects that could be studied long after the original case had disappeared. This partnership reflects an older form of scientific communication, where handmade objects played the role now often taken by photographs, scans and digital simulations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uzh.ch/en/explore/museums/moulagenmuseum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uzh.ch">[UZH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; University of Zurich &#124; UZH...</span></span></span>

### A public collection with an academic purpose

Unlike many unusual collections that are built around mystery, spectacle or belief, Zurich’s wax moulages are firmly evidence-based. Their fascination comes from their authenticity as records of medical history rather than from claims about hidden knowledge or unexplained phenomena.

The museum remains connected to medical education, with the collection still used as a teaching resource while also being presented to general visitors. Exhibitions explain both the medical meaning of the objects and the historical significance they have acquired over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zuercher-museen.ch/en/museums/museum-of-wax-moulages" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zuercher-museen.ch">[Zürcher Museen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zuercher-museen.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zürcher Museen Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; Zürcher Museen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zürcher Museen Museum of Wax Moulages &#124; Zürcher Museen</span></span></span>

For Switzerland’s wider landscape of unusual collections, the Zurich moulages occupy a distinctive position. They are not relics of superstition, fictional monsters or theatrical curiosities. They are carefully preserved examples of how science once documented human bodies, turning illness into an archive of wax that continues to teach long after its original purpose has changed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moulagen.uzh.ch/en/IWWM.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moulagen.uzh.ch">[Moulagen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moulagen.uzh.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Moulagen Home &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Moulagen Home &#124; Moulagenmuseum &#124; UZH</span></span></span>

        exactMarket = localeMarketMap[normalized];
        break;
      }
      var languageOnly = normalized.split('-')[0];
      if (!languageMarket && localeMarketMap[languageOnly]) languageMarket = localeMarketMap[languageOnly];
    }

  }
  function inferFromLocale() {
    var matches = localeMatches();
    return matches.exact || matches.language || '';
  }
  function inferFromTimezone() {
    var tz = '';
    try { tz = String(Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone || ''); } catch (err) {}
    if (!tz) return '';
    for (var i = 0; i< timezoneRules.length; i += 1) {
      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }
  function inferMarket(available, options) {
    available = Array.isArray(available) ? available : [];
    options = options || {};
    var storageKey = options.storageKey || 'phoenix-affiliate-market';
    try {
      var saved = window.localStorage && localStorage.getItem(storageKey);
      if (saved && available.indexOf(saved) >= 0) return saved;
    } catch (err) {}
    var timezoneMarket = inferFromTimezone();
    if (timezoneMarket && available.indexOf(timezoneMarket) >= 0) return timezoneMarket;
    var localeMarkets = localeMatches();
    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
    if (options.defaultMarket && available.indexOf(options.defaultMarket) >= 0) return options.defaultMarket;
    return available[0] || '';
  }

    normalize: normalize,
    inferMarket: inferMarket,
    inferFromLocale: inferFromLocale,
    inferFromTimezone: inferFromTimezone
  };
