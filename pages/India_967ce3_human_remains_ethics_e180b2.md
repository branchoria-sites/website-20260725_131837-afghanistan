## Introduction

Human remains occupy a distinctive place in [India]({{ 'what-makes-indias-museums-so-unexpected/' | relative_url }})'s museum landscape because they are rarely displayed simply to shock or entertain. Instead, preserved brains, Egyptian mummies and anatomical collections are typically presented within medical education, archaeology or scientific research. Even so, these displays raise difficult questions that extend far beyond conservation: who gave consent, how were the remains acquired, what responsibilities do museums owe to the dead, and how should visitors behave in front of real human bodies?

In India, these debates are particularly relevant because many collections were assembled during the colonial era or through teaching institutions established under colonial administration. Modern museums increasingly balance educational value with concerns about provenance, dignity, cultural sensitivity and the changing expectations of visitors. International museum ethics have also shifted towards greater transparency, careful interpretation and, where appropriate, consultation with descendant communities or consideration of repatriation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icom.museum">[International Council of Museums]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icom.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Council of Museums Code of Ethics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Council of MuseumsCode of Ethics - International Council of Museums - International Council of Museums...</span></span></span>

## When Do Human Remains Become Museum Objects?

Human remains become museum objects only after their original identity as a person's body has been transformed into something intended for education, research or heritage preservation. That transformation is never ethically neutral.

### Preserved brains at NIMHANS

The Brain Museum at the National Institute of Mental Health and Neurosciences (NIMHANS) in Bengaluru contains hundreds of preserved human brains showing the effects of neurological disease, trauma, infections, tumours and degenerative disorders. Rather than presenting specimens as curiosities, guided visits explain how different illnesses physically alter the brain and why early diagnosis matters.

The museum forms part of a research brain bank rather than an independent tourist attraction. Visitors encounter labelled specimens alongside explanations of medical conditions, making the experience closer to a university laboratory than a cabinet of horrors. This educational framing is central to the institution's public mission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icom.museum">[International Council of Museums]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icom.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Council of Museums Code of Ethics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Council of MuseumsCode of Ethics - International Council of Museums - International Council of Museums...</span></span></span>

Yet the collection also illustrates modern ethical practice. The brains originate from individuals donated through recognised medical and research processes rather than anonymous historical collecting. Their continued preservation is justified by scientific and educational value rather than spectacle.

### Egyptian mummies in Indian museums

Several Indian museums preserve Egyptian mummies acquired during the nineteenth century, the best known being the mummy housed in the Indian Museum in Kolkata. These bodies entered Indian collections during an era when European archaeological expeditions, imperial networks and wealthy collectors circulated Egyptian antiquities across the world.

For visitors, a mummy is often the most memorable exhibit in an archaeology gallery. For curators, however, it is also a human body requiring conservation, environmental control and careful interpretation.

Unlike fictional portrayals of cursed mummies, museum displays increasingly emphasise the individual's life, burial customs and archaeological context instead of treating the remains as exotic entertainment. Conservation work also attempts to minimise handling because ancient tissue is extremely fragile and susceptible to fluctuations in humidity, temperature and light.

## Consent, Provenance and Dignity

The hardest ethical questions are rarely about preservation technology. They concern how museums acquired human remains in the first place.

Many nineteenth-century collections around the world were assembled before modern ideas of informed consent existed. Medical schools often retained anatomical material under legal frameworks that would no longer be considered acceptable. Colonial archaeology also removed human remains across imperial networks with little consultation of source communities.

Consequently, museums increasingly ask several linked questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Was the individual or their family able to consent?
* Can the origin of the remains be documented?
* Were they acquired legally under the standards of their time?
* Would display cause unnecessary distress to descendant communities?
* Does public exhibition still provide genuine educational value?

</div>

For ancient Egyptian mummies, living descendants usually cannot be identified in any practical sense, but debates continue over whether displaying an individual's body remains appropriate centuries after death. For more recent remains, especially those linked to Indigenous peoples or identifiable communities, provenance and consultation have become much more significant parts of museum practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icom.museum">[International Council of Museums]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icom.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Council of Museums Code of Ethics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Council of MuseumsCode of Ethics - International Council of Museums - International Council of Museums...</span></span></span>

These debates are not theoretical. Museums internationally have reassessed displays, reconsidered labels, increased consultation and, in some cases, removed human remains from permanent exhibition altogether while discussing future display policies with visitors and stakeholders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2025/jun/29/manchester-museum-asks-visitors-if-egyptian-womans-body-should-be-taken-off-display" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Asru&#x27;s remains were brought to the UK by the Garnett family—cotton merchants linked to the transatlantic slave trade—after being acquired...</span></span></span>

## Conservation Failures and Visitor Responsibility

Human remains cannot simply be treated like ordinary artefacts.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/human-remains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[britishmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

Organic material continues to deteriorate unless carefully protected. Ancient skin, bone, hair and soft tissue respond to changes in humidity, vibration, biological contamination and excessive lighting. Even repeated photography with flash was once considered a potential conservation concern, although modern LED lighting has reduced some earlier risks.

Visitors also influence how respectful a display feels. Museums increasingly discourage behaviour that turns human remains into novelty photographs or social media props. The expectation is not solemn silence, but recognition that these exhibits represent real people rather than theatrical objects.

For museums, this means balancing three responsibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* protecting fragile material from physical damage;
* explaining why the remains are displayed;
* encouraging respectful behaviour without making galleries inaccessible.

</div>

Where these elements are missing, displays can quickly appear exploitative even when the scientific intentions are genuine.

## Why This Debate Matters in India

India's unusual museums often attract visitors precisely because they contain things rarely encountered elsewhere. Human remains undoubtedly fall into that category, but they differ fundamentally from mechanical curiosities, odd inventions or eccentric private collections.

A preserved brain can help explain Alzheimer's disease. An Egyptian mummy can illuminate ancient funerary beliefs and nineteenth-century collecting practices. An anatomical specimen can train future doctors. Their educational value is genuine, yet it exists alongside obligations that ordinary museum objects do not carry.

As international ethical standards evolve, museums increasingly present human remains less as astonishing objects and more as evidence of individual lives, scientific knowledge and historical responsibility. That shift reflects a broader change in museum philosophy: preserving the dead is no longer considered sufficient on its own.     if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
