## Introduction

Akodessewa Market in Lomé, Togo, is one of the world’s most unusual examples of a “living collection”: a place where strange-looking objects are not preserved behind museum glass but remain part of an active system of belief, healing and ritual practice. Often described as a fetish market, it is a working marketplace where traditional practitioners and their clients seek materials associated with Vodun spiritual traditions and traditional medicine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/akodessewa-fetish-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Akodessewa Fetish Market in Lome</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraAkodessewa Fetish Market in LomeOctober 22, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 22, 2013</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in unusual collections, Akodessewa is important because it challenges the usual idea of a museum. Its displays of animal remains, carved objects, herbs, charms and ritual materials can resemble a cabinet of curiosities, yet the objects are not collected simply because they are rare or strange. Their meaning comes from the relationships between practitioners, communities, spiritual beliefs and healing practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/akodessewa-fetish-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Akodessewa Fetish Market in Lome</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraAkodessewa Fetish Market in LomeOctober 22, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 22, 2013</span></span></span>

The market is therefore best understood neither as a “museum of magic” nor merely as a tourist attraction. ## What visitors see at Akodessewa

Akodessewa is located in the Akodessewa district of Lomé and functions as a specialist market connected to traditional spiritual and healing practices. Research on the site describes it as one of West Africa’s largest traditional medicine markets, serving urban customers, healers and people travelling from surrounding areas to obtain materials that may not be available elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2021.612355/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on frontiersin.org.</span></span></span>

A first-time visitor often notices the visual contrast with an ordinary marketplace. Instead of food stalls or household goods, many displays contain unusual biological materials and ritual objects arranged on tables or inside small stalls. Commonly reported items include dried animal parts, skins, bones, shells, herbs, powders, carved figures and protective objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.petitfute.co.uk/v52221-lome/c1173-visites-points-d-interet/c937-monuments/c953-halles-marche/261987-marche-d-akodessewa-marche-aux-fetiches.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: petitfute.co.uk">[Petit Futé]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">petitfute.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Petit FutéAKODESSEWA MARKET (FETISH MARKET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Halls / Market - Lomé - TogoMay 31, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 31, 2015</span></span></span>

The appearance of the market has made it famous among travellers interested in unusual places. Photographs often focus on skulls, preserved animals and unfamiliar objects, creating an image of a mysterious or frightening location. However, the market’s everyday function is more practical within its own cultural setting: customers come seeking advice, ritual services and prescribed materials rather than simply buying curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guardian.ng/life/exploring-the-worlds-largest-fetish-market-in-lome/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guardian.ng">[The Guardian Nigeria]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guardian.ng</span><span class="citation-popover-title">exploring the worlds largest fetish market in lome</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Guardian NigeriaExploring The World’s Largest Fetish Market In LoméJanuary 26, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 26, 2020</span></span></span>

### A collection without museum labels

Unlike an ethnographic museum, Akodessewa does not separate objects from their original use. There are no glass cases explaining a forgotten tradition or displaying items as historical remains. The “collection” changes according to demand, available materials and the needs of practitioners.

This makes Akodessewa unusual within the world of strange collections. A traditional curiosity cabinet usually transforms objects into specimens to be observed. Akodessewa does almost the opposite: objects remain embedded in living practices. A visitor may see an item that appears unusual, but its significance may depend on a practitioner’s knowledge, ritual context and the person seeking help.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.voyage-togo.com/guide/attraction/marche-de-fetiches" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: voyage-togo.com">[Voyage Togo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">voyage-togo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Voyage Togo Marché des fétiches d&#x27;Akodessewa: guide &#124; Nomadays</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Voyage Togo Marché des fétiches d&#x27;Akodessewa: guide &#124; Nomadays</span></span></span>

The distinction matters because outside descriptions have sometimes reduced the market to sensational images of “voodoo” or “black magic”. Such descriptions overlook the wider role of Vodun-related traditions in parts of West Africa, where spiritual practices can include ideas about protection, health, ancestors, relationships and community life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/akodessewa-fetish-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Akodessewa Fetish Market in Lome</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraAkodessewa Fetish Market in LomeOctober 22, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 22, 2013</span></span></span>

## Belief, healing and ritual objects

The objects at Akodessewa are connected to a wider West African tradition in which physical materials can carry symbolic, spiritual or ritual importance. Practitioners may use particular items in consultations, protective practices, healing traditions or ceremonies. The market itself supplies materials, while ritual specialists determine how those materials are used.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guardian.ng/life/exploring-the-worlds-largest-fetish-market-in-lome/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guardian.ng">[The Guardian Nigeria]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guardian.ng</span><span class="citation-popover-title">exploring the worlds largest fetish market in lome</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Guardian NigeriaExploring The World’s Largest Fetish Market In LoméJanuary 26, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 26, 2020</span></span></span>

This relationship between object and meaning is what makes Akodessewa a “living ritual collection”. The value of an object is not simply its age, rarity or appearance. Instead, its importance comes from knowledge systems surrounding it: who uses it, why it is selected, and what role it plays in a ritual process.

Researchers studying the market have found that wildlife-derived materials are often linked more strongly to spiritual beliefs than to straightforward medicinal use. A 2020 study of the market identified a wide range of animal-derived products and noted that many were associated with belief-based practices, while also highlighting concerns about threatened species and unclear legal status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A85428a34-1ab2-463d-8f0d-3920357a8278" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ora.ox.ac.uk">[Oxford University Research Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ora.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ford University Research ArchiveSnake oil and pangolin scales: insights into wild animal use at “Marché des Fétiches” traditional medic...</span></span></span>

The market also includes plant-based materials and other non-animal objects. These less visually dramatic elements are important because they show that Akodessewa is not simply a place of preserved animal remains; it is part of a broader system of traditional knowledge involving plants, craft, ritual expertise and commerce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.voyage-togo.com/guide/attraction/marche-de-fetiches" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: voyage-togo.com">[Voyage Togo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">voyage-togo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Voyage Togo Marché des fétiches d&#x27;Akodessewa: guide &#124; Nomadays</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Voyage Togo Marché des fétiches d&#x27;Akodessewa: guide &#124; Nomadays</span></span></span>

## Cultural debates around the market

Akodessewa sits at the centre of several competing interpretations. For some visitors, it is one of the most fascinating cultural sites in Togo because it offers direct contact with a tradition that is still practised. For others, the market raises concerns about wildlife exploitation, commercialisation and the way sacred traditions are presented to outsiders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A85428a34-1ab2-463d-8f0d-3920357a8278" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ora.ox.ac.uk">[Oxford University Research Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ora.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ford University Research ArchiveSnake oil and pangolin scales: insights into wild animal use at “Marché des Fétiches” traditional medic...</span></span></span>

### Conservation and animal welfare concerns

One of the strongest debates concerns the use of animal-derived materials. Scientific studies of the market have documented a large variety of wildlife products and identified conservation risks. One investigation estimated that vendors’ descriptions represented hundreds of possible species, including some classified as threatened, while noting that more research was needed to understand the scale and impact of the trade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A85428a34-1ab2-463d-8f0d-3920357a8278" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ora.ox.ac.uk">[Oxford University Research Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ora.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ford University Research ArchiveSnake oil and pangolin scales: insights into wild animal use at “Marché des Fétiches” traditional medic...</span></span></span>

Another study focusing on African grey parrots at the market examined belief-based use of wildlife products and described Akodessewa as a major centre for such trade in West Africa. These studies do not simply dismiss the market as a cultural curiosity; instead, they highlight the need to balance respect for cultural practices with wildlife protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2021.612355/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on frontiersin.org.</span></span></span>

The issue is complicated because the market serves real social and religious functions. Conservation discussions therefore involve questions about alternatives, regulation, community involvement and how traditions can adapt without losing their meaning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A85428a34-1ab2-463d-8f0d-3920357a8278" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ora.ox.ac.uk">[Oxford University Research Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ora.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ford University Research ArchiveSnake oil and pangolin scales: insights into wild animal use at “Marché des Fétiches” traditional medic...</span></span></span>

### Between heritage and spectacle

Akodessewa’s fame has also created a tension between local practice and international curiosity. The market attracts visitors precisely because it appears unusual, but treating it only as an exotic attraction can distort its purpose.

The most useful way to approach the market is as a place where several worlds meet: a marketplace, a centre of traditional knowledge, a religiously significant space and a destination for travellers interested in unusual cultural heritage. Its strangeness comes not from being a collection of forgotten objects, but from seeing objects that still matter to living communities.

## Why Akodessewa matters in Togo’s strange-history landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Within Togo’s unusual collections and curiosity culture, Akodessewa occupies a rare position. It is not a museum, archive or historical display, yet it preserves something that conventional museums often struggle to capture: a living relationship between objects and belief.

</div>

Its importance lies in the fact that the “collection” is active. Objects arrive, leave, change hands and enter new ritual contexts. The market records a continuing tradition rather than presenting a finished historical chapter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.voyage-togo.com/guide/attraction/marche-de-fetiches" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: voyage-togo.com">[Voyage Togo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">voyage-togo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Voyage Togo Marché des fétiches d&#x27;Akodessewa: guide &#124; Nomadays</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Voyage Togo Marché des fétiches d&#x27;Akodessewa: guide &#124; Nomadays</span></span></span>

For visitors exploring unusual heritage in Togo, Akodessewa offers a different kind of encounter from a conventional museum visit. It asks viewers to consider why objects become meaningful, how cultures decide what should be preserved, and what happens when sacred materials become global symbols of curiosity. Its mystery is not that the objects are unexplained, but that their meanings depend on a cultural world that cannot be separated from the people who continue to use them.

      if (!languageMarket && localeMarketMap[languageOnly]) languageMarket = localeMarketMap[languageOnly];
    }

    var matches = localeMatches();

  }

    var tz = '';

    if (!tz) return '';
    for (var i = 0; i< timezoneRules.length; i += 1) {
      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }

    available = Array.isArray(available) ? available : [];
    options = options || {};

      if (saved && available.indexOf(saved) >= 0) return saved;

    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
    if (options.defaultMarket && available.indexOf(options.defaultMarket) >= 0) return options.defaultMarket;
    return available[0] || '';
  }
