## Introduction

[Trinidad and Tobago]({{ 'trinidad-and-tobago/' | relative_url }})’s supernatural heritage is not housed in a single “monster museum”. Its unusual collection is a living archive of stories: spirits, warnings, forest guardians and mysterious figures preserved through oral tradition, libraries, cultural programmes and artistic interpretation. The country’s best-known folklore figures — including the Soucouyant, La Diablesse, Papa Bois, Douens and Jumbies — are not presented as physical evidence of creatures, but as cultural records showing how communities explained danger, nature, morality and the unseen world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archivespace.sta.uwi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/6660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivespace.sta.uwi.edu">[ArchivesSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivespace.sta.uwi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchivesSpaceTrinidad and Tobago Folklore., 2008 &#124; ArchivesSpace Public Interface...</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in strange history and curiosity culture, Trinidad and Tobago’s folklore archives offer a different kind of collection. Instead of objects in display cases, the “artefacts” are recorded tales, printed calendars, storytelling traditions, performances, paintings and archival descriptions. They reveal how African, European, Indigenous and wider Caribbean influences combined to create one of the region’s richest supernatural traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archivespace.sta.uwi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/6660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivespace.sta.uwi.edu">[ArchivesSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivespace.sta.uwi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchivesSpaceTrinidad and Tobago Folklore., 2008 &#124; ArchivesSpace Public Interface...</span></span></span>

## The Spirits and Legends Behind Trinidad Folklore

### Soucouyant, La Diablesse and other figures

The Soucouyant is among the most recognisable figures in Trinidad and Tobago folklore. Traditionally described as an old woman who can remove her skin and take the form of a fiery presence at night, the figure belongs to a wider Caribbean tradition of transformation stories and fears surrounding hidden identities. In Trinidad folklore collections, the Soucouyant appears alongside other supernatural beings as part of a wider storytelling system rather than as an isolated “monster”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archivespace.sta.uwi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/6660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivespace.sta.uwi.edu">[ArchivesSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivespace.sta.uwi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchivesSpaceTrinidad and Tobago Folklore., 2008 &#124; ArchivesSpace Public Interface...</span></span></span>

The importance of the Soucouyant lies less in whether the story is believed literally and more in what the story does culturally. Like many folklore figures, it has functioned as a warning tale: a way of teaching children about danger, secrecy and social boundaries while giving communities a memorable character through which fears could be discussed.

La Diablesse is another major figure in Trinidad and Tobago’s folklore landscape. Usually portrayed as a mysterious woman whose beauty hides a supernatural danger, she is associated with temptation, isolation and the risks of travelling alone in unfamiliar places. Accounts vary, but many versions describe her as having a hidden animal-like foot beneath her clothing. The legend continues to circulate through family storytelling and modern folklore documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folklore.usc.edu/la-diablesse/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folklore.usc.edu">[USC Digital Folklore Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folklore.usc.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Digital Folklore Archives La Diablesse &#124; USC Digital Folklore Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USC Digital Folklore ArchivesLa Diablesse &#124; USC Digital Folklore ArchivesApril 30, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2025</span></span></span>

## Archives preserving oral traditions

Because Trinidad and Tobago does not have a dedicated supernatural museum, researchers and curious visitors often encounter its folklore through archives and libraries. The preservation process itself is part of the country’s unusual heritage: stories once passed between generations are now held in collections alongside books, recordings, photographs and cultural documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library2.nalis.gov.tt/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library2.nalis.gov.tt">[Library 2]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library2.nalis.gov.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Library 2Digital Library</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library 2Digital Library</span></span></span>

The University of the West Indies’ archival collections include material documenting Trinidad and Tobago folklore, including references to Soucouyants, La Diablesse, Douens, Jumbies, Papa Bois and other traditional figures. One recorded collection, the *Trinidad and Tobago Folklore* calendar material from NALIS, demonstrates how folklore has been deliberately gathered and preserved as part of national cultural heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archivespace.sta.uwi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/6660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivespace.sta.uwi.edu">[ArchivesSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivespace.sta.uwi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchivesSpaceTrinidad and Tobago Folklore., 2008 &#124; ArchivesSpace Public Interface...</span></span></span>

The National Library and Information System Authority (NALIS) also maintains digital folklore resources. Its collections explain that folk tales were historically transmitted through oral tradition before being recorded in print and later preserved digitally. This transition from spoken story to searchable archive is important: it changes folklore from something temporary and local into a resource that can be studied, taught and revisited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library2.nalis.gov.tt/gsdl/cgi-bin/library.cgi?a=p&amp;c=folkstor&amp;l=en&amp;p=about&amp;site=localhost&amp;w=utf-8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library2.nalis.gov.tt">[Library 2]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library2.nalis.gov.tt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Library 2Folk Tales, Fables and Legends</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library 2Folk Tales, Fables and Legends</span></span></span>

Another important archive route comes through sound and performance records. The Library of Congress’ American Folklife Center holds ethnographic material relating to Trinidad and Tobago expressive culture, including recordings connected with music, storytelling and performance traditions. Such collections show that folklore is not only about characters and legends; it also survives through voice, rhythm, ceremony and communal participation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guides.loc.gov/trinidad-tobago-folklife" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guides.loc.gov">[Research Guides]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guides.loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Introduction - American Folklife Center Collections: Trinidad and Tobago - Research Guides at Library of Congress...</span></span></span>

## Belief, identity and cultural memory

The strange figures of Trinidad and Tobago folklore occupy an unusual position between belief and heritage. They are not normally displayed as proven supernatural entities, nor are they simply fictional inventions. Instead, they exist in the space where memory, storytelling and social values meet.

A figure such as Papa Bois, for example, can be read as a supernatural character while also representing older ideas about environmental responsibility and respect for forests. The Soucouyant can be approached as a frightening night story, but also as part of a broader Caribbean tradition of explaining hidden dangers and social anxieties through dramatic characters. These multiple meanings are why folklore archives remain valuable even when communities no longer interpret every story literally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archivespace.sta.uwi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/6660" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archivespace.sta.uwi.edu">[ArchivesSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archivespace.sta.uwi.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ArchivesSpaceTrinidad and Tobago Folklore., 2008 &#124; ArchivesSpace Public Interface...</span></span></span>

      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
