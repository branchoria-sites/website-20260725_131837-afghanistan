## Introduction

The Museum of Dreaming Marine Stones, or Las Piedras Marinas Soñadoras, is one of [Venezuela]({{ 'venezuelas-strangest-collections-beyond/' | relative_url }})’s most unusual art environments: a hillside world of balanced stones, symbolic sculptures and natural spaces created by the self-taught artist Gonzalo Barrios Pérez, known as Zóez. Located in San José de Galipán on the slopes of the Ávila mountain range above the Caribbean coast, it is not an archaeology museum or a conventional gallery. Instead, it sits between sculpture garden, personal sanctuary, ecological artwork and outsider art environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

Its importance within Venezuela’s strange-collection landscape comes from the way it turns an individual vision into a place visitors can physically enter. The “collection” is not a group of rare objects displayed behind glass; it is a living arrangement of stones, paths and symbolic installations shaped by one person’s philosophy of nature, balance and spirituality. The site has been documented internationally as an example of a self-taught artist-built environment, a category that includes personal creative worlds built outside normal museum systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacesarchives.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About Us &#124; SPACES Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2019</span></span></span>

## The creator and personal stone landscape

### A mountain artist’s lifelong project

The creator of the garden, Gonzalo Barrios Pérez (1948–2021), developed the identity of Zóez after a personal spiritual experience in 1967. According to documentation by the SPACES Archives, he moved towards a life centred on meditation and transformed the Galipán site over decades into an ecological museum. The name Zóez became part of his artistic persona and his interpretation of life, nature and inner discovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

The environment grew through accumulation rather than through a formal architectural plan. Zóez used marine stones collected from the Caribbean and arranged them into sculptural compositions, creating a landscape where the natural material remained visually important. Unlike a traditional sculptor who cuts or reshapes stone, his approach emphasised placement, balance and relationship between existing forms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

This method places the site close to the tradition of artist-built environments and land art, where the artwork is inseparable from its setting. SPACES Archives, an organisation dedicated to documenting large-scale self-taught artistic environments, identifies the museum as an example of this wider international phenomenon. Such places often challenge the boundary between artwork, architecture, garden and personal belief system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacesarchives.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About Us &#124; SPACES Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2019</span></span></span>

### A museum without conventional museum objects

Visitors expecting labelled displays, historical artefacts or a chronological exhibition may find the experience unfamiliar. The central attraction is the environment itself: stone formations, symbolic spaces and viewpoints designed to encourage reflection. The work is closer to a pilgrimage through an artist’s imagination than a visit to a standard museum collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

Among the best-known features are stone constructions designed around ideas of fertility, harmony and connection with nature. One installation, described as a “Window to the World”, uses the landscape itself as part of the artwork by framing views of the surrounding sea and sky. Other areas invite visitors to interact with stones and participate in simple acts of contemplation or creation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

The result is an unusual form of heritage: the value lies not in the age or rarity of the stones but in the meaning assigned to them through the artist’s arrangement and the visitor’s experience.

## Art, spirituality and interpretation

### Between outsider art and personal philosophy

The Museum of Dreaming Marine Stones is often best understood through the idea of outsider art: creative work produced outside established artistic institutions and academic systems. Zóez was not a formally trained artist in the conventional sense, and his environment developed from personal beliefs rather than from a gallery or government commission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

Calling the site outsider art does not mean the work lacks cultural importance. Around the world, artist-built environments have become important records of individual creativity, local identity and alternative approaches to art-making. Organisations such as SPACES Archives document these places precisely because they often disappear if they are not recognised and preserved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacesarchives.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About Us &#124; SPACES Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2019</span></span></span>

The Venezuelan example is distinctive because the material language is so closely tied to place. The stones connect the mountain environment of Galipán with the nearby Caribbean coastline, while the arrangement of those stones reflects Zóez’s belief in natural forces, balance and the symbolic importance of life-giving nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

### Belief, symbolism and visitor experience

The spiritual elements of the museum belong to the artist’s own worldview rather than to a recognised religious institution. Visitors encounter ideas about energy, harmony and nature, but these should be understood as artistic interpretation rather than as evidence of an archaeological tradition or established local cult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/zoez-gonzalo-barrios-perez-el-museo-de-arte-ecologico-las-piedras-marinas-soadoras-eco-art-museum-of-the-dreamy-marine-stones/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 3, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2017</span></span></span>

This distinction is what makes the site especially interesting for a curiosity-focused visitor. It is not mysterious because it hides a forgotten civilisation or unexplained object. Its strangeness comes from seeing how one person transformed a landscape into a physical expression of a private philosophy.

## Access, preservation and uncertainty

The Museum of Dreaming Marine Stones remains one of Venezuela’s most distinctive examples of a personal art environment, but it also illustrates the challenges faced by fragile, creator-dependent attractions. Unlike a national museum with permanent staff, conservation departments and regular institutional funding, an artist-built environment depends heavily on local stewardship and continued recognition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacesarchives.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About Us &#124; SPACES Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2019</span></span></span>

The site is located in San Antonio de Galipán, Vargas state, within the mountain landscape of El Ávila, now protected as Waraira Repano National Park. The park authority lists the Museum of the Dreaming Stones among attractions and points of interest within this wider natural area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inparques.gob.ve/parque-nacional-waraira-repano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inparques.gob.ve">[Inparques]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inparques.gob.ve</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inparques Parque Nacional Waraira Repano – Inparques</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inparques Parque Nacional Waraira Repano – Inparques</span></span></span>

For visitors, the practical appeal is the combination of landscape and art: the journey to Galipán provides views over the coast, while the museum offers an encounter with a place shaped by a single artistic vision. However, because access arrangements and local conditions can change, visitors should verify current opening details before travelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inparques.gob.ve/parque-nacional-waraira-repano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inparques.gob.ve">[Inparques]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inparques.gob.ve</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inparques Parque Nacional Waraira Repano – Inparques</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inparques Parque Nacional Waraira Repano – Inparques</span></span></span>

The museum’s long-term significance lies in preserving a rare type of Venezuelan cultural site. It represents a form of creativity that sits outside mainstream museums: not a collection of famous objects, but a complete environment where nature, sculpture and personal belief have become inseparable. In the landscape of Venezuela’s unusual heritage, it is a reminder that a curiosity can be created not only by what people discover, but also by what one person chooses to imagine and build.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacesarchives.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacesarchives.org">[spacesarchives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacesarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About Us &#124; SPACES Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2019</span></span></span>
