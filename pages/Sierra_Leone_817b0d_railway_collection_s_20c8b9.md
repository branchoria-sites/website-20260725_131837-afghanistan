## Introduction

[Sierra Leone]({{ 'where-sierra-leone-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s National Railway Museum is unusual not because it displays the country's railway history, but because the collection itself almost became part of that history. After the national railway closed in the mid-1970s, a small group of locomotives, coaches and railway artefacts was set aside for a planned museum. Instead of being scrapped, these vehicles remained locked inside the former railway workshops at Cline Town, where they endured decades of neglect, vandalism, civil war and occupation by displaced families. For many years, both inside and outside Sierra Leone, it was widely assumed that the collection had been destroyed. Its rediscovery and eventual restoration turned what had been a forgotten industrial relic into one of the country's most remarkable heritage survival stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/museum/railway-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[sierraleoneheritage.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

Unlike many preserved railways that have enjoyed continuous institutional care, Sierra Leone's collection survived largely through circumstance and the determination of later conservation efforts. That improbable journey is what makes the museum one of the country's most distinctive historical collections.

## Closure and disappearance from public memory

The Sierra Leone Government Railway operated a narrow-gauge network linking Freetown with Bo, Makeni and eventually Pendembu. By the 1960s, however, investment shifted towards roads, the railway had become increasingly unprofitable, and the government decided to close the system. Passenger services ended in 1974 and the remaining railway infrastructure was dismantled during 1975.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/museum/railway-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

Before the railway was sold for scrap, officials retained a representative collection of locomotives, rolling stock and historic vehicles. The intention was to preserve part of the system for future generations rather than allow every surviving train to be dismantled. That decision proved crucial. Without it, Sierra Leone would have lost virtually all physical evidence of the country's railway age.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/XgXhOtlpqsUK4Q" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arts &amp; Culture Sierra Leone National Railway Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; CultureSierra Leone National Railway Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

Once the railway disappeared from everyday life, however, the preserved collection also faded from public awareness. Stored inside the former workshops at Cline Town, the vehicles were largely inaccessible and received little maintenance. As the years passed, knowledge of their existence spread only among railway enthusiasts and a handful of former railway employees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/museum/railway-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

## Survival inside the Cline Town workshops

The greatest threat came not from age but from Sierra Leone's turbulent recent history.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Government_Railway" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span></span></span>

During the civil war between 1991 and 2002, the workshops were damaged, looted and neglected. Metal fittings disappeared, components were stripped from vehicles and buildings deteriorated. Yet the core collection remained surprisingly intact. During parts of the conflict the workshops also sheltered large numbers of internally displaced people seeking safety, meaning the locomotives and coaches became part of an improvised refuge rather than an abandoned industrial site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Government_Railway" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span></span></span>

This survival was never guaranteed. Railway historians outside Sierra Leone often assumed the preserved engines had been destroyed during the conflict because virtually no information emerged from the country for many years. Only after peace returned did it become clear that the locomotives, although weathered and vandalised, had escaped complete destruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Government_Railway" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span></span></span>

The survival of the collection is therefore remarkable for two reasons. It outlasted the economic decision that eliminated the railway itself, and it also survived a decade-long conflict that devastated much of the country's infrastructure.

## Rediscovery, restoration and museum opening

A turning point came in 2004 when British Army officer Colonel Steve Davies located the forgotten collection and began working with Sierra Leonean partners to document and preserve it. What had long been regarded as a lost railway heritage site suddenly became a realistic restoration project. Government support followed, including backing from President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah for preserving the country's railway heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Government_Railway" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Government Railway</span></span></span>

The National Railway Museum officially opened in 2005 within the restored Cline Town workshops. Rather than rebuilding the railway itself, the project focused on conserving what remained, stabilising locomotives and coaches, creating interpretation for visitors and preserving archives, photographs and railway artefacts. Local conservation staff have continued restoration work with technical guidance from railway specialists, including collaboration with the UK's National Railway Museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/museum/railway-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

Among the collection's most notable exhibits are:

* Beyer-Garratt steam locomotive No. 73, built in 1955.
* Hunslet steam locomotive No. 81.

* Passenger coaches, freight wagons, railway equipment, photographs and archival material documenting the former national railway.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/museum/railway-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

None of the locomotives currently operates. Missing components, wartime damage, conservation priorities and limited funding mean the emphasis is on preservation rather than returning the engines to steam.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierraleonerailwaymuseum.org/the-collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleonerailwaymuseum.org">[slrailwaymuseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleonerailwaymuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">slrailwaymuseum The Collection &#124; slrailwaymuseum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">slrailwaymuseum The Collection &#124; slrailwaymuseum</span></span></span>

## Why the collection matters

Many railway museums celebrate engineering achievement. Sierra Leone's collection tells a different story: how heritage can survive despite political upheaval, economic decline and armed conflict.

The museum preserves evidence of a transport network that once connected much of the country before roads replaced rail. At the same time, the vehicles themselves have acquired a second history. Their decades hidden inside abandoned workshops have become almost as significant as their years in railway service. Visitors encounter not simply colonial-era locomotives but artefacts marked by closure, abandonment, rediscovery and recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/XgXhOtlpqsUK4Q" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arts &amp; Culture Sierra Leone National Railway Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; CultureSierra Leone National Railway Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

Within Sierra Leone's wider landscape of unusual collections, this makes the National Railway Museum distinctive.
