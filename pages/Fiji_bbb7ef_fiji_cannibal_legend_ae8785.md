## Introduction

Fiji's most famous cannibal stories are attached not to museum cases but to places: a cave in the Sigatoka Valley and a grave in Rakiraki. Both are genuine heritage sites with real historical importance, yet both have also become tourist attractions where archaeology, oral tradition, nineteenth-century missionary accounts and modern marketing overlap. For visitors, the challenge is not deciding whether Fiji once practised cannibalism—it did in some regions before the late nineteenth century—but understanding which details are well supported, which come from oral history, and which have grown through repeated retelling. Looking at Naihehe Cave and the grave of Udre Udre together shows how authentic cultural landscapes can become wrapped in increasingly dramatic stories without losing their historical value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X21004144" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[sciencedirect.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Investigating geoarchaeological deposits from Naihehe cave in the Sigatoka River valley of Viti Levu, Fiji - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

## How much of Naihehe Cave's reputation is historical?

Naihehe Cave, in the Sigatoka Valley on Viti Levu, is widely promoted as Fiji's "Cannibal Cave". Guided tours typically describe it as a fortified refuge used during periods of inter-tribal warfare, with chambers identified as priestly areas, defensive positions, cooking places and ritual spaces. The cave itself is unquestionably real, and its geography makes it easy to understand why it could function as a defensive stronghold. Narrow entrances, multiple chambers and reliable water sources would have made it an effective refuge during conflict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.travelonline.com/fiji/attractions/naihehe-cave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: travelonline.com">[travelonline.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">travelonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel Online Naihehe Cave &#124; Discover Fiji</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Travel Online Naihehe Cave &#124; Discover Fiji</span></span></span>

Recent archaeological research has strengthened the historical significance of the site without confirming every popular tour narrative. Excavations and geoarchaeological analysis have identified clear evidence of prolonged human occupation, anthropogenic deposits and repeated use of the cave over time. The research supports the idea that Naihehe was an important inhabited landscape rather than merely a legendary location. However, archaeology cannot independently verify many of the specific stories told during tours, such as the precise use of individual chambers or dramatic accounts attached to particular rock formations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X21004144" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Investigating geoarchaeological deposits from Naihehe cave in the Sigatoka River valley of Viti Levu, Fiji - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because visitor interpretation often blends several different layers:

* local oral traditions passed through generations;
* nineteenth-century missionary and colonial descriptions;
* modern tourism designed to create a memorable visitor experience.

The result is a tour that is culturally meaningful but not every detail should be treated as independently verified historical fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X21004144" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[sciencedirect.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Investigating geoarchaeological deposits from Naihehe cave in the Sigatoka River valley of Viti Levu, Fiji - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

### Why the cave became a tourism symbol

Naihehe illustrates how dark heritage is marketed in Fiji. Promotional material frequently emphasises "last cannibal tribe", "cannibal ovens" and mysterious rituals because these themes attract international visitors. At the same time, local guides often place equal emphasis on community history, warfare, survival and the arrival of Christianity.

For many visitors, the strongest impression comes less from proven archaeological discoveries than from walking through a real landscape where oral history still forms part of community identity. The cave's atmosphere encourages storytelling, and that storytelling has become part of the attraction itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.travelonline.com/fiji/attractions/naihehe-cave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: travelonline.com">[TravelOnline]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">travelonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel Online Naihehe Cave &#124; Discover Fiji</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Travel Online Naihehe Cave &#124; Discover Fiji</span></span></span>

## Udre Udre's grave and the disputed body count

The second major location associated with Fiji's cannibal reputation is the burial site of Ratu Udre Udre near Rakiraki in northern Viti Levu. He is widely described as Fiji's most notorious cannibal, with claims that he consumed between 872 and 999 people. His grave remains a visitor destination because of the line of stones traditionally said to represent each victim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.suncoastfiji.org/experiences/udre-udre-tomb/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: suncoastfiji.org">[Suncoast]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">suncoastfiji.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Suncoast Udre Udre Tomb</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SuncoastUdre Udre Tomb - Fiji&#x27;s Suncoast - the land of endless summersJuly 15, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2026</span></span></span>

The number itself deserves careful treatment.

The figure of 872 does not emerge from modern archaeological excavation. Instead, it comes primarily from nineteenth-century missionary Reverend Richard Lyth, who recorded that Udre Udre's son showed him a row of memorial stones. Lyth reported counting 872 stones, while also noting apparent gaps suggesting that some stones had already disappeared. Later writers repeated the count, while local oral traditions sometimes expanded the total to around 900 or even 999 victims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fijitimes.com.fj/mystery-surrounds-udre-udres-victim-count/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fijitimes.com.fj">[Fiji Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fijitimes.com.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fiji Times Mystery surrounds Udre Udre&#x27;s victim count</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fiji TimesMystery surrounds Udre Udre&#x27;s victim count - The Fiji Times...</span></span></span>

This creates several overlapping versions of the story:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **872 victims:** based on the missionary's recorded count of surviving stones.
* **Around 900 victims:** inferred because some stones appeared to be missing.
* **999 victims:** preserved in some local oral traditions and frequently repeated in tourism material.

</div>

None of these figures can be independently verified through archaeological evidence alone. The stones themselves are significant heritage objects, but they cannot demonstrate that every represented individual existed or that every stone originally survived intact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fijitimes.com.fj/mystery-surrounds-udre-udres-victim-count/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fijitimes.com.fj">[Fiji Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fijitimes.com.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fiji Times Mystery surrounds Udre Udre&#x27;s victim count</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fiji TimesMystery surrounds Udre Udre&#x27;s victim count - The Fiji Times...</span></span></span>

### Why the story persisted

Udre Udre became internationally famous because his story combined several elements that fascinated nineteenth-century European audiences: warfare, cannibalism, missionary encounters and apparently countable evidence in the form of memorial stones.

Modern tourism often reinforces this reputation by describing him as "the world's most prolific cannibal". Although that label appears in popular reference works and travel literature, historians remain cautious because the underlying evidence depends heavily on one early eyewitness account supported by oral testimony rather than extensive independent documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fijitimes.com.fj/mystery-surrounds-udre-udres-victim-count/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fijitimes.com.fj">[Fiji Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fijitimes.com.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fiji Times Mystery surrounds Udre Udre&#x27;s victim count</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fiji TimesMystery surrounds Udre Udre&#x27;s victim count - The Fiji Times...</span></span></span>

## Why these legends matter in Fiji's curiosity landscape

    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
