## Introduction

The Tareq Rajab Museum is one of [Kuwait]({{ 'where-kuwaits-strangest-collections/' | relative_url }})’s most distinctive private collections: a family-built archive that transformed personal collecting into a public record of Islamic art, craftsmanship and history. Founded in 1980 by Tareq S. Rajab and Jehan S. Rajab, the museum grew from decades of collecting across the Islamic world and now holds around 30,000 objects, with more than 10,000 displayed permanently. Its importance lies not in a single “mystery” object, but in the unusual scale of one family’s attempt to preserve the everyday and elite arts of Islamic civilisation — from Qur’anic manuscripts and calligraphy to jewellery, textiles, ceramics, weapons and metalwork.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/trm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum TRM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumTRM - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

## A collector’s archive of Islamic treasures

### How a personal passion became a museum

Tareq Rajab’s collection began as a personal interest in the artistic heritage of the Islamic world. His early collecting focused on objects such as ceramics, manuscripts, metalwork and glass, gradually expanding as he travelled and developed relationships with dealers and collectors. By the 1970s, the collection had grown beyond the scale of a private hobby, and Rajab began considering how it could serve an educational purpose in Kuwait.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/start-of-the-collection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Start Of The Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumStart Of The Collection - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

The museum was established in a villa in Jabriya in 1980, making it one of the earliest institutions in the Gulf dedicated specifically to Islamic art. Rather than building a collection around a narrow national story, the Rajabs assembled objects from across the wider Islamic world, reflecting connections between regions that stretched from the Middle East and North Africa to South Asia and beyond.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/start-of-the-collection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Start Of The Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumStart Of The Collection - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

This approach gives the museum the character of an old-fashioned cabinet of curiosities, but with a scholarly purpose. The fascination comes from the range of objects gathered together: a visitor can move from a finely written manuscript to jewellery, costume, weapons and decorative arts, seeing not isolated artworks but evidence of how people lived, worshipped, travelled and displayed status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/?sa=X&amp;ved=2ahUKEwi11e-ejrb8AhUPV8UKHVbQCTUQyK4DegQIAxAi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumHome - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

### A private collection with a public role

Private collections often raise questions about access, interpretation and ownership. The Tareq Rajab Museum represents one answer to that challenge: a family collection deliberately opened to the public rather than remaining hidden. Its founders described their aim as preserving objects for future educational purposes and inspiring artists, craftspeople and visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/trm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum TRM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumTRM - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

The museum’s significance is therefore partly about preservation. Many historic objects survive because collectors recognised their value before they disappeared into scattered private holdings or inaccessible archives. At the same time, presenting such a collection publicly requires interpretation: visitors need context about dates, regions, materials and craftsmanship rather than simply being shown beautiful objects. The museum’s legacy comes from turning accumulation into education.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/trm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum TRM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumTRM - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

## Manuscripts, jewellery, and craftsmanship on display

### The written word as an artistic tradition

One of the museum’s strongest collections is its manuscripts and calligraphy. These works show that Islamic manuscript culture was not only about preserving text but also about creating visual art through handwriting, illumination and design. The collection includes Qur’anic manuscripts from different periods and regions, along with works connected to important calligraphers and scholars.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/calligraphy-and-manuscripts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Calligraphy and Manuscripts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumCalligraphy and Manuscripts - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

Among the notable examples highlighted by the museum are an early “Ma’il” script Qur’an fragment from the 7th century, 9th-century Qur’ans written on vellum, and manuscripts associated with figures such as Yaqut al-Musta’simi, one of the most influential calligraphers in Islamic history. The collection also includes scientific and philosophical manuscripts, including a work connected with the scholar Ya‘qub Ibn Ishaq Al-Kindi.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/calligraphy-and-manuscripts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Calligraphy and Manuscripts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumCalligraphy and Manuscripts - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

These objects are especially important because they challenge a common misunderstanding of Islamic art as mainly decorative. The manuscripts reveal a tradition where writing itself became a major artistic medium, combining religious devotion, intellectual culture and visual design.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/calligraphy-and-manuscripts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Calligraphy and Manuscripts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumCalligraphy and Manuscripts - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

### Jewellery, textiles and objects of everyday life

The museum’s appeal also comes from its attention to objects beyond famous manuscripts. Its collections include silver and gold jewellery, costumes, textiles, musical instruments, ceramics, glass and metalwork. These categories show the social side of Islamic art: how people dressed, decorated themselves, furnished spaces and marked important occasions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/?sa=X&amp;ved=2ahUKEwi11e-ejrb8AhUPV8UKHVbQCTUQyK4DegQIAxAi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumHome - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

The jewellery collection is particularly central to the museum’s identity. Rather than treating jewellery only as luxury objects, the displays reveal craftsmanship, regional identity and changing ideas of beauty and status. Pieces from different parts of the Islamic world demonstrate how techniques, materials and designs travelled across borders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tareq_Rajab_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab Museum</span></span></span>

A striking example of the collection’s range is the large bronze door of Sultan Barquq, a Mamluk ruler of Egypt. The object connects the museum not only to decorative art but also to architecture and political history, preserving the craftsmanship associated with a major medieval Islamic dynasty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/books/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Books</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumBooks - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

### The calligraphy museum extension

The museum’s focus on writing expanded with the opening of the Tareq Rajab Museum of Islamic Calligraphy in 2007. This separate branch was created to explore Arabic script across manuscripts, artworks and decorative objects. ## How private collecting became public heritage

### A collection shaped by Kuwait’s history

The museum’s story is also tied to Kuwait’s modern history. During the Iraqi invasion of Kuwait in 1990, the Rajab family took extensive measures to protect parts of the collection, hiding manuscripts, jewellery and other objects to prevent them from being looted. The museum’s own account describes how objects were packed away and concealed behind false walls and altered displays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/invasion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Invasion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumInvasion - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

### Why the museum matters in Kuwait’s unusual heritage landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The Tareq Rajab Museum is unusual because its “curiosity” comes from collecting itself. Unlike museums built around a single archaeological discovery, strange phenomenon or historical event, this institution grew from a lifetime of searching, studying and preserving. Its fascination lies in the human story behind the objects: a family deciding that manuscripts, jewellery, crafts and artworks deserved to be gathered, catalogued and shared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/trm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum TRM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumTRM - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in unusual museums, the collection offers a different kind of wonder. The mystery is not whether the objects are unexplained, but how many different histories can exist inside a single private collection. A manuscript may reveal the movement of ideas, a piece of jewellery may reveal trade and identity, and a decorated object may reveal the skills of craftsmen whose names are often forgotten.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trmkt.org/calligraphy-and-manuscripts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trmkt.org">[Tareq Rajab Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trmkt.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tareq Rajab Museum Calligraphy and Manuscripts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tareq Rajab MuseumCalligraphy and Manuscripts - Tareq Rajab Museum...</span></span></span>
