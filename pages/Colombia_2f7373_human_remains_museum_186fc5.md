## Introduction

[Colombia]({{ 'inside-colombias-strangest-museums-and/' | relative_url }})’s collections of human remains sit at the intersection of science, education and public curiosity. Unlike archaeological mummy museums that focus on distant history, the country's best-known anatomical collections contain real human organs, skeletons and preserved bodies assembled for medical teaching. They raise difficult questions that many visitors now ask before they even enter the gallery: Who gave permission for these remains to be displayed? How well is each specimen documented? When does education become spectacle?

These questions are particularly relevant to Bogotá's Museum of the Human Being, where scientific collections are presented to the public in ways that reflect both older traditions of anatomical display and modern expectations of transparency and dignity. Rather than asking whether such museums should exist at all, the more useful debate is how they should explain provenance, respect the dead and balance educational value against the possibility of turning human remains into curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icom.museum">[International Council of Museums]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icom.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Council of Museums Code of Ethics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Council of MuseumsCode of Ethics - International Council of Museums - International Council of Museums...</span></span></span>

## Inside the Museum of the Human Being

Bogotá's Museum of the Human Being occupies an unusual place within Colombia's landscape of strange museums. It is neither a conventional medical school collection closed to the public nor a large national science museum with extensive interpretive galleries. Instead, visitors encounter hundreds of genuine anatomical specimens, including preserved organs, foetuses, skeletons and examples of congenital abnormalities, presented with the stated aim of teaching anatomy and illustrating human disease.

The educational purpose is genuine. Anatomical collections have long been central to training doctors, surgeons and nurses, particularly before digital imaging and three-dimensional modelling became commonplace. Real specimens preserve details that are difficult to reproduce artificially and continue to hold scientific value for teaching pathology and anatomy.

Yet the museum's intimate scale and cabinet-like atmosphere also evoke nineteenth- and early twentieth-century medical collections, when unusual bodies were frequently displayed because they were visually striking as much as scientifically informative. That historical legacy shapes modern reactions. Many visitors leave with both admiration for the educational mission and discomfort about the presentation itself, illustrating how scientific collections can also function as powerful emotional experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927479/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

## From Medical Teaching to Public Spectacle

The central ethical tension is not whether human remains may ever be displayed. Museums, universities and medical schools around the world continue to exhibit genuine human material. The real question is what changes when a teaching collection becomes a public attraction.

Medical students normally study specimens within a professional environment governed by ethical rules, supervision and educational objectives. Public museums serve a broader audience whose motivations vary widely. Some visitors arrive seeking medical knowledge, while others are drawn by horror, curiosity or dark tourism.

That shift changes the responsibilities of the institution. Modern museum ethics increasingly encourage curators to provide visitors with enough context to understand why the remains are present rather than simply presenting unusual bodies as extraordinary objects. Good interpretation explains:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* how the specimen contributed to medical knowledge;
* why it remains scientifically important;
* whether identities are known or intentionally protected;
* what ethical standards govern display today.

</div>

Without that context, authentic scientific collections can unintentionally resemble historical "cabinets of curiosities", where rarity rather than understanding became the organising principle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icom.museum">[icom.museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icom.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Council of Museums Code of Ethics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Council of MuseumsCode of Ethics - International Council of Museums - International Council of Museums...</span></span></span>

## Consent, Provenance and Respect

The most significant questions surrounding Colombia's public anatomical collections concern provenance—the documented history of how specimens entered the collection—and consent.

Many anatomical collections worldwide contain material assembled decades before today's ethical standards existed. Historical specimens may have originated from hospitals, medical schools or unclaimed bodies handled under laws that differed substantially from current expectations. This historical reality does not automatically make every specimen unethical, but it does mean museums should explain what is known and acknowledge where documentation is incomplete rather than implying certainty that does not exist.

Visitors increasingly expect museums to answer questions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Was the body donated voluntarily?
* If not, under what legal framework was it obtained?
* Can the individual still be identified?
* Are relatives or descendant communities involved in decisions about display?
* Why is public exhibition still justified today?

</div>

Public descriptions of Bogotá's Museum of the Human Being emphasise scientific education but provide relatively limited information about the provenance of every individual specimen. As a result, the debate is less about authenticity—there is no serious dispute that the anatomical material is genuine—than about the level of documentation and interpretation accompanying it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icom.museum">[International Council of Museums]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icom.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Council of Museums Code of Ethics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Council of MuseumsCode of Ethics - International Council of Museums - International Council of Museums...</span></span></span>

## How International Museum Standards Change the Conversation

International museum practice has evolved considerably over recent decades. The International Council of Museums (ICOM) treats human remains as especially sensitive collections rather than ordinary museum objects.

Its ethical guidance stresses several principles that are directly relevant to Colombian collections:

## Why Colombia's Debate Is Different

Colombia's discussion differs from countries whose museums primarily display ancient archaeological mummies or prehistoric skeletons. Some Colombian collections include comparatively recent human remains, meaning the individuals may have descendants, identifiable biographies or continuing cultural connections.

This makes ethical decisions more immediate. Questions about consent and dignity are harder to separate from living memory than they are with remains thousands of years old. The nearby example of [San Bernardo]({{ 'san-bernardo/' | relative_url }})'s naturally preserved mummies illustrates a related but distinct challenge: bodies that became tourist attractions while still belonging to recognisable local families and communities. Although the Museum of the Human Being and San Bernardo represent different kinds of collections—medical versus funerary—they both remind visitors that displayed human bodies were once identifiable people rather than anonymous exhibits.

That shared reality has encouraged museums internationally to move away from displays that rely on shock value and towards interpretation that foregrounds biography, scientific purpose and ethical reflection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icom.museum">[International Council of Museums]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icom.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Council of Museums Code of Ethics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Council of MuseumsCode of Ethics - International Council of Museums - International Council of Museums...</span></span></span>

## What Visitors Should Expect

Visitors interested in Colombia's unusual museums should approach anatomical collections differently from conventional attractions.

Rather than asking only whether the displays are authentic, it is worth considering:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Does the exhibition explain why these remains are displayed?
* Is scientific learning clearly prioritised over entertainment?
* Is the provenance described where known?
* Are sensitive subjects handled respectfully?
* Does the museum encourage reflection rather than simple fascination?

</div>

Viewed through those questions, the Museum of the Human Being becomes more than an unsettling curiosity. It represents an important point where Colombia's history of medical education meets changing international expectations about transparency, consent and the respectful treatment of the dead.

    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
