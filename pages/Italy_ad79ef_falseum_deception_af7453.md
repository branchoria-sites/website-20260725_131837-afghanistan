## Introduction

Falseum is an unusual museum in [Italy]({{ 'where-italys-weirdest-histories-come/' | relative_url }}) because its subject is not a collection of rare objects, but the human ability to create, spread and believe false stories. Located inside the medieval Castle of Verrone in the province of Biella, Piedmont, Falseum presents deception as a historical and social phenomenon rather than simply a collection of lies or hoaxes. Opened in 2015 by the municipality of Verrone, it describes itself as Italy’s first museum entirely dedicated to falsehood as a cultural subject.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.falseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: falseum.com">[Museo Falseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">falseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on falseum.com.</span></span></span>

The museum’s central idea is that false beliefs are not only the result of deliberate fraud. Some emerge from misunderstandings, rumours, attractive explanations, social pressures or the way people interpret evidence. By exploring famous deceptions, scientific errors, misleading images and historical myths, Falseum turns a strange subject into a lesson in critical thinking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.falseum.com/chi-siamo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: falseum.com">[Museo Falseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">falseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Falseum Chi Siamo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo FalseumChi Siamo - Museo Falseum...</span></span></span>

Unlike traditional museums that preserve physical treasures, Falseum preserves examples of how ideas travel. Its curiosity lies in examining the mechanisms behind persuasion: why a convincing story can sometimes become more powerful than the truth.

## Inside the Museum of Falsehood and Deception

Falseum is designed as an interactive experience rather than a conventional display of artefacts. The museum occupies part of Castle of Verrone and uses installations, multimedia stations and themed rooms to show how false information has influenced science, politics, history and everyday communication. It does not mainly exhibit objects; instead, it recreates situations in which visitors must question what they see and hear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.falseum.com/visita/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: falseum.com">[Museo Falseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">falseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Falseum Visita</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo FalseumVisita - Museo Falseum...</span></span></span>

The visitor journey moves through different forms of deception. One section explores the broad idea of the “kingdom of falsehood”, while another focuses on scientific falsehoods and the importance of testing claims through evidence. The museum also examines historical cases where misleading stories shaped events, showing that false information can have consequences beyond simple mistakes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.falseum.com/visita/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: falseum.com">[Museo Falseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">falseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Falseum Visita</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo FalseumVisita - Museo Falseum...</span></span></span>

A particularly distinctive feature is the museum’s use of participation. Visitors are encouraged to think about how they decide whether something is trustworthy. This makes Falseum closer to a modern curiosity cabinet of ideas: instead of displaying unusual objects from nature or the past, it displays unusual examples of human reasoning, persuasion and error.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.falseum.com/chi-siamo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: falseum.com">[Museo Falseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">falseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Falseum Chi Siamo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo FalseumChi Siamo - Museo Falseum...</span></span></span>

The setting itself adds to the experience. A medieval castle is traditionally associated with old stories, legends and inherited memories, making it an appropriate home for a museum concerned with the relationship between stories and truth. Rather than presenting deception as a modern internet problem alone, Falseum places it within a much longer human history.

## How myths and misinformation spread

Falseum’s main subject is the process by which false beliefs become convincing. A false claim can spread because it is repeated often, fits existing assumptions, creates strong emotions or appears to come from an apparently reliable source. The museum’s approach reflects a wider understanding in research on misinformation: people do not encounter information as neutral receivers but interpret it through existing beliefs, social networks and expectations. Studies of online information sharing, including research involving Italian social media users, have examined how communities built around shared narratives can contribute to the spread of unverified claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2651" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Social determinants of content selection in the age of (mis)information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Social determinants of content selection in the age of (mis)informationSeptember 9, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2014</span></span></span>

The distinction between a lie and a false belief is important. A lie normally involves an intention to deceive, while a person may spread inaccurate information because they genuinely believe it is true. Modern research on belief-based deception highlights this difficulty: false statements can be repeated without deliberate dishonesty, making the boundary between deception, misunderstanding and conviction complicated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10185" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Can Factual Statements be Deceptive? The DeFaBel Corpus of Belief-based DeceptionMarch 15, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 15, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction gives Falseum its unusual cultural value. The museum is not simply asking visitors to identify villains who create fake stories. It asks why ordinary people, institutions and societies can accept incorrect ideas. That question connects historical examples of deception with present-day concerns about misleading online content, manipulated images and unreliable sources.

Falseum’s educational activities also reflect this focus. The museum offers school programmes designed to develop critical thinking, encourage checking sources and help younger visitors understand how false information can be created and circulated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.falseum.com/visita/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: falseum.com">[Museo Falseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">falseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Falseum Visita</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo FalseumVisita - Museo Falseum...</span></span></span>

## Why deception remains a modern curiosity

Falseum is part of Italy’s wider tradition of unusual museums that examine the boundaries between reality, belief and imagination. However, its unusual collection is intangible: it collects examples of human mistakes, inventions, rumours and persuasive stories. This makes it different from museums focused on physical oddities, relics or historical objects. Its “specimens” are ideas.

The museum is especially relevant in a period when the difference between authentic and manipulated information has become harder to judge. Digital technology has made it easier to create convincing images, altered media and rapidly spreading claims, increasing the importance of understanding how people evaluate evidence. Falseum’s message is therefore not that every unusual story is false, but that every story benefits from careful examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.falseum.com/chi-siamo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: falseum.com">[Museo Falseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">falseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Falseum Chi Siamo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo FalseumChi Siamo - Museo Falseum...</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in Italy’s stranger cultural attractions, Falseum offers a different kind of mystery. It does not ask whether a monster, relic or legend is real; it asks a deeper question: why do humans create stories that feel true, and why do those stories sometimes survive even when evidence changes?

That makes Falseum an unusual museum not because it preserves a strange object, but because it explores one of humanity’s strangest abilities — the power to build entire worlds from ideas.

    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
