## Introduction

Fang reliquary guardians are among the best-known African sculptures in museums around the world, yet the sculptures themselves tell only part of the story. For Fang communities whose territory extended across present-day [Equatorial Guinea]({{ 'where-did-equatorial-guineas-strangest/' | relative_url }}), Gabon and southern Cameroon, these carvings were never intended to stand alone as art objects. They formed one element of a sacred ensemble centred on carefully preserved ancestral remains. Understanding what these figures originally protected changes how they are viewed: they were guardians of lineage, memory and spiritual continuity rather than mysterious statues created for display. Today, many museums make a deliberate effort to explain this wider context, acknowledging that the sculptures were separated from the bark containers and ancestral relics that originally gave them their purpose.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/310984" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtFigure from a Reliquary Element: Seated Male (Nlo Bieri) - Fang peoples - The Metropolitan Museum of Art...</span></span></span>

## How reliquary guardians connected families and ancestors

Among Fang communities, selected bones—especially skulls and other remains of respected ancestors—were preserved inside bark containers rather than buried and forgotten. These reliquaries represented the continuing presence of important family members whose wisdom, protection and authority remained active within the lineage. The carved guardian figure attached to or placed upon the container was part of this protective system rather than the sacred focus itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/310984" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtFigure from a Reliquary Element: Seated Male (Nlo Bieri) - Fang peoples - The Metropolitan Museum of Art...</span></span></span>

The guardians helped express a view of ancestry in which the dead continued to influence the fortunes of the living. Ritual consultation could accompany major decisions affecting the community, including migration, farming, hunting and warfare. In this setting, the figures functioned less as portraits than as visible signs that the container beneath held spiritually significant remains deserving respect and protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumfigure &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumfigure &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

The sculptures also embodied admired personal qualities. Museum research consistently notes characteristics such as physical strength balanced with calmness, alertness restrained by self-control, and vitality held in disciplined stillness. These qualities reflected ideals of responsible ancestors whose continuing influence was expected to benefit future generations rather than simply commemorate the past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/310870" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art Okak-Fang artist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOkak-Fang artist - Eyema byeri (reliquary guardian figure) - Okak-Fang peoples - The Metropolitan Museum of...</span></span></span>

## What was lost when figures were separated from remains

The international art market became fascinated by Fang sculpture during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries because of its striking forms, which also influenced modern European artists. As a result, guardian figures were frequently collected independently from the reliquary boxes they once protected.

This separation fundamentally altered their meaning. A museum visitor now typically encounters an isolated wooden figure displayed on a pedestal under gallery lighting. Originally, however, that figure was only one component of a complete ritual assemblage that included:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a cylindrical bark container holding selected ancestral bones;
* ritual substances such as palm oil and resin applied repeatedly during ceremonies;
* family knowledge about the named ancestors connected with the relics;
* ceremonies that activated relationships between the living and the dead.

</div>

Without these elements, the sculpture survives but much of its original social and religious function cannot be directly experienced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu/items/show/9009" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu">[digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Byeri Guardian Figure · Michael C. Carlos Museum Collections Online</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Byeri Guardian Figure · Michael C. Carlos Museum Collections Online</span></span></span>

Many surviving figures still show traces of this earlier life. Layers of palm oil, resin and repeated handling created the rich, dark surfaces admired by collectors today. These finishes were not decorative varnishes but evidence of long-term ritual care. Brass eyes, enlarged heads and muscular bodies likewise carried symbolic meanings connected with vigilance, fertility, prestige and protective power rather than purely artistic style.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumfigure &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumfigure &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

Colonial expansion, missionary activity and changing religious practices also contributed to the decline of traditional reliquary use during the twentieth century. In many places the sacred containers disappeared while the carved guardians entered museum and private collections, further separating the visible artwork from the ancestral remains that had originally justified its existence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu/items/show/9009" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu">[digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Byeri Guardian Figure · Michael C. ## How museums now interpret sacred sculpture

Many museums have moved away from presenting Fang reliquary guardians simply as masterpieces of African carving. Current interpretation increasingly explains that the sculptures belonged to the wider institution often described as the *byeri* ancestral tradition, in which images, relics and ritual practice formed an inseparable whole.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/310984" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtFigure from a Reliquary Element: Seated Male (Nlo Bieri) - Fang peoples - The Metropolitan Museum of Art...</span></span></span>

Institutions such as the British Museum explain that the figures protected bark containers holding the remains of influential ancestors and were consulted during important communal decisions. Rather than describing them merely as "fetishes" or mysterious ritual objects—a common colonial-era label—modern displays place greater emphasis on family history, ancestor veneration and the continuity between generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumfigure &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumfigure &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

The Metropolitan Museum of Art similarly stresses that many guardian figures were removed from Africa without their reliquary containers, encouraging visitors to imagine the complete ensemble rather than judging the sculpture as an isolated artwork. This approach helps explain why a relatively small carved figure once carried such profound importance within Fang society.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/art/collection/search/310870" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art Okak-Fang artist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOkak-Fang artist - Eyema byeri (reliquary guardian figure) - The Metropolitan Museum of Art...</span></span></span>

At the same time, museums increasingly acknowledge difficult questions surrounding provenance and collecting history. While many sculptures entered collections through legal art-market transactions of their era, their removal often reflected broader colonial conditions that disrupted religious practice and dispersed culturally significant objects far from the communities that created them. Interpreting the figures today therefore involves not only explaining their craftsmanship but also recognising the historical circumstances that transformed sacred guardians into museum exhibits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu/items/show/9009" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu">[digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalprojects.carlos.emory.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Byeri Guardian Figure · Michael C. Carlos Museum Collections Online</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Byeri Guardian Figure · Michael C. Carlos Museum Collections Online</span></span></span>

## Why this matters for Equatorial Guinea's curiosity heritage

Within Equatorial Guinea's unusual cultural landscape, Fang reliquary guardians illustrate an important distinction between a museum object and a sacred object. The carvings may appear in galleries across Europe and North America, yet their deepest significance lies in the relationships they once protected within Fang families whose historical homeland includes mainland Equatorial Guinea.

For visitors exploring the country's unusual heritage, these sculptures are therefore best understood not as isolated masterpieces or mysterious relics of a vanished belief system, but as surviving fragments of a much larger cultural practice centred on ancestry, memory and the continuing responsibilities between the living and the dead.     var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
