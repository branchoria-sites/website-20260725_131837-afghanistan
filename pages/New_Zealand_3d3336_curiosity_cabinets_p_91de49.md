## Introduction

[New Zealand]({{ 'where-new-zealands-strangest-collections/' | relative_url }}) does not have a long tradition of grand, aristocratic cabinets of curiosity in the European sense. Instead, its most distinctive private museums tend to grow from the passions of individual artists, collectors, naturalists and local historians who gradually transform homes, workshops or small galleries into public spaces filled with remarkable objects. The result is a curiosity culture that feels unusually personal. Visitors are often meeting not only a collection but also the person who assembled it, hearing stories about how objects were found, restored or reinterpreted rather than following the polished narrative of a national museum.

This approach gives New Zealand's private curiosity museums a character that differs sharply from major public institutions. They mix art with natural history, historical artefacts with personal creativity, and careful documentation with the collector's own sense of wonder. Rather than claiming to present a complete picture of the past, they invite visitors into an individual vision of what is worth preserving and why.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.royaldunedinmuseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royaldunedinmuseum.com">[royaldunedinmuseum.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royaldunedinmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span></span></span>

## Cabinets of curiosity revived

The classic European *cabinet of curiosities* (or *Wunderkammer*) gathered natural specimens, artworks, scientific instruments and exotic objects into a single room where categories deliberately overlapped. Before museums separated collections into archaeology, zoology, ethnography and art, these cabinets celebrated surprise and comparison as much as systematic knowledge. Modern New Zealand has relatively few direct descendants of that tradition, but several private collections consciously recreate its atmosphere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thesuter.org.nz/exhibitions/2023/2/22/cabinet-of-curiosities" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thesuter.org.nz">[The Suter Art Gallery]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thesuter.org.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Suter Art Gallery Cabinet of Curiosities — The Suter Art Gallery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Suter Art Gallery Cabinet of Curiosities — The Suter Art Gallery</span></span></span>

Unlike many contemporary museums, these collections rarely follow a strict chronological or scientific order. Instead they encourage visitors to notice unexpected relationships:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* animal skulls displayed beside cultural artefacts;
* handmade sculptures sitting alongside genuine biological specimens;
* historical ephemera mixed with natural history;
* labels that explain provenance while also telling personal stories about acquisition or local history.

</div>

This style reflects curiosity as a method of exploration rather than classification. The collection grows organically as its owner discovers objects that seem to belong together, even if they originate from different disciplines or centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mahalski.org/news/the-dunedin-museum-of-natural-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mahalski.org">[mahalski.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mahalski.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span></span></span>

## Collectors shape the museum as much as the objects

Perhaps the clearest example is the Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery, also known as the Royal Dunedin Museum. Created by artist Bruce Mahalski, it occupies rooms within an old villa rather than a purpose-built museum. The collection includes skulls, bones, preserved biological specimens, ethnological art, historical curiosities and Mahalski's own bone sculptures, all arranged in dense display cabinets reminiscent of nineteenth-century curiosity rooms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.royaldunedinmuseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royaldunedinmuseum.com">[royaldunedinmuseum.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royaldunedinmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span></span></span>

What makes the museum distinctive is not simply the material on display but the collector's presence throughout it. Mahalski has described himself as a lifelong collector rather than a wealthy purchaser, explaining that many objects were found, exchanged or donated over decades. Labels frequently record where an item came from and why it mattered enough to keep, reinforcing that the museum is as much about collecting as about the finished collection itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mahalski.org/news/the-dunedin-museum-of-natural-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mahalski.org">[mahalski.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mahalski.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span></span></span>

The museum also demonstrates how private collections can blur boundaries between museum, studio and gallery. Visitors move between authentic zoological material, artistic reinterpretations made from bone and carefully researched historical objects without rigid institutional divisions. That mixture creates an experience closer to visiting an artist's world than walking through a conventional natural history museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.royaldunedinmuseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royaldunedinmuseum.com">[royaldunedinmuseum.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royaldunedinmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span></span></span>

## Why private museums feel different from national institutions

Large public museums generally work under formal collecting policies, conservation standards and academic interpretation. Their displays are designed to represent broad histories supported by documented provenance and specialist research.

Private curiosity museums operate differently.

They often prioritise:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the collector's individual interests rather than comprehensive coverage;
* emotional or visual impact alongside historical interpretation;
* unusual juxtapositions that encourage exploration;
* ongoing change as new discoveries continually reshape displays;
* direct conversation between visitors and the museum's creator.

</div>

This does not necessarily make them less informative, but it changes how visitors should approach them. A personal museum reflects one person's lifelong interests and judgement, while a national museum aims to represent wider scholarly consensus. Understanding that distinction helps visitors appreciate each on its own terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mahalski.org/news/the-dunedin-museum-of-natural-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mahalski.org">[mahalski.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mahalski.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span></span></span>

## Creativity often matters as much as authenticity

An important feature of New Zealand's curiosity museums is that they frequently combine authentic artefacts with original creative work.

Bruce Mahalski's museum illustrates this particularly well. Genuine animal skulls and natural history specimens appear alongside sculptures assembled from bones, coral and other natural materials. The artistic pieces are not presented as archaeological finds or scientific specimens but as contemporary artworks inspired by the same fascination with anatomy and nature that shaped the museum itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mahalski.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mahalski.org">[mahalski.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mahalski.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Home &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Home &#124; The Bone Art of Bruce Mahalski</span></span></span>

This blending of collecting and making reflects a broader characteristic of New Zealand's independent museum culture. Rather than preserving objects exactly as received, collectors often become interpreters, artists or storytellers who add another layer of meaning through display, restoration or creative reuse.

## What visitors should expect

Visitors accustomed to major museums sometimes find private curiosity museums surprisingly intimate.

Typical characteristics include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Small spaces.** Collections may occupy only a few rooms rather than an entire building.
* **Dense displays.** Cabinets, shelves and walls are often packed with hundreds of objects.
* **Personal interpretation.** Labels frequently include anecdotes or the collector's own observations.
* **Flexible opening hours.** Many independent museums have limited opening days or encourage visitors to check availability before travelling.
* **Conversation with the curator.** Owners are often present and willing to discuss the history behind individual pieces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.royaldunedinmuseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royaldunedinmuseum.com">[royaldunedinmuseum.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royaldunedinmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Dunedin Museum &gt; Home</span></span></span>

</div>

The experience rewards slow looking. Instead of following a prescribed route, visitors often discover overlooked details on a second or third circuit of the rooms, reinforcing the feeling of exploring rather than consuming a finished exhibition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dunedinnz.com/insiders/museum-of-natural-mystery2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dunedinnz.com">[DunedinNZ official website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dunedinnz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dunedin NZ official website Museum of Natural Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DunedinNZ official websiteMuseum of Natural Mystery - Ōtepoti &#124; Dunedin New Zealand official website...</span></span></span>

## A distinctive part of New Zealand's curiosity culture

Private curiosity museums occupy an unusual place within New Zealand's heritage landscape. They preserve objects that may never enter national collections while also keeping alive the older idea that collecting is driven by fascination rather than institutional duty.

Some eventually influence public collections or inspire exhibitions elsewhere. Others remain deeply personal projects whose identity cannot easily be separated from their creators. That individuality is precisely what makes them valuable. They remind visitors that museums need not begin with governments or universities; they can begin with one person who spends decades noticing unusual things and deciding they deserve to be remembered.

Within New Zealand's wider landscape of strange collections—from scientific specimens in major museums to local folklore displays and eccentric regional attractions—private curiosity museums provide the closest modern equivalent to the historic cabinet of curiosities: places where natural history, art, craftsmanship and storytelling coexist under a collector's singular vision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thesuter.org.nz/exhibitions/2023/2/22/cabinet-of-curiosities" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thesuter.org.nz">[thesuter.org.nz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thesuter.org.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Suter Art Gallery Cabinet of Curiosities — The Suter Art Gallery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Suter Art Gallery Cabinet of Curiosities — The Suter Art Gallery</span></span></span>

    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
