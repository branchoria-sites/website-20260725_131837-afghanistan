## Introduction

The Oceanographic Museum of [Monaco]({{ 'where-does-monaco-keep-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}) is best known for its aquariums, but its most unusual feature is arguably **Oceanomania**, a permanent installation that transforms the institution into the closest thing Monaco has to a traditional cabinet of curiosities. Rather than separating science from spectacle, the museum brings together expedition instruments, marine skeletons, fossils, taxidermy, rare books, models, ethnographic objects and historical diving equipment to show how people have tried to understand the sea over more than a century. The result is not a collection of mysterious relics for their own sake, but a carefully curated exploration of wonder, discovery and scientific history that echoes the Renaissance curiosity cabinets from which many modern museums evolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/museum-areas/ocenomania/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Ocenomania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOcenomania - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

Unlike many historical cabinets of curiosities, whose contents mixed fact with myth, Monaco's collection is firmly rooted in documented exploration. ## Prince Albert I's expeditions and collections

The foundation of the museum's cabinet-like character lies in the work of Prince Albert I of Monaco, often called the "Prince of the Seas". Between 1884 and 1915 he organised 28 major oceanographic expeditions across the Mediterranean, North Atlantic and Arctic waters, collecting both natural specimens and the equipment used to obtain them. These expeditions came at a time when much of the deep ocean remained poorly understood, so almost every voyage returned with objects that expanded scientific knowledge while also inspiring public fascination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/our-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Our collections</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOur collections - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

The museum's historical collections therefore contain far more than preserved marine animals. They include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Original measuring and sampling instruments developed specifically for Prince Albert's expeditions.
* Early dredges, nets and collection devices designed to recover specimens from great depths.
* Historic diving equipment, navigation tools and scientific prototypes.
* Natural history specimens ranging from shells and corals to large marine skeletons.
* Expedition archives, artworks and models documenting the growth of oceanography as a science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/our-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Our collections</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOur collections - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

</div>

One of the museum's distinctive claims is that it preserves what it describes as the world's richest collection of historical oceanographic instruments, including numerous prototypes created for Albert I's research campaigns. These objects matter not because they are merely old, but because they document how marine science developed before electronic sensors, remotely operated vehicles and satellite mapping transformed exploration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/measuring-instruments/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Measuring instruments</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoMeasuring instruments - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

In a conventional natural history museum these tools might appear in separate technological displays. In Monaco they become part of the wider story of human curiosity, sitting alongside the animals they helped reveal.

## What visitors see in Oceanomania

Oceanomania is the museum's clearest expression of the cabinet-of-curiosities tradition. Created by American artist Mark Dion and opened permanently in 2011, the installation occupies an immense wall approximately 18 metres wide and 10 metres high, presenting hundreds of objects from the museum's reserves in densely packed historic display cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/museum-areas/ocenomania/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Ocenomania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOcenomania - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

Rather than arranging objects according to modern scientific taxonomy, Dion intentionally recreates the visual language of seventeenth- and eighteenth-century curiosity cabinets. Visitors encounter natural specimens beside cultural artefacts, scientific equipment beside artworks, and exploration technology beside objects once regarded as marvels of the natural world. The arrangement encourages visitors to think about how people have collected and interpreted the ocean across different historical periods rather than simply memorising species names.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/museum-areas/ocenomania/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Ocenomania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOcenomania - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

Among the memorable objects highlighted by the museum are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A taxidermied polar bear from Greenland.
* Historic diving apparatus, including an early diving suit designed by Karl Heinrich Klingert in 1797.
* Fossils and articulated marine skeletons.
* Ship models and navigational artefacts.
* Rare books and illustrations documenting exploration.
* Curious composite displays that deliberately blur the boundary between scientific classification and artistic presentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/museum-areas/ocenomania/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Ocenomania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOcenomania - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

</div>

An especially revealing exhibit is the narwhal tusk. For centuries, narwhal tusks were sold across Europe as the horns of unicorns before zoology explained their true origin. By including this object within Oceanomania, the museum illustrates how curiosity cabinets once mixed genuine discoveries with mistaken interpretations and commercial myths. Rather than celebrating the misconception, the display explains how scientific understanding replaced folklore over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/the-virtual-visit-of-the-museum/chapter-2-the-history-of-oceanography/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Chapter 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoChapter 2 - The History of Oceanography - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

Interactive digital displays complement many historical objects, allowing visitors to examine selected artefacts in three dimensions while preserving the historic atmosphere created by Dion's installation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/museum-areas/ocenomania/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Ocenomania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOcenomania - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

## Science, spectacle and changing interpretation

The Oceanographic Museum deliberately embraces spectacle, but it no longer presents wonder as an end in itself. Instead, it encourages visitors to reflect on how museums have changed.

Traditional cabinets of curiosities often existed to impress wealthy guests with rare and exotic possessions. Scientific classification was incomplete, and collectors sometimes displayed mythical creatures, fabricated specimens or misleading interpretations alongside genuine discoveries. Oceanomania deliberately evokes this visual tradition while making clear that its own objects have documented histories and scientific value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/museum-areas/ocenomania/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Ocenomania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOcenomania - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

This creates several layers of interpretation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Historical curiosity**, showing how earlier generations experienced unfamiliar marine life.
* **Scientific evidence**, demonstrating how specimens and instruments contributed to marine research.
* **Artistic reflection**, with Mark Dion using historical display methods to encourage visitors to question how museums shape knowledge.

## Why it stands out within Monaco's curiosity culture

Monaco has relatively few institutions devoted to unusual collections, making the Oceanographic Museum especially significant within the principality's landscape of curious heritage.

Unlike museums centred on folklore, crime or the occult elsewhere in Europe, Monaco's great collection of wonders comes from scientific exploration. The unusual objects are not presented as supernatural mysteries but as evidence of humanity's long attempt to investigate one of Earth's least accessible environments. Deep-sea organisms, obsolete instruments, preserved specimens and expedition equipment achieve much the same emotional effect as older cabinets of curiosities while remaining grounded in documented research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://musee.oceano.org/en/our-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musee.oceano.org">[Musée Océanographique de Monaco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musee.oceano.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Musée Océanographique de Monaco Our collections</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musée Océanographique de MonacoOur collections - Musée Océanographique de Monaco...</span></span></span>

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
