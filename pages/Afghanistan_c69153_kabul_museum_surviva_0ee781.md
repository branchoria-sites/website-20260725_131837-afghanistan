## Introduction

The National Museum of [Afghanistan]({{ 'what-makes-afghanistans-collections-so/' | relative_url }}) in Kabul is unusual not simply because of what it displays, but because so much of what it displays was expected to be gone forever. During decades of war, rocket attacks, looting and ideological destruction, the museum lost vast portions of its collection. Buildings were damaged, storerooms were emptied and thousands of artefacts were smashed. Yet some of the country's most important treasures survived through a combination of secrecy, luck, professional courage and painstaking restoration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/lost-found-7605081/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineLost &amp; FoundIn retrospect, National Museum of Afghanistan director Omara Khan Masoudi&#x27;s decision to hide the Bactrian...</span></span></span>

## How Did Kabul's Museum Survive the Wars?

The National Museum stands near Darulaman on the outskirts of Kabul. Before the conflicts of the late twentieth century it held one of Central Asia's richest archaeological collections, documenting thousands of years of Afghan history. The museum's holdings ranged from prehistoric artefacts to Buddhist sculpture, Hindu imagery, Islamic manuscripts, coins and luxury goods from ancient trade routes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/lost-found-7605081/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineLost &amp; FoundIn retrospect, National Museum of Afghanistan director Omara Khan Masoudi&#x27;s decision to hide the Bactrian...</span></span></span>

The institution suffered repeated disasters. During the civil war of the 1990s, the museum sat close to front lines and was struck by rockets. Looters entered damaged buildings and removed huge numbers of objects. Later, in 2001, Taliban authorities ordered the destruction of many figurative works still remaining in the museum, smashing statues and reliefs they regarded as religiously unacceptable. Thousands of artefacts were damaged or destroyed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_art_in_Afghanistan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Destruction of art in Afghanistan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Destruction of art in Afghanistan</span></span></span>

What visitors encounter today is therefore not merely a collection of ancient objects. It is a collection that has already survived several attempted erasures.

## A Silk Roads Cabinet of Cultures

Among the most remarkable survivors are objects illustrating Afghanistan's role as a crossroads of civilisations. Rather than representing a single culture, many pieces show influences from Greece, India, Central Asia, China and the ancient Iranian world existing side by side.

The most famous examples come from Tillya Tepe, often known as the [Bactrian Gold]({{ 'bactrian-gold/' | relative_url }}). Excavated in 1978 from elite burials in northern Afghanistan, the treasure included more than 20,000 gold objects: jewellery, ornaments, weapon fittings and ceremonial items. The artefacts combine motifs from multiple traditions, making them one of the clearest demonstrations of cultural exchange along the Silk Roads.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eurasianet.org/afghanistan-nation-protects-storied-bactrian-treasure" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eurasianet.org">[eurasianet.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eurasianet.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">afghanistan nation protects storied bactrian treasure</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Afghanistan: Nation Protects Storied Bactrian Treasure10 June 2006 — &quot;The Bactrian treasure was found in Jowzjan Province in six graves t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 2006</span></span></span>

Particularly striking is the folding gold crown discovered in one of the graves. Unlike a rigid royal crown, it could be dismantled and transported, suggesting a mobile aristocratic culture connected to the wider Eurasian steppe. The object has become an emblem not only of ancient Afghanistan but also of the survival of Afghan heritage itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/archaeology/a71825064/gold-artifacts-of-afghanistan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popularmechanics.com">[Popular Mechanics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popularmechanics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unearthed in 1978 at Tillya Tepe (&quot;Hill of Gold&quot;) in northern Afghanistan by a Soviet-Afghan team led by Viktor Sarianidi, the site revea...</span></span></span>

## Looting, Iconoclasm and Missing Objects

The museum's story is inseparable from loss. Archaeologists and museum officials estimate that a large proportion of the collection disappeared during the wars. Some reports suggest that around seventy percent of holdings were looted, destroyed or otherwise lost during the worst years of conflict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/afghan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.archaeology.org">[Archaeology Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.archaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magazine Museum Under Siege: Full Text</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology MagazineMuseum Under Siege: Full Text - Magazine Issue ArchiveAbout 70 percent of the museum&#x27;s collections are now missing. Certain categories suffered especially heavily. Coin collections, sculptures and portable antiquities were attractive targets for traffickers because they could be transported and sold relatively easily. Looted Afghan artefacts began appearing in international markets, often stripped of clear provenance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/afghan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.archaeology.org">[Archaeology Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.archaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magazine Museum Under Siege: Full Text</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology MagazineMuseum Under Siege: Full Text - Magazine Issue ArchiveAbout 70 percent of the museum&#x27;s collections are now missing. The destruction carried out in 2001 was different from ordinary looting. Instead of theft for profit, it was an attempt to eliminate images considered unacceptable. Museum staff later described how statues and reliefs were systematically smashed. The attack became one of the most notorious episodes in the broader campaign that also destroyed the giant Buddhas of Bamiyan.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/lost-found-7605081/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineLost &amp; FoundIn retrospect, National Museum of Afghanistan director Omara Khan Masoudi&#x27;s decision to hide the Bactrian...</span></span></span>

This history explains why many museum displays are stories of survival rather than pristine preservation. A visitor may see reconstructed fragments, repaired sculptures or objects that returned only after years abroad.

## The Secret That Saved the Bactrian Gold

The museum's greatest survival story concerns what was not left inside the museum at all.

As political conditions deteriorated in the late 1980s, senior Afghan officials and museum professionals secretly transferred the Bactrian Gold and other exceptional treasures into heavily protected vaults beneath the central bank. Knowledge of the hiding place was restricted to a small group of trusted keyholders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/lost-found-7605081/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineLost &amp; FoundIn retrospect, National Museum of Afghanistan director Omara Khan Masoudi&#x27;s decision to hide the Bactrian...</span></span></span>

During the following years, rumours spread that the treasure had been stolen, melted down or smuggled abroad. Meanwhile the museum itself was shelled, looted and partially destroyed. Because almost nobody knew where the collection had been hidden, the secret survived alongside the artefacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/lost-found-7605081/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineLost &amp; FoundIn retrospect, National Museum of Afghanistan director Omara Khan Masoudi&#x27;s decision to hide the Bactrian...</span></span></span>

After the fall of the Taliban government, the keyholders were eventually reunited and the vaults were opened. The treasure emerged largely intact, turning what many assumed was a story of irreversible loss into one of the most dramatic museum recoveries of modern times.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillya_Tepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tillya Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tillya Tepe</span></span></span>

In the context of world museums, few collections have experienced such a complete cycle of disappearance, rumour and rediscovery.

## Recovery, Conservation and Uncertain Access

The museum's survival did not end when hidden treasures reappeared. Conservators then faced the challenge of rebuilding what remained.

Damaged sculptures have been reassembled from fragments. Archaeologists have catalogued surviving collections, digitised records and worked to identify stolen artefacts circulating internationally. Repatriation efforts have returned numerous objects seized by police and customs authorities abroad. Some sculptures and archaeological pieces looted decades earlier have been traced and sent back to Kabul.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projects.artgallery.nsw.gov.au/afghanistan/credits/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projects.artgallery.nsw.gov.au">[nsw.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projects.artgallery.nsw.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFGHANISTA N: HIDDEN TREASURES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeologists register objects to create a digital record at the National Museum of Afghanistan in Kabul...Read more...</span></span></span>

Recovery remains incomplete. Many artefacts are still missing, and concerns about security and political instability continue to shape how collections are managed. International organisations and museum professionals have repeatedly emphasised the vulnerability of Afghan cultural heritage during periods of political change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/taliban-afghan-heritage" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: architecturaldigest.com">[Architectural Digest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">architecturaldigest.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on architecturaldigest.com.</span></span></span>

For this reason, the National Museum is best understood not as a finished institution but as an ongoing conservation project. Its galleries preserve remarkable ancient objects, but they also preserve evidence of modern rescue efforts.

## Why the Museum's Survivors Matter

Many countries possess richer museums or larger archaeological collections. What makes Kabul's National Museum distinctive is that its most famous exhibits are also survivors of modern history.

The Bactrian Gold, restored Buddhist sculptures, recovered antiquities and reconstructed fragments all tell two stories at once. One story belongs to the ancient Silk Roads, empires and religions that shaped Afghanistan over millennia. The other belongs to curators, archaeologists and ordinary custodians who protected cultural heritage during war, often at considerable personal risk.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/lost-found-7605081/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineLost &amp; FoundIn retrospect, National Museum of Afghanistan director Omara Khan Masoudi&#x27;s decision to hide the Bactrian...</span></span></span>

  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
