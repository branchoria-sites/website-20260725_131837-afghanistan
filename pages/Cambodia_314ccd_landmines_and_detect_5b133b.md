## Introduction

[Cambodia]({{ 'what-makes-cambodias-collections-so/' | relative_url }})'s landmine displays are unlike almost any other museum experience. In Siem Reap, collections of deactivated mines, bomb fragments and military equipment are not simply relics from a finished war. They explain a danger that still affects parts of the countryside, while some visitor centres demonstrate the technologies and animals used in ongoing humanitarian clearance. The result is an unusual blend of museum, memorial and active public education: visitors encounter objects that once threatened lives, alongside organisations still working to remove explosive remnants from the landscape. Rather than treating conflict as distant history, these displays emphasise that Cambodia's legacy of landmines remains a practical, humanitarian issue with continuing consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apopo.org/what-we-do/detecting-landmines-and-explosives/where-we-work/apopo-in-cambodia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apopo.org">[apopo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apopo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in cambodia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">• APOPOMarch 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2024</span></span></span>

## The Cambodia Landmine Museum and Aki Ra

The best-known collection is the Cambodia Landmine Museum near Siem Reap, founded by the former child soldier and deminer Aki Ra. During and after decades of conflict, Cambodia became heavily contaminated with anti-personnel mines and unexploded ordnance. Aki Ra spent years locating and removing explosives, eventually gathering thousands of inert mines, rockets, mortar shells, grenades and other weapons that would otherwise have disappeared into scrapyards or remained anonymous hazards.

What makes the museum unusual is that the collection exists primarily to explain humanitarian work rather than military history. The exhibits are largely deactivated weapons arranged to show the variety of explosive devices left behind by successive conflicts. Information panels explain how mines were laid, why they remain dangerous for decades and how clearance teams identify and remove them. Instead of celebrating military technology, the displays deliberately strip weapons of their original purpose and reinterpret them as evidence of long-term civilian harm.

The museum has also become associated with Aki Ra's wider humanitarian work, although that history has not been without controversy. Cambodian authorities have investigated aspects of the museum's permits and the orphanage formerly linked to it, leading to legal disputes and organisational changes. Those controversies concern administration rather than the basic reality of Cambodia's mine contamination, and the museum continues to be recognised primarily as a public education centre focused on mine awareness and humanitarian demining.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apopo.org/what-we-do/detecting-landmines-and-explosives/where-we-work/apopo-in-cambodia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apopo.org">[apopo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apopo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in cambodia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">• APOPOMarch 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2024</span></span></span>

## War machinery in open-air collections

Around Siem Reap, landmine interpretation often overlaps with open-air displays of military hardware. Visitors may see disabled tanks, artillery pieces, armoured vehicles and helicopters displayed outdoors alongside smaller explosive devices recovered from the countryside.

These displays differ from conventional military museums in several important ways:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Most equipment is presented as physical evidence of conflict rather than as engineering achievements.
* The collections frequently include damaged or abandoned machinery recovered locally rather than carefully restored vehicles.
* Landmines, bomb casings and unexploded ordnance are interpreted as everyday hazards that affected farming communities, not simply battlefield artefacts.
* Exhibits commonly explain clearance techniques, protective equipment and the long process of making contaminated land safe again.

</div>

Because the collections emerged in one of the world's most mine-affected countries, they encourage visitors to think less about military campaigns and more about reconstruction. Even apparently ordinary pieces of battlefield equipment gain a different meaning when displayed beside photographs of clearance teams and stories from survivors or deminers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apopo.org/what-we-do/detecting-landmines-and-explosives/where-we-work/apopo-in-cambodia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apopo.org">[apopo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apopo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in cambodia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">• APOPOMarch 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2024</span></span></span>

## How APOPO's detection rats work

Perhaps the most distinctive visitor experience is offered by APOPO's Visitor Center in Siem Reap, where trained African giant pouched rats—known as HeroRATs—demonstrate how animals assist humanitarian demining. Unlike the museum's static displays, these are living working animals whose counterparts are deployed in real clearance operations across Cambodia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://booking.apopo.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: booking.apopo.org">[APOPO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">booking.apopo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visitor Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APOPO Visitor Center...</span></span></span>

### Why rats?

The idea initially sounds improbable, but it rests on straightforward biology. The rats are trained to recognise the scent of TNT, the explosive found in many landmines. During training they receive food rewards whenever they correctly indicate a buried explosive.

Their light body weight means they are too small to trigger most anti-personnel mines. Working on long guide lines across carefully prepared search lanes, they rapidly cover areas that would take human deminers much longer to inspect using metal detectors alone. When a rat identifies the scent of explosives, it scratches the ground to mark the location. Human specialists then investigate and safely remove or destroy the device.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apopo.org/what-we-do/detecting-landmines-and-explosives/where-we-work/apopo-in-cambodia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apopo.org">[apopo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apopo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in cambodia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">• APOPOMarch 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2024</span></span></span>

### What visitors actually see

The APOPO Visitor Center is designed as a guided educational experience rather than a zoo or animal show. Visitors typically receive:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an introduction to Cambodia's landmine legacy;
* an explanation of humanitarian demining methods;
* a live demonstration of a HeroRAT locating buried explosives in a simulated search area;
* opportunities to ask questions about training, operations and mine clearance.

</div>

The demonstrations use controlled environments and are intended to illustrate detection techniques rather than recreate dangerous field conditions. Guides explain how rats work alongside dogs, mechanical equipment and manual deminers, showing that no single technology clears mines on its own.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://booking.apopo.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: booking.apopo.org">[APOPO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">booking.apopo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visitor Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APOPO Visitor Center...</span></span></span>

## Why these displays still matter

Many war museums gradually become detached from the conflicts they describe. Cambodia's landmine displays remain unusually immediate because clearance continues in affected provinces. The objects behind the glass resemble devices that trained teams are still locating in fields and forests.

This ongoing relevance changes the atmosphere for visitors. Rather than presenting conflict as finished history, the museums and visitor centres explain why clearance remains expensive, technically demanding and essential for agriculture, infrastructure and everyday safety. Seeing inert mines alongside demonstrations by detection animals reinforces that humanitarian demining is not simply about preserving history but about removing hazards that continue to affect communities decades after the fighting ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apopo.org/what-we-do/detecting-landmines-and-explosives/where-we-work/apopo-in-cambodia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apopo.org">[apopo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apopo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in cambodia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">• APOPOMarch 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2024</span></span></span>

## A distinctive place in Cambodia's curiosity landscape

Within Cambodia's wider landscape of unusual museums and collections, landmine museums occupy a distinctive position. They preserve objects that are undeniably strange and unsettling, yet their purpose is educational rather than sensational. Visitors encounter rows of deactivated explosives, improvised displays of recovered weapons and, uniquely, living detection animals whose work bridges museum interpretation and active humanitarian operations.

Few countries offer a comparable combination of historical collection and ongoing field practice. In Cambodia, the museum object and the real-world problem remain closely connected, making these institutions some of the country's most unusual—and most socially significant—public displays.

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
