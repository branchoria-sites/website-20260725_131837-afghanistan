## Introduction

[Slovenia]({{ 'where-slovenia-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s carnival masks are unusual because they were never intended to be museum pieces. They were made to shake, thunder, frighten, joke and confront entire villages. Today, some of the country's most distinctive collections preserve these costumes without separating them from the living traditions that created them. Rather than displaying anonymous masks behind glass, Slovenian museums often explain who wears them, when they appear, why they make so much noise and how communities continue to recreate them.

This makes Slovenia's carnival collections different from many folklore displays elsewhere in Europe. Visitors are not simply looking at historic artefacts but at objects that still leave museum cases each year in the form of modern performances, festivals and village customs. The result is a rare combination of ethnographic preservation and living cultural practice, where questions of authenticity, revival and tourism are as important as the masks themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmpo.si/stalne_zbirke/collection-of-traditional-carnival-masks/?lang=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmpo.si">[PMPO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmpo.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Collection of Traditional Carnival Masks – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collection of Traditional Carnival Masks – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož...</span></span></span>

## Kurents and the Ptuj Castle collection

The best place to understand this relationship is the traditional carnival costume collection at Ptuj Castle, managed by the Ptuj-Ormož Regional Museum. The exhibition presents the extraordinary diversity of carnival figures from north-eastern Slovenia rather than focusing solely on the famous Kurent. Costumes are displayed as part of a wider seasonal procession, helping visitors understand that these figures originally belonged to noisy communal performances rather than theatrical entertainment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmpo.si/stalne_zbirke/collection-of-traditional-carnival-masks/?lang=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmpo.si">[PMPO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmpo.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Collection of Traditional Carnival Masks – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collection of Traditional Carnival Masks – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož...</span></span></span>

The Kurent dominates the collection because it has become Slovenia's best-known carnival character. Dressed in thick sheepskin, carrying heavy bells and wearing a dramatic mask with a protruding tongue, the figure exists to create movement and sound. Traditional rounds through villages involve jumping, ringing bells and visiting households during the carnival season. According to longstanding popular belief, this noise drives winter away and encourages fertility and prosperity for the coming year, although historians remain cautious about treating these beliefs as direct survivals from an identifiable pre-Christian religion. The precise origins of the Kurent remain debated, with theories linking the tradition to several different historical and cultural influences rather than a single ancient source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmpo.si/stalne_zbirke/collection-of-traditional-carnival-masks/?lang=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmpo.si">[PMPO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmpo.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Collection of Traditional Carnival Masks – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collection of Traditional Carnival Masks – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož...</span></span></span>

The museum deliberately preserves these competing interpretations instead of presenting one definitive origin story. Visitors therefore encounter both folklore and scholarship: local belief about chasing away winter alongside academic uncertainty about how far such traditions can be traced into the distant past. This evidence-aware approach gives the collection much of its value.

The collection also reminds visitors that the Kurent is only one member of a much larger carnival world. Devils, ploughmen, log-haulers, whip crackers, bears, wedding characters and many other regional figures appear across the exhibition, illustrating how neighbouring villages developed distinctive ritual characters while sharing similar seasonal themes of renewal, fertility, disorder and communal identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmpo.si/en/nadstropja/stables/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmpo.si">[PMPO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmpo.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stables – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stables – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož...</span></span></span>

One reason these costumes still feel alive is that they remain part of annual practice. The internationally known Kurentovanje festival in Ptuj grew from local traditions after its establishment in 1960, while the older door-to-door rounds of the Kurents themselves were recognised by UNESCO in 2017 as Intangible Cultural Heritage. Museums therefore preserve objects that visitors can still see performing in streets only a short distance away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.slovenia.info/en/press-centre/news-of-the-tourism-press-agency/36640-carnival-traditions-in-slovenia-s-historical-towns" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slovenia.info">[I feel Slovenia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slovenia.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on slovenia.info.</span></span></span>

## Cerkno's revived carnival characters

Cerkno offers a different perspective on living folklore because its celebrated carnival tradition demonstrates that heritage can survive through reconstruction as well as uninterrupted continuity.

The town's distinctive carnival group, known for its carved wooden masks, represents a complex cast of symbolic characters rather than a single iconic figure. Their masks are individually carved from linden wood, while costumes incorporate natural materials such as moss, straw, ivy and animal skins. Each character embodies recognisable human behaviours or social types, allowing carnival to become both celebration and gentle public satire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitcerkno.si/en/cerkljanska-laufarija-eng/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitcerkno.si">[LTO Laufar Cerkno &#124; Visit Cerkno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitcerkno.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">LTO Laufar Cerkno &#124; Visit Cerkno Cerkljanska laufarija – Visit Cerkno</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LTO Laufar Cerkno &#124; Visit Cerkno Cerkljanska laufarija – Visit Cerkno</span></span></span>

Unlike romantic ideas that traditions either survive unchanged or disappear forever, the Cerkno example reveals a more complicated history. After interruptions during the twentieth century, the carnival tradition was revived and expanded. New generations recreated characters, maintained carving techniques and re-established performances that gradually became accepted once again as part of local identity. Instead of weakening authenticity, this revival illustrates how communities actively choose which traditions deserve continuation.

The annual ritual centres on the symbolic trial and execution of the figure known as Pust, who is blamed for the previous year's misfortunes before being ceremonially punished. Although theatrical and humorous, the performance preserves older ideas about transferring communal problems onto a symbolic figure before the arrival of spring. Today the tradition is recognised within Slovenia's Register of Intangible Cultural Heritage and has also been declared heritage of national importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitcerkno.si/en/cerkljanska-laufarija-eng/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitcerkno.si">[LTO Laufar Cerkno &#124; Visit Cerkno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitcerkno.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">LTO Laufar Cerkno &#124; Visit Cerkno Cerkljanska laufarija – Visit Cerkno</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LTO Laufar Cerkno &#124; Visit Cerkno Cerkljanska laufarija – Visit Cerkno</span></span></span>

For museum visitors, this makes the surviving masks especially interesting. They are not archaeological relics from a vanished culture but working objects connected to performances that continue to evolve.

## Authenticity, reinvention and tourism

Slovenia's carnival collections raise an important question: when does a living tradition become a museum exhibit, and when does a museum help keep that tradition alive?

Ptuj demonstrates both sides of this relationship. The castle collection documents local customs, while the success of Kurentovanje has transformed the Kurent into a national cultural symbol and a major tourist attraction. International recognition has brought wider audiences, but it has also encouraged careful documentation and conservation of costumes that might otherwise have disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.slovenia.info/en/press-centre/news-of-the-tourism-press-agency/36640-carnival-traditions-in-slovenia-s-historical-towns" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slovenia.info">[I feel Slovenia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slovenia.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on slovenia.info.</span></span></span>

This visibility inevitably changes the tradition. Performances that once belonged primarily to neighbouring villages are now watched by international visitors, photographed extensively and promoted through cultural tourism. Yet the continued importance of local participation distinguishes the Slovenian example from folklore recreated solely for spectators. Many of the customs remain rooted in village organisations and seasonal community obligations rather than existing only as festival entertainment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmpo.si/en/nadstropja/stables/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmpo.si">[PMPO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmpo.si</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stables – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stables – Pokrajinski muzej Ptuj Ormož...</span></span></span>

At the same time, museums avoid presenting the masks as timeless relics. Curators increasingly explain that carnival traditions have always changed. Costumes have been repaired, replaced and adapted; participation rules have shifted; and some characters have disappeared while others have been revived. This emphasis on continuity through change challenges the common assumption that folklore is authentic only if it remains completely unchanged.

For visitors interested in unusual collections, Slovenia's carnival museums therefore offer something distinctive. The masks are visually striking, but their greatest curiosity lies in their unfinished story. Every winter they leave the display cases in cultural memory, reappear on living performers and continue accumulating new meanings without losing their connection to the communities that first created them.
