## Introduction

[Malta]({{ 'where-malta-keeps-its-strangest-stories/' | relative_url }}) never experienced the vast witch-hunting panics that scarred parts of Germany, Scotland or colonial New England. Instead, the island's history of alleged witchcraft survives in a remarkably different form: thousands of pages of inquisitorial records, preserved alongside the tribunal rooms and prison cells of the Inquisitor's Palace in Birgu. Today the palace allows visitors to see not only where suspects were questioned, but also how ordinary people became entangled in accusations involving love charms, fortune telling, healing rituals and popular superstition rather than organised devil worship. These records reveal a society where magic was treated primarily as a religious and moral offence, offering an unusually detailed glimpse into everyday fears, relationships and beliefs in early modern Malta.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/mt/explore/the-inquisitors-palace/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Malta Inquisitor&#x27;s Palace</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage MaltaInquisitor&#x27;s Palace - Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

## Inside the Inquisitor's Palace tribunal and prisons

The Inquisitor's Palace is one of the best-preserved surviving inquisitorial palaces in Europe. Built originally as a civil courthouse in the sixteenth century, it became the headquarters of the Roman Inquisition in Malta after the arrival of the papal inquisitor Pietro Dusina in 1574. For more than two centuries it served simultaneously as a courtroom, administrative centre, residence and prison. Visitors can still walk through the tribunal chamber, the chancery where records were prepared, and the small punitive cells where prisoners were held while awaiting questioning or judgement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/mt/explore/the-inquisitors-palace/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Malta Inquisitor&#x27;s Palace</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage MaltaInquisitor&#x27;s Palace - Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

Unlike many popular depictions of the Inquisition, the building presents a more bureaucratic reality than a torture chamber. Heritage Malta notes that torture was authorised in fewer than one per cent of recorded Maltese inquisitorial cases and only under tightly regulated circumstances after approval from Rome. The tribunal relied far more heavily on interrogation, confession, witness testimony and written documentation than on physical coercion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/mt/explore/the-inquisitors-palace/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Malta Inquisitor&#x27;s Palace</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage MaltaInquisitor&#x27;s Palace - Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

The palace is unusual because the building and its archives reinforce one another. Visitors are not simply shown reconstructed scenes but are standing where documented cases were heard, while surviving proceedings allow historians to match rooms with real individuals, accusations and verdicts. Exhibitions have displayed original trial documents, magical charms, handwritten proceedings and plans from the Vatican Archives, making the palace both a museum and a rare historical archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/news/unique-exhibition-on-the-roman-inquisition-in-malta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Malta Unique exhibition on the Roman Inquisition in Malta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage MaltaUnique exhibition on the Roman Inquisition in Malta - Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

## Love spells, divination and everyday accusations

The surviving records show that most Maltese witchcraft investigations concerned practical problems rather than fears of secret satanic conspiracies. People sought magical help to attract lovers, repair marriages, cure illness, discover thieves, improve their fortunes or break perceived curses. Such practices often blended Catholic prayers, folk remedies and magical rituals, producing a blurred boundary between accepted devotion and prohibited superstition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/26130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L-Università ta&#x27; Malta OAR@UM: Malta [Encyclopedia of witchcraft</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L-Università ta&#x27; Malta OAR@UM: Malta [Encyclopedia of witchcraft</span></span></span>

Love magic appears particularly frequently. One well-known case from 1617, reconstructed by historian Carmel Cassar from inquisitorial records, centred on Sulpitia de Lango, a former courtesan caught preparing a magical mixture in the crypt beneath St John's church in Valletta during an important religious ceremony. The investigation expanded to include several other people accused of producing love charms and magical concoctions intended to influence affection and desire. Rather than describing spectacular acts of sorcery, the case illustrates how anxieties about sexuality, reputation and social order became matters for religious justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/106791" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L-Università ta&#x27; MaltaOAR@UM: Sex, magic, and the periwinkle: a trial at the Malta Inquisition Tribunal in 1617...</span></span></span>

Other cases involved women who claimed to possess remedies for illness, methods of divination or recipes for attracting lovers. Records describe mixtures containing everyday materials such as salt, charcoal, olive leaves, palm leaves, water and herbs, accompanied by spoken formulas that participants believed carried supernatural power. These rituals reflected ordinary hopes and fears more than organised occult traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://foreign.gov.mt/malta-360-insights/a-darker-history-tales-of-maltese-women-in-the-18th-century/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foreign.gov.mt">[foreign]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foreign.gov.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">foreign A darker history: tales of Maltese women in the 18th century</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A darker history: tales of Maltese women in the 18th century - foreignOctober 23, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2023</span></span></span>

The archive also demonstrates that accusations did not automatically imply belief in an organised cult of witches. Many defendants admitted using charms or folk remedies while insisting they intended to help neighbours or solve personal problems. Only a minority of cases explicitly referred to the Devil, and even then the tribunal often focused on whether prohibited religious practices had occurred rather than proving elaborate conspiracies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://foreign.gov.mt/malta-360-insights/a-darker-history-tales-of-maltese-women-in-the-18th-century/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foreign.gov.mt">[foreign]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foreign.gov.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">foreign A darker history: tales of Maltese women in the 18th century</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A darker history: tales of Maltese women in the 18th century - foreignOctober 23, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Malta avoided a Salem-style panic

One of the most striking features of Malta's history is what did **not** happen. Although the Inquisition actively investigated magical practices, the islands never experienced the mass executions associated with the great European witch hunts or the Salem trials of 1692.

Several factors help explain this difference.

First, Malta was governed by the highly centralised Roman Inquisition rather than by numerous competing secular courts. The tribunal operated within established legal procedures, maintained extensive documentation and remained answerable to authorities in Rome. This encouraged careful investigation rather than waves of local panic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/mt/explore/the-inquisitors-palace/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Malta Inquisitor&#x27;s Palace</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage MaltaInquisitor&#x27;s Palace - Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

Second, Maltese inquisitors generally interpreted magic as a matter of religious error, superstition or improper devotional practice instead of evidence that vast networks of devil-worshipping witches threatened society. As a result, investigations commonly ended with confession, penance, fines, imprisonment or public acts of repentance rather than execution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/26130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L-Università ta&#x27; Malta OAR@UM: Malta [Encyclopedia of witchcraft</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L-Università ta&#x27; Malta OAR@UM: Malta [Encyclopedia of witchcraft</span></span></span>

Finally, the surviving records reveal a society where magical belief was woven into everyday life. Healers, charm-makers and fortune tellers occupied ambiguous positions within their communities. People often sought their help before denouncing them, creating complicated relationships that differed from the explosive accusations seen in regions consumed by large-scale witch panics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/29176" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.mt.</span></span></span>

## What visitors can expect today

Modern visitors encounter the Inquisitor's Palace as both a historic building and a place of interpretation rather than a sensational horror attraction. Exhibits explain how inquisitorial justice operated, display authentic documents and place witchcraft accusations within the wider social history of early modern Malta. Prison cells, tribunal rooms, kitchens and private apartments help illustrate the institution's daily workings, while temporary exhibitions have explored themes such as magical spells, inquisitorial archives and popular belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/mt/explore/the-inquisitors-palace/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Malta Inquisitor&#x27;s Palace</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage MaltaInquisitor&#x27;s Palace - Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

The palace also addresses persistent myths surrounding the Inquisition.
