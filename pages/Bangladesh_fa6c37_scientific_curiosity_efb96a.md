## Introduction

[Bangladesh]({{ 'where-bangladesh-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s strangest scientific collections are not built around dinosaurs or spectacular fossils. Instead, they preserve everyday natural objects that have become extraordinary because they document a changing landscape. At Bangladesh Agricultural University in Mymensingh, hundreds of freshwater fish are stored in jars as permanent scientific specimens, creating an unusual museum where visitors can see species that have become increasingly rare in the country's rivers. In the far north, the Rocks Museum at Panchagarh Government Women's College turns river stones, petrified wood and geological samples into a public record of the region's deep natural history. Together, these collections reveal how Bangladeshi universities and colleges have transformed ordinary fish and stones into evidence of biodiversity, geology and environmental change rather than tourist curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fs.bau.edu.bd/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fs.bau.edu.bd">[edu.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fs.bau.edu.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.bd.</span></span></span>

## Inside the Mymensingh fish specimen collection

The Professor Mostafa Ali Reza Hossain Fish Museum and Germplasm Centre at Bangladesh Agricultural University is one of Bangladesh's most distinctive scientific collections. Rather than displaying dramatic marine creatures, it concentrates on the country's freshwater ecosystems, preserving fish in alcohol-filled glass jars so that researchers, students and visitors can compare species over time. The museum is closely tied to the university's teaching and conservation work, making it as much a research resource as a public exhibition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fs.bau.edu.bd/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fs.bau.edu.bd">[fs.bau.edu.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fs.bau.edu.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.bd.</span></span></span>

Walking through the galleries can feel unexpectedly striking. Shelf after shelf of preserved fish creates the atmosphere of a nineteenth-century natural history collection, yet every specimen has a practical purpose. The preserved examples allow scientists to identify species accurately, study anatomical variation and maintain reference material long after wild populations have declined. The collection also includes fishing equipment, skeletons and selected aquatic reptiles, amphibians and birds that help explain Bangladesh's freshwater ecosystems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bau.edu.bd/public/file_manager/apa/65646ad205675.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bau.edu.bd">[bau.edu.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bau.edu.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-title">evsjv‡`k K…wl wek¦we`¨vjq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">evsjv‡`k K…wl wek¦we`¨vjq</span></span></span>

Earlier descriptions of the museum reported more than 230 preserved freshwater fish species from the roughly 265 then recorded in Bangladesh, making it one of the country's most comprehensive reference collections. More recent university documentation instead lists around 240 preserved specimens, reflecting continuing curation rather than a fixed exhibition. The changing figures illustrate that the museum is a living scientific collection whose contents evolve as specimens are replaced, catalogued and conserved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.bdfish.org/2011/02/fish-museum-biodiversity-center-bau/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.bdfish.org">[bdfish.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.bdfish.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Institute Profile: Fish Museum and Biodiversity Center, BAU – Bd FISH Feature</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Institute Profile: Fish Museum and Biodiversity Center, BAU – BdFISH FeatureFebruary 1, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 1, 2011</span></span></span>

What makes the museum especially unusual is that many jars represent species that have become difficult to encounter in the wild. Instead of preserving rare animals simply because they are visually impressive, the collection documents river biodiversity before further losses occur. The specimens therefore serve both as teaching material and as a historical archive of Bangladesh's freshwater life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://just.edu.bd/research/item/5f6627b050404333da404a75" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: just.edu.bd">[just.edu.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">just.edu.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 31, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 31, 2020</span></span></span>

## How Panchagarh became a museum of stones

Bangladesh's only dedicated public rocks museum grew from a local geological curiosity rather than a national museum programme. Established around 2000 on the campus of Panchagarh Government Women's College, the Rocks Museum began with collections assembled under the initiative of the college's then principal after recognising the remarkable variety of stones found across the district.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://beautifulbangladesh.gov.bd/loc/rangpur/215" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beautifulbangladesh.gov.bd">[Beautiful Bangladesh]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beautifulbangladesh.gov.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Beautiful Bangladesh Rocks Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beautiful Bangladesh Rocks Museum</span></span></span>

Panchagarh occupies an unusual position in Bangladesh's far north, where rivers descending from the Himalayan foothills have deposited large quantities of pebbles, gravel and older rock fragments. That geological setting explains why the district contains rock types that are uncommon elsewhere in the country's predominantly alluvial plains. Instead of treating these stones as construction material, the museum presents them as evidence of ancient geological processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/Rare-rocks-speak-of-the-past-at-Panchagarh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.prothomalo.com">[Prothomalo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.prothomalo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo...</span></span></span>

Visitors encounter a surprisingly broad collection that includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Granite, quartzite, basalt, limestone and sandstone.
* Petrified wood showing the gradual replacement of organic material by minerals.
* Silica-rich pebbles and river stones of different colours and textures.
* Decorated and carved stones alongside archaeological finds.
* Ancient pottery fragments, coins and large traditional wooden boats that provide historical context for the region's landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/Rare-rocks-speak-of-the-past-at-Panchagarh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.prothomalo.com">[prothomalo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.prothomalo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo...</span></span></span>

Although the museum contains archaeological material, its distinctive character comes from treating rocks themselves as the principal exhibits. Many specimens are arranged to illustrate the geological history of the district rather than artistic or monetary value, making the collection an unusual blend of local geology and regional heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/Rare-rocks-speak-of-the-past-at-Panchagarh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.prothomalo.com">[Prothomalo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.prothomalo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo...</span></span></span>

## What these collections reveal about rivers and loss

Despite looking very different, the fish museum and the rocks museum tell related stories about Bangladesh's environment.

The preserved fish capture a biological record of rivers whose ecology has been altered by overfishing, habitat change, pollution, dams and changing water flows. A preserved specimen can outlast the disappearance of a local population, allowing future researchers to verify what once lived in a particular river system. This gives the collection importance beyond education alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://just.edu.bd/research/item/5f6627b050404333da404a75" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: just.edu.bd">[edu.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">just.edu.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 31, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 31, 2020</span></span></span>

The rocks in Panchagarh record change on a much longer timescale. Their shapes, mineral composition and distribution reflect geological processes extending back thousands or millions of years, while the surrounding river deposits show how Himalayan sediments continue to reshape northern Bangladesh. The museum therefore encourages visitors to think about landscape history rather than simply individual specimens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/Rare-rocks-speak-of-the-past-at-Panchagarh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.prothomalo.com">[Prothomalo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.prothomalo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rare rocks speak of the past at Panchagarh &#124; Prothom Alo...</span></span></span>

Seen together, the collections offer two complementary forms of evidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Fish specimens** preserve biological diversity that can disappear within decades.
* **Rock specimens** preserve geological evidence formed over immense spans of time.
* **Both collections** transform ordinary natural objects into reference material that supports education, research and public understanding rather than commercial spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bau.edu.bd/public/file_manager/apa/65646ad205675.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bau.edu.bd">[bau.edu.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bau.edu.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-title">evsjv‡`k K…wl wek¦we`¨vjq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">evsjv‡`k K…wl wek¦we`¨vjq</span></span></span>

## Why these scientific collections stand out in Bangladesh's curiosity culture

Neither collection was designed as a cabinet of oddities, yet both create the same sense of wonder that visitors often associate with curiosity museums. Hundreds of preserved fish in glass jars can appear unsettling to casual visitors, while rooms filled entirely with stones challenge expectations of what deserves museum space. Their appeal lies in careful documentation rather than theatrical presentation.

That difference is important within Bangladesh's broader landscape of unusual collections. These institutions prioritise evidence over mystery. Labels explain species, habitats and rock types instead of encouraging supernatural interpretations or sensational stories. The result is a quieter but more enduring form of curiosity, where the fascination comes from recognising that familiar rivers and stones can preserve scientific knowledge about a country undergoing rapid environmental change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fs.bau.edu.bd/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fs.bau.edu.bd">[edu.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fs.bau.edu.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.bd.</span></span></span>
