## Introduction

[Canada]({{ 'what-makes-canadas-strangest-museums/' | relative_url }})'s curiosity cabinets and community-built oddity museums are less about shock value than about the people who assembled them. Instead of grand national institutions devoted to the bizarre, Canada has developed a distinctive tradition of small museums where local collectors, artists and volunteers preserve unusual objects that might otherwise disappear. These collections often combine humour, craftsmanship, natural history and personal obsession, creating spaces that recall the nineteenth-century cabinet of curiosities while remaining deeply rooted in the communities that built them.

The result is a strand of Canadian museum culture in which dressed fleas, taxidermied prairie rodents, handcrafted anatomical figures and eclectic collections of natural specimens all become ways of telling local stories. Although these museums differ greatly in purpose and style, they share an interest in transforming ordinary places into destinations through imagination, careful collecting and public display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.samwallermuseum.ca/sam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: samwallermuseum.ca">[samwallermuseum.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">samwallermuseum.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sam Waller Museum Sam… Our Founder – Sam Waller Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sam Waller Museum Sam… Our Founder – Sam Waller Museum</span></span></span>

## From private collections to public museums

Canada's community-built oddity museums rarely begin as formal cultural institutions. More often they start with an individual whose lifelong fascination gradually grows into something larger.

One of the clearest examples is the Sam Waller Museum in The Pas, Manitoba. Sam Waller described himself as a "pack rat" and built what he affectionately called his "Clutter-torium", later known as the Little Northern Museum. A teacher, naturalist and taxidermist, Waller originally used his growing collection to help pupils understand subjects that were difficult to explain through language alone. After retiring in 1958, he opened the museum to the public, where visitors could browse everything from natural history specimens to historical artefacts and outright curiosities. Its popularity quickly exceeded the original premises, leading to a larger museum supported by the local Rotary Club before eventually becoming today's professionally managed Sam Waller Museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.samwallermuseum.ca/sam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: samwallermuseum.ca">[Sam Waller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">samwallermuseum.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sam Waller Museum Sam… Our Founder – Sam Waller Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sam Waller Museum Sam… Our Founder – Sam Waller Museum</span></span></span>

This trajectory is significant because it mirrors the development of many Canadian local museums. Private enthusiasm provides the spark, but community organisations, volunteers and municipalities often become responsible for preserving the collection once it acquires wider historical value.

The Museum of Fear and Wonder near Bergen, Alberta represents a different evolution. Rather than preserving a single collector's lifetime accumulation, it is an intentionally curated private museum created by Brendan and Jude Griebel. It consciously draws on the tradition of prairie museums and historical curiosity cabinets, using emotionally charged objects to encourage reflection on identity, belief, medicine and mortality rather than simply displaying "strange things". Visits are by appointment, emphasising conversation and interpretation instead of mass tourism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fearandwonder.ca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fearandwonder.ca">[fearandwonder]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fearandwonder.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Museum of Fear and Wonder &#124; Private museum collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Museum of Fear and Wonder &#124; Private museum collection...</span></span></span>

## Dressed fleas, artificial bodies and taxidermied towns

What distinguishes Canada's curiosity museums is not merely that they contain unusual objects, but the very different meanings attached to those objects.

### The dressed fleas of The Pas

Perhaps the most famous exhibit at the Sam Waller Museum is its collection of dressed fleas. Five pairs of fleas wear miniature costumes, including several dressed as bridal couples. The display fascinates visitors precisely because the practical question—how such tiny garments were ever made—remains difficult to answer with certainty.

Museum research traces the tradition to the Mexican tourist trade during the early twentieth century, although some accounts place its origins earlier in convent workshops. The museum openly acknowledges that aspects of flea dressing remain poorly documented and that the craft itself is widely regarded as a lost art. Rather than presenting speculation as fact, the exhibit uses uncertainty as part of the object's appeal, inviting visitors to appreciate both the extraordinary craftsmanship and the incomplete historical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.samwallermuseum.ca/collections/feature/dressed-fleas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: samwallermuseum.ca">[Sam Waller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">samwallermuseum.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sam Waller Museum Dressed Fleas – Sam Waller Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sam Waller Museum Dressed Fleas – Sam Waller Museum</span></span></span>

### Crafted bodies in Alberta

The Museum of Fear and Wonder explores a very different kind of curiosity. Its "Crafting Bodies" collection examines the long human tradition of constructing representations of the body through wax figures, automatons, mannequins, anatomical models, devotional objects and medical teaching aids.

Rather than treating these artefacts as horror props, the museum places religious, scientific, theatrical and artistic objects alongside one another. This comparison encourages visitors to consider how societies have used artificial bodies for education, healing, ritual, entertainment and remembrance. The collection therefore resembles a modern cabinet of curiosities organised around ideas instead of chronology or geography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fearandwonder.ca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fearandwonder.ca">[fearandwonder]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fearandwonder.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Museum of Fear and Wonder &#124; Private museum collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Museum of Fear and Wonder &#124; Private museum collection...</span></span></span>

### A town populated by gophers

The World Famous Gopher Hole Museum in Torrington, Alberta demonstrates another Canadian approach to oddity museums: humour grounded in local identity.

Created and operated by volunteers, the museum features taxidermied Richardson's ground squirrels posed in handcrafted miniature scenes depicting everyday prairie life. Visitors encounter rodents acting as firefighters, musicians, shopkeepers, hunters and wedding guests, each vignette reflecting familiar aspects of rural Alberta.

What could easily have become a novelty attraction instead functions as a celebration of community history. The museum explicitly describes its mission as preserving stories of rural Alberta while encouraging curiosity, creativity and appreciation of prairie ecosystems and agriculture. The anthropomorphic displays are intentionally playful, yet they remain rooted in local memory rather than random eccentricity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gopherholemuseum.org/about-3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gopherholemuseum.org">[World Famous Gophers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gopherholemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Famous Gophers About &#124; World Famous Gophers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World Famous Gophers About &#124; World Famous Gophers</span></span></span>

## Why these collections feel like modern curiosity cabinets

Historically, cabinets of curiosity gathered natural specimens, artworks, scientific instruments and exotic objects into a single room where visitors could wonder about the world's diversity before museums separated collections into specialist disciplines.

Several Canadian institutions consciously preserve this older style of collecting.

Sam Waller's original museum mixed local history with zoological specimens, unusual artefacts and imported curiosities without rigid disciplinary boundaries. Modern displays still preserve something of that atmosphere through galleries where natural history, folk craftsmanship and unexpected oddities appear together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.samwallermuseum.ca/sam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: samwallermuseum.ca">[Sam Waller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">samwallermuseum.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sam Waller Museum Sam… Our Founder – Sam Waller Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sam Waller Museum Sam… Our Founder – Sam Waller Museum</span></span></span>

The Museum of Fear and Wonder explicitly embraces the cabinet tradition, although with contemporary interpretation. Instead of claiming that every object possesses supernatural significance, it examines why people become emotionally attached to masks, death masks, anatomical models, puppets and other crafted bodies. The museum's founders argue that objects themselves tell stories about fear, identity and myth-making when viewed collectively rather than individually.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fearandwonder.ca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fearandwonder.ca">[fearandwonder]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fearandwonder.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Museum of Fear and Wonder &#124; Private museum collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Museum of Fear and Wonder &#124; Private museum collection...</span></span></span>

Both museums therefore differ from conventional history museums. Their organising principle is curiosity itself—the connections between seemingly unrelated objects become part of the visitor experience.

## What these displays reveal about local identity

Canada's community-built oddity museums reveal as much about their creators as about the objects they contain.

Several themes recur across these collections:

* **Local humour becomes heritage.** The Gopher Hole Museum transforms a familiar prairie pest into an affectionate symbol of rural resilience and community life rather than treating it simply as agricultural nuisance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gopherholemuseum.org/about-3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gopherholemuseum.org">[World Famous Gophers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gopherholemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Famous Gophers About &#124; World Famous Gophers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World Famous Gophers About &#124; World Famous Gophers</span></span></span>
* **Collectors shape historical memory.** Sam Waller's personal enthusiasm became the foundation for a museum holding more than 70,000 objects, yet the institution has since narrowed new acquisitions to reflect the history of The Pas and its surrounding region, balancing eccentric origins with professional stewardship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.samwallermuseum.ca/collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: samwallermuseum.ca">[Sam Waller Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">samwallermuseum.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sam Waller Museum Our Collections – Sam Waller Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sam Waller Museum Our Collections – Sam Waller Museum</span></span></span>

## How to interpret Canada's oddity museums

Visitors benefit from recognising that not every curiosity museum serves the same purpose.

Some collections, such as the Sam Waller Museum, combine rigorous regional history with inherited cabinets of curiosity. Others, like the Museum of Fear and Wonder, deliberately use unusual objects to stimulate reflection rather than to document local history. Meanwhile, the Gopher Hole Museum openly embraces humour while preserving stories about prairie communities through handcrafted displays.

None of these institutions asks visitors to suspend critical thinking. Instead, they invite people to consider why certain objects become memorable enough to preserve, how communities decide what is worth displaying, and how curiosity itself can become a form of cultural heritage.

Within Canada's broader landscape of unusual museums, these community-built collections demonstrate that the country's strangest exhibits often emerge not from sensational discoveries but from ordinary people whose passions gradually became public institutions.

    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
