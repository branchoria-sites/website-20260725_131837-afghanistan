## Introduction

[Grenada]({{ 'where-does-grenada-keep-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})’s Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park is one of the world's most unusual visitor attractions because it was designed not to remain unchanged. Installed in 2006 in the waters of Molinere Bay, the submerged sculptures were created to become living artificial reefs rather than permanent, pristine works of art. Coral, algae, sponges and other marine organisms gradually colonise their surfaces, so every visit reveals a slightly different museum beneath the sea. This approach transformed the idea of public sculpture from something preserved against nature into something deliberately reshaped by it, making Molinere a landmark in both environmental art and Grenada's curiosity culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/projects/molinere-underwater-sculpture-park/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureMolinere Underwater Sculpture Park - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

Rather than presenting mysterious ruins or a fictional lost civilisation, the park openly embraces its identity as contemporary art. Its fascination lies in watching familiar human figures slowly disappear beneath marine growth while simultaneously creating new habitat for sea life. That balance between artistic expression and ecological restoration has become the defining feature of Grenada's underwater museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/projects/molinere-underwater-sculpture-park/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureMolinere Underwater Sculpture Park - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

## How the underwater park began

The project was created by British-Guyanese sculptor Jason deCaires Taylor after Hurricane Ivan severely damaged reefs around Grenada in 2004. Instead of simply placing sculptures underwater as decoration, Taylor worked with local authorities to create structures that could function as artificial reefs while also attracting divers away from the most fragile surviving coral areas. The result was the world's first large-scale public underwater sculpture park, established in the Molinere Beauséjour Marine Protected Area in 2006.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatermuseum.com/projects/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatermuseum.com">[underwatermuseum.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatermuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater Museums by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

The sculptures were engineered for the marine environment rather than adapted from ordinary gallery works. They were cast in pH-neutral marine cement with rough, textured surfaces that encourage coral larvae to settle and grow. Stainless steel reinforcement provides long-term structural stability while avoiding materials likely to damage surrounding ecosystems. Cavities and crevices within some sculptures also create shelter for fish, lobsters and other marine animals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/coral-carnival/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture The Coral Carnival</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureThe Coral Carnival - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

This combination of art and habitat restoration became highly influential. Molinere demonstrated that underwater sculpture could serve conservation goals while creating a distinctive tourism experience, inspiring later underwater museums across the Caribbean, Europe, Asia and Australia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/about/biography/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture Biography</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureBiography - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

## The sculptures visitors recognise

Although the collection contains dozens of works, several pieces have become internationally recognised because they illustrate how ordinary human scenes change beneath the sea.

**Vicissitudes** is the park's best-known installation. Twenty-six life-sized children stand hand in hand in a circle, facing outward into the surrounding water. Cast from local Grenadian children, the sculpture symbolises shared humanity and the way environments shape people. As coral growth spreads unevenly across the figures, each child gradually develops a unique appearance, reinforcing the work's central idea of transformation through surroundings. Local interpretations have also linked the circle and its setting in the Atlantic to memories of slavery and the Middle Passage, adding layers of meaning beyond the artist's original concept.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/projects/molinere-underwater-sculpture-park/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureMolinere Underwater Sculpture Park - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

**The Lost Correspondent** depicts a journalist seated at a desk with a typewriter covered in newspaper cuttings. Above water, it resembles an ordinary office scene; underwater, it appears like a forgotten room preserved by the sea. Marine organisms slowly coat the desk and typewriter until the sculpture itself becomes a fossil-like object, reinforcing themes of memory, obsolete technology and changing forms of communication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/projects/molinere-underwater-sculpture-park/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureMolinere Underwater Sculpture Park - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

Other early works, including **Grace Reef** and **The Unstill Life**, similarly relocate familiar domestic subjects into an unfamiliar underwater setting. Rather than preserving these scenes, the ocean steadily alters them, making biological change an essential part of the artwork rather than accidental damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_deCaires_Taylor" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jason de Caires Taylor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jason de Caires Taylor</span></span></span>

## How marine growth transforms the art

Unlike conventional sculpture, Molinere's installations were never intended to remain visually complete. Their appearance changes through a process known as ecological succession, as different marine organisms colonise the surfaces over months and years.

Initially, microscopic organisms and algae establish themselves on the textured concrete. These are followed by sponges, soft corals and eventually hard coral species where conditions allow. Fish, crustaceans and other reef animals increasingly use the sculptures for shelter, feeding and breeding. Instead of resisting these changes through cleaning or restoration, the project welcomes them as the final stage of the artwork.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/projects/molinere-underwater-sculpture-park/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureMolinere Underwater Sculpture Park - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

Currents, wave action, sunlight and depth also influence how individual sculptures develop. Two identical figures can evolve very differently depending on water movement or exposure to light, meaning the collection never reaches a finished state. Visitors returning years later often find familiar works almost unrecognisable beneath coral colonies and marine life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.puregrenada.com/underwatersculpturepark/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: puregrenada.com">[Grenada Tourism Authority]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">puregrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Grenada Tourism Authority Underwater Sculpture Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Grenada Tourism AuthorityUnderwater Sculpture Park - Grenada Tourism Authority...</span></span></span>

This continual transformation challenges traditional museum ideas about conservation. Most museums attempt to prevent objects from changing; Molinere treats change itself as the artwork's intended outcome. The sculptures are simultaneously cultural artefacts and living ecological structures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_deCaires_Taylor" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jason de Caires Taylor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jason de Caires Taylor</span></span></span>

## Why it matters within Grenada's curiosity landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

For travellers interested in unusual collections rather than conventional museums, Molinere occupies a distinctive place because it combines contemporary art, environmental engineering and natural history in a single experience. Visitors do not simply observe exhibits—they watch an ecosystem actively rewriting them.

</div>

The sculptures also avoid the common misconception that they represent ancient ruins or submerged archaeological remains. Every figure is a documented modern artwork whose gradual disappearance beneath coral is intentional. The haunting atmosphere comes from the interaction between human-made forms and natural processes, not from fabricated legends about lost civilisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://underwatersculpture.com/projects/molinere-underwater-sculpture-park/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: underwatersculpture.com">[Underwater Sculpture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">underwatersculpture.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Underwater Sculpture Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Underwater SculptureMolinere Underwater Sculpture Park - Underwater Sculpture by Jason deCaires Taylor...</span></span></span>

In 2023 the park gained a significant expansion through **Coral Carnival**, a series inspired by Grenada's Spicemas celebrations.       if (!languageMarket && localeMarketMap[languageOnly]) languageMarket = localeMarketMap[languageOnly];

  function inferFromTimezone() {
    var tz = '';
    try { tz = String(Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone || ''); } catch (err) {}
    if (!tz) return '';
    for (var i = 0; i< timezoneRules.length; i += 1) {
      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }

    available = Array.isArray(available) ? available : [];
    options = options || {};
    var storageKey = options.storageKey || 'phoenix-affiliate-market';
    try {
      var saved = window.localStorage && localStorage.getItem(storageKey);
      if (saved && available.indexOf(saved) >= 0) return saved;
    } catch (err) {}
    var timezoneMarket = inferFromTimezone();
    if (timezoneMarket && available.indexOf(timezoneMarket) >= 0) return timezoneMarket;
    var localeMarkets = localeMatches();
    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
    if (options.defaultMarket && available.indexOf(options.defaultMarket) >= 0) return options.defaultMarket;
    return available[0] || '';
  }

    normalize: normalize,
    inferMarket: inferMarket,
    inferFromLocale: inferFromLocale,
    inferFromTimezone: inferFromTimezone
  };
