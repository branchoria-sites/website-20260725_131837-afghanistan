## Introduction

[Cambodia]({{ 'what-makes-cambodias-collections-so/' | relative_url }})'s best-known memorial sites are unusual because they function simultaneously as preserved crime scenes, museums, archives, places of mourning and educational institutions. The former S-21 prison at Tuol Sleng and the execution and burial site at Choeung Ek do not simply display historical objects. They preserve material evidence of genocide while asking difficult questions about how human suffering should be presented to the public. Their curators must balance historical accuracy, respect for victims, religious traditions, survivor testimony, tourism and education. Those tensions make these sites central not only to Cambodia's history, but also to wider debates about the ethics of displaying atrocity.

Since 2025, Tuol Sleng, Choeung Ek and the former M-13 prison have been jointly recognised by UNESCO as the serial World Heritage property "Cambodian Memorial Sites: From Centres of Repression to Places of Peace and Reflection". That designation explicitly frames them as places for remembrance, education and reflection rather than dark attractions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-congratulates-cambodia-inscription-its-memorial-sites-world-heritage-list" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Congratulates Cambodia on the Inscription of Its Memorial Sites</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Congratulates Cambodia on the Inscription of Its Memorial SitesJuly 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2025</span></span></span>

## Tuol Sleng: Prison, archive and memorial

Unlike many memorial museums created after conflict, Tuol Sleng retains much of the original fabric of the prison itself. Former classrooms remain divided into tiny cells, interrogation rooms still contain iron bedframes, and thousands of prisoner photographs, confessions and administrative records survived because the prison bureaucracy meticulously documented its victims.

This creates an unusual ethical challenge. The power of the museum comes from its authenticity, yet many of the surviving objects were originally created as instruments of persecution. Mugshots that today help identify victims were once tools of dehumanisation. Forced confessions are historical evidence despite having been extracted through torture. Rather than treating these documents as neutral archives, curators increasingly interpret them within the context of coercion and state violence.

The museum has also evolved significantly since it first opened in 1979. Early displays were designed soon after the Khmer Rouge fell, when the immediate aim was to document crimes and demonstrate what had happened. Some exhibitions relied heavily on shock value, including the famous arrangement of hundreds of human skulls into the outline of Cambodia. As attitudes towards memorialisation changed, that display was dismantled in 2002 because of deterioration, conservation concerns and growing recognition that victims' remains deserved more respectful treatment. The shift reflected broader international museum practice, which increasingly favours contextual interpretation over graphic spectacle.

Today, survivor testimony, educational programmes and archival interpretation play a larger role alongside the preserved buildings themselves. UNESCO likewise highlights the site's archives, educational mission and conservation rather than presenting it as a static museum of horror.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1748/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreCambodian Memorial Sites: From centres of repression to places of peace and reflection - UNESCO World Heritag...</span></span></span>

## Human remains and ritual at Choeung Ek

Choeung Ek presents a different ethical problem because it remains both a former mass grave and an active place of remembrance. Thousands of victims were buried there after execution, and human remains continue to emerge naturally from the ground during heavy rain or seasonal erosion.

Visitors often notice fragments of bone or clothing surfacing along pathways. Rather than removing every fragment immediately, staff have historically documented and respectfully collected newly exposed remains while acknowledging that the landscape itself continues to reveal evidence of the killings. This reinforces that Choeung Ek is not merely an archaeological site but a burial ground.

The memorial's best-known feature is the glass stupa containing thousands of recovered skulls and other human remains. For some visitors, the transparent display provides undeniable evidence against genocide denial and conveys the scale of the crimes more powerfully than photographs ever could. For others, displaying human remains raises profound ethical questions about consent, dignity and whether victims become objects of observation.

Cambodian religious practice complicates simple answers. Buddhist traditions place strong emphasis on honouring the dead, yet there are also local understandings that preserving remains within memorial structures can serve remembrance and spiritual respect when carried out appropriately. The stupa therefore functions simultaneously as evidence, memorial and ritual space rather than simply as a museum display.

These overlapping meanings distinguish Choeung Ek from many Western memorial museums, where human remains are often displayed only rarely or not at all.

## Can genocide memorials avoid becoming dark tourism?

The enormous international profile of Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek creates an unavoidable tension. Both sites receive large numbers of foreign visitors alongside Cambodian school groups, survivors' families and religious pilgrims. Their educational mission depends partly on public access, yet tourism inevitably changes how visitors experience places associated with immense suffering.

Curators have increasingly responded by shaping visitor behaviour rather than attempting to eliminate tourism altogether. Audio guides emphasise individual lives rather than anonymous statistics. Interpretation increasingly explains how the prison system operated instead of relying solely on graphic evidence. Quiet spaces for reflection have become more prominent, and educational outreach to Cambodian students has expanded alongside international visitation. UNESCO's management framework similarly emphasises visitor management, interpretation, conservation and the appropriate treatment of human remains across the memorial sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1748" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Cambodian Memorial Sites: From centres of repression to places of peace and reflection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreCambodian Memorial Sites: From centres of repression to places of peace and reflection - UNESCO World Heritag...</span></span></span>

The challenge remains that visitors arrive with very different expectations:

## Politics, memory and changing display ethics

Memorial sites are never politically neutral. Tuol Sleng opened under the government that replaced the Khmer Rouge, and for decades debates have continued over how remembrance intersects with national politics, transitional justice and reconciliation.

The conclusion of the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia (the Khmer Rouge Tribunal) added another layer by reinforcing the evidential importance of surviving archives, photographs and prison records. Rather than functioning solely as museum exhibits, many objects preserved at Tuol Sleng have also served as legal evidence in criminal proceedings.

The 2025 UNESCO World Heritage inscription marks another important shift. Rather than recognising the sites purely as locations of atrocity, UNESCO frames them as places transformed "from centres of repression to places of peace and reflection". The management plans accompanying the inscription stress conservation, education, community involvement, careful visitor management and the respectful treatment of human remains, signalling an international endorsement of memorial practices that privilege remembrance over sensational display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1748/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreCambodian Memorial Sites: From centres of repression to places of peace and reflection - UNESCO World Heritag...</span></span></span>

## Why these memorials matter within Cambodia's unusual heritage

Within Cambodia's wider landscape of unusual museums and collections, Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek occupy a unique position. They are neither curiosity museums nor conventional historical collections. Their most significant exhibits are preserved places, documentary archives and, in some cases, the physical remains of victims.

Their importance lies not in displaying the strange for entertainment, but in demonstrating how museums can preserve uncomfortable evidence while continually reconsidering the ethics of doing so. Every curatorial decision—whether to exhibit human remains, how much graphic material to show, how to balance survivor testimony with archival documents, or how to accommodate millions of visitors without trivialising suffering—reflects an ongoing negotiation between historical truth, cultural values and respect for those who died.

The result is a form of memorial museum in which the ethics of display are inseparable from the collection itself. The debate is not simply about what should be exhibited, but about how a society remembers genocide without allowing remembrance to become spectacle.

    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
