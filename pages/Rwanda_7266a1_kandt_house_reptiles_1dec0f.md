## Introduction

Kandt House Museum in Kigali is unusual because it combines two experiences that would normally be found in separate institutions. Inside, visitors encounter a museum about [Rwanda]({{ 'what-makes-rwandas-museums-so-unusual/' | relative_url }}) before colonial rule, German colonial administration and the early history of Kigali. Outside, they find a compact reptile exhibit containing snakes and crocodiles. The result is a museum whose identity has changed several times without entirely abandoning its earlier role as Rwanda's natural history museum. Rather than being a full zoological collection, the surviving reptile displays act as a living reminder of the building's previous mission while creating one of Rwanda's most distinctive museum experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visitrwanda.com/interests/kandt-house-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitrwanda.com">[visitrwanda.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitrwanda.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visit Rwanda Kandt House Museum – Visit Rwanda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit Rwanda Kandt House Museum – Visit Rwanda</span></span></span>

## Why Does a Colonial Museum Keep Reptiles?

The apparent contradiction is explained by the museum's history. The building itself dates from 1908 and was the residence of Richard Kandt, Germany's first colonial resident in Rwanda. After restoration in the early 2000s, it reopened as the Kandt House Museum of Natural History, with exhibitions devoted to Rwanda's wildlife, geology and ecosystems. Years later, the institution was reimagined to focus primarily on colonial history, pre-colonial Rwanda and the development of Kigali. Instead of removing every trace of its earlier purpose, museum managers retained a small outdoor reptile collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandt-Haus-Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

This layered identity makes Kandt House different from both a conventional history museum and a conventional natural history museum. Visitors are effectively moving through two different museum philosophies: one centred on historical interpretation and another centred on living animals.

### From colonial residence to natural-history museum

The building's evolution mirrors changing priorities in Rwanda's heritage sector.

Initially, its significance lay in its association with German colonial administration. Restoration preserved one of the oldest surviving colonial-era buildings in Kigali, preventing its demolition and giving it a new public function. It then became Rwanda's principal natural history museum, displaying preserved specimens and explaining the country's wildlife.

Later, much of that natural history role shifted elsewhere, particularly to the Museum of Environment in Karongi (formerly Kibuye). When Kandt House reopened under its current identity in 2017, the permanent galleries concentrated on three broad themes:

## Snakes and Crocodiles as a Separate Attraction

The reptiles are treated almost as an additional attraction rather than part of the historical galleries. Official museum information even lists the reptile exhibition separately from the main museum visit, with its own ticket category and visitor guidance. This separation reflects the fact that the animals are not simply decorative exhibits but an inherited feature from the museum's previous natural history identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rwandaheritage.gov.rw/en/visit-national-museums" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rwandaheritage.gov.rw">[rwandaheritage.gov.rw]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rwandaheritage.gov.rw</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rwanda Heritage Visit National Museums</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rwanda Heritage Visit National Museums</span></span></span>

The collection is modest rather than comprehensive. Official descriptions refer to live snakes and a crocodile, while other museum documentation identifies species such as the Nile crocodile alongside several snakes native to Rwanda, including black mambas, cobras and African rock pythons. Unlike a zoo, the aim is educational display rather than maintaining a large animal collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandt-Haus-Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

For many visitors, this creates an unexpected shift in atmosphere. After galleries devoted to political history and colonial administration, they step into an outdoor area where living reptiles become the focus. The contrast is memorable precisely because it is so unusual.

## What the Unusual Pairing Tells Visitors

The reptiles are more than an isolated curiosity. They reveal how museums can accumulate layers of purpose instead of replacing one identity with another.

Many museums completely reinvent themselves by removing earlier displays. Kandt House instead preserves a visible trace of its former natural history mission. The reptiles therefore function almost like living museum artefacts: they are not relics from the colonial period, yet they document an earlier phase in the institution's own history.

This also reflects wider developments in Rwanda's museum network. Rather than concentrating every subject in a single institution, the country has gradually distributed different themes across specialised museums. Natural history and environmental interpretation expanded elsewhere, while Kandt House increasingly became the principal site for understanding Rwanda's early colonial period and the origins of Kigali. ## A Distinctive Place in Rwanda's Curiosity Landscape

Within Rwanda's unusual museum attractions, Kandt House occupies a distinctive niche because its curiosity lies less in exotic animals than in the collision of museum identities.

Visitors do not encounter reptiles inside a dedicated reptile park or zoological garden. Instead, they find them attached to a historic colonial residence whose galleries explore politics, empire and urban history. That combination makes the museum memorable for reasons beyond the animals themselves.

The surviving snakes and crocodiles also demonstrate that museum collections can have histories of their own. In Kandt House, the reptiles are not merely exhibits to observe. They are evidence that the museum itself has changed—from colonial residence, to natural history museum, to historical museum—while deliberately preserving one small, living reminder of what it used to be.
