## Introduction

The Kôrè Museum in Ségou is one of the best places to understand why some of Mali's most extraordinary objects were never meant to stand still. Its collections of masks, puppets and giant animal figures make sense only when viewed as parts of living performances involving music, dance, storytelling and communal participation. Rather than displaying strange creatures as isolated sculptures, the museum emphasises the traditions that brought them to life, preserving both the physical objects and the performance knowledge that gave them meaning. This approach reflects a wider understanding in Mali that heritage includes movement, sound and social memory as much as carved wood or painted surfaces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koresegou.org/en/patrimony/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koresegou.org">[Kôrè Cultural Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koresegou.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kôrè Cultural Center Patrimony &#124; Kôrè Cultural Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kôrè Cultural Center Patrimony &#124; Kôrè Cultural Center</span></span></span>

## Inside Ségou's puppet traditions

The Kôrè Museum forms part of the Kôrè Cultural Center, an institution devoted to safeguarding both Mali's tangible and intangible heritage. Alongside historical objects, it documents music, dance, masks and puppets that have shaped cultural life across the Niger River region. Instead of treating puppets as decorative curiosities, the museum presents them as evidence of performance traditions that continue to influence artists and cultural practitioners today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koresegou.org/en/patrimony/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koresegou.org">[Kôrè Cultural Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koresegou.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kôrè Cultural Center Patrimony &#124; Kôrè Cultural Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kôrè Cultural Center Patrimony &#124; Kôrè Cultural Center</span></span></span>

Research on the Ségou region has shown that puppet theatre developed as a collective activity organised by youth associations. Villages commissioned skilled carvers and metalworkers to build increasingly inventive figures, while performers, singers and drummers worked together to animate them. A puppet therefore represented only one element of a much larger theatrical event involving choreography, songs, audience interaction and humour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2190/EN7J-51HL-1Y3D-72VD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sage Journals Puppet Theatre: Form and Ideology in Bamana Performances</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsPuppet Theatre: Form and Ideology in Bamana Performances - Mary Jo Arnoldi, 1986...</span></span></span>

Unlike European string marionettes, many Malian puppets are carried or worn by concealed performers. Some are handheld, while others are large enough to hide the operator completely beneath cloth, woven fibres or elaborate costumes. Their movement comes from the coordinated work of human performers rather than mechanical devices, allowing the figures to dance, leap, bow or appear to chase spectators through an open performance space.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mau.museumofafricanart.org/en/past-exhibitions/traditional-theatre-puppets-of-mali.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mau.museumofafricanart.org">[Museum of African Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mau.museumofafricanart.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of African Art Traditional Theatre Puppets of Mali</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of African ArtTraditional Theatre Puppets of MaliApril 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2021</span></span></span>

## Animals, hybrids and social meaning

The creatures preserved through these traditions are deliberately imaginative. Performances feature recognisable animals such as antelope, buffalo, crocodiles, birds, hippopotamuses and lions, but they also include exaggerated hybrids, fantastic beings and humorous human characters. The goal is not naturalistic representation. Instead, each figure embodies particular behaviours, virtues, dangers or comic flaws that audiences immediately recognise through local storytelling traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mau.museumofafricanart.org/en/past-exhibitions/traditional-theatre-puppets-of-mali.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mau.museumofafricanart.org">[Museum of African Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mau.museumofafricanart.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of African Art Traditional Theatre Puppets of Mali</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of African ArtTraditional Theatre Puppets of MaliApril 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2021</span></span></span>

Animal figures often become social commentators rather than simple representations of wildlife. A powerful buffalo may symbolise strength and authority, while more playful creatures can mock arrogance, laziness or greed. Human puppets likewise portray farmers, hunters, traders or travellers, creating performances that combine entertainment with gentle satire and moral reflection. The theatrical format allows communities to discuss social behaviour publicly without directly attacking individuals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2190/EN7J-51HL-1Y3D-72VD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[sagepub.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sage Journals Puppet Theatre: Form and Ideology in Bamana Performances</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsPuppet Theatre: Form and Ideology in Bamana Performances - Mary Jo Arnoldi, 1986...</span></span></span>

The visual appearance of these creatures also reflects continual artistic innovation. Alongside carved wood, makers have incorporated cloth, leather, mirrors, sheet metal and recycled manufactured materials. New characters have periodically entered the repertoire in response to changing society, demonstrating that these traditions have never been frozen relics but living forms of creativity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mau.museumofafricanart.org/en/past-exhibitions/traditional-theatre-puppets-of-mali.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mau.museumofafricanart.org">[Museum of African Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mau.museumofafricanart.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of African Art Traditional Theatre Puppets of Mali</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of African ArtTraditional Theatre Puppets of MaliApril 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why movement matters more than the sculpture


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

A visitor encountering one of these puppets behind museum glass can easily mistake it for a striking sculpture. In performance, however, its appearance changes completely.

</div>

Movement creates the illusion of personality. Carefully timed gestures, rhythmic drumming and coordinated singing transform carved wood into an apparently living creature. The audience responds not simply to craftsmanship but to the convincing interaction between performer, music and object. Anthropological studies of Bamana puppet theatre argue that separating the sculpture from its performance strips away much of its intended meaning because the object was designed specifically for action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2190/EN7J-51HL-1Y3D-72VD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sage Journals Puppet Theatre: Form and Ideology in Bamana Performances</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsPuppet Theatre: Form and Ideology in Bamana Performances - Mary Jo Arnoldi, 1986...</span></span></span>

This helps explain why photographs of isolated masks or puppets can be misleading. The dramatic effect depends upon complete costumes, hidden operators, musicians, dancers and the surrounding community, not merely the carved head or body. In many performances, spectators actively participate through singing, laughter and commentary, making the event a shared social experience rather than a staged spectacle observed in silence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mau.museumofafricanart.org/en/past-exhibitions/traditional-theatre-puppets-of-mali.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mau.museumofafricanart.org">[Museum of African Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mau.museumofafricanart.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of African Art Traditional Theatre Puppets of Mali</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of African ArtTraditional Theatre Puppets of MaliApril 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2021</span></span></span>

## How museums preserve live performance

The Kôrè Museum approaches preservation differently from museums that collect only finished objects. Its stated mission includes safeguarding knowledge, practices and artistic skills that risk disappearing, linking collections with education, documentation and cultural transmission. Music, dance, puppetry and craftsmanship are therefore treated as interconnected heritage rather than separate disciplines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koresegou.org/en/patrimony/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koresegou.org">[Kôrè Cultural Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koresegou.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kôrè Cultural Center Patrimony &#124; Kôrè Cultural Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kôrè Cultural Center Patrimony &#124; Kôrè Cultural Center</span></span></span>

This approach mirrors wider international recognition of Mali's performance traditions. UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity includes the "Coming forth of the masks and puppets in Markala", a ritual celebration practised by Bambara, Bozo, Marka and Somono communities. The inscription highlights that the significance lies not simply in the masks themselves but in the performances, initiation practices, songs, dances and community relationships surrounding them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/coming-forth-of-the-masks-and-puppets-in-markala-01004?RL=01004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intangible Cultural Heritage Coming forth of the masks and puppets in Markala</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageComing forth of the masks and puppets in Markala - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in unusual collections, this distinction is important. The museum is not preserving mysterious creatures because they are strange-looking. It is preserving evidence of a performance culture in which carved animals, fantastic hybrids and comic characters became meaningful only through coordinated movement, music and collective memory. That makes Mali's puppet traditions unusual not because of what the figures are, but because of how they were designed to come alive.
