## Introduction

Bijagós masks are among the most visually striking ritual objects associated with [Guinea-Bissau]({{ 'where-guinea-bissau-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}), yet treating them simply as museum pieces misses their original purpose. Many visitors first encounter these dramatic animal masks in ethnographic museums or international collections, where they are labelled as examples of African art. Within Bijagó society, however, they have traditionally been embedded in systems of initiation, authority, age grades and relationships with spiritual forces. Their meaning comes not from their appearance alone but from the ceremonies, restrictions and community responsibilities surrounding them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1960-18-1-a-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

## Are Bijagós Masks Art, Ritual Objects or Both?

The simplest answer is that they are both—but only if "art" is understood in its original social setting. The masks are carefully carved, painted and designed to evoke powerful animals, yet they were not traditionally created for galleries or collectors. They existed to be worn, danced, witnessed and interpreted within specific ceremonial events.

Many surviving examples depict animals rather than human faces. Oxen are especially well known, but museum collections also include masks representing creatures such as sawfish and other powerful animals. These choices reflect qualities admired or feared within Bijagó society rather than a desire for naturalistic portraiture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/objects/148087" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brooklynmuseum.org">[Brooklyn Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brooklynmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Brooklyn Museum Ox Mask (Dugn&#x27;be) · Brooklyn Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brooklyn Museum Ox Mask (Dugn&#x27;be) · Brooklyn Museum</span></span></span>

When museums display only the wooden mask itself, much of the original performance disappears. The complete costume often included fibre coverings, attachments, movement, music and dance. The object in a display case therefore represents only one part of a much larger ceremonial performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1960-18-1-a-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

## Animal Forms and Initiation Ceremonies

Animal imagery is central to understanding Bijagós masks. Rather than representing decorative wildlife, the animals express qualities that initiates are expected to confront, control or acquire during their passage into new stages of community life.

One of the best documented examples is the *dugn'be*, usually translated as the village ox. Although cattle arrived in the islands through historical contact with Portuguese traders, the ox became incorporated into local ritual symbolism. During initiation ceremonies, young men wearing ox masks imitate the animal's strength, aggression and controlled power while moving through recognised stages of social development. Museum catalogues frequently note that different bovine masks correspond to different phases of initiation rather than representing interchangeable designs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.maskmuseum.org/mask/bidjogo-dugnbe-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maskmuseum.org">[maskmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maskmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mask Museum Bidjogo Dugn-be – Second Face</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mask Museum Bidjogo Dugn-be – Second Face</span></span></span>

Scholars also note an important distinction between domesticated and dangerous animals. Some masks represent creatures associated with particular forms of strength or authority, while others express more unpredictable powers that initiates must learn to master. The emphasis is therefore educational as well as spiritual: the mask helps communicate social expectations, not merely religious symbolism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.christies.com/en/lot/lot-5556847" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: christies.com">[Christie&#x27;s]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">christies.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Christie&#x27;s BIDJOGO MASK, BISSAGOS ISLANDS, GUINEA BISSAU, KAISSI &#124; Christie&#x27;s</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Christie&#x27;s BIDJOGO MASK, BISSAGOS ISLANDS, GUINEA BISSAU, KAISSI &#124; Christie&#x27;s</span></span></span>

Unlike societies where specialist workshops dominate mask production, ethnographic accounts describe many Bijagó masks as being made within family or community settings, reinforcing that they belong to social life rather than an independent commercial art market.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.maskmuseum.org/mask/bidjogo-dugnbe-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maskmuseum.org">[Mask Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maskmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mask Museum Bidjogo Dugn-be – Second Face</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mask Museum Bidjogo Dugn-be – Second Face</span></span></span>

## Sacred Power Beyond the Museum Label

Museum labels often identify these objects simply as "mask" or "ritual equipment". While technically correct, such descriptions can flatten ideas that remain central in Bijagó religious life.

Many ritual objects in the Bijagós Islands are understood as places where spiritual power is present or activated rather than as symbolic representations alone. British Museum documentation discussing Bijagó ritual equipment explains that carvings, drums, stools and other objects may be associated with powerful spiritual forces, sometimes described through the concept of *iran*. Decorative materials such as horns, bells, shells and red cloth are therefore not merely ornamental but contribute to an object's ritual significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1979" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumartefact; religious/ritual equipment &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumartefact; religious/ritual equipment &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

This helps explain why removing a mask from its ceremonial environment changes its meaning. Inside a museum it becomes an artefact to be classified, conserved and interpreted. Within its original community it could be part of an active relationship involving ancestors, initiation, authority and ritual obligations.

For this reason, modern curators increasingly avoid presenting Bijagós masks simply as masterpieces of carving. Exhibition texts now more commonly explain who could use them, when ceremonies occurred and why performance, costume and community participation mattered as much as the carved wood itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1960-18-1-a-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

## Collecting, Consent and Objects Held Abroad

Large numbers of Bijagó ritual objects are now preserved outside Guinea-Bissau. Examples are held by institutions including the British Museum, the Smithsonian, the Brooklyn Museum and the AfricaMuseum in Belgium, while others entered museum collections through private collectors, auctions and donations during the twentieth century.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1960-18-1-a-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[britishmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

The history of acquisition varies considerably. Some objects were collected directly in the islands during fieldwork before being donated to museums. Others passed through art dealers or auction houses, making their original circumstances less transparent. Museum records increasingly document these acquisition histories because provenance has become an important part of responsible interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1960-18-1-a-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

Questions of consent are especially important for sacred material. Guinea-Bissau's own National Ethnographic Museum developed a reputation for negotiating with communities rather than simply removing objects considered spiritually important. Researchers involved in reconstructing the museum after the civil war have emphasised that some sacred items remained deliberately within local communities instead of being absorbed into national collections. That approach recognised that preserving living traditions sometimes meant leaving powerful ritual objects where they continued to hold cultural authority.

The debate also connects to wider discussions about African collections held abroad. Some museum pieces provide valuable opportunities for conservation and international research, while others raise questions about whether ceremonial objects can ever be fully understood once separated from the communities that gave them meaning. For Bijagós masks, the issue is not only ownership but whether an object designed for restricted ritual can ever be adequately represented by a museum label alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1960-18-1-a-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museummask &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
