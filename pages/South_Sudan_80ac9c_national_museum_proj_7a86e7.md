## Introduction

[South Sudan]({{ 'where-are-south-sudans-strangest-objects/' | relative_url }})'s most unusual museum story is not about an extraordinary building filled with rare artefacts. It is about an attempt to create a national museum without repeating the history of removing cultural heritage from the people who value it most. Rather than beginning with a permanent collection in Juba, South Sudan's museum planners and UNESCO launched a travelling exhibition that visited communities, asked how they wished to be represented, and collected stories alongside carefully chosen objects. This participatory approach reflected both practical realities in a country affected by conflict and a conscious effort to avoid the extractive collecting practices associated with colonial-era museums.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

The result is one of the most distinctive museum experiments in Africa: a national museum conceived first as a conversation rather than a building.

## Can a national museum be built differently?

Many countries inherited national museums from colonial administrations or established them soon after independence. South Sudan entered independence in 2011 without such an institution. The museums of the former Sudan were concentrated in Khartoum, while decades of war prevented the development of comparable facilities in the south. This unusual starting point allowed South Sudanese heritage professionals to ask a more fundamental question: what should a national museum actually be for?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/ssmn-juba-workshop-report-2018.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com">[southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Workshop report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Workshop report</span></span></span>

Instead of assuming that valuable objects should simply be transferred to the capital, planners proposed a museum whose collection would emerge through consultation. Communities would help determine which stories mattered, which objects could safely travel, and which should remain where they had cultural significance. The museum was intended to represent living relationships with heritage rather than simply accumulate possessions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

This approach also recognised that heritage in South Sudan extends well beyond physical artefacts. Oral histories, music, family memories, photographs, ritual knowledge and local traditions all contribute to cultural identity and can be documented without permanently removing culturally important possessions from their communities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

## Why a permanent museum in Juba has stalled

The absence of a conventional national museum is not simply the result of limited funding. Security problems, repeated humanitarian crises and displacement have repeatedly interrupted cultural projects, making it difficult to establish and maintain a permanent institution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

There are also deeper questions of trust and representation. South Sudan contains many ethnic communities spread across a vast territory. Concentrating heritage in Juba risks creating the impression that national culture belongs primarily to the capital or to whichever groups are most visible there. Museum workshops organised by the South Sudan Museum Network found that participants repeatedly questioned how every community could genuinely see itself reflected in a national institution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/ssmn-juba-workshop-report-2018.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com">[southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Workshop report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Workshop report</span></span></span>

The challenge therefore became more than constructing galleries. It involved designing a museum that people across the country would recognise as their own rather than as another distant state institution.

## How the travelling exhibition gathered stories

The pilot travelling exhibition, launched in 2014, functioned as a mobile museum and consultation project. Rather than waiting for visitors to come to Juba, museum teams travelled to communities in Eastern Equatoria, Western Equatoria and Western Bahr el Ghazal, where security conditions allowed the work to proceed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

Community members contributed:

UNESCO reported that the early pilot gathered around 81 objects, 55 audio recordings and 44 short videos while also encouraging public celebrations in many villages, where storytelling, music and dance became part of the museum-making process. The emphasis was not only on collecting material but on recording the meanings attached to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

For many elders, the project provided an opportunity to pass knowledge directly to younger generations while ensuring that these traditions would become part of the country's historical record. In this sense, the museum was preserving relationships and memories as much as physical collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

## The risks of repeating colonial collecting

The participatory model was shaped by an uncomfortable historical reality. Throughout the colonial period, thousands of objects from what is now South Sudan entered museums in Europe through military expeditions, missionaries, colonial officials and researchers. Many communities had little influence over how these collections were assembled or interpreted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com">[southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">South Sudan Museum Network</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">South Sudan Museum Network</span></span></span>

During discussions organised by the South Sudan Museum Network, heritage practitioners warned that a new national museum could unintentionally reproduce the same logic if it simply removed objects from villages and displayed them elsewhere. Workshop participants noted that some communities viewed collecting with suspicion and regarded the idea of transferring important heritage permanently to Juba as unfamiliar or even alien.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/ssmn-juba-workshop-report-2018.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com">[southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Workshop report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Workshop report</span></span></span>

The workshops also questioned traditional ethnographic display methods that classify people primarily by ethnic group. Participants instead discussed exhibition styles that organise objects by shared human experiences and functions, reducing the risk of reinforcing rigid colonial categories while helping visitors recognise common cultural practices across different communities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/ssmn-juba-workshop-report-2018.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com">[southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southsudanmuseumnetwork.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Workshop report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Workshop report</span></span></span>

These debates demonstrate that the museum project is not only about preserving artefacts. It is equally concerned with deciding who has authority to tell cultural stories and how those stories should be presented.

## What makes this approach unusual

Many museums consult communities after collections have already been assembled. South Sudan attempted to reverse that sequence by making consultation the foundation of the collection itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>

## Why it matters within South Sudan's heritage landscape

For travellers looking for an unusual museum experience, South Sudan's national museum project is remarkable precisely because it remains unfinished. Instead of presenting a completed collection behind glass, it highlights the difficult choices involved in preserving heritage in a young nation where memories, identities and ownership remain deeply significant.

The country's most distinctive museum experiment therefore lies not in spectacular objects but in an unusual philosophy: cultural heritage should not be safeguarded by separating it from the communities that created it unless those communities choose that path. Whether a permanent museum in Juba ultimately emerges from this process, the travelling exhibition has already demonstrated a different model of museum building—one based on dialogue, shared authority and respect for living cultural traditions rather than simply acquiring collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/communities-south-sudan-build-their-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">communities south sudan build their national museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Communities of South Sudan build their National Museum &#124; UNESCOJune 19, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2014</span></span></span>
