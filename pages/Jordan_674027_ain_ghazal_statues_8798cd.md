## Introduction

The Ain Ghazal statues are among [Jordan]({{ 'jordans-strange-collections-beyond/' | relative_url }})’s most extraordinary prehistoric discoveries: human figures made nearly 9,000 years ago, long before the pyramids, classical sculpture, or written history. Created by Neolithic communities near modern Amman, these fragile lime-plaster figures are some of the earliest known large-scale human representations and remain fascinating because archaeologists can study how they were made but cannot say with certainty what they meant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museums.visitjordan.com/en/Museum/5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museums.visitjordan.com">[museums.visitjordan.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museums.visitjordan.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Jordan Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Jordan Museum</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Jordan_674027_ain_ghazal_statues_8798cd-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Ain Ghazal illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Jordan_674027_ain_ghazal_statues_8798cd-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Jordan_674027_ain_ghazal_statues_8798cd-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Today, the statues are among the most unusual objects in Jordan’s heritage collections. Their strange, almost modern faces, oversized eyes and occasional double-headed forms make them feel surprisingly familiar yet deeply distant. They are not mysterious because they are fake or unexplained artefacts; their importance comes from a genuine archaeological puzzle: what kind of society created such ambitious images, and why were they carefully buried after being made?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mci.si.edu/ain-ghazal-statues" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mci.si.edu">[mci.si.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mci.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ain Ghazal Statues &#124; Museum Conservation Institute</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ain Ghazal Statues &#124; Museum Conservation Institute</span></span></span>

## The discovery of the Ain Ghazal figures

### A prehistoric village beneath modern Amman

The Ain Ghazal site was a large Neolithic settlement near Amman, occupied for more than two thousand years during a period when communities in the Levant were moving towards settled farming and village life. The site was first identified during road construction in the 1970s, with major archaeological discoveries following in the early 1980s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://acorjordan.org/ain-ghazal/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: acorjordan.org">[Acor Jordan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">acorjordan.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Acor Jordan‘Ain Ghazal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Acor Jordan...</span></span></span>

The most remarkable finds came from two underground caches discovered in 1983 and 1985. Archaeologists uncovered groups of lime-plaster statues and busts deliberately placed in pits. The finds included full human figures, smaller busts and unusual two-headed examples, creating one of the most important collections of prehistoric art ever found in the Near East.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mci.si.edu/ain-ghazal-statues" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mci.si.edu">[mci.si.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mci.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ain Ghazal Statues &#124; Museum Conservation Institute</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ain Ghazal Statues &#124; Museum Conservation Institute</span></span></span>

The discovery was especially significant because these were not simple decorative objects. The figures show a level of planning and craftsmanship that changed assumptions about early human creativity. They demonstrate that communities thousands of years before writing were already producing complex symbolic art on a large scale.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museums.visitjordan.com/en/Museum/5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museums.visitjordan.com">[museums.visitjordan.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museums.visitjordan.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Jordan Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Jordan Museum</span></span></span>

### How the statues were made

The Ain Ghazal figures were constructed from materials that seem simple but required considerable skill. Craftspeople created internal frameworks from bundles of reeds and other plant materials, then covered them with layers of lime plaster. Some features, especially the eyes, were carefully emphasised using materials such as bitumen, creating the intense expressions that make the figures memorable today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/506791" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.uchicago.edu">[Chicago Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.uchicago.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uchicago.edu.</span></span></span>

The construction process also reveals practical choices. Researchers studying the statues found that many were built in stages while lying horizontally, with parts such as legs attached separately before the final plaster covering was applied. Their flattened bodies and delicate structure suggest they were not intended as permanent outdoor monuments but as carefully made symbolic objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/506791" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.uchicago.edu">[Chicago Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.uchicago.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uchicago.edu.</span></span></span>

Their survival is remarkable because lime plaster is fragile. Conservation teams had to treat the figures as highly vulnerable archaeological remains, with some blocks of material being carefully removed and transported for specialist conservation work before the statues could be displayed safely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mci.si.edu/ain-ghazal-statues" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mci.si.edu">[mci.si.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mci.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ain Ghazal Statues &#124; Museum Conservation Institute</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ain Ghazal Statues &#124; Museum Conservation Institute</span></span></span>

## Why the statues remain mysterious

### The unanswered question: what were they for?

The greatest mystery of Ain Ghazal is not how the statues were created but why. Archaeologists have several possible explanations, but there is no single agreed answer.

One interpretation is that the figures had a ritual or symbolic role within Neolithic communities. Their careful burial suggests they may have had a special significance rather than being ordinary household objects. Some researchers have connected them with ideas about ancestors, identity or communal memory, although the evidence does not allow a precise reconstruction of the beliefs behind them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mci.si.edu/node/1288243" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mci.si.edu">[mci.si.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mci.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">40221 &#124; Museum Conservation Institute...</span></span></span>

Another important clue is that the statues appear to have been intentionally placed underground rather than simply discarded. Their condition suggests they may have been buried soon after creation, possibly as part of a practice whose meaning was understood by the people who made them but is now lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayn_Ghazal_statues" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ayn Ghazal statues</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ayn Ghazal statues</span></span></span>

This uncertainty is part of what makes the figures so compelling. Unlike later artworks with inscriptions, historical records or known religious contexts, the Ain Ghazal statues force modern viewers to interpret a world without written explanations.

### Faces that look surprisingly modern

The visual impact of the statues is one reason they have become icons of prehistoric art. Their large open eyes, carefully shaped faces and upright human forms create an unusual sense of presence. They do not look like rough attempts at human representation; instead, they show deliberate choices about how people should be portrayed.

The figures also challenge the idea that sophisticated artistic traditions appeared only with later civilisations. Thousands of years before ancient Egypt’s monumental sculpture, Neolithic communities in Jordan were already experimenting with human images, materials and symbolic expression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museums.visitjordan.com/en/Museum/5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museums.visitjordan.com">[museums.visitjordan.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museums.visitjordan.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Jordan Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Jordan Museum</span></span></span>

At the same time, visitors should be cautious about modern interpretations. The statues are sometimes described in popular culture as mysterious “ancient faces” or compared with later religious imagery, but there is no firm evidence that they represented gods, kings or specific named individuals. Their meaning remains an archaeological question rather than a solved mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mci.si.edu/node/1288243" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mci.si.edu">[mci.si.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mci.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">40221 &#124; Museum Conservation Institute...</span></span></span>

## How visitors encounter prehistoric Jordan

### Seeing Ain Ghazal in museums

For visitors interested in unusual collections in Jordan, the Ain Ghazal statues are among the clearest examples of archaeology becoming a curiosity display without relying on legend. They are genuine prehistoric objects whose strangeness comes from their age, appearance and unanswered purpose.

The figures are displayed through Jordan’s archaeological institutions, especially the The Jordan Museum, where they form part of a wider story of human history in the region. The museum presents the statues as among Jordan’s most important archaeological discoveries, alongside other major heritage objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museums.visitjordan.com/en/Museum/5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museums.visitjordan.com">[museums.visitjordan.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museums.visitjordan.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Jordan Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Jordan Museum</span></span></span>

### Why Ain Ghazal matters in Jordan’s strange-history landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The Ain Ghazal statues represent a different kind of mystery from legends, folklore creatures or disputed artefacts. Their fascination comes from the gap between what can be proven and what cannot be recovered. Archaeology can reveal their materials, age, construction and burial context, but the thoughts of the people who shaped those faces remain beyond reach.

</div>

That combination makes them especially powerful curiosities: real objects from the distant past that still invite questions. In Jordan’s landscape of unusual heritage, Ain Ghazal stands out because the mystery is not whether the figures existed, but what their makers wanted them to say.

      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
