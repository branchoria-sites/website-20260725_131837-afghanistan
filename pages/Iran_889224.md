## Iran’s strangest collections and where to find them

### The Saltmen of Zanjan: ancient bodies preserved by nature

Perhaps Iran’s most famous “mystery exhibit” is the collection of naturally mummified bodies known as the [Saltmen]({{ 'saltmen/' | relative_url }}), discovered in the Chehrabad salt mine near Zanjan. Unlike Egyptian mummies, these remains were not deliberately preserved through ritual preparation. The salt environment removed moisture and slowed decay, creating an extraordinary archaeological survival. The discoveries include several individuals from different historical periods, many believed to have died in mining accidents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nypost.com/2024/09/03/world-news/eerie-photos-show-the-freakishly-well-preserved-ancient-saltmen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nypost.com">[New York Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nypost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New York Post Meet the &#x27;Saltmen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These ancient Persian mine workers were preserved remarkably well due to the salt and lack of oxygen in the mine. The first was discovere...</span></span></span>

The most accessible place to see these remains is the Archaeology Museum of Zanjan, where salt-preserved human remains and related objects are displayed. Another Saltman specimen is held by the National Museum of Iran in Tehran. The exhibits are unusual because they are not simply “mummies” in the popular sense: they are archaeological records of working people, showing clothing, tools, injuries and aspects of ancient labour rather than royal burial traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.toiran.com/index.php/en/city-zanjan/museums/Archeology-Museum/7746" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: toiran.com">[ToIran, Tourism Platform]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">toiran.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">To Iran, Tourism Platform Archeology Museum &#124; To Iran, Tourism Platform</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">To Iran, Tourism Platform Archeology Museum &#124; To Iran, Tourism Platform</span></span></span>

The evidence points towards mining disasters and natural preservation, not supernatural causes. Their importance comes from what researchers can learn about ordinary lives in ancient Iran.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nypost.com/2024/09/03/world-news/eerie-photos-show-the-freakishly-well-preserved-ancient-saltmen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nypost.com">[New York Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nypost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New York Post Meet the &#x27;Saltmen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These ancient Persian mine workers were preserved remarkably well due to the salt and lack of oxygen in the mine. ### Doll museums: preserving forgotten lives through miniature worlds

Iran’s [doll collections]({{ 'doll-collections/' | relative_url }}) offer a different kind of curiosity: instead of ancient remains, they preserve social memory. The International Doll Museum in Tehran displays hundreds of dolls and puppets from Iran and other countries, including examples showing traditional clothing and cultural identities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.toiran.com/index.php/en/city-tehran/museums/International-Doll-Museum/1905" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: toiran.com">[ToIran, Tourism Platform]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">toiran.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">To Iran, Tourism Platform International Doll Museum &#124; To Iran, Tourism Platform</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">To Iran, Tourism Platform International Doll Museum &#124; To Iran, Tourism Platform</span></span></span>

Its setting in a historic house adds to the feeling of a private collector’s cabinet rather than a conventional museum hall.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visitiran.ir/attraction/Kashan--Puppet--and--Toy--Museum--%28Isfahan-Province%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitiran.ir">[Visit Iran]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitiran.ir</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visit Iran Kashan Puppet and Toy Museum (Isfahan Province) &#124; Visit iran</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit Iran Kashan Puppet and Toy Museum (Isfahan Province) &#124; Visit iran</span></span></span>

## Medical curiosities: Iran’s collections of bodies, healing and science

### The Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History

For visitors interested in the history of medicine, the Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History in Tehran provides one of the country’s most unusual collections. It brings together historic medical instruments, manuscripts, documents and objects connected with physicians, dentistry, nursing, veterinary medicine and traditional healing practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_National_Museum_of_Medical_Sciences_History" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History</span></span></span>

The collection is interesting because medicine often creates its own form of curiosity cabinet. Old surgical tools, anatomical materials and medical records show changing attitudes towards illness and the body. Rather than presenting medicine as a story of simple progress, such museums reveal periods when treatments, theories and technologies could look very different from modern expectations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_National_Museum_of_Medical_Sciences_History" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History</span></span></span>

Iran’s medical heritage is especially rich because Persian scholars and physicians played important roles in wider histories of science. Collections connected with medicine therefore sit between scientific history and cultural heritage, showing both practical knowledge and older systems of understanding health.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_National_Museum_of_Medical_Sciences_History" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Iranian National Museum of Medical Sciences History</span></span></span>

## Objects of belief: amulets, charms and mysterious artefacts

Iran’s museums and collections also preserve objects connected with protection, spirituality and personal belief. These include amulets, inscribed objects and items that were carried or worn for religious, protective or symbolic purposes. ## Why Iran matters in the world of unusual collections


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Iran’s curiosity culture is distinctive because its strange and memorable objects are rarely isolated from wider history. A preserved miner, a traditional doll, an old medical instrument or an inscribed amulet each connects to larger questions: how people worked, what they feared, how they treated illness, how they expressed identity and how societies remembered the past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.toiran.com/index.php/en/city-zanjan/museums/Archeology-Museum/7746" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: toiran.com">[toiran.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">toiran.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">To Iran, Tourism Platform Archeology Museum &#124; To Iran, Tourism Platform</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">To Iran, Tourism Platform Archeology Museum &#124; To Iran, Tourism Platform</span></span></span>

</div>

The country’s unusual collections are therefore not mainly about spectacle. Their appeal comes from the tension between the ordinary and the extraordinary: a worker frozen in salt, a handmade doll preserving a vanished occupation, or a small object carried for protection across generations. Iran’s strangest museum experiences often begin with something that looks unusual and end with a much broader story about human life.
