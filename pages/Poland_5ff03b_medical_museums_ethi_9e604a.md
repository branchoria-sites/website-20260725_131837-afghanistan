## Introduction

[Poland]({{ 'where-poland-keeps-its-strangest-stories/' | relative_url }})’s medical museums preserve far more than antique instruments and elegant pharmacy jars. Behind their displays lie the history of anatomy, pathology and medical education, including collections of real human remains and preserved organs that were assembled over centuries for teaching and research. These collections are among the country's most unusual museum holdings because they sit at the intersection of science, history and ethics. Visitors encounter specimens that once trained generations of doctors, but they also confront modern questions about consent, dignity, privacy and whether every historic teaching collection should remain on public view.

Unlike commercial exhibitions built around shock value, Poland's university medical museums generally present these materials as educational and historical evidence. Even so, attitudes have changed significantly. Institutions increasingly balance scientific value with respect for the people whose bodies became teaching specimens, and many limit access, prohibit photography or present sensitive material within guided educational contexts rather than as open tourist attractions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cm-uj.krakow.pl/museums/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cm-uj.krakow.pl">[krakow.pl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cm-uj.krakow.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jagiellonian University</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collegium MedicumMuseums - Jagiellonian University - Medical College...</span></span></span>

## From alchemy to professional pharmacy

Poland's pharmacy museums illustrate how medicine evolved from a mixture of herbal knowledge, alchemy and empirical observation into a regulated scientific profession. The collections are unusual not because they contain human remains but because they preserve the intellectual world in which earlier medicine operated.

The Pharmacy Museum of the Jagiellonian University in Kraków is one of the largest institutions of its kind in Poland. Visitors find historic laboratories, apothecary furniture, ceramic jars, scales, prescription books and remedies whose ingredients now seem extraordinary, ranging from minerals and animal products to imported botanical materials. The museum demonstrates how pharmacists gradually replaced inherited traditions and magical thinking with laboratory science and standardised medicines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cm-uj.krakow.pl/museums/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cm-uj.krakow.pl">[Jagiellonian University - Collegium Medicum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cm-uj.krakow.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jagiellonian University</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collegium MedicumMuseums - Jagiellonian University - Medical College...</span></span></span>

Seen alongside anatomy collections, pharmacy museums help explain why medical museums should not be judged solely by their most dramatic exhibits. The preserved specimens often attract attention, but they represent only one part of a much broader story about how physicians learned to diagnose disease, prepare medicines and understand the human body.

## Anatomy, pathology and former teaching collections

The most ethically challenging collections are found in university anatomy and pathology museums rather than public history museums.

At the Jagiellonian University Medical College in Kraków, the Museum of Anatomy occupies the nineteenth-century anatomical theatre. Its collections include preserved organs, skeletal material, injected blood-vessel preparations, embryological models and anatomical specimens created primarily for teaching medical students. Some preparations made by Professor Ludwik Teichmann remain internationally significant because of their technical quality and rarity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cm-uj.krakow.pl/museums/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cm-uj.krakow.pl">[Jagiellonian University - Collegium Medicum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cm-uj.krakow.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jagiellonian University</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collegium MedicumMuseums - Jagiellonian University - Medical College...</span></span></span>

Nearby, the Museum of Anatomopathology preserves pathological specimens collected since the early nineteenth century. Historically, the collection expanded through surgical practice and post-mortem examinations, eventually numbering thousands of preparations. Today it includes examples of diseases that modern medicine rarely encounters because vaccination, antibiotics and improved healthcare have transformed clinical practice. The collection therefore records the history of disease as much as the history of anatomy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cm-uj.krakow.pl/museums/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cm-uj.krakow.pl">[Jagiellonian University - Collegium Medicum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cm-uj.krakow.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jagiellonian University</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collegium MedicumMuseums - Jagiellonian University - Medical College...</span></span></span>

Warsaw's Medical University has taken a noticeably different approach in its Museum of the History of Medicine. Rather than centring displays on preserved human material, it combines historical instruments with multimedia anatomical presentations, educational demonstrations and selected anatomical collections designed for public education, particularly school groups. Historic dissecting tables and forensic material remain part of the interpretation, but modern digital displays reduce the need for constant reliance on real specimens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wum.edu.pl/en/node/16295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wum.edu.pl">[wum.edu.pl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wum.edu.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Discover the secrets of the human body &#124; Medical University of Warsaw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 17, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 17, 2021</span></span></span>

These collections were never assembled as cabinets of curiosity in the traditional sense. Their primary purpose was professional education. What makes them unusual today is that techniques once considered routine now prompt questions about how museums should care for, interpret and display human remains.

## Why access and display ethics matter

The central ethical question is no longer whether these collections have scientific value. Most historians of medicine agree that they do. Instead, debate focuses on how they should be displayed, interpreted and accessed.

Several principles increasingly shape museum practice:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **Respect before spectacle.** Human specimens are interpreted as former patients or teaching donors rather than curiosities. Institutions generally avoid theatrical presentation.
* **Educational purpose.** Displays are justified through medical history, anatomy or pathology rather than entertainment.
* **Restricted access where appropriate.** Many university collections require advance booking, guided visits or organised educational groups instead of unrestricted public admission.
* **Limits on photography.** Some museums prohibit photographing specimens to discourage sensational circulation of images outside their educational context.
* **Historical transparency.** Curators increasingly explain when collections were assembled and acknowledge that documentation about individual consent is often incomplete for nineteenth- and early twentieth-century specimens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muzeauczelniane.pl/en/museum-of-the-department-of-anatomy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muzeauczelniane.pl">[Association of University Museums]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muzeauczelniane.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on muzeauczelniane.pl.</span></span></span>

</div>

The issue of consent is especially difficult. Modern anatomical donation programmes rely on explicit informed consent, but many historical teaching collections were created before such standards existed. Some specimens originated from hospital autopsies or surgical practice under legal and ethical frameworks very different from those expected today. Museums cannot rewrite that history, but they can acknowledge it openly instead of presenting collections as ethically uncomplicated.

## How Polish museums balance history and dignity

Polish university museums generally favour a restrained style of interpretation compared with commercial anatomy exhibitions seen elsewhere in the world.

Visitors are encouraged to understand specimens as historical evidence of medical practice rather than as shocking objects. Labels frequently explain diseases, anatomical techniques or the development of surgery instead of dwelling on rarity alone. The atmosphere is typically closer to an academic department than a curiosity attraction.

The educational context also affects who visits. Many anatomy museums primarily serve medical students, biology classes and researchers, opening to the wider public only through organised visits or special events such as museum nights. This reinforces the idea that preserved human material remains part of a continuing educational mission rather than simply a tourist attraction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cm-uj.krakow.pl/museums/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cm-uj.krakow.pl">[krakow.pl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cm-uj.krakow.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jagiellonian University</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Collegium MedicumMuseums - Jagiellonian University - Medical College...</span></span></span>

## What these collections reveal about Poland's curiosity culture

Within Poland's wider landscape of unusual collections, medical museums occupy a distinctive position. They are neither folklore attractions nor macabre entertainment. Instead, they preserve evidence of changing scientific knowledge alongside changing moral standards.

A nineteenth-century anatomical specimen can simultaneously be a teaching tool, a historical document, an example of remarkable preservation techniques and the remains of a real person. Recognising all of those identities at once is the essence of contemporary museum ethics.

This balance between scientific value and human dignity is what makes Poland's medical museums especially thought-provoking. Their most memorable exhibits are not merely unusual objects but reminders that every advance in medical knowledge has depended upon real human lives, and that modern museums increasingly see their responsibility as explaining both the science and the ethical questions that accompany it.

      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
