## Introduction

The masks of Binche are unusual because they belong to a collection that is still in active use rather than to a vanished tradition. At MUMASK, the International Carnival and Mask Museum in Binche, visitors encounter costumes, wax masks, feather hats, photographs, recordings and workshop knowledge that remain part of an annual civic ritual rather than a reconstructed historical spectacle. The museum's role is therefore different from that of a conventional folklore museum: it preserves evidence of a living tradition while recognising that its most important exhibits only become fully meaningful when they return to the streets during the Carnival of Binche. This approach reflects why the carnival was recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage: the knowledge, craftsmanship and social rules are as important as the physical objects themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mumask.be/en/carnival-in-binche/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mumask.be">[Mumask]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mumask.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Carnival in Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carnival in Binche - Mumask...</span></span></span>

## What the Gilles wear and why it matters

The best-known figure in the carnival is the Gille, whose appearance is governed by strict local custom rather than personal creativity. Every element of the costume carries practical and symbolic weight. The red, yellow and black outfit is padded with straw to create its distinctive shape, wooden clogs produce the characteristic rhythm on cobbled streets, copper bells add sound as the wearer walks, and the famous wax mask is worn only during part of Shrove Tuesday before being replaced by the towering ostrich-feather hat for the afternoon procession.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.binche.be/en/things-to-do/carnivals/carnival-of-binche/the-carnival/participants/the-gille" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: binche.be">[Binche]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">binche.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Gille — Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Gille — Binche - English...</span></span></span>

The museum's collection demonstrates that these are not theatrical props assembled for tourists. Many pieces are made by specialist craftspeople whose skills continue to be passed between generations. Costumes are traditionally rented from a small number of established costume makers rather than owned outright, while the distinctive wax masks remain tightly controlled. The City of Binche registered the mask's design, and authentic examples are reserved for participants in the carnival, reinforcing the idea that the object belongs to a living community rather than an open commercial market.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.binche.be/en/things-to-do/carnivals/carnival-of-binche/the-carnival/participants/the-gille" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: binche.be">[Binche]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">binche.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Gille — Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Gille — Binche - English...</span></span></span>

Just as significant are the rules governing who may wear the costume. Participation is limited by longstanding local customs, and the Gille costume may only be worn during the carnival itself. A well-known saying captures the principle: "a Gille never leaves", reflecting the rule that the costume does not leave the town. These restrictions are central to understanding why the museum treats the costume as evidence of a continuing social system rather than simply an attractive historical artefact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.binche.be/en/things-to-do/carnivals/carnival-of-binche/the-carnival/participants/the-gille" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: binche.be">[Binche]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">binche.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Gille — Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Gille — Binche - English...</span></span></span>

## How carnival rules shape the museum collection

Unlike many folklore museums that preserve traditions after they have faded, MUMASK actively documents a celebration that continues to evolve. Its Binche Carnival Interpretation Centre brings together more than 2,000 objects, alongside photographs, film, oral testimony and audio recordings that explain not only the famous Gilles but also other recognised carnival figures such as the Peasant, Pierrot, Sailor and Harlequin. The collection continues to grow as new material is documented and researched, reflecting changes within the tradition instead of freezing it at a single historical moment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mumask.be/en/carnival-in-binche/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mumask.be">[Mumask]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mumask.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Carnival in Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carnival in Binche - Mumask...</span></span></span>

This curatorial approach highlights an important distinction between tangible and intangible heritage. The museum can preserve masks, hats and costumes indefinitely, but it also records the annual calendar, dressing rituals, music, dance, family participation and local organisation that give those objects meaning. UNESCO's recognition specifically emphasises the preservation of craftsmanship, music, costume making and community knowledge, illustrating that the carnival survives because people continue to perform it rather than because museums display it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/carnival-of-binche-00033?RL=00033" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICH UNESCOCarnival of Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

Visitors therefore encounter two overlapping collections. One consists of physical objects displayed in galleries. The other is effectively the town itself during carnival season, where those same traditions are activated through rehearsal, performance and inherited local practice. The museum serves as an interpretation centre between these two worlds rather than as a substitute for the event.

## The risks of displaying masks without their communities

Binche also illustrates a challenge faced by museums that collect living traditions. A mask displayed in a glass case can easily appear mysterious or anonymous, encouraging visitors to interpret it as an isolated work of folk art. In reality, the Binche mask only functions within an intricate network of local rules, family participation, music, costume rental, craftsmanship and civic identity.

MUMASK deliberately addresses this problem by placing the Binche collections alongside documentation of performances, interviews and historical development instead of presenting masks as detached curiosities. The emphasis shifts from asking "What does this mask look like?" to "Who is permitted to wear it, when, why and according to whose traditions?"<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mumask.be/en/carnival-in-binche/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mumask.be">[Mumask]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mumask.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Carnival in Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carnival in Binche - Mumask...</span></span></span>

The museum also avoids suggesting that the Gilles alone define the carnival. Recent interpretation increasingly explains the wider cast of carnival characters and acknowledges the broader community that sustains the event, including costume makers, musicians, organisers and families. This reflects a wider understanding of heritage as a collective practice rather than a display of iconic objects alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mumask.be/en/carnival-in-binche/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mumask.be">[Mumask]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mumask.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Carnival in Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carnival in Binche - Mumask...</span></span></span>

## Why Binche matters in Belgium's curiosity landscape

Among [Belgium]({{ 'why-belgium-keeps-its-strangest-objects/' | relative_url }})'s unusual collections, Binche occupies a distinctive position because its central objects have never become purely historical. Many curiosity collections preserve extinct beliefs, abandoned rituals or obsolete technologies. MUMASK instead documents an annual tradition that still defines local identity and continues to require specialist craftsmanship, financial commitment and strict adherence to inherited customs.

For visitors interested in Belgium's stranger cultural heritage, this makes Binche unusual in a different way from museums devoted to witchcraft, medical oddities or cabinets of curiosity. Its fascination lies not in bizarre artefacts alone but in watching museum objects repeatedly return to public life. The wax mask, feather hat and straw-filled costume remain significant precisely because they are still embedded in a community that insists they belong first to the carnival and only second to the museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mumask.be/en/carnival-in-binche/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mumask.be">[mumask.be]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mumask.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Carnival in Binche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carnival in Binche - Mumask...</span></span></span>

    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
