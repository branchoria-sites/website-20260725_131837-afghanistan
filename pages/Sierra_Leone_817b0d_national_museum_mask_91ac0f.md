## Introduction

Visitors often arrive at [Sierra Leone]({{ 'where-sierra-leone-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s National Museum expecting to see mysterious ritual masks linked to secret societies. They leave with a more complicated picture. The museum's mask collection shows that these objects are not simply symbols of hidden ceremonies or supernatural belief. Instead, they express ideas about leadership, beauty, moral authority, humour, education and community identity. Once removed from ceremonies and displayed in museum cases, they become historical artefacts as well as works of art, raising important questions about how much of their original meaning can ever be publicly explained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/OAWBeYEzXLjMCw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arts &amp; Culture Sierra Leone&#x27;s National Treasure — Google Arts &amp; Culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; CultureSierra Leone&#x27;s National Treasure — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

## Sande and Bondo helmet masks

The museum's best-known objects are the carved wooden helmet masks worn by senior women during Sande society ceremonies. Unlike many African masks that cover only the face, these fit over the entire head and were originally worn with elaborate raffia costumes that concealed the performer's body. Displayed alone in a museum, they appear to be sculptures, but in their original setting they formed only one part of a complete performance involving music, movement and carefully controlled appearances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1960.26.131/sowei-mask" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

Rather than representing anonymous spirits or frightening supernatural beings, these masks embody an idealised public image of womanhood and authority. Their polished dark surfaces, calm expressions, carefully arranged hairstyles and distinctive rings around the neck communicate dignity, self-control and maturity. Museum interpretation emphasises that the mask represents the authority of the dancing *ndoli jowei*—a respected figure within the Sande tradition—rather than serving as a simple object of worship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/SQXRx0nWO3Rrrg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; Culture10 Highlights from Sierra Leone National Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

The collection also reminds visitors that museum labels can oversimplify. One striking object long catalogued as a Sowei mask is now thought to have been created primarily as a prestigious gift or demonstration of artistic skill rather than as a wearable ceremonial mask. Although it borrows the familiar helmet-mask form, the carving develops into a sculptural female figure with another figure riding on her back, making normal use impossible. The object was presented to the museum by Sir Milton Margai, Sierra Leone's first Prime Minister after independence, illustrating how museum collections preserve not only ritual objects but also gifts, political history and changing interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1958.04.07/sowei-mask" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

## Masquerade, secrecy and public display

One of the museum's greatest challenges is explaining traditions that deliberately limit what may be publicly discussed. Initiation societies preserve knowledge that is intended for members, meaning museums must balance education with cultural respect. As a result, displays generally explain visible artistic features and historical context rather than revealing restricted ceremonial knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/OAWBeYEzXLjMCw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arts &amp; Culture Sierra Leone&#x27;s National Treasure — Google Arts &amp; Culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; CultureSierra Leone&#x27;s National Treasure — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

This changes the way visitors experience the masks. In ceremonies, meaning emerges through movement, costume, music and timing. A helmet mask sitting motionless inside a display cabinet cannot reproduce that experience. The museum therefore encourages visitors to understand the objects as parts of living performance traditions rather than isolated carvings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/SQXRx0nWO3Rrrg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; Culture10 Highlights from Sierra Leone National Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

The distinction also helps correct a common misunderstanding. International media have often portrayed West African initiation societies primarily through secrecy, leading outsiders to assume the masks exist chiefly for occult purposes. The museum instead places them within broader systems of education, community organisation and social responsibility. Their importance lies as much in governance and public authority as in ritual symbolism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/SQXRx0nWO3Rrrg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; Culture10 Highlights from Sierra Leone National Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

## Humour, authority and mistaken labels

Not every museum mask represents solemn ceremony. Sierra Leone's masquerade traditions include characters whose performances entertain audiences, mock poor behaviour or reinforce community values through comedy. Humour has long been one of the ways masquerades communicate authority. A laughing crowd can learn social lessons as effectively as a formal speech, and some masquerade figures deliberately exaggerate behaviour to make those lessons memorable. Although the museum cannot recreate these performances, its collections remind visitors that masks could amuse as well as command respect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/SQXRx0nWO3Rrrg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; Culture10 Highlights from Sierra Leone National Museum — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

The museum also demonstrates how older catalogues sometimes attached misleading labels to unfamiliar objects. Colonial administrators, early collectors and even museum staff occasionally classified carvings according to incomplete information, especially when dealing with traditions whose knowledge was intentionally restricted. Modern cataloguing projects have re-examined many objects, comparing accession records with specialist research and community knowledge rather than accepting early descriptions at face value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1958.04.07/sowei-mask" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

This process is especially important because similar-looking carvings may have had very different purposes. Some were ceremonial objects, others were prestige gifts, teaching examples, diplomatic presents or later works made for collectors. Recognising these differences prevents every carved face from being interpreted as evidence of mysterious ritual, producing a more accurate and more respectful understanding of Sierra Leone's artistic heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1958.04.07/sowei-mask" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

## Why the masks matter today


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The National Museum's mask collection remains one of the country's most significant cultural resources because it preserves objects that connect artistic excellence with living traditions. Many visitors first notice the remarkable craftsmanship, but the deeper lesson concerns meaning rather than appearance. A carved face is only one element in a larger cultural system involving performance, social authority, education and memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/OAWBeYEzXLjMCw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arts &amp; Culture Sierra Leone&#x27;s National Treasure — Google Arts &amp; Culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Arts &amp; CultureSierra Leone&#x27;s National Treasure — Google Arts &amp; Culture...</span></span></span>

</div>

Seen through that lens, the museum's masks are not relics of superstition or exotic curiosities. They are carefully curated reminders that objects can carry multiple meanings at once: works of sculpture, symbols of governance, expressions of cultural identity, records of colonial collecting and evidence of how museums continue to reinterpret the past as new research emerges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1958.04.07/sowei-mask" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[Sierra Leone Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sierraleoneheritage.org.</span></span></span>

      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
