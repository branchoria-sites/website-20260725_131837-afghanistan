## Introduction

The Stone of Possession is one of the smallest yet most consequential historical objects in [Seychelles]({{ 'what-makes-seychelles-collections-so/' | relative_url }}). Carved with the royal symbols of France and placed on Mahé on 1 November 1756, it represented a formal claim that transformed an uninhabited archipelago into a territory of the French Crown. Today the original stone is preserved in the National Museum of History in Victoria, where it is displayed not as a mysterious relic but as the physical reminder of a legal ceremony whose consequences shaped settlement, slavery and the creation of modern Seychelles. Although modest in size, it remains one of the country's most significant historical artefacts because it marks the moment when imperial ambition was translated into a tangible object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://seychellesnationalmuseums.org/the-history-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: seychellesnationalmuseums.org">[seychellesnationalmuseums.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">seychellesnationalmuseums.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on seychellesnationalmuseums.org.</span></span></span>

## How Could One Stone Claim Seychelles?

In the eighteenth century, European empires often asserted ownership of newly claimed territory through carefully staged ceremonies. A commissioned officer would land, raise a flag, read a declaration and leave behind a permanent marker carrying the sovereign's symbols. Such acts did not create control in any practical sense, but they served as political statements intended to discourage rival powers and establish a legal basis for future occupation.

That was the purpose of the Stone of Possession in Seychelles. Captain Corneille Nicholas Morphey, commanding the French East India Company ship *Le Cerf*, landed on Mahé in November 1756 and formally claimed the islands in the name of King Louis XV and the French East India Company. The carved stone was set on a rocky prominence overlooking what is now Victoria, recording France's claim and giving the islands the name "Séchelles" in honour of Jean Moreau de Séchelles, the French Controller-General of Finance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nation.sc/articles/6677/the-stone-of-possession" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nation.sc">[nation.sc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nation.sc</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Seychelles Nation The Stone of Possession -Seychelles Nation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seychelles Nation The Stone of Possession -Seychelles Nation</span></span></span>

The ceremony did not immediately establish a colony. No permanent settlement followed for another fourteen years. Nevertheless, in the diplomatic language of eighteenth-century Europe, the ceremony announced French intentions before another colonial power could do the same.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdc.unk83018617/?q=seychelles&amp;sp=45&amp;st=text" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressImage 45 of Unpublished documents on the history of the Seychelles Islands anterior to 1810 &#124; Library of Congress...</span></span></span>

## The French Ceremony of Possession

The act of possession was highly symbolic rather than practical. Morphey's expedition explored the islands, selected a suitable anchorage and completed an official record of the ceremony before departing. Contemporary documents describe the expedition as acting under orders from the French authorities on Isle de France (modern Mauritius), demonstrating that the ceremony formed part of a wider strategy to strengthen French influence across the western Indian Ocean.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/divers14-11/02901.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: horizon.documentation.ird.fr">[Horizon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">horizon.documentation.ird.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Horizon Histoire des Seychelles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Histoire des SeychellesNovember 18, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 2014</span></span></span>

The stone itself became the enduring witness to that ceremony. Rather than preserving a written proclamation alone, French officials left behind a carved object that future visitors could recognise as evidence of an existing claim. Similar possession markers appeared elsewhere in European colonial expansion, but relatively few survive in their original form. That survival gives the Seychelles example unusual historical importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nation.sc/articles/6677/the-stone-of-possession" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nation.sc">[Seychelles Nation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nation.sc</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Seychelles Nation The Stone of Possession -Seychelles Nation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seychelles Nation The Stone of Possession -Seychelles Nation</span></span></span>

## What the Carved Symbols Represented

The Stone of Possession measures roughly 57 centimetres square and bears the royal fleur-de-lis together with the inscription identifying the islands as "Isle de Sechelles". These were not decorative carvings. Every element communicated political authority.

The fleur-de-lis represented the French monarchy and announced that the territory belonged to the Crown. Naming the islands after Jean Moreau de Séchelles linked the new claim directly to influential figures within the French government. Together, the carvings transformed an ordinary block of stone into a public legal statement according to European imperial practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nation.sc/articles/19946/replica-of-seychelles-historic-stone-of-possession-unveiled" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nation.sc">[Seychelles Nation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nation.sc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seychelles NationReplica of Seychelles&#x27; historic Stone of Possession unveiled -Seychelles NationNovember 2, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2023</span></span></span>

Modern visitors often expect a dramatic monument but instead encounter a surprisingly compact artefact. Its modest appearance highlights an important historical reality: colonial claims often depended less on the physical size of an object than on the political system that recognised its symbolism.

## Settlement, Slavery and the Stone's Legacy

The Stone of Possession did not itself create a settlement, but it established the legal framework that allowed one to follow.

In 1770, the first permanent French settlers arrived on Sainte Anne Island accompanied by enslaved Africans, beginning the permanent human occupation of Seychelles. Plantation agriculture expanded during the following decades, relying on enslaved labour imported from Africa and Madagascar. The archipelago's distinctive Creole society ultimately developed from this colonial settlement and the experiences of enslaved and later emancipated people rather than from the symbolic ceremony alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://seychellesnationalmuseums.org/special-events/250-years-anniversary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: seychellesnationalmuseums.org">[Seychelles National Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">seychellesnationalmuseums.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Seychelles National Museum250 Years Anniversary – Seychelles National Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 4, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 4, 2020</span></span></span>

Historians therefore treat the stone as more than an isolated curiosity. It represents the legal moment that preceded profound demographic, economic and social change. French colonial law—including the *Code Noir*, which regulated slavery in French colonies—became the governing framework once settlement began. Although the stone itself caused none of these developments directly, it marked the beginning of the political authority under which they occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nation.sc/archive/238898/the-judicial-history-of-seychelles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nation.sc">[Seychelles Nation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nation.sc</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Seychelles Nation The Judicial History of Seychelles -Archive -Seychelles Nation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seychelles NationThe Judicial History of Seychelles -Archive -Seychelles NationJune 17, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2013</span></span></span>

This makes the artefact significant for understanding both colonial expansion and its human consequences. Rather than celebrating imperial achievement, many modern interpretations present it as the starting point of a history that includes forced labour, cultural mixing, resistance and the eventual emergence of the Seychellois Creole nation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nation.sc/articles/7073/exhibition-showcases-history-of-slavery-in-seychelles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nation.sc">[nation.sc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nation.sc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nation.sc.</span></span></span>

## Why It Matters as a Curiosity Today


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Among Seychelles' unusual historical objects, the Stone of Possession stands apart because its significance comes almost entirely from meaning rather than appearance. Unlike archaeological treasures or elaborate royal regalia, it is an ordinary stone invested with extraordinary political symbolism.

</div>

Its survival allows visitors to connect a ceremonial act from 1756 with the later realities of colonisation, settlement and slavery. As one of the oldest surviving historical artefacts associated directly with the founding of colonial Seychelles, it occupies a central place in the National Museum of History's collections and remains one of the country's most important heritage objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://seychellesnationalmuseums.org/the-history-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: seychellesnationalmuseums.org">[Seychelles National Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">seychellesnationalmuseums.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on seychellesnationalmuseums.org.</span></span></span>

The stone also illustrates how museums can transform an object's meaning over time. What was originally intended as an unmistakable declaration of imperial authority is now displayed as evidence for critical reflection on colonial history.   function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
</section>
