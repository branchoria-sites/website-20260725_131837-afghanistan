## Introduction

The masks and featherwork at Museo del Barro in Asunción are among [Paraguay]({{ 'where-paraguay-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s most visually striking museum objects, yet the institution deliberately resists presenting them as exotic curiosities. Instead, they are displayed as works of art created within living Indigenous traditions, where masks, feathers and body ornaments are inseparable from ceremony, performance, social identity and spiritual relationships. This approach makes the museum unusual within South America's museum landscape: rather than separating Indigenous material into an ethnographic display and modern painting into an art museum, Museo del Barro presents Indigenous, popular and contemporary art as equally important expressions of Paraguayan culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_museo_del_barro_mask_283768-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Masks and Feathers illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_museo_del_barro_mask_283768-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_museo_del_barro_mask_283768-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

For visitors interested in Paraguay's stranger cultural heritage, the collection demonstrates that some of the country's most remarkable objects are not mysterious because they hide supernatural secrets, but because they embody ways of seeing the world that cannot be understood by appearance alone. A feathered ceremonial costume may look spectacular in a gallery, yet its deeper meaning lies in ritual, community and performance rather than spectacle.

## How Museo del Barro changes the way visitors see Indigenous art

Museo del Barro combines three major collections: Indigenous art, popular or rural art, and modern and contemporary art. This curatorial decision reflects decades of work by curator and cultural critic Ticio Escobar and colleagues, who argued that Indigenous artistic production should be treated as art in its own right rather than as anthropological evidence or folkloric decoration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

That philosophy shapes how masks and featherwork are interpreted.

Instead of presenting them as relics from vanished cultures or as anonymous "tribal" objects, the museum places emphasis on:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* artistic skill alongside cultural function;
* the continuing creativity of Indigenous communities;
* relationships between ritual, craftsmanship and aesthetics;
* documented provenance rather than anonymous collecting wherever possible.

</div>

The result is a museum experience that feels different from many nineteenth- and early twentieth-century ethnographic museums, where ceremonial objects were often displayed primarily as scientific specimens or evidence of supposedly "primitive" societies.

## Masks, ornaments and the transformation of the human figure

The museum's Indigenous Art collection contains more than 1,750 objects produced by members of numerous Indigenous peoples across Paraguay. Alongside pottery, basketry and textiles are ceremonial masks, feather ornaments and ritual costumes that reveal how the human body itself becomes part of artistic expression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

One of the collection's best-known groups consists of five ceremonial costumes created by the Ishir people of Alto Paraguay for the annual Debylyby ritual. These ensembles combine woven materials, masks made from caraguatá fibre and elaborate feather decorations from various Chaco bird species. They were created for performance rather than permanent display, meaning that movement, dance and ritual action originally completed the artwork.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

Seen behind museum glass, the costumes can appear theatrical or even uncanny. Large masks conceal individual identity, while feathers dramatically enlarge the human silhouette and alter movement. Yet this visual transformation is not intended simply to entertain. Within ceremony, changing the outward appearance of the participant helps express relationships between people, ancestors, animals and spiritual forces that cannot be conveyed through ordinary dress alone. The museum therefore encourages viewers to understand these objects as part of living ceremonial systems rather than isolated sculptures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

## Why feathers carry meaning beyond decoration

To many visitors, feathers first register as colourful ornament. Within the museum's interpretation, however, featherwork is presented as a sophisticated artistic medium with cultural, ecological and symbolic importance.

Feathers are carefully selected, arranged and combined with fibres, textiles and other materials to create objects whose significance depends on more than visual beauty. Different bird species, colours and construction techniques may relate to particular ceremonies, social roles or community traditions. Because many ceremonial garments were made from organic materials intended for ritual use rather than centuries of preservation, surviving examples are especially valuable records of artistic practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

This emphasis helps visitors avoid a common misunderstanding. The remarkable appearance of featherwork is not evidence that Indigenous makers were primarily interested in spectacle. Rather, visual impact serves ritual communication, identity and performance.

## Provenance, named collectors and responsible interpretation

One reason the collection has attracted scholarly attention is that the museum openly explains how it was assembled.

According to the museum, more than ninety per cent of the Indigenous collection was acquired by Ticio Escobar through purchases made with his own resources in Indigenous communities, craft markets and private collections. Additional works entered through donations from anthropologists, missionaries and other contributors, whose names are documented by the museum rather than hidden behind vague institutional language.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

This transparency matters because museums holding Indigenous ceremonial objects increasingly face questions about collecting history, consent and cultural ownership. While Museo del Barro acknowledges the role of individual collectors, it also frames the collection within continuing relationships with Indigenous communities rather than presenting it as a closed historical archive.

The museum has also produced an extensive digital catalogue for researchers, making much of the collection accessible beyond the gallery itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

## The difference between ceremonial objects and tourist souvenirs

Museo del Barro offers an important corrective to a misunderstanding that affects many travellers. Across Latin America, masks and feathered ornaments are frequently reproduced as souvenirs, festival decorations or generic symbols of "Indigenous culture". Once removed from their original setting, they can easily become detached from the ceremonies that gave them meaning.

The museum deliberately moves in the opposite direction. Labels, catalogues and gallery organisation encourage visitors to see each object within its artistic, cultural and historical context rather than as an anonymous curiosity. Even visually dramatic masks are interpreted through their makers, communities and ritual uses instead of being marketed as mysterious relics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museodelbarro.org/colecciones/museo-de-arte-indigena" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museodelbarro.org">[Museo del Barro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museodelbarro.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo del Barro Museo de Arte Indígena</span></span></span>

This approach distinguishes the museum from attractions built around spectacle. The visual impact remains powerful, but interpretation consistently redirects attention from exotic appearance to cultural meaning.

    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
