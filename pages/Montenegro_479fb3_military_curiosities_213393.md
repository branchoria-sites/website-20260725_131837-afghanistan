## Introduction

[Montenegro]({{ 'where-montenegro-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s most unusual military attractions are not collections of weapons behind glass but two enormous objects that visitors can physically experience. In Tivat, the former Yugoslav Navy submarine **P-821 Heroj** allows visitors to step inside a genuine Cold War patrol vessel and see how its crew lived and worked beneath the Adriatic. In Cetinje, a vast three-dimensional relief map of Montenegro, built during the Austro-Hungarian occupation in the First World War, transforms the country's mountains into a walk-around military planning tool. Together, these exhibits show how equipment created for warfare can become historical artefacts in their own right. Rather than celebrating military power, they preserve the technologies, geography and strategic thinking that shaped Montenegro's turbulent past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.narodnimuzej.me/posjeta-reljefna-karta-crne-gore/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.narodnimuzej.me">[Narodni Muzej]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.narodnimuzej.me</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Narodni Muzej Relief map of Montenegro – National Museum of Montenegro</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Narodni Muzej Relief map of Montenegro – National Museum of Montenegro</span></span></span>

## Walking Through the P-821 *Heroj* Submarine

The most immersive military exhibit in Montenegro is the **P-821 *Heroj***, displayed at the Naval Heritage Collection in Tivat beside Porto Montenegro's modern marina. Unlike a conventional naval museum, the submarine itself is the attraction. Visitors enter through the original hatches and move through the narrow compartments used by sailors during patrols, experiencing first-hand how confined life aboard a diesel-electric submarine could be.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.montenegrofortravellers.com/en/place/podmornica-heroj-p-821-submarine-museum-tivat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: montenegrofortravellers.com">[Montenegro For Travellers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">montenegrofortravellers.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Montenegro For TravellersPodmornica Heroj P-821, a submarine-museum in Tivat &#124; Montenegro For TravellersJune 21, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2019</span></span></span>

Launched in 1967 as part of the Yugoslav Navy's *Heroj*-class submarines, the vessel served during the Cold War as a patrol submarine designed for operations in the Adriatic. After its retirement from active service, it was preserved rather than scrapped, making it one of the few submarines in the region that the public can explore internally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.montenegrofortravellers.com/en/place/podmornica-heroj-p-821-submarine-museum-tivat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: montenegrofortravellers.com">[Montenegro For Travellers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">montenegrofortravellers.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Montenegro For TravellersPodmornica Heroj P-821, a submarine-museum in Tivat &#124; Montenegro For TravellersJune 21, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2019</span></span></span>

What makes the submarine especially memorable is that very little has been sanitised. Visitors encounter:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* cramped sleeping bunks stacked between machinery;
* the control room with its gauges, valves and periscopes;
* torpedo compartments that reveal the submarine's original military purpose;
* engine spaces illustrating the complexity of diesel-electric propulsion.

</div>

The result is less like viewing an exhibit and more like entering an intact industrial machine. The experience highlights how twentieth-century military technology depended as much on engineering, endurance and human adaptability as on weaponry.

Its setting creates an unusual contrast. Outside lie luxury yachts and waterfront cafés; inside is a preserved Cold War vessel built for secrecy and conflict. That juxtaposition reinforces how Montenegro's former naval infrastructure has been repurposed for cultural tourism rather than military use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.montenegrofortravellers.com/en/place/maritime-heritage-museum-tivat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: montenegrofortravellers.com">[Montenegro For Travellers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">montenegrofortravellers.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on montenegrofortravellers.com.</span></span></span>

## Cetinje's First World War Relief Map

If the submarine demonstrates military engineering, Cetinje's enormous relief map demonstrates military geography.

Standing in the grounds of Biljarda, today part of the National Museum of Montenegro, the relief map was created during the Austro-Hungarian occupation of Montenegro in 1916–1917. Built at a scale of 1:10,000, it depicts the mountainous terrain of Montenegro together with neighbouring areas of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Albania and Serbia. Constructed from concrete on a stone base using detailed Austro-Hungarian topographic surveys, it ranks among Europe's most remarkable surviving military terrain models.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.narodnimuzej.me/posjeta-reljefna-karta-crne-gore/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.narodnimuzej.me">[narodnimuzej.me]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.narodnimuzej.me</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Narodni Muzej Relief map of Montenegro – National Museum of Montenegro</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Narodni Muzej Relief map of Montenegro – National Museum of Montenegro</span></span></span>

Rather than serving as decoration, the model had a practical purpose. Austro-Hungarian officers used it to understand one of Europe's most difficult landscapes after occupying Cetinje. Montenegro's steep mountains, narrow passes and isolated valleys made conventional maps harder to interpret quickly, while a three-dimensional model allowed commanders to visualise routes, elevations and defensive positions at a glance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tib.oeaw.ac.at/subprojects/montenegro" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tib.oeaw.ac.at">[Tabula Imperii Byzantini]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tib.oeaw.ac.at</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tabula Imperii ByzantiniCultural Heritage in Times of World War I: The Case of the Austro-Hungarian Relief Map of Montenegro (1916-1918)...</span></span></span>

The relief map is unusual because it survives almost exactly where it was created. Although protected today beneath a modern pavilion, it remains a genuine military planning object rather than a later reconstruction. It has been recognised as a cultural monument, valued both for its technical craftsmanship and as evidence of how military cartography evolved during the First World War.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unescomontenegro.com/images/stories/documents/vodich_EN_screen.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unescomontenegro.com">[unescomontenegro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unescomontenegro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum Guide for Montenegro &gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt; English Museums</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum Guide for Montenegro &gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt;&gt; English Museums</span></span></span>

Recent heritage projects have also documented the relief digitally through three-dimensional recording, reflecting its importance not only as a historical curiosity but also as a unique cartographic monument worthy of long-term preservation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tib.oeaw.ac.at/balkan/digital/3dobjects" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tib.oeaw.ac.at">[Tabula Imperii Byzantini]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tib.oeaw.ac.at</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tabula Imperii Byzantini3D objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tabula Imperii Byzantini3D objects</span></span></span>

## How Military Tools Become Visitor Attractions

These two exhibits illustrate different ways that military objects can acquire new meanings once their operational lives end.

The submarine preserves the physical experience of naval service. Visitors leave with a clearer understanding of the practical realities of Cold War submarine operations rather than simply viewing technical specifications.

The relief map preserves military knowledge instead of military hardware. It demonstrates that controlling territory depended not only on armies but also on detailed surveying, mapping and the ability to interpret mountainous landscapes accurately.

Both attractions also show that historical context matters. Neither exhibit was originally intended for public display. The submarine was built as a functioning weapon system, while the relief map was created by an occupying army for strategic planning. Their current value lies precisely in that transformation—from instruments of war into objects that help explain Montenegro's history.

## Why These Displays Stand Out

Many military museums focus on medals, uniforms or weapons. Montenegro instead offers two unusually immersive exhibits that allow visitors to experience military history through scale and space.

The **P-821 *Heroj*** invites people inside a working machine where every corridor, hatch and compartment reveals how submariners lived beneath the sea. The relief map in Cetinje invites them to look down upon an entire country as military planners once did, turning Montenegro's famously rugged landscape into a giant strategic model.

Together they represent one of the country's most distinctive forms of curiosity heritage: preserved military objects whose fascination lies not in battlefield glory but in the extraordinary physical forms they take. One is a complete submarine; the other is an entire nation recreated in miniature. Both remain rare examples of military technology becoming compelling public history.
