## Introduction

León’s Museum of Traditions and Legends is one of the most unusual heritage sites in [Nicaragua]({{ 'where-nicaragua-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}) because it combines two subjects that are rarely presented together. Visitors enter a former prison known as **La XXI**, once associated with detention and torture under the Somoza dictatorship, but instead of finding conventional prison displays they encounter life-sized papier-mâché figures representing witches, ghosts, supernatural animals and other characters from Nicaraguan folklore. The result is neither a simple folklore museum nor a straightforward memorial. It is a place where oral tradition, political violence and local identity occupy the same rooms, forcing visitors to move between imagination and documented history in a single visit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inc.gob.ni/museos/1000/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inc.gob.ni">[gob.ni]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inc.gob.ni</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.ni.</span></span></span>

## Why Are León's Monsters Displayed in Prison Cells?

The museum occupies the former prison of **La XXI**, transforming cells that once confined prisoners into exhibition spaces filled with legendary figures. Rather than erasing the building's past, the museum deliberately preserves it. Murals depicting torture remain on the walls while colourful folk characters stand inside the same cells, creating one of the most striking examples in Central America of a historic prison being reused as a cultural museum rather than simply restored as a memorial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitleon.info/museo-leyendas-tradiciones.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitleon.info">[visitleon.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitleon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">VISIT LEÓNMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VISIT LEÓN...</span></span></span>

This unusual setting reflects two different forms of memory.

On one level, the museum preserves stories that were traditionally passed between generations through family storytelling, festivals and neighbourhood life. On another, it preserves the memory of a prison that became notorious during the Somoza era for the imprisonment and abuse of political opponents. Visitors therefore experience folklore and historical trauma in the same physical space rather than in separate institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicaragua.com/attractions/museums/traditions-and-legends/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicaragua.com">[nicaragua.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicaragua.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Traditions and Legends, Leon &#124; Nicaragua.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Traditions and Legends, Leon &#124; Nicaragua.com...</span></span></span>

The contrast is intentional rather than accidental. Local tourism organisations describe the museum as a place where León's myths and traditions coexist with visible reminders of the prison's history, making the emotional transition between wonder and discomfort part of the visit itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitleon.info/museo-leyendas-tradiciones.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitleon.info">[VISIT LEÓN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitleon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">VISIT LEÓNMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VISIT LEÓN...</span></span></span>

## Legends, Effigies and Popular Characters

Instead of displaying archaeological artefacts behind glass, the museum relies largely on handmade, life-sized papier-mâché sculptures. Many were created by the museum's founder, **Carmen Toruño**, whose aim was to preserve stories that were increasingly vulnerable to disappearing from everyday oral tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitleon.info/museo-leyendas-tradiciones.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitleon.info">[VISIT LEÓN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitleon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">VISIT LEÓNMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VISIT LEÓN...</span></span></span>

Among the best-known characters represented are:

* **La Llorona**, the wandering ghost associated with grief and loss.
* **La Carreta Nagua**, the supernatural death cart heard at night in many Central American traditions.
* **El Padre sin Cabeza**, the headless priest whose appearances warn against moral failings.
* **La Chancha Bruja**, the witch who transforms into a pig.
* **The Cadejos**, supernatural dogs that may either protect or threaten travellers depending on the version of the legend.
* **La Cegua**, a terrifying female apparition associated with deception.
* Giant festival figures such as **La Gigantona** and her companion, celebrating León's popular street traditions alongside its supernatural folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitleon.info/museo-leyendas-tradiciones.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitleon.info">[visitleon.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitleon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">VISIT LEÓNMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VISIT LEÓN...</span></span></span>

The sculptures are intentionally theatrical rather than academically reconstructed. Bright paint, exaggerated facial expressions and dramatic poses give many displays the appearance of a permanent folk performance. This presentation reflects the living nature of these legends: they were never simply stories to read but tales meant to be performed, retold and adapted by each generation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/museo-de-leyendas-y-tradiciones/1037164" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lonelyplanet.com">[Lonely Planet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lonelyplanet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lonely Planet Museo de Leyendas y Tradiciones &#124; León, Nicaragua &#124; Attractions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lonely PlanetMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones &#124; León, Nicaragua &#124; Attractions - Lonely Planet...</span></span></span>

Rather than asking visitors to decide whether the legends are literally true, the displays emphasise their cultural role. Many functioned as moral lessons, explanations for strange events, warnings about travelling after dark or expressions of local identity tied specifically to León and neighbouring regions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitleon.info/museo-leyendas-tradiciones.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitleon.info">[VISIT LEÓN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitleon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">VISIT LEÓNMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VISIT LEÓN...</span></span></span>

## The Prison History of La XXI

The building itself has an equally significant history.

Construction began in the early twentieth century, and the prison became operational in 1921. Initially it held ordinary prisoners, but during the Somoza dictatorship it developed into a detention centre associated with political imprisonment and interrogation. Following the assassination of Anastasio Somoza García in 1956, La XXI became increasingly linked with the repression of political opponents by the National Guard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitleon.info/museo-leyendas-tradiciones.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitleon.info">[visitleon.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitleon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">VISIT LEÓNMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VISIT LEÓN...</span></span></span>

The prison remained in use until the final months of the Nicaraguan Revolution in 1979, when Sandinista forces captured the facility and released its remaining prisoners. The building itself suffered damage during the fighting, but enough survived for later preservation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/museo-de-leyendas-y-tradiciones/1037164" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lonelyplanet.com">[lonelyplanet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lonelyplanet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lonely Planet Museo de Leyendas y Tradiciones &#124; León, Nicaragua &#124; Attractions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lonely PlanetMuseo de Leyendas y Tradiciones &#124; León, Nicaragua &#124; Attractions - Lonely Planet...</span></span></span>

When the museum opened in 1993, its creators deliberately avoided stripping away the evidence of the prison's past. Official descriptions note that the walls still display paintings illustrating methods of torture used inside the prison, alongside photographs showing the original appearance of the building. These features ensure that visitors are continually reminded that the architecture was designed for imprisonment rather than exhibition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inc.gob.ni/museos/1000/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inc.gob.ni">[inc.gob.ni]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inc.gob.ni</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.ni.</span></span></span>

Some guides also draw attention to surviving features such as the courtyard and well, explaining their role during the prison's operation. Whether every individual anecdote attached to these spaces can be independently verified, the broader history of La XXI as a notorious detention centre is well established in historical accounts of León during the Somoza period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicaragua.com/attractions/museums/traditions-and-legends/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicaragua.com">[Nicaragua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicaragua.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Traditions and Legends, Leon &#124; Nicaragua.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Traditions and Legends, Leon &#124; Nicaragua.com...</span></span></span>

## How Folklore and Political Memory Coexist

The museum's most distinctive quality is not the folklore collection alone but the way it refuses to separate cultural imagination from historical memory.

In many museums, folklore is presented as cheerful heritage while prisons are interpreted solely through political history. Here the two narratives overlap. Visitors can leave a room containing witches and ghost stories only to enter another where murals depict torture, imprisonment and fear. ## What Visitors Can Expect Today
