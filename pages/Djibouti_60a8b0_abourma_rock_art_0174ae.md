## Introduction

Abourma is not a conventional museum, yet it is one of [Djibouti]({{ 'where-djibouti-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s most remarkable open-air [collections]({{ 'collections/' | relative_url }}). Spread across the basalt slopes of the Makarrassou Massif in the Tadjourah region, the site preserves thousands of prehistoric engravings carved over many centuries. Among its most striking images are elephants, giraffes, rhinoceroses, hippopotamuses and other large animals that no longer inhabit Djibouti. Together, these carvings form an extraordinary visual archive of a landscape that was once greener, wetter and far richer in wildlife than the country is today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Les Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreLes Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJanuary 5, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2015</span></span></span>

For anyone interested in unusual heritage, Abourma stands out because it preserves evidence rather than legend. ## Why Are African Giants Carved at Abourma?

The most surprising aspect of Abourma is the contrast between the carvings and the modern landscape. Today's northern Djibouti is largely arid, rocky and volcanic, yet the engraved panels depict animals associated with rivers, woodlands and savannahs.

The best-known figures include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Elephants with prominent tusks.
* Giraffes shown with elongated necks and distinctive proportions.
* Rhinoceroses and hippopotamuses.
* Antelope and other wild game.
* Cattle, goats and other domesticated livestock.
* Human hunters, warriors and herders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Les Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreLes Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJanuary 5, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2015</span></span></span>

</div>

Rather than representing mythical creatures, these animals are widely interpreted as records of species that once inhabited the wider Horn of Africa during wetter climatic phases of the Holocene. As rainfall declined over thousands of years, habitats shifted and large mammals gradually disappeared from what is now Djibouti. The engravings therefore preserve a memory of ecological change as well as human activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Les Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreLes Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJanuary 5, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2015</span></span></span>

The sheer scale of the site also makes it exceptional. UNESCO's Tentative List describes nearly three kilometres of engraved rock surfaces containing thousands of figures and scenes spread across basalt outcrops, making it the largest known rock art complex in Djibouti and one of the most extensive in eastern Africa.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Les Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreLes Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJanuary 5, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2015</span></span></span>

## What the Engravings Show

Although the famous elephants and giraffes attract most attention, Abourma is far more than a prehistoric animal gallery.

Researchers have identified scenes that include hunting parties, combat between groups of archers, herding, and people interacting with domestic animals. One particularly unusual composition shows people forming a circle around a cow, suggesting that livestock held social or ceremonial importance beyond simple food production.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003552109000818" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ScienceDirect Les Hommes d’Abourma (République de Djibouti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les Hommes d’Abourma (République de Djibouti) - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

The variety of subjects matters because it transforms the site from a collection of isolated pictures into a narrative landscape. Instead of depicting animals alone, many panels illustrate relationships between people, wildlife and domesticated herds. These scenes hint at changing economies as communities combined hunting with increasingly organised pastoral life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Les Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreLes Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJanuary 5, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2015</span></span></span>

Different engraving styles and levels of weathering also suggest that the site accumulated over long periods rather than being created during a single episode. Archaeologists therefore view Abourma as the product of many generations, recording changing traditions across several millennia rather than a single prehistoric culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Les Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreLes Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJanuary 5, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2015</span></span></span>

## How Scholars Interpret the Images

The animal carvings are generally treated as archaeological evidence rather than symbolic fantasy. Because many depicted species are well known from African environments, scholars see them as reflecting real fauna present when the engravings were made.

This interpretation fits wider environmental evidence showing that parts of the Sahara and Horn of Africa experienced much wetter conditions during the African Humid Period, before increasing aridity transformed the landscape. Abourma's engravings complement that broader picture by providing direct visual testimony from people who lived through those changing environments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5957/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Les Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreLes Gravures Rupestre d’Abourma - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJanuary 5, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2015</span></span></span>

Researchers are more cautious when interpreting social meaning. Hunting scenes, battle scenes and cattle imagery clearly indicate important activities, but the motivations behind individual compositions cannot always be reconstructed with certainty. Some images may record everyday life, while others could commemorate significant events or express beliefs that are no longer recoverable from the archaeological record. The engravings therefore reveal much about prehistoric behaviour while leaving important questions open.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003552109000818" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ScienceDirect Les Hommes d’Abourma (République de Djibouti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les Hommes d’Abourma (République de Djibouti) - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

The site's importance today extends beyond archaeology. UNESCO experts have repeatedly identified Abourma as the country's strongest candidate for World Heritage nomination because of its scale, preservation and ability to illustrate vanished pastoral societies. Recent international work has focused not only on documenting the engravings but also on developing management plans and long-term conservation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2815" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreDjibouti advances in the preparation of its first nomination dossier to the World Heritage List - UNESCO Worl...</span></span></span>

## What Visiting the Remote Site Involves

Abourma remains a remote archaeological landscape rather than a fully developed tourist attraction.

Visitors should expect an outdoor heritage site with natural rock surfaces instead of galleries, interpretation centres or extensive visitor facilities. Access generally involves travelling into the Tadjourah region and crossing rugged volcanic terrain, where local knowledge and appropriate transport are valuable. ## Why Abourma Matters in Djibouti's Curiosity Landscape

For a country with relatively few conventional museums devoted to unusual history, Abourma functions as an immense open-air archive of lost worlds. Its carved elephants, giraffes and rhinoceroses challenge modern assumptions about Djibouti's environment, revealing that today's desert landscapes once supported dramatically different ecosystems.

The site is unusual not because it presents mysterious or supernatural claims, but because it preserves an authentic prehistoric record that seems almost unbelievable when compared with the modern surroundings. That combination of striking imagery, environmental history and archaeological evidence makes Abourma one of the most distinctive heritage attractions in Djibouti and a fitting centrepiece of the country's emerging cultural conservation efforts.
