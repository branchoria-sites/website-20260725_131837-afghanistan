## Introduction

The mummies discovered beneath the former Dominican church in Vác are remarkable not simply because their bodies survived, but because many of the people themselves can still be identified. Thanks to coffin inscriptions, parish registers and civic records, researchers have matched numerous mummified individuals with their names, ages, occupations and family relationships. This transforms the collection from an archaeological curiosity into an unusually intimate record of everyday life in an eighteenth- and early nineteenth-century Hungarian town. Rather than presenting anonymous remains, the Memento Mori exhibition allows visitors to encounter identifiable townspeople whose lives, illnesses and family connections can often be reconstructed with surprising precision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-v%C3%A1c-mummy-project-investigation-of-265-eighteenth-century-mum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchers.mq.edu.au">[Macquarie University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchers.mq.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Macquarie UniversityThe Vác mummy project: investigation of 265 eighteenth-century mummified remains from the TB Pandemic Era - Macquarie...</span></span></span>

## How the sealed crypt preserved the bodies

The people displayed in Vác were never intentionally embalmed. Their preservation resulted from the unusual conditions inside a sealed crypt used between 1731 and 1838. Cool temperatures, constant but limited ventilation, low humidity and tightly stacked wooden coffins—many packed with pine shavings—created an environment in which soft tissues dried naturally instead of decomposing. When the crypt was rediscovered in 1994, more than 200 bodies were found, with around 70 per cent preserved either completely or partially through natural mummification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/628/1/618.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.ucl.ac.uk">[UCL Discovery]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.ucl.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UCL Discovery Widespread Occurrence of Mycobacterium tuberculosis-DNA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UCL Discovery Widespread Occurrence of Mycobacterium tuberculosis-DNA</span></span></span>

What makes this discovery exceptional is that the physical remains were accompanied by unusually rich documentary evidence. Painted coffins often carried inscriptions naming the deceased, while church burial registers and civic records recorded deaths, occupations, family members and dates. Instead of relying solely on archaeological interpretation, researchers could compare physical evidence directly with written historical records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-v%C3%A1c-mummy-project-investigation-of-265-eighteenth-century-mum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchers.mq.edu.au">[Macquarie University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchers.mq.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Macquarie UniversityThe Vác mummy project: investigation of 265 eighteenth-century mummified remains from the TB Pandemic Era - Macquarie...</span></span></span>

## Names, clothing and family records bring the dead back into history

Because many individuals can be identified, the Vác collection functions almost like a preserved community archive rather than a collection of isolated bodies.

Researchers have been able to connect:

* named individuals with their surviving remains;
* husbands, wives, parents and children buried in the same crypt;
* occupations and social status recorded in parish documents;
* original clothing, textiles and personal burial objects with documented people rather than anonymous skeletons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-v%C3%A1c-mummy-project-investigation-of-265-eighteenth-century-mum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchers.mq.edu.au">[edu.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchers.mq.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Macquarie UniversityThe Vác mummy project: investigation of 265 eighteenth-century mummified remains from the TB Pandemic Era - Macquarie...</span></span></span>

This combination allows historians to study everyday life in unusual detail. Clothing demonstrates how middle-class citizens and clergy dressed for burial, while surviving fabrics, caps and decorative coffin paintings reveal local customs that would otherwise survive only in written descriptions. Because the identities are known, these objects become evidence for individual biographies rather than generic examples of eighteenth-century material culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-v%C3%A1c-mummy-project-investigation-of-265-eighteenth-century-mum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchers.mq.edu.au">[Macquarie University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchers.mq.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Macquarie UniversityThe Vác mummy project: investigation of 265 eighteenth-century mummified remains from the TB Pandemic Era - Macquarie...</span></span></span>

Family relationships are especially valuable. Instead of analysing isolated remains, researchers can investigate inherited characteristics, household disease patterns and the effects of shared living conditions across generations. The collection therefore bridges archaeology, genealogy and social history in a way that few European mummy discoveries can match.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25814300/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Two positive tuberculosis cases in the late Nigrovits family, 18th century, Vác, Hungary - PubMed...</span></span></span>

## Individual stories behind the scientific research

Several named individuals have become well-documented case studies.

One example is the Nigrovits family. Researchers examined the remains of József Nigrovits, who died aged 55 in 1793, and his son Antal, who died aged 22 in 1803. Historical records confirmed their relationship, allowing scientists to compare physical remains with documented family history. Advanced imaging and laboratory analysis showed that both had tuberculosis infections, even though the father displayed no obvious skeletal signs of the disease. Antal's dramatic spinal and neck deformity, once suspected to be tuberculosis, instead proved to be a developmental abnormality unrelated to the infection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25814300/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Two positive tuberculosis cases in the late Nigrovits family, 18th century, Vác, Hungary - PubMed...</span></span></span>

Another closely studied individual is an eighteenth-century midwife whose preserved remains showed evidence of severe illness. Modern ancient DNA analysis recovered tuberculosis genomes from her body and demonstrated that she carried more than one strain of the disease simultaneously. Beyond the medical findings, knowing her occupation places the scientific evidence within the social world of eighteenth-century Vác, illustrating how infectious disease affected ordinary working people before antibiotics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S147297922200018X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Metagenomic analysis reveals mixed Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection in a 18th century Hungarian midwife - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

These examples illustrate why the collection continues to attract international researchers. The written records make it possible to ask questions that anonymous archaeological finds cannot answer.

## What visitors see and what researchers continue to study

The Memento Mori exhibition does not attempt to display every mummy discovered in the crypt. Instead, it presents a carefully selected group of naturally preserved bodies alongside their painted coffins, burial garments and personal artefacts. Interpretation focuses on who these people were rather than treating them as macabre spectacles. Visitors encounter children, adults and elderly townspeople whose names and family histories are often known, making the experience notably more personal than many mummy exhibitions. CT scanning, radiography, textile analysis, stable historical documentation and increasingly sophisticated ancient DNA techniques continue to reveal information about disease, nutrition, occupations and living conditions. ## Why the identified lives matter

Many collections of human remains can reveal how people died, but relatively few can also reveal who they were. The Vác mummies stand apart because physical preservation coincides with exceptionally complete historical documentation. Researchers can move from a preserved face to a coffin inscription, then to parish records, family relationships and sometimes even occupation, creating biographies that span both archival history and biological evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-v%C3%A1c-mummy-project-investigation-of-265-eighteenth-century-mum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchers.mq.edu.au">[Macquarie University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchers.mq.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Macquarie UniversityThe Vác mummy project: investigation of 265 eighteenth-century mummified remains from the TB Pandemic Era - Macquarie...</span></span></span>

Within [Hungary]({{ 'where-hungary-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s landscape of unusual museums and collections, this makes the Vác Mummies distinctive.     if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
