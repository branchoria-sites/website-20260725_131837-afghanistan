## Introduction

The mummies of Guanajuato are among [Mexico]({{ 'where-mexico-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s most famous and most controversial museum exhibits. Unlike deliberately embalmed Egyptian mummies, these are the naturally preserved bodies of ordinary people who were buried in the Santa Paula municipal cemetery during the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Their remarkable state of preservation made them a curiosity long before they were placed in a dedicated museum, but their continuing display raises difficult questions about consent, dignity, conservation and the commercial use of human remains. The debate is no longer simply about whether the bodies are scientifically interesting. It is about how identifiable people should be treated when they become a major tourist attraction, and whether educational value can outweigh the ethical responsibilities owed to the dead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

## How the bodies were naturally preserved

The Guanajuato mummies were not intentionally created. Most were exhumed after relatives failed to continue paying cemetery burial fees, a practice once used in parts of Mexico's municipal cemeteries. Workers expected to recover skeletons but instead found unusually well-preserved bodies with dried skin, hair, clothing and, in some cases, facial features still recognisable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

Researchers attribute this preservation primarily to environmental conditions rather than any mysterious process. The cemetery's dry, well-ventilated burial niches encouraged rapid dehydration, slowing normal decomposition. The preservation varies from body to body, reflecting differences in burial conditions rather than a single cause. Earlier popular stories blamed mineral-rich soil, cholera victims or even premature burial, but modern forensic research has found these explanations to be exaggerated or unsupported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

Their accidental preservation is central to the ethical debate. Unlike archaeological mummies prepared according to religious traditions, these individuals expected ordinary burials and had no reason to imagine their remains would eventually become permanent public exhibits.

## What visitors see inside the museum

The Museum of the Mummies of Guanajuato displays around one hundred naturally preserved bodies, many standing upright behind glass. Visitors encounter adults, children and infants, with some still wearing fragments of their original clothing or footwear. Several bodies have acquired memorable nicknames and stories over the decades, although not all of these narratives are historically reliable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

The museum experience is striking because the dead feel historically close rather than remote. Most lived only a century or two ago, making them easier to imagine as identifiable members of the local community rather than anonymous figures from the distant past. This closeness creates a different emotional response from many ancient mummy collections.

The exhibition also illustrates how folklore can develop around museum collections. Tales that certain individuals were buried alive or possessed supernatural qualities became part of local tourism long before careful historical research began examining the identities and life stories of the deceased. Modern researchers have worked to replace sensational legends with documentary evidence from death certificates, church records and municipal archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

## Why consent has become the central ethical question

The strongest criticism of the museum is not that it displays human remains, but that the people displayed never agreed to become museum exhibits.

Museum ethics increasingly emphasise informed consent, cultural sensitivity and the dignity of the deceased. The Guanajuato mummies present a particularly difficult case because they are neither anonymous prehistoric skeletons nor members of a clearly defined descendant community with established repatriation procedures. Instead, they are relatively recent local residents, many of whom could still have living descendants.

Anthropologists involved in current research have argued that identifying individual bodies is more than a historical exercise. If descendants can be located and object to public display, some specialists have suggested the remains should be reburied rather than exhibited indefinitely. This shifts attention away from the bodies as tourist objects and back towards the people they once were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

The museum therefore sits within a broader international discussion about displaying human remains. Increasingly, museums are expected to explain why bodies are exhibited, what educational purpose they serve and whether respectful alternatives have been considered, rather than assuming public display is automatically acceptable.

## Conservation versus tourism

The museum's popularity creates a practical dilemma. Large visitor numbers generate significant income for Guanajuato and have made the mummies one of the city's defining attractions. Yet frequent movement, exhibition and environmental fluctuations can threaten fragile remains that require careful conservation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

Specialists have questioned several past practices, including transporting the mummies to promotional events and displaying them outside carefully controlled museum conditions. Conservation experts have also argued that some bodies would be better preserved lying horizontally rather than standing upright, reducing physical stress on fragile tissues. Improvements to climate control and long-term conservation have likewise been recommended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

These concerns illustrate a recurring tension in unusual museums: the more successful an attraction becomes, the greater the pressure to balance public access with preservation.

## Recent disputes over their treatment

The ethical debate has intensified in recent years through disagreements between Guanajuato's municipal authorities and Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH), the federal body responsible for protecting much of the country's cultural heritage.

INAH has criticised changes made to the museum without sufficient consultation and has argued that recognised conservation standards should guide any alterations to the display or handling of the bodies. In 2024, the institute publicly condemned unilateral modifications to the museum and reaffirmed that the collection should be managed with greater respect for both its heritage value and its status as human remains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inah.gob.mx/boletines/condenan-secretaria-de-cultura-e-inah-el-manejo-unilateral-de-cuerpos-momificados-de-guanajuato" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inah.gob.mx">[Inah]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inah.gob.mx</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.mx.</span></span></span>

The dispute followed earlier controversies, including criticism over travelling exhibitions and reports of conservation damage during museum works. One widely reported incident involved the detachment of a mummy's arm during renovations, reinforcing concerns that inadequate handling could permanently damage the collection. Federal authorities argued that such incidents reflected insufficient conservation expertise rather than unavoidable accidents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/1f268720e46264b533b8eb62bdcbd377" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This incident highlights an ongoing jurisdictional dispute between INAH and Guanajuato&#x27;s conservative local government over the mummies...</span></span></span>

These disagreements are not simply bureaucratic. They reflect competing priorities: local authorities emphasise tourism and municipal ownership, while federal heritage specialists prioritise conservation standards and the respectful treatment of human remains.

## Why the Guanajuato mummies matter beyond Guanajuato


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The Museum of the Mummies occupies a distinctive place within Mexico's landscape of unusual collections because it forces visitors to confront uncomfortable questions rather than merely offering macabre spectacle.

</div>

The bodies demonstrate how accidental scientific phenomena can become tourist attractions, but they also reveal how museum values evolve. Earlier generations often treated the mummies as curiosities or entertainment. Contemporary scholarship increasingly asks who these people were, how they should be interpreted, whether descendants should have a voice, and how museums can balance education with respect.

That tension explains why the Guanajuato mummies remain one of Mexico's most discussed museum collections. Their significance lies not only in their extraordinary preservation but also in the continuing debate over whether displaying identifiable human beings can ever be fully reconciled with modern expectations of dignity, consent and responsible stewardship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/these-mexican-mummies-draw-crowds-and-controversy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThese Mexican mummies draw crowds—and controversy &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2022</span></span></span>

      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
