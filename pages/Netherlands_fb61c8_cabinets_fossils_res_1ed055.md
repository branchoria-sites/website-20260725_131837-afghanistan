## Introduction

A cabinet of curiosities was never just a room full of odd objects. In the [Netherlands]({{ 'netherlands/' | relative_url }}), it became a way of organising knowledge, displaying wealth and demonstrating global connections. Long before museums divided art, geology, archaeology and science into separate departments, collectors placed paintings beside fossils, scientific instruments, minerals, shells and objects gathered through international trade. Nowhere is that older way of thinking more visible than at Teylers Museum in Haarlem, where many of the original eighteenth- and nineteenth-century displays survive almost intact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/discover/building" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span></span></span>

Yet these collections also reveal a more uncomfortable story. Many Dutch museums grew during the age of overseas commerce and colonial expansion, when scientific specimens and cultural objects moved through unequal networks of power. ## Inside Teylers Museum's surviving curiosity world

Teylers Museum is often described as the oldest museum in the Netherlands, but what makes it remarkable is not simply its age. It preserves one of Europe's closest surviving experiences of an Enlightenment cabinet of curiosities transformed into a public museum. The Oval Room, opened in 1784, still combines scientific instruments, books, minerals and works of art in much the same architectural setting that visitors encountered more than two centuries ago.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/what-s-on/the-oldest-museum-in-the-netherlands" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The oldest museum in the Netherlands</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The oldest museum in the Netherlands</span></span></span>

Rather than separating disciplines, the museum presented them as parts of a single intellectual enterprise. Visitors could watch electrical demonstrations, examine fossil specimens, consult books and admire drawings within one interconnected environment. Knowledge was expected to emerge through comparison: natural history informed philosophy, art illustrated science, and scientific instruments demonstrated invisible natural forces.

This arrangement reflected Enlightenment ideals rather than modern museum practice. The building itself was designed as a place where research, public education and collecting happened together. Laboratories, an observatory, lecture spaces and collections existed side by side, making the museum an active centre of investigation rather than simply a repository of finished knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/discover/building" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span></span></span>

The continued survival of these interiors is important because they allow modern visitors to experience how eighteenth-century scholars classified the world before today's academic boundaries emerged. Instead of reconstructing a cabinet of curiosities from fragments, Teylers preserves one that evolved naturally into a museum without losing its original intellectual character.

## How fossils and instruments shaped public science

One of the most distinctive features of Teylers Museum is that fossils and scientific instruments are treated as equal partners in telling the history of discovery.

The museum's science collection includes around 75,000 fossil remains and traces alongside nearly 18,000 scientific instruments. Together they document how ideas about geology, extinction, evolution and the Earth's history developed during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Rather than being displayed as isolated treasures, the specimens illustrate changing scientific theories and debates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/discover/collection/science" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The science collection of Teylers Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The science collection of Teylers Museum</span></span></span>

The fossil collections include internationally significant specimens such as one of the earliest known *Mosasaurus* fossils and the famous "Deluge Man" fossil, whose interpretation shifted as geological science moved away from Biblical explanations towards deep geological time. Visitors therefore encounter not only fossils themselves but also changing scientific ideas about what those fossils meant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/discover/collection/science" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The science collection of Teylers Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The science collection of Teylers Museum</span></span></span>

The scientific instruments tell a parallel story. Electrical generators, telescopes, optical devices and precision measuring equipment were not decorative objects but working research tools. Museum visitors watched experiments that demonstrated electricity, magnetism, heat and light, bringing cutting-edge science into a public setting decades before the emergence of modern science centres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/discover/building" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span></span></span>

The historic Fossil Rooms reinforce this educational purpose. Their nineteenth-century display cases remain largely arranged according to contemporary scientific classifications, allowing visitors to see not only the specimens but also how palaeontologists once organised evidence before later revisions to evolutionary science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/what-s-on/the-oldest-museum-in-the-netherlands" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The oldest museum in the Netherlands</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The oldest museum in the Netherlands</span></span></span>

## Colonial collecting and the return of the Dubois fossils

The most important contemporary debate surrounding Dutch scientific collections concerns not how objects are displayed, but how they were acquired.

For much of the nineteenth century, Dutch museums benefited from the country's colonial presence, particularly in what is now Indonesia. Scientists could excavate, purchase or remove archaeological material under political conditions that overwhelmingly favoured colonial authorities. While museums often viewed these acquisitions as scientific achievements, modern research increasingly asks whether local communities genuinely consented to losing objects that held cultural, spiritual or economic significance.

This question became internationally prominent through the Dubois Collection. Dutch anatomist and geologist Eugène Dubois excavated fossils on Java during the late nineteenth century, including remains associated with the discovery of *Homo erectus*, widely known as "Java Man". These fossils transformed ideas about human evolution and became one of the world's most famous palaeoanthropological collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/cfb33babd5ae3e4e881ec1ad0d0d9028" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dutch Colonial Collections Committee concluded that the fossils hold spiritual and economic importance to Indonesians and were obtain...</span></span></span>

However, a Dutch advisory committee examining colonial collections concluded that many of the fossils were probably removed under coercive colonial conditions. The committee found evidence that local people were pressured into revealing fossil sites and that the collection's removal constituted an historical injustice. In response, the Dutch government agreed to return more than 28,000 fossils from the Dubois Collection to Indonesia, with both countries intending the material to remain available for future scientific research through cooperation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/cfb33babd5ae3e4e881ec1ad0d0d9028" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dutch Colonial Collections Committee concluded that the fossils hold spiritual and economic importance to Indonesians and were obtain...</span></span></span>

The case is significant because it expands restitution debates beyond art and ceremonial objects. Fossils were long treated as universally scientific material with little connection to questions of ownership. The Dubois decision recognises that scientific specimens can also possess local cultural meaning and that the circumstances of their collection matter alongside their scientific value.

## Why curiosity cabinets now raise questions about ownership

Modern visitors often admire historic cabinets of curiosities for their beauty and intellectual ambition. At the same time, museums increasingly encourage audiences to ask different questions from those posed in the eighteenth century.

Instead of asking only what an object is, curators also ask:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Who collected it?
* Under what circumstances was it acquired?
* Who benefited from its removal?
* Whose knowledge helped locate or interpret it?
* Who should have authority over it today?

</div>

These questions do not diminish the scientific importance of collections such as those at Teylers Museum. Rather, they broaden the story, recognising that scientific discovery, commercial expansion and colonial power frequently developed together.

The result is a richer understanding of Dutch curiosity culture. Historic cabinets become records not only of intellectual ambition but also of global trade, empire, unequal exchange and changing ethical standards.

## Why this matters in the Netherlands today

The Netherlands occupies a distinctive position in the history of curiosity cabinets because it preserves both the physical spaces where Enlightenment collecting flourished and the modern debates reshaping their interpretation.

Teylers Museum demonstrates how art, fossils, books and scientific instruments once formed a unified vision of knowledge, allowing visitors to experience an intellectual world that largely disappeared elsewhere. At the same time, the restitution of the Dubois fossils shows that museums are no longer judged solely by the quality of their collections, but also by how honestly they address the histories behind them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teylersmuseum.nl/en/discover/building" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teylersmuseum.nl">[Teylers Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teylersmuseum.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teylers Museum The building of Teylers Museum</span></span></span>

Seen together, these developments transform the cabinet of curiosities from a celebration of collecting into a place for asking deeper questions about science, authority and justice. The most fascinating objects are no longer simply the rarest fossils or the most ingenious instruments, but the stories they reveal about who had the power to collect the world—and who now has the right to decide where that world belongs.

    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
