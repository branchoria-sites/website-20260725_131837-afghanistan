## Introduction

[Bangladesh]({{ 'where-bangladesh-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}) has no recognised public museum devoted to witchcraft, occult practice or ceremonial magic. That absence does not mean supernatural belief is missing from the country's museum landscape. Instead, museums and cultural institutions present folk belief through the safer, broader framework of heritage, craftsmanship and ethnology. Masks, painted scrolls, ritual objects, protective ornaments and ceremonial equipment appear as evidence of everyday life and traditional culture rather than as proof of supernatural forces. This approach reflects both Bangladesh's museum tradition and the country's predominantly Islamic social context, where explicit celebration of witchcraft would be controversial. Rather than staging the uncanny as entertainment, museums preserve the material culture that surrounded beliefs in spirits, protection, healing and ritual while leaving visitors to interpret the stories behind the objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Ethnological_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.banglapedia.org">[en.banglapedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.banglapedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 17, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why Bangladesh has no dedicated witchcraft museum

Unlike parts of Europe where museums of witchcraft developed around documented witch trials or collections assembled by occult enthusiasts, Bangladesh has no comparable institutional tradition. Historical records contain local beliefs about spirits, healing rituals, protective charms and supernatural dangers, but these practices were rarely collected as a separate museum category.

Instead, state museums have generally focused on archaeology, ethnology, language, folk art and national history. Objects connected with ritual life are interpreted as examples of cultural identity, craftsmanship or religious practice rather than as exhibits about magic itself. This reflects the priorities of Bangladesh's heritage institutions, which emphasise documentation and education over sensational displays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Ethnological_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.banglapedia.org">[en.banglapedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.banglapedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 17, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2021</span></span></span>

The result is a quieter but arguably more informative visitor experience. Rather than encountering reconstructed "witches' rooms" or dramatic claims about curses, visitors see the physical objects that communities actually used during festivals, seasonal ceremonies and everyday life.

## Masks, scrolls and protective objects

The closest public route into Bangladesh's supernatural traditions is through folk-art and ethnological collections rather than specialist occult displays.

Several categories of objects are particularly revealing:

* **Ceremonial masks**, associated with ritual dances, festivals and performances. Historically these could represent spirits, mythical beings or protective figures rather than theatrical entertainment alone. Their meanings varied between communities and regions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thedailystar.net/news/masks-of-bangladesh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thedailystar.net">[The Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thedailystar.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Daily Star Masks of Bangladesh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Daily StarMasks of BangladeshApril 22, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2013</span></span></span>
* **Painted narrative scrolls**, which combine storytelling, religious instruction and folklore. Many include miraculous events, legendary heroes or supernatural episodes without presenting them as literal history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://banglaacademy.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922de22933eb65569e1879b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: banglaacademy.gov.bd">[banglaacademy.gov.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">banglaacademy.gov.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ফোকলোর উপবিভাগ &#124; পাতা &#124; বাংলা একাডেমি</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ফোকলোর উপবিভাগ &#124; পাতা &#124; বাংলা একাডেমি</span></span></span>
* **Protective ornaments and ritual equipment**, displayed alongside everyday clothing and household objects, showing that ideas of protection, blessing and misfortune were woven into ordinary life rather than separated into a distinct magical tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Ethnological_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.banglapedia.org">[en.banglapedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.banglapedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 17, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2021</span></span></span>
* **Festival objects and musical instruments**, which demonstrate how performances, seasonal ceremonies and community rituals transmitted beliefs across generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Ethnological_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.banglapedia.org">[en.banglapedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.banglapedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 17, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2021</span></span></span>

Seen together, these collections reveal that Bangladesh's supernatural heritage survives primarily through material culture rather than through museums dedicated to paranormal themes.

## Where visitors encounter these traditions

The country's principal ethnological collections provide the strongest introduction to these themes.

The Ethnological Museum in Chattogram, Bangladesh's only ethnological museum, presents the material culture of numerous Indigenous and ethnic communities. Its galleries include ceremonial objects, ornaments, musical instruments, clothing, models, ritual equipment and festival materials that illustrate how belief, identity and daily life intersected. Although the museum does not frame these objects as evidence of witchcraft or occult practice, visitors can clearly see how ritual formed part of social life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ethnologicalmuseum.chittagong.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/699149ba516a96d4de417380" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ethnologicalmuseum.chittagong.gov.bd">[ethnologicalmuseum.chittagong.gov.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ethnologicalmuseum.chittagong.gov.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">এক নজরে &#124; পাতা &#124; জাতিতাত্ত্বিক জাদুঘর আগ্রাবাদ, চট্টগ্রামJuly 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 25, 2025</span></span></span>

University and folk-art museums add another perspective by displaying masks, embroidered textiles, household objects, bows, musical instruments and other traditional artefacts. These collections emphasise craftsmanship and community memory while still preserving objects originally used within ceremonial or symbolic contexts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museum.cu.ac.bd/?page_id=49" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museum.cu.ac.bd">[museum.cu.ac.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museum.cu.ac.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Folk Art Gallery – CU Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folk Art Gallery – CU Museum</span></span></span>

Bangla Academy's long-running folklore programme also illustrates that preservation in Bangladesh extends beyond museum cases. Since the 1960s it has systematically collected folk tales, oral traditions, ritual customs, proverbs, songs, manuscripts and beliefs from across the country, recognising folklore as part of the nation's intangible cultural heritage rather than treating supernatural traditions as curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://banglaacademy.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922de22933eb65569e1879b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: banglaacademy.gov.bd">[banglaacademy.gov.bd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">banglaacademy.gov.bd</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ফোকলোর উপবিভাগ &#124; পাতা &#124; বাংলা একাডেমি</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ফোকলোর উপবিভাগ &#124; পাতা &#124; বাংলা একাডেমি</span></span></span>

## How museums separate folklore from spectacle

Bangladesh's institutions generally avoid presenting supernatural traditions as evidence that spirits or magical powers are objectively real. Instead, they distinguish between three related but different subjects.

First is **belief**, documenting what communities believed or still believe.

Second is **material culture**, preserving the masks, clothing, ritual equipment and artworks associated with those beliefs.

Third is **performance**, showing how songs, dances, storytelling and seasonal festivals carried supernatural narratives through generations.

This approach allows museums to discuss miraculous stories, protective symbols and legendary beings without endorsing them as historical fact. It also avoids reducing living traditions to tourist attractions or paranormal entertainment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Ethnological_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.banglapedia.org">[en.banglapedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.banglapedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ethnological Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 17, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2021</span></span></span>

## What these collections reveal about Bangladesh's curiosity culture


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

For travellers seeking unusual museums, Bangladesh can initially appear to lack the dramatic occult attractions found elsewhere. Yet its folk collections reveal a different model of preserving the strange.

</div>

Instead of assembling cabinets of cursed objects or exhibits devoted to witchcraft, museums preserve the ordinary artefacts through which supernatural ideas entered everyday life: a ritual mask worn in ceremony, a painted scroll recounting miraculous events, an ornament believed to protect its owner, or festival equipment linking communities with ancestral traditions. These objects are displayed as history, art and ethnography rather than spectacle.

That restrained style makes Bangladesh's collections valuable in a different way. They encourage visitors to understand supernatural belief as part of cultural history, showing how stories of unseen forces became embedded in craft, ritual and collective memory without requiring a dedicated museum of witchcraft.
