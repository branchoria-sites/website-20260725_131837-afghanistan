## Introduction

[Panama]({{ 'where-panama-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s devil masks are often misunderstood by visitors who associate horned faces and dramatic costumes with occult practice. In reality, the country's best-known masked devils belong to living traditions of theatre, history and religious symbolism. They represent temptation, oppression, colonial power and moral conflict rather than objects of worship. The most distinctive examples appear within the Congo cultural traditions of the Caribbean coast, especially around Portobelo, where masked performances preserve the historical memory of Afro-Panamanian communities descended from enslaved Africans. Rather than celebrating evil, these performances stage its defeat through humour, music, dance and ritual reversal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ritual-and-festive-expressions-of-the-congo-culture-01383?RL=01383" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO ICH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Why Panama's Devil Masks Are Not Occult

The devil is one of the most recognisable figures in Panamanian folklore, but its meaning differs sharply from popular horror imagery. Within Congo performance traditions, the devil symbolises the forces that enslaved and oppressed Afro-descendant communities during the colonial era. Costumes exaggerate fearsome features precisely because the character is meant to be challenged, mocked and ultimately overcome.

This theatrical use of the devil has deep roots in Catholic religious drama, where moral struggles were often represented through symbolic characters. In Panama, Afro-descendant communities adapted these traditions to express their own historical experience. The result is a uniquely Panamanian form of ritual theatre in which Christian symbolism merges with memories of resistance and survival. UNESCO describes the performances as celebrations of the descendants of formerly enslaved people that preserve identity, historical memory and communal values rather than occult beliefs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ritual-and-festive-expressions-of-the-congo-culture-01383?RL=01383" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO ICH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

The climax of the Congo season reinforces this interpretation. During Ash Wednesday ceremonies, the devils are symbolically defeated and ritually transformed, illustrating that evil has no lasting victory. The masks therefore represent a temporary dramatic role rather than a sacred or supernatural identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ritual-and-festive-expressions-of-the-congo-culture-01383?RL=01383" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO ICH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Masks, Costumes and Folklore Museums

Visitors interested in Panama's unusual collections may encounter devil masks in museums dedicated to folklore rather than supernatural beliefs. Removed from performance, the masks become artefacts documenting craftsmanship, regional identity and evolving traditions.

One of the country's most relevant institutions is the Manuel F. Zárate Museum in Guararé. Dedicated to the preservation of Panamanian folklore, it holds traditional costumes, historical materials and cultural objects connected with festivals and regional customs. Although the museum represents many forms of folklore rather than focusing solely on devil traditions, it helps explain how ceremonial clothing, masks and performance have become part of Panama's cultural heritage rather than mere tourist spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sicultura.gob.pa/espacios-culturales/museos-otros-espacios-exhibicion-interpretacion-patrimonial/museo-manuel-f-zarate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sicultura.gob.pa">[Sicultura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sicultura.gob.pa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sicultura · Museo Manuel F. Seen in a museum, a devil mask reveals details that may be missed during a performance:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Carved or moulded facial features designed to exaggerate expressions.
* Bright colours, especially reds and blacks, associated with theatrical visibility rather than magical meaning.
* Horns, teeth and animal elements that heighten the dramatic contrast between the devils and the Congo characters.
* Signs of repeated use, repairs and repainting that reflect their continuing role in annual community celebrations.

</div>

Unlike commercial souvenir masks produced for visitors, authentic performance masks often carry evidence of long-term community use and are closely associated with specific performers or local traditions.

## Portobelo's Congo Performance Tradition

Portobelo, on Panama's Caribbean coast, is the centre most closely associated with Congo culture. The town's annual celebrations transform streets into an open-air stage where music, dance, comedy and symbolic conflict recreate memories of slavery and resistance.

The devil characters appear in striking red-and-black costumes and imposing masks. Rather than acting as supernatural beings, they embody slave owners, colonial authority and oppressive power. Congo performers respond through wit, dancing, trickery and collective action instead of military force. The resulting performance preserves historical memory while entertaining audiences with improvisation and satire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tourismpanama.com/places-to-visit/portobelo/things-to-do/culture-arts/congo-culture/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tourismpanama.com">[Tourism Panama]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tourismpanama.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on tourismpanama.com.</span></span></span>

UNESCO recognised the ritual and festive expressions of Congo culture as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2018 because they continue to transmit oral history, music, dance and social values across generations. Participation extends beyond professional performers, involving families and communities who inherit songs, costumes and dramatic roles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ritual-and-festive-expressions-of-the-congo-culture-01383?RL=01383" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO ICH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## How Ritual Theatre Reverses Power

One of the most distinctive aspects of Panama's masked devil tradition is its reversal of social hierarchy.

Throughout the Congo season, characters who historically possessed little political power become central figures in the performance. Humour, mockery and music undermine the authority represented by the devils. Audiences witness not a realistic reconstruction of slavery but a symbolic world in which oppressed communities control the narrative.

This theatrical reversal serves several purposes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It preserves historical memory without reducing it to a solemn historical lecture.
* It transforms painful experiences into communal resilience.
* It allows satire to criticise authority through performance.
* It reinforces shared identity across generations.

</div>

UNESCO notes that the season concludes with the symbolic neutralisation of the devils, who remove their masks and undergo a ritual of liberation before the annual cycle begins again. This ending makes clear that the devil is a dramatic character whose defeat is essential to the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ritual-and-festive-expressions-of-the-congo-culture-01383?RL=01383" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO ICH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHRitual and festive expressions of the Congo culture - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage...</span></span></span>

## Why These Masks Matter in Panama's Curiosity Culture

For travellers seeking Panama's stranger heritage, devil masks occupy a distinctive place between museum collection and living tradition. They may appear visually unsettling, yet their significance lies not in mystery or the occult but in historical storytelling.

They also illustrate an important difference between preserved objects and active heritage. A carved mask displayed in a gallery records artistic skill and folklore, while the same mask worn during a Congo performance becomes part of an evolving drama that connects religion, resistance, music and community identity.

Within Panama's broader landscape of unusual collections, devil masks stand alongside Indigenous textiles, colonial devotional objects and archaeological artefacts as examples of material culture whose meanings remain active rather than merely historical. Their enduring power comes not from fear but from the way they transform symbols of oppression into annual celebrations of survival, memory and cultural continuity.

    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
