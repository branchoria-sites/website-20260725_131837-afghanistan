## Introduction

Ussher Fort in Accra is unusual because it never belonged to just one chapter of history. Unlike sites remembered solely as slave forts or military outposts, Ussher Fort was repeatedly adapted to new systems of confinement. Built as a European trading post, it became part of the Atlantic slave trade, later functioned as a colonial prison, military detention centre, police facility and court-martial unit, and even served humanitarian purposes as a refugee shelter before becoming a museum. That succession of roles makes it one of [Ghana]({{ 'where-ghanas-strangest-objects-still/' | relative_url }})'s clearest examples of how the same physical spaces were repeatedly reused to control, imprison and displace people across more than three centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2300" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreGhana inaugurates the rehabilitated Ussher Fort Slave Museum and Documentary Centre - UNESCO World Heritage C...</span></span></span>

## From European trading fort to slave-trade site

Originally constructed by the Dutch as Fort Crèvecœur during the seventeenth century, the fort occupied a strategic position on the coast of what is now Accra. Following the Anglo-Dutch exchange of forts in 1868, it passed to British control, was partly rebuilt after earthquake damage and renamed Ussher Fort after the British administrator Herbert Ussher.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gmmb.gov.gh/ussher-fort-crevecoeur-accra/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gmmb.gov.gh">[Ghana Mining and Minerals Board]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gmmb.gov.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gmmb.gov.gh.</span></span></span>

Although the fort initially supported commerce in commodities such as gold, its purpose shifted as the transatlantic slave trade expanded. Like many coastal forts in the region, it became one link in a wider trading network that imprisoned Africans before forced transportation across the Atlantic. The architecture itself reflects this change: storage areas, enclosed courtyards and secure rooms were adapted to hold people awaiting shipment rather than ordinary merchandise. UNESCO identifies Ussher Fort as one of the coastal fortifications that illustrate the transformation of European trading posts into infrastructure supporting the Atlantic slave trade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/34" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreForts and Castles, Volta, Greater Accra, Central and Western Regions - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

Unlike larger castles such as Cape Coast or Elmina, Ussher Fort is not primarily known for the scale of its slave dungeons. Its importance lies in demonstrating that slavery relied upon a chain of interconnected forts of different sizes rather than only a handful of famous castles. The museum therefore places the fort within the broader commercial system that linked inland capture, coastal imprisonment and overseas transportation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gmmb.gov.gh/ussher-fort-museum-2007/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gmmb.gov.gh">[Ghana Mining and Minerals Board]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gmmb.gov.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ussher fort museum 2007</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghana Mining and Minerals BoardUssher Fort Museum (2007) – Ghana Museums and Monuments Board...</span></span></span>

## Military detention, courts and twentieth-century imprisonment

One of Ussher Fort's most distinctive characteristics is that imprisonment did not end with the abolition of the Atlantic slave trade. Instead, confinement became one of the fort's defining functions.

Under British colonial administration the fort operated as a prison and detention facility. It also served as a police post, military detention centre and court-martial unit. These successive uses demonstrate how colonial authorities reused existing fortified buildings rather than abandoning them once the slave trade declined. The spaces originally associated with commercial and slave-trading activities instead became part of the machinery of colonial law, punishment and political control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2300" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreGhana inaugurates the rehabilitated Ussher Fort Slave Museum and Documentary Centre - UNESCO World Heritage C...</span></span></span>

Among the best-known prisoners was Kwame Nkrumah, later Ghana's first president, who was imprisoned there during the colonial period because of his political activities in the independence movement. His imprisonment adds another historical layer: the same fort that had once confined enslaved Africans later confined one of the leading figures in Ghana's struggle against colonial rule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gmmb.gov.gh/ussher-fort-crevecoeur-accra/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gmmb.gov.gh">[Ghana Mining and Minerals Board]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gmmb.gov.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gmmb.gov.gh.</span></span></span>

The prison function continued remarkably late. Ussher Fort remained an operational prison until 1993, long after many comparable colonial facilities had ceased serving that purpose. This extended use reinforces the idea that the site's history is not divided neatly into separate eras of trade, slavery and heritage, but instead represents a continuous story of changing forms of incarceration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gmmb.gov.gh/ussher-fort-crevecoeur-accra/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gmmb.gov.gh">[Ghana Mining and Minerals Board]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gmmb.gov.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gmmb.gov.gh.</span></span></span>

The fort's later history is equally revealing. In 2005 parts of the complex were temporarily used as accommodation for refugees from conflicts in Liberia and South Sudan. Although very different from earlier forms of confinement, this episode again transformed the fort into a place associated with displacement and vulnerable populations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/director-general-unesco-tours-world-heritage-site-ghana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Director-General of UNESCO tours World Heritage Site in Ghana</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director-General of UNESCO tours World Heritage Site in Ghana...</span></span></span>

## Reading cells and courtyards through guided interpretation

Visitors today encounter Ussher Fort as both a historic structure and a museum. The museum first opened in 2007 through collaboration between Ghana's Ministry of Tourism, UNESCO and European partners, and after conservation work it reopened in 2021 as the rehabilitated Ussher Fort Slave Museum and Documentary Centre. Restoration focused not simply on preserving masonry but on improving interpretation of the site's complex history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gmmb.gov.gh/ussher-fort-museum-2007/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gmmb.gov.gh">[gmmb.gov.gh]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gmmb.gov.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ussher fort museum 2007</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghana Mining and Minerals BoardUssher Fort Museum (2007) – Ghana Museums and Monuments Board...</span></span></span>

Rather than relying solely on surviving architecture, the exhibitions combine physical spaces with interpretive displays. Collections include shackles, weapons, domestic objects, models illustrating slave ships and European forts, wall paintings explaining the development of the slave trade, and displays introducing major abolitionists including Harriet Tubman, Sojourner Truth and William Wilberforce. These objects are intended to explain processes rather than to create a cabinet of curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gmmb.gov.gh/ussher-fort-museum-2007/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gmmb.gov.gh">[Ghana Mining and Minerals Board]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gmmb.gov.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ussher fort museum 2007</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghana Mining and Minerals BoardUssher Fort Museum (2007) – Ghana Museums and Monuments Board...</span></span></span>

Guided tours encourage visitors to move through the fort chronologically. Cells, passageways and courtyards are interpreted according to their changing functions across different historical periods. A room associated with slave confinement may also be discussed as a later prison cell or detention space, making visitors aware that architecture can outlive the political systems that first created it. This emphasis on continuity distinguishes Ussher Fort from museums that focus almost exclusively on the Atlantic slave trade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gmmb.gov.gh/museums/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gmmb.gov.gh">[Ghana Mining and Minerals Board]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gmmb.gov.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ghana Mining and Minerals Board Museums – Ghana Museums and Monuments Board</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghana Mining and Minerals Board Museums – Ghana Museums and Monuments Board</span></span></span>

## Why Ussher Fort matters in Ghana's unusual heritage


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Within Ghana's landscape of unusual historic sites, Ussher Fort stands apart because its significance lies less in rare artefacts than in the repeated reuse of one place for different forms of human confinement. The fort embodies several overlapping histories: European commercial expansion, the Atlantic slave trade, colonial punishment, nationalist resistance, military justice, refugee accommodation and modern public remembrance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/director-general-unesco-tours-world-heritage-site-ghana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Director-General of UNESCO tours World Heritage Site in Ghana</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director-General of UNESCO tours World Heritage Site in Ghana...</span></span></span>
