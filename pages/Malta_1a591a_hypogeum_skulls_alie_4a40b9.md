## Introduction

The answer is no: there is no credible archaeological evidence that the unusual skulls from [Malta]({{ 'where-malta-keeps-its-strangest-stories/' | relative_url }})'s Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum belonged to aliens, giants or another non-human species. The skulls are genuine prehistoric human remains, excavated from one of Europe's most remarkable underground burial complexes, but decades of speculation have transformed a handful of scientific observations into a much larger modern myth. The real story is more interesting than the conspiracy theories because it shows how early archaeological terminology, incomplete excavations and popular fascination with ancient mysteries combined to create one of Malta's most persistent legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/explore/hal-saflieni-hypogeum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[heritagemalta.mt]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage MaltaĦal Saflieni Hypogeum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

## Are Malta's Hypogeum Skulls Really Alien?

The short answer is no. The "alien skull" story rests on a chain of exaggerations rather than on archaeological evidence.

Early excavators recovered many human bones from the Hypogeum, which had served as a communal burial place over many centuries. Most of the remains were fragile because of the damp underground conditions. Although thousands of bones were found, only a small number of skulls were sufficiently preserved for detailed measurement and description. Temi Zammit's landmark 1912 report discussed eleven measurable skulls and described them as predominantly **dolichocephalic**, meaning relatively long-headed—a recognised anatomical variation in human populations rather than an abnormal or non-human trait. The report interpreted them as belonging to prehistoric inhabitants of Malta, not to a mysterious race.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/9125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.mt.</span></span></span>

Over time, the phrase "long-headed" became detached from its anthropological meaning. Popular books, television programmes and internet articles gradually recast it as evidence for dramatically elongated skulls resembling those associated with artificial cranial modification in parts of South America. Eventually some versions claimed the skulls lacked normal anatomical features or represented extraterrestrials. These claims are not supported by the excavation reports or by modern archaeological institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/news/hal-saflieni-skulls-to-be-studied-in-interdisciplinary-collaboration-under-international-research-grant/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

## What Archaeologists Actually Found Underground

The skulls cannot be understood in isolation from the burial complex itself.

The Hypogeum was used between roughly 4000 BC and 1500 BC as a ritual and funerary complex. Excavators recovered pottery, figurines, ornaments, animal carvings and extensive human remains, indicating that the site served as a communal place where the dead were deposited over many generations rather than as a cemetery of individually marked graves. UNESCO notes that bodies probably underwent stages of decomposition before their bones were gathered together, often with red ochre, suggesting complex funerary practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/explore/hal-saflieni-hypogeum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage MaltaĦal Saflieni Hypogeum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

Several points are often misunderstood:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The skulls are unquestionably human.** No recognised archaeological study has concluded otherwise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/9125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.mt.</span></span></span>
* **Long-headed does not mean artificially elongated.** Dolichocephaly describes one end of the normal range of human skull shapes and was common enough for early physical anthropologists to classify population differences using it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/9125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.mt.</span></span></span>
* **Many excavated bones did not survive intact.** Their poor preservation has contributed to later misunderstandings about what was originally recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/9125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.mt.</span></span></span>

Some early twentieth-century archaeological methods also fall short of modern standards. The site's accidental discovery, early excavation history and incomplete documentation left genuine gaps in the record, creating opportunities for later speculation. Missing documentation, however, is not evidence that extraordinary discoveries were concealed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/explore/hal-saflieni-hypogeum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage MaltaĦal Saflieni Hypogeum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

## How Long-Headed Skulls Became an Alien Story

The transformation from archaeology to conspiracy happened gradually rather than through a single event.

One important factor was changing language. Early physical anthropology routinely classified skulls by proportions using terms such as dolichocephalic and brachycephalic. Outside anthropology, "long-headed" easily became interpreted as visibly elongated in an unusual or even non-human way.

A second factor was the popularity of alternative history. Writers connected Malta with stories about Atlantis, prehistoric giants and ancient advanced civilisations. Once these themes merged with reports of unusual skulls, the leap to extraterrestrial explanations became almost inevitable in fringe literature.

Another persistent claim is that the skulls "disappeared". This is misleading. Surviving Hypogeum skulls remain in Malta's national collections and have been publicly displayed. Heritage Malta has explicitly addressed conspiracy theories through exhibitions such as *Alien Headaches? The Hypogeum Skulls Enigma*, using the museum itself to explain how myths developed from genuine archaeological finds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/news/the-hypogeum-skulls-on-display-at-the-national-museum-of-archaeology/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage MaltaThe Hypogeum skulls on display at the National Museum of Archaeology - Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

Recent announcements by Heritage Malta also show that the surviving skulls are now the subject of new interdisciplinary scientific research rather than hidden away. The institution directly notes that Zammit's brief reference to "long" skulls became the starting point for decades of conspiracy theories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/news/hal-saflieni-skulls-to-be-studied-in-interdisciplinary-collaboration-under-international-research-grant/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>

## Sound, Ritual and the Limits of Interpretation

The skull myths are often linked to another genuine feature of the Hypogeum: its unusual acoustics.

Researchers have measured distinctive resonant frequencies within parts of the underground complex and argue that aspects of its architecture may have been designed to enhance sound. The famous "Oracle Chamber" can amplify certain voices in striking ways, making the experience memorable for visitors today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13697" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Frequency Spectrum and Geometry of the Hal Saflieni Hypogeum Appear TunedOctober 26, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 26, 2020</span></span></span>

This has encouraged some authors to combine several ideas into a single narrative: mysterious acoustics, unusual skulls, hidden knowledge and extraterrestrial builders. Archaeology supports only the first point with confidence.

Current evidence suggests:

## Why the Myth Persists

The Hypogeum occupies a rare position where genuine archaeological mystery overlaps with modern fascination for unexplained phenomena.

Several features encourage myth-making:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The monument is underground and visually unlike most prehistoric sites.
* It contains thousands of human remains, immediately giving it an aura of mystery.
* No written records survive from the people who built it.
* Early excavations inevitably left unanswered questions.
* Visitors encounter striking architecture and remarkable acoustics that feel unusual even today.

</div>

These are real characteristics, but they do not point towards extraterrestrials. Instead, they demonstrate how easily authentic archaeological uncertainty can become filled with speculative stories.

For Malta's wider curiosity landscape, the Hypogeum illustrates an important pattern. Many of the country's most compelling "mysteries" arise not because archaeologists have discovered impossible artefacts, but because incomplete knowledge invites imaginative retellings. The skulls are fascinating precisely because they belong to real prehistoric people whose lives can only be reconstructed through careful scientific study rather than sensational mythology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagemalta.mt/explore/hal-saflieni-hypogeum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagemalta.mt">[Heritage Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagemalta.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage MaltaĦal Saflieni Hypogeum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Malta...</span></span></span>
