## Introduction

The stone figures at Esie in Kwara State are among the greatest archaeological mysteries in West Africa. More than a thousand carved soapstone sculptures, representing men, women and children with remarkably individual features, were discovered around the town centuries ago and are now preserved largely at the National Museum, Esie. Despite decades of archaeological, geological and art-historical research, no consensus exists on exactly who made them, when they were first installed, or why such a large sculptural community was created. That uncertainty has allowed scientific investigation, local folklore and living community memory to coexist rather than replace one another. For visitors interested in [Nigeria]({{ 'where-nigeria-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s strangest heritage, the Esie figures are compelling not because they promise an unexplained supernatural mystery, but because they reveal how evidence, tradition and unanswered questions can exist side by side.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the Esie figures look like

One reason the Esie sculptures continue to fascinate researchers is their extraordinary variety. Rather than repeating a single design, the figures portray a broad cross-section of what appears to have been a complex society.

Many sculptures are seated, although others kneel or stand. They display elaborate hairstyles, necklaces, bracelets, scarification marks, weapons and ceremonial objects. Some appear to represent rulers or wealthy individuals, while others resemble hunters, musicians, attendants or craftspeople. Their faces are highly individual rather than generic, suggesting that the sculptors deliberately distinguished different identities and social roles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

The sculptures also share recognisable artistic conventions. Large heads, almond-shaped eyes and carefully carved hairstyles recur throughout the collection, indicating a coherent artistic tradition despite the diversity of individual figures. Their craftsmanship demonstrates advanced knowledge of carving soapstone, a relatively soft rock that nevertheless requires considerable skill to transform into detailed human portraits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

Researchers have estimated that well over 1,000 figures originally existed, making Esie one of the largest known concentrations of prehistoric or early historic stone sculpture anywhere south of the Sahara. Their importance was recognised early enough that Nigeria established its first national museum at Esie in 1945 specifically to protect them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440307002130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Provenance studies of Esie sculptural soapstone from southwestern Nigeria - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

## What archaeology can and cannot explain

Scientific research has gradually answered some questions while leaving the central mystery unresolved.

One major breakthrough concerns the stone itself. Mineralogical studies, elemental analysis and geological comparison strongly suggest that many of the sculptures were carved from soapstone available within or near the Esie region rather than being imported from a distant civilisation. Earlier speculation about exotic foreign origins has therefore become much less convincing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440307002130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Provenance studies of Esie sculptural soapstone from southwestern Nigeria - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

Dating has also improved. Earlier estimates placed the sculptures somewhere between the twelfth and seventeenth centuries. More recent archaeological work, including radiocarbon dating of associated charcoal and thermoluminescence dating of related materials discussed by recent scholarship, points towards a flourishing sculptural tradition roughly between the late thirteenth and mid-fifteenth centuries. While individual dates remain debated, current evidence generally favours a substantial precolonial Yoruba artistic tradition rather than a much later creation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

Yet several crucial questions remain unanswered:

* No workshop has been conclusively identified.
* No written records describe who commissioned the sculptures.
* Their precise original arrangement is uncertain because many had already been disturbed before systematic archaeological investigation began.
* Their original function—whether commemorative, religious, political or funerary—remains open to interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

This incomplete archaeological context is one reason the figures continue to resist a single definitive explanation.

## Why the makers remain unknown

Over nearly a century of research, several explanations have been proposed.

Some scholars have suggested that the sculptures represented ancestors or important members of a settled community. Others have interpreted them as royal or ritual portraits connected with political authority. Their detailed clothing, scarification and jewellery imply a society with recognised status distinctions rather than anonymous symbolic figures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

Older theories proposed that the sculptures originated outside the local area or represented the work of an otherwise unknown civilisation. Geological evidence has weakened these arguments by demonstrating that the raw material itself was probably sourced locally. Archaeological discoveries of comparable soapstone carvings, including an unfinished figure from the nearby Igbomina region, have further strengthened the case that stone carving formed part of a broader regional artistic tradition rather than an isolated mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?DB=p&amp;RID=182159671" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[Africabib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AfricaBib &#124; The unfinished stone figurine from Pee-Igbomina: results from recent archaeological research in north central Yoruba...</span></span></span>

Even so, "local origin" does not solve the central puzzle. Archaeologists still cannot confidently identify the particular community, political organisation or religious institution responsible for producing such an exceptional body of sculpture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61643/chapter-abstract/539823001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Archaeology of Soapstone Figurines in West Africa &#124; Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Anthropology &#124; Oxford Academic...</span></span></span>

## How folklore reshaped their meaning

Long before archaeologists attempted scientific explanations, local communities developed stories that explained the remarkable stone population.

The best-known tradition describes the figures as people who were transformed into stone through supernatural intervention. Different versions attribute the transformation to divine punishment, disobedience or protection from danger. These stories vary between tellings, but they share a common purpose: explaining why an apparently complete human community exists in carved stone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440307002130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Provenance studies of Esie sculptural soapstone from southwestern Nigeria - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

From an archaeological perspective, these narratives are not treated as historical evidence for the sculptures' manufacture. Instead, they are valued as evidence of something equally important: how later generations interpreted enigmatic monuments whose original meaning had already been forgotten.

This distinction matters. Archaeology investigates physical evidence such as excavation layers, dating techniques and geological sourcing. Folklore records how living communities incorporated mysterious objects into their cultural memory. The two approaches answer different questions rather than competing for the same one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[The Metropolitan Museum of Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why Esie still matters

The Esie figures occupy an unusual place in Nigeria's heritage because they are simultaneously archaeological artefacts, museum collections and living cultural landmarks.

For visitors, the National Museum presents the sculptures as carefully preserved heritage objects, yet the surrounding community continues to associate them with local identity and inherited tradition. The result is a site where scientific research has expanded understanding without eliminating mystery.

Rather than proving spectacular lost-civilisation theories, decades of investigation have produced a subtler picture. Researchers now understand far more about the materials, probable dates and regional context of the sculptures than they did a generation ago. At the same time, the identity of the sculptors, the organisation that commissioned them and their original purpose remain unresolved.

That combination of increasing evidence and enduring uncertainty is precisely what makes the Esie Stone Figures one of Nigeria's most distinctive archaeological curiosities. They are not mysterious because nothing is known about them, but because each new discovery answers one question while revealing how much of their original story has yet to be recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/soapstone-sculptures-esie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metmuseum.org">[metmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Metropolitan Museum of Art The Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Metropolitan Museum of ArtThe Soapstone Sculptures of Èṣìẹ - The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
