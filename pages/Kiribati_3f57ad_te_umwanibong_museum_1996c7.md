## Introduction

Te Umwanibong, the [Kiribati]({{ 'where-kiribatis-strange-collections-tell/' | relative_url }}) National Museum and Cultural Centre in Bikenibeu, South Tarawa, is unusual because it does not treat heritage as a collection of things that belong only to the past. Its purpose is to preserve, protect and promote I-Kiribati culture by keeping alive the knowledge attached to objects, buildings, skills and community practices. Rather than being a conventional museum of rare relics, Te Umwanibong works as a living heritage centre where woven items, fishing equipment, traditional architecture, performances and oral knowledge show how people have adapted to life on coral islands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://internalaffairs.gov.ki/cmd2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internalaffairs.gov.ki">[Internal Affairs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internalaffairs.gov.ki</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internal Affairs Culture and Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internal AffairsCulture and Museum - Ministry of Culture and Internal Affairs...</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in unusual museums, Te Umwanibong offers a different kind of curiosity. The interest is not in mysterious specimens or extraordinary discoveries, but in the hidden knowledge inside everyday materials: how pandanus becomes a woven object, how a meeting house expresses social organisation, how fishing tools record environmental understanding, and how stories give meaning to objects. The museum’s collection is therefore less a storehouse of finished artefacts and more a record of a living relationship between people, land and ocean.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

## Traditional objects and cultural displays

### A museum where ordinary objects carry knowledge

Te Umwanibong’s collections focus on I-Kiribati material culture: objects made and used within island communities rather than objects selected only for rarity or age. The museum displays items connected with traditional meeting houses, pandanus weaving, dance clothing, fishing practices, warfare traditions and local stories. These objects are presented as evidence of skills, values and social systems, not simply as historical decorations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

This approach changes how visitors understand a museum object. A woven mat, for example, is not only a textile. It represents knowledge of plant materials, preparation techniques, patterns, labour and the social occasions in which it might be used. Fishing equipment is not merely a tool; it reflects generations of observation about the sea, seasons and resources around low coral islands. Te Umwanibong’s collections preserve these connections between objects and the practical knowledge behind them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

The museum also includes displays linked to historical events, including a smaller collection relating to warfare and the Second World War. These historical objects sit alongside cultural displays, creating a broader picture of Kiribati life rather than separating “history” from “culture”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

### The importance of the mwaneaba

One of Te Umwanibong’s defining features is that the museum experience extends beyond display cases. The grounds include a traditional mwaneaba (meeting house), built with local materials, alongside museum buildings and gardens. This reflects the institution’s wider idea that heritage includes spaces where communities meet, share knowledge and maintain social relationships.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

The mwaneaba is especially important because it represents more than architecture. In Kiribati society, these meeting houses are connected with community decision-making, ceremony, hospitality and the transmission of knowledge between generations. By including a living architectural form within the museum site, Te Umwanibong preserves a practice rather than only preserving a structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://intersections.anu.edu.au/issue27/whincup.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intersections.anu.edu.au">[Intersections]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intersections.anu.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intersections: The Woman&#x27;s Role in Traditional I-Kiribati DanceNovember 29, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2011</span></span></span>

## Craft skills, architecture and community knowledge

### Preserving skills that cannot be stored in a cabinet

A major challenge for cultural museums is that some heritage cannot be preserved simply by keeping objects safe. Weaving techniques, songs, construction methods and oral traditions depend on people continuing to practise and teach them. Te Umwanibong addresses this by combining collections with cultural activities, documentation and community engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

The museum has been involved in documenting cultural knowledge across Kiribati, including research into local practices and cultural sites. This work reflects the reality that important heritage is spread across many islands rather than concentrated in one building. Cultural mapping activities have helped record traditions, locations and knowledge that might otherwise become harder to access as lifestyles change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://piurn.org/24/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1st-PIURN-Conference-proceeding-_2014.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: piurn.org">[PIURN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">piurn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Réseau de Recherche des Universités du Pacifique Insulaire (PIURN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Réseau de Recherche des Universités du Pacifique Insulaire (PIURN)August 19, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 19, 2024</span></span></span>

This is particularly significant in a country where communities are closely connected to fragile island environments. Traditional knowledge about plants, fishing, construction and resource use is not only cultural memory; it also contains practical lessons about living in an atoll environment. The museum’s role is therefore partly archival and partly educational.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maa.cam.ac.uk/research/maa-research-projects/heritage-matters-culture-and-development-pacific" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maa.cam.ac.uk">[Museum of Archaeology and Anthropology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maa.cam.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cam.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

### The museum as a meeting point between generations

Te Umwanibong was created to strengthen cultural identity, and its activities include exhibitions, workshops and cultural events. The institution has worked with schools, community groups and cultural organisations to encourage awareness of local heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

This community role is what makes the museum different from many traditional collections. A conventional museum often asks visitors to look back at a finished past. Te Umwanibong attempts to connect the past with present practice by showing that cultural knowledge is still used, adapted and passed on.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maa.cam.ac.uk/research/maa-research-projects/heritage-matters-culture-and-development-pacific" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maa.cam.ac.uk">[Museum of Archaeology and Anthropology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maa.cam.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cam.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

## Why Te Umwanibong differs from conventional collections

### A national museum built around living heritage

Many museums define themselves through objects: famous artworks, ancient remains or unusual specimens. Te Umwanibong’s significance comes from a different model. Its collection is valuable because it represents relationships between objects and people. The museum preserves not only what was made, but why it was made and what knowledge surrounds it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

This makes Te Umwanibong particularly relevant within Kiribati’s unusual heritage landscape. The country’s most distinctive collections are not cabinets of unexplained curiosities, but records of adaptation in one of the world’s most environmentally challenging settings. Traditional materials such as coconut fibre, pandanus and local timber reveal how island communities created sophisticated solutions using available resources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

The museum’s approach also reflects a wider shift in Pacific heritage work, where museums are increasingly viewed as places that support community identity, education and sustainable development rather than simply preserving old objects. The University of Cambridge’s Heritage Matters project, which worked with Te Umwanibong, examined how Pacific museums can contribute to areas such as education, sustainable communities and cultural wellbeing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maa.cam.ac.uk/research/maa-research-projects/heritage-matters-culture-and-development-pacific" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maa.cam.ac.uk">[Museum of Archaeology and Anthropology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maa.cam.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cam.ac.uk.</span></span></span>

### Limits and challenges of keeping heritage alive

The living heritage approach also creates difficulties. Objects can be conserved in controlled conditions, but skills and traditions require people, time and resources. Research into Te Umwanibong has noted challenges including limited capacity, the need for stronger public engagement and the difficulty of balancing preservation work with wider community needs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readkong.com/page/te-umwanibong-culture-and-development-in-kiribati-2656081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readkong.com">[Readkong]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readkong.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TE UMWANIBONG, CULTURE AND DEVELOPMENT IN KIRIBATI - TE UMWANIBONG Museum &amp; Culture Centre...</span></span></span>

There is also a broader question about ownership and representation. Many cultural objects and practices are connected to families, islands or communities rather than belonging simply to a national institution. A museum preserving living heritage must therefore work carefully between collecting, documenting and respecting the communities from which traditions come.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://piurn.org/24/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1st-PIURN-Conference-proceeding-_2014.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: piurn.org">[PIURN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">piurn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Réseau de Recherche des Universités du Pacifique Insulaire (PIURN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Réseau de Recherche des Universités du Pacifique Insulaire (PIURN)August 19, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 19, 2024</span></span></span>

For visitors, this is what makes Te Umwanibong distinctive. It is not a museum of a vanished culture.
