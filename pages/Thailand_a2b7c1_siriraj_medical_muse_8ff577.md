## Introduction

Bangkok’s Siriraj Medical Museum is one of [Thailand]({{ 'where-thailand-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})’s most unusual public collections because it places the human body, disease and death at the centre of a museum experience. Often nicknamed the “Museum of Death”, it is not a horror attraction but a group of medical teaching collections created to support anatomy, pathology, parasitology and forensic science. Its preserved organs, anatomical specimens, disease examples and crime-related evidence can feel unsettling, yet the purpose is educational: to show how medicine investigates the body and learns from illness, injury and mortality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www2.si.mahidol.ac.th/museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: www2.si.mahidol.ac.th">[Mahidol University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">www2.si.mahidol.ac.th</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mahidol University Siriraj Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mahidol University Siriraj Museum</span></span></span>

The museum’s importance within Thailand’s strange-history landscape comes from this tension between curiosity and science. Visitors arrive expecting something macabre, but the deeper story is about how medical institutions preserve difficult material to train doctors, explain public health and record human experiences that would otherwise disappear. Siriraj is therefore less a “museum of death” than a museum about how society studies life through death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www2.si.mahidol.ac.th/en/forensic-medicine/forensic-medicine-about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: www2.si.mahidol.ac.th">[Mahidol University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">www2.si.mahidol.ac.th</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mahidol UniversityForensic Medicine – About – Faculty of Medicine Siriraj Hospital, Mahidol UniversityJanuary 4, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 4, 2017</span></span></span>

## Why Siriraj became Thailand’s famous “Museum of Death”

Siriraj Medical Museum developed from the teaching collections of the Faculty of Medicine Siriraj Hospital, Thailand’s oldest modern medical school and one of the country’s most important medical institutions. The collection is not a single exhibition but a network of specialist museums, including anatomical, pathological, forensic, parasitological and historical displays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siriraj_Medical_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Siriraj Medical Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siriraj Medical Museum</span></span></span>

The unusual reputation comes mainly from the fact that many museums hide medical material from public view, while Siriraj places carefully preserved specimens in a visitor setting. A person walking through the galleries may see real human organs affected by disease, anatomical preparations showing body systems, examples of parasites and forensic displays linked to investigations of unnatural death. These objects are presented as evidence rather than curiosities, with the aim of explaining how doctors identify conditions, understand anatomy and investigate causes of death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.si.mahidol.ac.th/en/department/parasitology/museum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: si.mahidol.ac.th">[Mahidol University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">si.mahidol.ac.th</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mahidol University Parasitology Museum &#124; SIRIRAJ Parasitology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mahidol University Parasitology Museum &#124; SIRIRAJ Parasitology</span></span></span>

This makes Siriraj different from traditional “oddity” collections. The museum does not claim that the bodies and specimens are mysterious or supernatural. Instead, the unusual element is the decision to display normally hidden aspects of human biology and medical history in a public institution.

## Pathology and forensic collections show how medicine reads the body

### Disease preserved as medical evidence

The pathological collections at Siriraj focus on what happens when normal body processes are disrupted. Rather than showing illness through photographs alone, the displays preserve physical examples of disease, allowing medical students and visitors to see changes caused by conditions such as cancers, developmental abnormalities and organ damage. [The Siriraj<br />Medical Museum](https://sirirajmedicalmuseum.weebly.com/cultural-memory.html)

The Ellis Pathological Museum is one of the oldest parts of the collection. It reflects the early development of pathology in Thailand, where doctors used preserved specimens to improve diagnosis and medical teaching. The collection demonstrates a key principle of medical museums: a specimen is not valuable because it is unusual alone, but because it reveals information about a biological process. [The Siriraj<br />Medical Museum](https://sirirajmedicalmuseum.weebly.com/cultural-memory.html)

For visitors, this changes the experience. A preserved organ may initially appear shocking, but the intended question is not “what strange thing is this?” but “what does this tell us about how the body works and how disease develops?”

### Forensic displays turn death into investigation

The Songkran Niyomsane Forensic Medicine Museum is the part most closely associated with Siriraj’s darker reputation. It developed from Thailand’s forensic medicine programme, which began providing forensic services in the early 1950s under Dr Songkran Niyomsane, a pioneer of Thai forensic pathology. The department investigated deaths and evidence from police cases while also teaching medical students.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www2.si.mahidol.ac.th/en/forensic-medicine/forensic-medicine-about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: www2.si.mahidol.ac.th">[Mahidol University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">www2.si.mahidol.ac.th</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mahidol UniversityForensic Medicine – About – Faculty of Medicine Siriraj Hospital, Mahidol UniversityJanuary 4, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 4, 2017</span></span></span>

Forensic medicine differs from ordinary pathology because it focuses on questions surrounding death: how someone died, whether injuries were accidental or intentional, and what evidence can reveal about an event. The museum’s displays include forensic specimens and materials used to demonstrate methods of investigation, such as identifying injuries and reconstructing circumstances around deaths.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www2.si.mahidol.ac.th/en/forensic-medicine/forensic-medicine-about/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: www2.si.mahidol.ac.th">[Mahidol University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">www2.si.mahidol.ac.th</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mahidol UniversityForensic Medicine – About – Faculty of Medicine Siriraj Hospital, Mahidol UniversityJanuary 4, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 4, 2017</span></span></span>

This is one reason the museum attracts visitors interested in crime history. However, its original purpose was not crime entertainment. The collection belongs to a medical and scientific tradition where difficult evidence is used to train professionals and improve justice systems.

## The Si Ouey display and the ethics of exhibiting human remains

The most controversial object associated with Siriraj was the preserved body of Si Ouey Sae Ung, a Chinese-Thai man executed in 1959 after being convicted of murdering children. For decades, his preserved remains were displayed in the forensic museum and became one of Thailand’s most recognisable examples of a death-related exhibit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siriraj_Medical_Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Siriraj Medical Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siriraj Medical Museum</span></span></span>

Over time, the display became controversial. Critics argued that presenting Si Ouey primarily as a symbol of evil ignored questions about his trial, his social background and the possibility that aspects of the case had been oversimplified. The exhibition also raised wider museum questions: when does a forensic teaching object become a public spectacle, and how should institutions treat the remains of real people?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Si_Ouey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Si Ouey</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Si Ouey</span></span></span>

Siriraj removed the body from public display in 2019 after public debate and discussions involving human-rights officials. His remains were later cremated in 2020. The case became an important example of how medical museums must balance historical evidence, education and respect for human dignity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationthailand.com/in-focus/30374402" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationthailand.com">[nationthailand]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationthailand.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Siriraj Hospital still mulling final destiny for Si Quey’s corpse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siriraj Hospital still mulling final destiny for Si Quey’s corpse...</span></span></span>

The controversy does not make the entire museum questionable; instead, it highlights a central issue for medical collections worldwide. Human remains carry scientific value, but they also represent individual lives. Modern museums increasingly face the challenge of explaining specimens without reducing people to objects.

## Teaching medicine through unusual specimens

The reason Siriraj remains significant is that its strange-looking exhibits have practical scientific uses. Medical students learn anatomy by studying real structures rather than relying only on diagrams, while visitors can see the physical effects of disease and injury in ways that textbooks cannot fully reproduce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sirirajgrad.com/en/life-in-siriraj/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sirirajgrad.com">[Siriraj Grad]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sirirajgrad.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">life in siriraj</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siriraj GradLife in Siriraj – งานการศึกษาระดับหลังปริญญา คณะแพทยศาสตร์ศิริราชพยาบาล มหาวิทยาลัยมหิดลJuly 6, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 6, 2017</span></span></span>

The Congdon Anatomical Museum is a major example. Established during the development of modern anatomy education at Siriraj, it contains extensive anatomical preparations, including detailed displays of body systems. Some of its most notable specimens demonstrate the nervous system and circulatory structures preserved for teaching purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museumthailand.com/en/museum/Congdons-Anatomical-Museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museumthailand.com">[Museum Thailand]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museumthailand.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum Thailand Congdon’s Anatomical Museum:: Museum Thailand</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum Thailand Congdon’s Anatomical Museum:: Museum Thailand</span></span></span>

The Parasitology Museum adds another unusual dimension by focusing on organisms that affect human health. It displays parasites, life cycles and examples of diseases linked to tropical environments, showing how public health depends on understanding relationships between humans, animals and microorganisms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.si.mahidol.ac.th/en/department/parasitology/museum.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: si.mahidol.ac.th">[Mahidol University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">si.mahidol.ac.th</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mahidol University Parasitology Museum &#124; SIRIRAJ Parasitology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mahidol University Parasitology Museum &#124; SIRIRAJ Parasitology</span></span></span>

Together, these collections show why Siriraj belongs in Thailand’s unusual museum landscape. Its “strangeness” comes not from fantasy or folklore but from exposing hidden biological realities.

## Visiting Siriraj: what visitors should expect

Siriraj is unlike a conventional history museum. The experience is quiet, clinical and sometimes emotionally challenging. Visitors should expect real medical specimens rather than replicas, and some displays may be disturbing for people uncomfortable with human remains, disease or forensic material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/museums/siriraj-medical-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timeout.com">[Time Out Worldwide]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timeout.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on timeout.com.</span></span></span>

The museum is best understood as a place of medical interpretation rather than a dark-tourism attraction. People interested in anatomy, medicine, criminology, medical history or unusual collections often find it memorable because it answers questions that ordinary museums rarely address: what happens inside the body, how doctors understand death, and how scientific knowledge is built from physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muic.mahidol.ac.th/en/news/siriraj-medical-museum-tour" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muic.mahidol.ac.th">[MUIC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muic.mahidol.ac.th</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUICSiriraj Medical Museum Tour – &#124; MUIC Mahidol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MUICSiriraj Medical Museum Tour – &#124; MUIC Mahidol</span></span></span>

At the same time, visitors should approach the collection with respect. The specimens are connected to real patients, donors and victims, not fictional monsters or curiosities. The most thoughtful way to view Siriraj is to see the displays as records of human experience: evidence of illness, survival, suffering and the continuing effort to understand the body.

## Why Siriraj matters in Thailand’s strange-history landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Siriraj occupies a unique position among Thailand’s unusual collections because it sits between museum, laboratory and historical archive. It shares some of the emotional impact of collections built around death, but its foundation is scientific education rather than entertainment.

</div>

Its lasting fascination comes from a simple but powerful idea: the human body itself can become a historical record. A diseased organ, an anatomical preparation or a forensic specimen can reveal stories about medicine, society and the limits of human knowledge. Siriraj’s displays are unusual because they make visitors confront subjects normally kept out of sight — not to create fear, but to show how science turns difficult realities into understanding.
