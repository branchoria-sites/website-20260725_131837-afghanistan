## Introduction

Were there really witches at Zugarramurdi? The historical evidence points to a very different story from the one suggested by the village's popular reputation. Zugarramurdi, in northern Navarre, became famous because of one of Europe's best-known witchcraft prosecutions, yet the surviving records reveal a panic driven by rumour, fear, coerced confessions and official investigation rather than proven supernatural practice. The village's Witchcraft Museum deliberately reframes the story as one of persecution and collective belief, not evidence that witches existed. That distinction makes Zugarramurdi one of [Spain]({{ 'what-makes-spains-strangest-museums/' | relative_url }})'s most thought-provoking sites of unusual heritage: visitors encounter both enduring folklore and a careful examination of how ordinary people became victims of extraordinary accusations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.turismozugarramurdi.com/museo-de-las-brujas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: turismozugarramurdi.com">[Turismo Zugarramurdi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">turismozugarramurdi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Turismo Zugarramurdi Museo de las Brujas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turismo ZugarramurdiMuseo de las Brujas - Turismo ZugarramurdiApril 12, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2023</span></span></span>

## What happened during the Basque witch trials?

The events usually called the Zugarramurdi witch trials formed part of a much wider outbreak of accusations across the western Pyrenees between 1609 and 1614. Although the village gave the episode its name, investigations eventually spread across dozens of communities in Navarre and neighbouring Basque territories. The trials generated an unusually large documentary record because so many people were questioned, confessed or accused.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.law.yale.edu/news/largest-witch-hunt-world-history-basque-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.law.yale.edu">[Lillian Goldman Law Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.law.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">largest witch hunt world history basque witch trials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lillian Goldman Law LibraryThe Largest Witch Hunt in World History: The Basque Witch Trials (1609-1614) &#124; Lillian Goldman Law Library...</span></span></span>

The panic began after stories circulated about secret nocturnal gatherings. Testimony rapidly expanded through denunciations, gossip and confessions, many of which were extracted under intense pressure. Children accused adults, neighbours accused neighbours, and increasingly elaborate descriptions of supposed meetings, flights and ceremonies emerged. Modern historians generally treat these accounts as products of social pressure, imagination, religious expectation and coercive questioning rather than eyewitness evidence of organised witchcraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.law.yale.edu/news/largest-witch-hunt-world-history-basque-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.law.yale.edu">[Lillian Goldman Law Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.law.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">largest witch hunt world history basque witch trials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lillian Goldman Law LibraryThe Largest Witch Hunt in World History: The Basque Witch Trials (1609-1614) &#124; Lillian Goldman Law Library...</span></span></span>

The most dramatic public moment came with the auto-da-fé in Logroño in November 1610. Six people were executed, while five others who had already died were condemned symbolically through the burning of their remains or effigies. Far more suspects survived the proceedings than popular legend often suggests, but thousands of examinations and over 11,000 pages of testimony illustrate how extensive the investigation became.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.law.yale.edu/news/largest-witch-hunt-world-history-basque-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.law.yale.edu">[Lillian Goldman Law Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.law.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">largest witch hunt world history basque witch trials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lillian Goldman Law LibraryThe Largest Witch Hunt in World History: The Basque Witch Trials (1609-1614) &#124; Lillian Goldman Law Library...</span></span></span>

One reason the Zugarramurdi case remains historically important is what happened afterwards. Inquisitor Alonso de Salazar y Frías reviewed many of the accusations, searched for independent evidence and concluded that confessions alone could not establish the reality of witchcraft. His sceptical investigation influenced new procedural rules issued by the Spanish Inquisition in 1614, making unsupported accusations far harder to prosecute and contributing to the rapid decline of major witch trials in Spain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.law.yale.edu/news/largest-witch-hunt-world-history-basque-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.law.yale.edu">[Lillian Goldman Law Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.law.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">largest witch hunt world history basque witch trials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lillian Goldman Law LibraryThe Largest Witch Hunt in World History: The Basque Witch Trials (1609-1614) &#124; Lillian Goldman Law Library...</span></span></span>

## How the museum separates folklore from prosecution

The Witchcraft Museum, opened in 2007 in the village's former hospital, does not present itself as a collection of magical objects proving that witches existed. Instead, it reconstructs the social world in which accusations flourished. Audiovisual displays, reconstructed settings and interactive exhibits explain everyday rural life, religious belief, local traditions and the mechanisms that transformed suspicion into criminal prosecution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.turismozugarramurdi.com/museo-de-las-brujas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: turismozugarramurdi.com">[Turismo Zugarramurdi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">turismozugarramurdi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Turismo Zugarramurdi Museo de las Brujas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turismo ZugarramurdiMuseo de las Brujas - Turismo ZugarramurdiApril 12, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2023</span></span></span>

A recurring theme is the distinction between three very different ideas that are often merged together:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Traditional folklore**, including stories about mysterious gatherings and supernatural beings.
* **Popular healing and herbal knowledge**, which formed part of ordinary village life.
* **The Inquisition's legal narrative**, which interpreted rumours and confessions as evidence of organised devil worship.

</div>

Rather than celebrating witchcraft, the museum explicitly presents the accused as victims of a wider social panic. Official descriptions emphasise remembrance, historical context and respect for those whose lives were destroyed, while rejecting familiar stereotypes of broomsticks, spells and fantasy witches as historical fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.turismozugarramurdi.com/museo-de-las-brujas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: turismozugarramurdi.com">[turismozugarramurdi.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">turismozugarramurdi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Turismo Zugarramurdi Museo de las Brujas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turismo ZugarramurdiMuseo de las Brujas - Turismo ZugarramurdiApril 12, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2023</span></span></span>

The exhibition also places local mythology alongside historical documentation instead of treating them as equivalent forms of evidence. Visitors therefore encounter legends because they shaped popular imagination, not because the museum claims they literally occurred. This evidence-led approach distinguishes Zugarramurdi from many commercial "witch museums" elsewhere in Europe that lean primarily on theatrical atmosphere.

## Why the caves still shape the village's identity

The famous caves remain central to Zugarramurdi's public identity because they occupy both the landscape of history and the landscape of legend. A large natural cave cut by a stream became associated in popular tradition with gatherings of witches, an image reinforced over centuries through literature, art, festivals and modern films. Today the cave is one of Navarre's best-known visitor attractions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitnavarra.es/en/web/guest/caves-navarra/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitnavarra.es">[Visit Navarra]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitnavarra.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visit Navarra Zugarramurdi and other caves &#124; Visit Navarra</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit NavarraZugarramurdi and other caves &#124; Visit Navarra - Official Website of the Navarre Tourist Board...</span></span></span>

The village has chosen neither to reject nor to uncritically embrace its reputation. Instead, the caves, museum and surrounding heritage work together to explain why the legends became so powerful. Visitors may come expecting tales of the supernatural, but they leave with a stronger understanding of how fear, folklore and official authority combined to create one of Europe's most famous witchcraft scares.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.turismozugarramurdi.com/museo-de-las-brujas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: turismozugarramurdi.com">[Turismo Zugarramurdi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">turismozugarramurdi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Turismo Zugarramurdi Museo de las Brujas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turismo ZugarramurdiMuseo de las Brujas - Turismo ZugarramurdiApril 12, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 12, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Zugarramurdi still matters

For anyone interested in Spain's stranger heritage, Zugarramurdi is unusual because its greatest curiosity is not an artefact but a change in historical understanding. The village demonstrates how a place can become permanently associated with the supernatural even when the surviving evidence increasingly points towards human psychology, judicial error and social panic.

That makes the site significant beyond local folklore. It illustrates how myths develop from real events, how institutions can later reassess their own actions, and how museums can preserve dramatic stories without endorsing them as fact. In Spain's landscape of unusual museums and curious heritage, Zugarramurdi stands out not because it proves witches were real, but because it shows how belief, fear and authority once convinced thousands that they were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.law.yale.edu/news/largest-witch-hunt-world-history-basque-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.law.yale.edu">[yale.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.law.yale.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">largest witch hunt world history basque witch trials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lillian Goldman Law LibraryThe Largest Witch Hunt in World History: The Basque Witch Trials (1609-1614) &#124; Lillian Goldman Law Library...</span></span></span>
