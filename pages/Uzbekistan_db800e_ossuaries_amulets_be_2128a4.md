## Introduction

Ancient [Uzbekistan]({{ 'where-uzbekistan-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}) preserves some of Central Asia's most intriguing evidence for how people understood death, spiritual danger and supernatural protection. Rather than burying whole bodies immediately, some pre-Islamic communities collected cleaned bones in decorated clay containers known as ossuaries, reflecting religious ideas about purity and the proper relationship between the dead and the natural world. Alongside these funerary objects, museums display jewellery, pendants and small personal ornaments that were not simply decorative but were believed to protect their wearers from illness, misfortune or hostile spiritual forces. Together, these artefacts reveal that ancient belief systems survive not only in monumental ruins but also in the intimate objects people carried, wore and entrusted with their dead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://iccu.uz/en/news/313" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iccu.uz">[iccu.uz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iccu.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Artifact: Ossuary &#124; Islamic Civilization Center in Uzbekistan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Artifact: Ossuary &#124; Islamic Civilization Center in Uzbekistan</span></span></span>

## How ossuaries fitted Zoroastrian funerary practice

One of the most unusual archaeological collections in Uzbekistan consists of clay ossuaries from ancient Khorezm and neighbouring regions. These containers held the cleaned bones of the deceased after an earlier stage of funerary ritual. Modern visitors often mistake them for ordinary urns or coffins, but they belonged to a very different understanding of death.

In the Zoroastrian tradition, a dead body was considered capable of polluting the sacred elements of earth, water and fire. Rather than placing a corpse directly into the ground, communities exposed it until birds and natural processes removed the flesh. Only afterwards were the purified bones gathered and placed inside specially made ossuaries, which could then be stored in family burial places or dedicated structures. This sequence was intended to preserve ritual purity rather than to dramatise death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.meros.uz/en/object/shilpiq-qala" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: meros.uz">[meros.uz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">meros.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ChilpykApril 23, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2015</span></span></span>

The surviving containers are far from plain. Some are rectangular, while others imitate miniature buildings with arched façades, columns or sculpted human figures. Relief decoration sometimes depicts priests, ceremonial scenes or symbolic motifs associated with ideas of renewal and the continuation of existence after death. Their artistic quality reminds visitors that they were meaningful ritual objects rather than simple storage vessels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://iccu.uz/en/news/313" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iccu.uz">[iccu.uz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iccu.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Artifact: Ossuary &#124; Islamic Civilization Center in Uzbekistan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Artifact: Ossuary &#124; Islamic Civilization Center in Uzbekistan</span></span></span>

Museums in Uzbekistan, including the State Museum of History of Uzbekistan and the [Savitsky Museum]({{ 'savitsky-museum/' | relative_url }}) in Nukus, preserve some of the largest collections of these ossuaries. Display cases therefore represent the final stage of a much longer ritual process that originally involved exposure, bone collection and carefully managed remembrance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://society.uz/news/detail/news/1065" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: society.uz">[society.uz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">society.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on society.uz.</span></span></span>

## Chilpyk and the landscape of exposure rites

No site explains this funerary system better than Chilpyk in Karakalpakstan. Rising above the surrounding plain on a natural hill, the circular mud-brick structure is widely interpreted as a dakhma, or "Tower of Silence", where bodies were exposed before their bones were collected.

The monument's elevated position was practical as well as symbolic. Exposure to birds and the elements formed part of a ritual intended to avoid contaminating the earth with decaying flesh. Archaeological investigations around the monument have uncovered clay and stone ossuaries, reinforcing the connection between the tower and the secondary burial of cleaned bones. Today, several of those finds are preserved in museums in Nukus and Tashkent, allowing visitors to connect the landscape with the artefacts displayed indoors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.meros.uz/en/object/shilpiq-qala" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: meros.uz">[meros.uz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">meros.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ChilpykApril 23, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2015</span></span></span>

Although Chilpyk is frequently presented to tourists as a mysterious "Tower of Silence", archaeologists remain careful about separating evidence from later storytelling. The site's identification as a Zoroastrian funerary monument is supported by its form, associated finds and regional burial traditions, but many details of the ceremonies carried out there remain impossible to reconstruct with certainty. The monument is therefore both a powerful heritage site and a reminder that archaeological interpretation is necessarily incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/fr/listesindicatives/6902/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chilpik the Tower of Silence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChilpik the Tower of Silence - UNESCO Centre du patrimoine mondial...</span></span></span>

## When jewellery also served as protective magic

Death rituals formed only one side of ancient protective belief. Everyday life also involved objects intended to guard the living from unseen dangers.

Many ornaments excavated from ancient settlements in Uzbekistan had practical and symbolic roles at the same time. Pendants, beads, metal plaques and decorated jewellery were often worn because their materials, shapes or images were believed to offer protection against illness, bad fortune or harmful supernatural forces. Modern museums frequently display these pieces as examples of craftsmanship, but their original owners probably understood them as active forms of spiritual defence.

Animal imagery appears repeatedly in Central Asian material culture. Camels, birds and powerful creatures could carry meanings linked to divine protection, strength or renewal. A remarkable example is the camel-shaped ossuary preserved in the Savitsky Museum, whose decorative symbols have been linked by researchers to Khorezmian royal and mythological traditions. While it functioned as a funerary container rather than personal jewellery, it demonstrates how symbolic animals could bridge ideas of protection, identity and the afterlife.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://society.uz/news/detail/news/894" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: society.uz">[society.uz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">society.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ossuary in the form of reclining camel found in Anka-kala</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ossuary in the form of reclining camel found in Anka-kala</span></span></span>

Protective objects also remind visitors that ancient religion was rarely divided neatly into separate categories of "magic" and "faith". What modern observers describe as amulets or magical jewellery often formed an ordinary part of religious life, expressing confidence that properly made objects could help maintain harmony between human beings, ancestors and the unseen world.

## Why museums can hide the original meaning

Displaying ossuaries and protective ornaments in museum cases inevitably changes how they are understood. A beautifully decorated ossuary can appear to be an artistic ceramic sculpture unless its ritual purpose is explained. Likewise, necklaces or pendants may seem to be purely decorative unless visitors learn that they were intended to defend their owners spiritually as well as socially.

This makes interpretation especially important in Uzbekistan's archaeological museums. The most effective displays place these artefacts within the wider landscape of ancient Khorezm, showing how exposure sites such as Chilpyk, family ossuaries and protective personal objects formed parts of the same cultural system. Instead of isolated curiosities, they become evidence of a coherent worldview in which death, purity and protection were closely connected.

For travellers interested in Uzbekistan's stranger heritage, these collections are valuable precisely because they avoid sensationalism. They reveal that practices which may seem unusual today were once practical expressions of deeply held beliefs about the relationship between people, nature and the sacred—a perspective that shaped everyday life across parts of Central Asia for many centuries before Islamic burial traditions became dominant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://society.uz/news/detail/news/1065" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: society.uz">[society.uz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">society.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on society.uz.</span></span></span>

    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
