## Introduction

Koagannu on the island of Hulhumeedhoo is far more than a historic burial ground. It is the [Maldives]({{ 'maldives/' | relative_url }})' largest surviving cemetery complex and one of the country's richest open-air archives, where more than 1,500 carved coral-stone gravemarkers preserve centuries of information about religion, family, social rank, craftsmanship and local history. Rather than storing records in shelves or display cases, Koagannu records them in stone, spread across a living landscape that continues to function as a place of remembrance and worship. For anyone interested in the Maldives' unusual heritage, it offers one of the clearest examples of how a cemetery can also serve as a historical collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maldives-heritage-survey/15F61521DF052F7FEA4CA8AEEEA554FC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Maldives Heritage Survey &#124; Antiquity &#124; Cambridge CoreApril 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2021</span></span></span>

Unlike a conventional museum, Koagannu has never been assembled for exhibition. Its monuments remain where they were created, allowing visitors to read the changing history of southern Maldives through architecture, inscriptions, carving styles and the arrangement of graves. This combination of archaeological importance, religious significance and artistic survival makes it one of the country's most distinctive heritage landscapes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maldives-heritage-survey/15F61521DF052F7FEA4CA8AEEEA554FC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Maldives Heritage Survey &#124; Antiquity &#124; Cambridge CoreApril 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why Koagannu Is More Than a Cemetery

Located on the northern shore of Hulhumeedhoo in Addu Atoll, Koagannu occupies roughly one hectare close to the sea. Surveys have documented 1,535 carved coral gravestones together with several historic mosques, mausoleums, wells and other religious structures, making it the largest known historic cemetery in the Maldives. Heritage researchers regard it as an exceptionally complete survival of a coral-built religious landscape rather than simply a burial ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maldives-heritage-survey/15F61521DF052F7FEA4CA8AEEEA554FC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[cambridge.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Maldives Heritage Survey &#124; Antiquity &#124; Cambridge CoreApril 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2021</span></span></span>

The cemetery developed over many centuries as Hulhumeedhoo became an important regional centre of Islamic learning. Local tradition associates the site with Yousuf Naib, who is credited in island tradition with introducing Islam to the area, while archaeological research suggests that the landscape may have been used even before Islam became established. Excavation and architectural analysis indicate several distinct phases of construction and reuse, reflecting the island's changing religious and political history rather than a single period of development.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

For visitors exploring the Maldives' more unusual heritage, Koagannu demonstrates that historical collections need not be housed inside museum walls. Every grave marker, carved panel and coral-built structure contributes another piece to a centuries-long archive that remains largely in its original setting.

## Gravestones, Mosques and Mausoleums

The most striking feature of Koagannu is the extraordinary density of carved coral monuments. The grave markers were fashioned from massive blocks of *Porites* coral, a material once quarried from ancient reef formations, carefully dried and then carved with remarkable precision. The stone's relatively soft texture when freshly cut allowed artisans to produce intricate geometric patterns, floral ornament and inscriptions before the coral hardened.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

The cemetery also preserves an interconnected religious complex rather than isolated graves. Today visitors encounter:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Four surviving coral-stone mosques dating from the late fourteenth to sixteenth centuries.
* A sheltered shrine-mausoleum associated with an important local religious figure.
* Numerous open mausoleums belonging to prominent families.
* Historic wells and other structures linked to communal religious life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

</div>

Researchers believe additional mosques once stood within the compound, maintained by different noble families until the twentieth century. Although only four survive today, the arrangement still illustrates how burial, worship and family identity were integrated within a single sacred landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

One particularly remarkable monument is believed to be the world's tallest known single-piece *Porites* coral gravestone, standing approximately 2.4 metres high. Its survival demonstrates both the ambition of Maldivian coral craftsmanship and the availability of exceptionally large coral blocks before modern conservation restrictions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

## How Carving Records Identity and Status

Koagannu functions as an archive because its gravestones communicate far more than names and dates. Their shapes, scale, decoration and inscriptions reveal distinctions that written records alone often cannot.

Large, elaborately carved monuments generally mark individuals of higher social standing, including scholars, judges, nobles and respected religious figures. Simpler markers usually indicate more ordinary burials. Decorative programmes vary across the cemetery, allowing specialists to identify changing artistic fashions as well as differences in wealth and prestige.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

The carvings themselves also preserve evidence of wider Indian Ocean connections. Architectural details, ornamental motifs and construction techniques reflect exchanges with neighbouring Islamic societies while retaining distinctly Maldivian approaches to coral architecture. Researchers note that some decorative traditions also hint at continuities from earlier Buddhist artistic practices, illustrating how cultural change often involved adaptation rather than complete replacement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

For historians, Koagannu therefore operates much like a documentary archive:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Names and inscriptions** identify individuals and family networks.
* **Grave size and form** reflect social hierarchy.
* **Carving styles** help date monuments and trace artistic development.
* **Mosques and mausoleums** reveal patterns of patronage and religious authority.
* **The layout of the cemetery** records how the community expanded and organised sacred space over centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

</div>

Unlike paper archives, however, these records are inseparable from the landscape in which they were created.

## A Living Heritage Landscape Under Threat

Koagannu remains an active place of cultural and religious significance rather than an abandoned archaeological site. That living role adds richness but also creates conservation challenges.

Its position only a short distance from the shoreline leaves the cemetery vulnerable to coastal erosion, storm surges and rising environmental pressures. Coral stone, although durable, gradually deteriorates through salt exposure, biological growth and weathering. Modern repairs carried out with unsuitable materials have also altered the appearance of some historic structures, while acts of vandalism have damaged carved gravestones in recent years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

## Visiting a Living Heritage Landscape

Visitors should approach Koagannu with the expectations they would bring to an active historic cemetery rather than an outdoor sculpture park. Respectful behaviour and modest dress are appropriate because the site retains religious and community importance alongside its archaeological value.

The experience differs markedly from a museum visit. There are no galleries or display cases guiding a route through curated exhibits. Instead, the reward comes from observing how architecture, gravestones and open space combine into a landscape that still conveys centuries of memory. Looking closely reveals subtle differences in carving, monument size and craftsmanship that explain why historians describe Koagannu as an archive written in coral rather than paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp/koagannu-an-endangered-heritage-site-in-the-maldives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp">[Maritime Asia Heritage Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maritimeasiaheritage.cseas.kyoto-u.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maritime Asia Heritage SurveyKOAGANNU – AN ENDANGERED HERITAGE SITE IN THE MALDIVES - Maritime Asia Heritage Survey...</span></span></span>

Within the Maldives' unusual heritage, Koagannu stands as perhaps the country's finest example of an open-air collection whose value lies not in rare portable objects but in the survival of an entire historical environment. It preserves faith, family history, artistic traditions and local identity in the very material from which the islands themselves were built.
