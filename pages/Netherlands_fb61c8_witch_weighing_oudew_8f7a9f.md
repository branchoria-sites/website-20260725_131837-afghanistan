## Introduction

The witch weighing test at Oudewater is one of the [Netherlands]({{ 'netherlands/' | relative_url }})' most unusual historical traditions because it combined a genuine civic institution with one of early modern Europe's darkest episodes: the persecution of alleged witches. Unlike many places where accusations could end in torture or execution, Oudewater's official public weigh house became known for issuing certificates that could help an accused person demonstrate their innocence. The weighing did not prove that witchcraft existed. Instead, it showed how local administration and trusted civic procedures could sometimes interrupt fear-driven prosecutions. Today, Museum De Heksenwaag preserves this story through its original fifteenth-century scales, exhibitions on witch persecution and a ceremonial weighing that has become an educational visitor experience rather than a test of belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heksenwaag.nl/en/about-the-weigh-house/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heksenwaag.nl">[Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heksenwaag.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater About the weigh house</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterAbout the weigh house - Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2021</span></span></span>

## Could a Weighing Scale Save an Accused Witch?

The idea behind the weighing test rested on a widespread early modern belief that witches were unnaturally light because they could supposedly fly on broomsticks or had been altered through a pact with the Devil. If someone weighed less than expected, this was sometimes presented elsewhere as suspicious "evidence". Modern historians regard this as superstition rather than science, but it influenced real legal proceedings in parts of Europe during the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heksenwaag.nl/en/about-the-weigh-house/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heksenwaag.nl">[Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heksenwaag.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater About the weigh house</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterAbout the weigh house - Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2021</span></span></span>

Oudewater's importance lay not in accepting that belief, but in insisting on an honest weighing process. The town already possessed a respected public weigh house used for commerce, where accurate measurements mattered for taxation and trade. Because its scales had a reputation for reliability, people accused of witchcraft travelled there seeking an official weighing rather than risking manipulated tests elsewhere. According to the museum and local historical records, Holy Roman Emperor Charles V granted Oudewater the privilege of issuing recognised certificates confirming the fairness of its weighing process, giving the documents authority beyond the town itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heksenwaag.nl/en/about-the-weigh-house/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heksenwaag.nl">[Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heksenwaag.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater About the weigh house</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterAbout the weigh house - Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2021</span></span></span>

## How the Oudewater Weighing Test Worked

The weighing was treated as an official civic procedure rather than a theatrical spectacle.

Historical descriptions indicate that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The accused was first examined to ensure no hidden weights had been concealed.
* Men were checked by a barber and women by the town midwife.
* The individual was weighed publicly on the official town scales while wearing a simple linen garment.
* The weighmaster worked alongside civic officials rather than acting alone.
* If the person's weight matched normal bodily proportions, an official certificate declared that the weighing showed nothing abnormal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl/geschiedenis-van-oudewater/heksenjacht/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl">[geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geschiedkundige vereniging Oudewater...</span></span></span>

</div>

The certificate did not declare that magic was impossible. Instead, it stated that the person's weight corresponded appropriately to their body. In the legal and cultural context of the time, this could undermine accusations based on the idea that witches possessed unnaturally light bodies. Surviving examples describe the body's weight as being "in proportion to its build", illustrating how administrative language was used to counter superstition with standardised measurement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk/richel/r5748-print-of-oudewater-and-city-seal-and-notes-on-the-weighing-of-witches/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk">[museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">print of Oudewater and city seal; and notes on the weighing of witches - Museum of Witchcraft and Magic...</span></span></span>

## Courts, Fear and Supposed Physical Proof

The Oudewater weighing test demonstrates an important distinction between evidence and belief. The scales themselves had no scientific ability to identify witches. Their significance came from the authority attached to trusted civic measurement.

Across Europe, witch trials often relied on supposed physical signs, confessions obtained under torture, rumours and theological assumptions rather than reliable evidence. In many jurisdictions, accusations became almost impossible to escape once formal proceedings had begun. Against that background, Oudewater offered something unusual: a recognised administrative process that could interrupt the momentum of suspicion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heksenwaag.nl/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heksenwaag.nl">[Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heksenwaag.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterHome - Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater &#124; The weigh house...</span></span></span>

Historical records indicate that people travelled from regions including parts of present-day Germany to obtain certificates from Oudewater because local authorities elsewhere sometimes accepted them as proof that an accusation lacked foundation. Although relatively few documented weighings survive, the town acquired a lasting reputation as a place where accused individuals received a fair hearing instead of a predetermined outcome. Local tradition maintains that nobody was condemned as a witch through the Oudewater weigh house.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl/geschiedenis-van-oudewater/heksenjacht/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl">[geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geschiedkundigeverenigingoudewater.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geschiedkundige vereniging Oudewater...</span></span></span>

The episode therefore illustrates a broader historical lesson. Administrative credibility—accurate weights, recognised officials and authenticated documents—could sometimes carry greater authority than popular fear.

## What Today's Visitor Certificate Really Means

Modern visitors can still stand on the original scales dating from 1482 and receive a personalised "Certificate of Weighing". Unlike its historical predecessor, today's document is an educational souvenir rather than a legal declaration. It commemorates the town's reputation for fair measurement while encouraging visitors to reflect on how easily societies can mistake prejudice for evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heksenwaag.nl/en/about-the-weigh-house/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heksenwaag.nl">[Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heksenwaag.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater About the weigh house</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterAbout the weigh house - Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2021</span></span></span>

Museum De Heksenwaag deliberately frames the weighing within the wider history of European witch persecutions instead of presenting it as proof that witches existed. Recent exhibitions connect historical witch hunts with modern forms of exclusion, discrimination and public shaming, encouraging visitors to consider how communities continue to label certain people as outsiders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heksenwaag.nl/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heksenwaag.nl">[Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heksenwaag.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum De Heksenwaag OudewaterHome - Museum De Heksenwaag Oudewater &#124; The weigh house...</span></span></span>

The weighing ceremony remains enjoyable, but its deeper purpose is historical interpretation. The experience succeeds because it combines a memorable interactive tradition with a careful explanation of why such accusations arose and why trusted institutions matter when fear threatens justice.

## Why the Witch Weighing Test Matters

Within the Netherlands' landscape of unusual museums and historical curiosities, the Oudewater weighing test stands apart because the remarkable object is not simply the ancient scale itself but the civic idea it represents.

Many collections about witchcraft focus on folklore, magical objects or dramatic trials. Museum De Heksenwaag instead centres on a practical mechanism of municipal government: standardised weighing. That ordinary administrative tool became an unexpected safeguard against extraordinary accusations.

For modern visitors, the lesson is less about witches than about evidence, authority and critical thinking. The original scales remind us that societies have long searched for physical proof of invisible fears.     });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
