## Introduction

[Georgia]({{ 'where-georgia-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }}) offers one of Europe's most striking examples of how museums can tell radically different stories about the same past. Within little more than an hour's journey, visitors can move from the grand Stalin Museum in Gori, which preserves thousands of authentic objects linked to Joseph Stalin's life, to Tbilisi's Museum of Soviet Occupation, which centres the experiences of political prisoners, executions and national repression. Between them sits the Underground Printing House, where the revolutionary movement that helped produce the Soviet state is remembered before its later transformation into dictatorship. Together, these institutions are not simply collections of historical objects. They are competing interpretations of memory, identity and responsibility, making them among Georgia's most unusual and politically revealing museums.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stalinmuseumi.ge/eng/museum/description/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stalinmuseumi.ge">[stalinmuseumi.ge]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stalinmuseumi.ge</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stalini Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stalini Museum</span></span></span>

## Why Stalin's museums are unlike ordinary historical museums

Many museums preserve controversial figures, but the Stalin collections in Georgia are unusual because they display genuine historical artefacts while existing inside an unresolved national argument about how those artefacts should be interpreted.

Joseph Stalin was born in Gori, making him both Georgia's most internationally famous native son and the ruler responsible for terror, mass repression and famine across the Soviet Union. For some visitors, the museum represents local history and an extraordinary political career. For others, it remains an uncomfortable relic of Soviet personality cults.

Unlike many former communist countries that dismantled similar memorials, Georgia retained the museum after independence. Rather than closing it, successive governments have debated whether it should remain a memorial, become a museum of totalitarianism or continue largely as it was. That unresolved status has made the museum itself an exhibit in the politics of memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stalinmuseumi.ge/eng/museum/description/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stalinmuseumi.ge">[stalinmuseumi.ge]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stalinmuseumi.ge</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stalini Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stalini Museum</span></span></span>

## The shrine-like displays of the Gori museum

The Stalin Museum in Gori opened in 1957, only four years after Stalin's death, and many parts of the complex still reflect Soviet commemorative design rather than modern historical interpretation. Its monumental architecture, marble interiors and ceremonial approach were intended to celebrate the leader rather than critically examine his rule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stalinmuseumi.ge/eng/museum/description/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stalinmuseumi.ge">[stalinmuseumi.ge]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stalinmuseumi.ge</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stalini Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stalini Museum</span></span></span>

The complex consists of three principal elements:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Stalin's preserved birthplace, enclosed within an elaborate protective pavilion.
* The main exhibition building, containing photographs, gifts, paintings, documents and personal possessions.
* Stalin's heavily armoured railway carriage, used for travel to wartime conferences because he reportedly disliked flying.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stalinmuseumi.ge/eng/museum/description/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stalinmuseumi.ge">[stalinmuseumi.ge]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stalinmuseumi.ge</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stalini Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stalini Museum</span></span></span>

</div>

Visitors encounter displays tracing Stalin's childhood, revolutionary years, rise to power and wartime leadership. For decades, these exhibitions largely omitted or minimised the Great Terror, forced collectivisation and other atrocities associated with his rule. Following criticism, the museum added a separate room devoted to political repression in 2010, but many observers argue that this section remains physically and emotionally disconnected from the museum's overwhelmingly commemorative presentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stalinmuseum.ge/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stalinmuseum.ge">[stalinmuseum.ge]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stalinmuseum.ge</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unlimited Stalin Museum: State Stalin Museum in Gori</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unlimited Stalin Museum: State Stalin Museum in Gori</span></span></span>

This imbalance is precisely what makes the museum unusual. The artefacts themselves are authentic and historically significant, yet the surrounding interpretation has long been criticised as preserving elements of Soviet hero worship rather than encouraging critical reflection.

## The underground press and revolutionary mythology

Tbilisi's Underground Printing House tells a very different story, although it also revolves around Stalin's early political career.

Hidden roughly 15 metres beneath an ordinary residential property, the clandestine press operated between 1903 and 1906. Revolutionaries secretly printed illegal newspapers, pamphlets and political literature in several languages, distributing them across the Russian Empire while evading the Tsarist police. The printing machinery was assembled underground after being smuggled into the city piece by piece, and ingenious warning systems and concealed passages helped protect those involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://georgia.to/en/places-to-go/tbilisi/underground-printing-house-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: georgia.to">[Georgia Travel &amp; Tours]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">georgia.to</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Georgia Travel &amp; ToursStalin&#x27;s Underground Printing House: A Hidden Revolutionary Base in Tbilisi...</span></span></span>

The reconstructed museum preserves:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the concealed underground chamber;
* the original-style printing equipment;
* secret entrances and escape routes;
* photographs, correspondence and revolutionary documents.

</div>

Unlike the Gori museum, the Underground Printing House focuses on Stalin before he became Soviet ruler. Here he appears as one revolutionary among many operating within Georgia's underground socialist movement. The museum therefore preserves revolutionary mythology rather than the later cult of leadership, allowing visitors to understand how illegal political activism functioned under the Russian Empire without requiring admiration for Stalin's later dictatorship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://georgia.to/en/places-to-go/tbilisi/underground-printing-house-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: georgia.to">[Georgia Travel &amp; Tours]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">georgia.to</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Georgia Travel &amp; ToursStalin&#x27;s Underground Printing House: A Hidden Revolutionary Base in Tbilisi...</span></span></span>

The atmosphere itself contributes to its curiosity value. Descending through hidden passages into a concealed industrial workspace gives visitors a tangible sense of the secrecy that shaped revolutionary politics long before the Soviet Union existed.

## How the occupation museum restores the victims' perspective

If Gori largely preserves the Soviet-era memorial landscape, the Museum of Soviet Occupation in Tbilisi deliberately reverses its perspective.

Opened in 2006 as part of the Georgian National Museum, the exhibition presents Soviet rule as an occupation rather than a period of national progress. Its chronological narrative follows Georgia from the loss of independence in 1921 through decades of repression, ending with the 1989 crackdown on demonstrators and the restoration of independence in 1991.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Soviet_Occupation_%28Tbilisi%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museum of Soviet Occupation (Tbilisi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museum of Soviet Occupation (Tbilisi</span></span></span>

Instead of celebrating political leaders, the museum foregrounds victims through:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* execution orders and exile documents;
* prison artefacts;
* secret police files;
* photographs of political prisoners;
* material relating to anti-Soviet resistance movements.

</div>

One particularly powerful curatorial device contrasts objects connected with Stalin's own imprisonment before the revolution with evidence from the prison system created under his later rule, encouraging visitors to reflect on how revolutionary ideals evolved into state repression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de/exhibitions/georgia/sites-of-memory-sites-of-contestation-the-tbilisi-museum-of-soviet-occupation?type=9999" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de">[cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-jena.de.</span></span></span>

The museum openly frames Soviet history as a story of lost sovereignty and human suffering. That approach has attracted both praise and criticism, with supporters arguing it restores neglected experiences while others view it as reflecting present-day political priorities as much as historical interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de/exhibitions/georgia/sites-of-memory-sites-of-contestation-the-tbilisi-museum-of-soviet-occupation?type=9999" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de">[cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-jena.de.</span></span></span>

## One country, three incompatible narratives

Seen together, these three museums reveal how public collections can become active participants in historical debate rather than neutral repositories.

The Gori museum largely preserves the material culture of Stalin's personality cult. The Underground Printing House reconstructs the idealistic and conspiratorial world of early revolutionaries. The Museum of Soviet Occupation focuses on the consequences of Soviet power for ordinary Georgians.

The same historical figure therefore appears in three incompatible roles:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a celebrated national-born world leader;
* a young underground revolutionary;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://georgia.to/en/places-to-go/tbilisi/underground-printing-house-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: georgia.to">[georgia.to]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">georgia.to</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Georgia Travel &amp; ToursStalin&#x27;s Underground Printing House: A Hidden Revolutionary Base in Tbilisi...</span></span></span>
* the architect of a system that imprisoned and killed countless people.

</div>

Rather than resolving these contradictions, Georgia has largely preserved them in separate institutions, allowing visitors to encounter competing historical narratives through museums themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stalinmuseumi.ge/eng/museum/description/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stalinmuseumi.ge">[stalinmuseumi.ge]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stalinmuseumi.ge</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stalini Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stalini Museum</span></span></span>

## Why these museums matter in Georgia's curiosity landscape

Among Georgia's unusual collections, the Stalin-related museums stand apart because their greatest curiosity lies not in bizarre artefacts but in conflicting interpretation.

The birthplace, railway carriage, clandestine printing press and prison records are all authentic historical objects. What makes them remarkable is the radically different meanings assigned to them depending on the institution displaying them.

For travellers interested in strange museums, these sites demonstrate that curiosity can arise from contested memory as much as from mysterious relics or unusual collections. They show how museums do more than preserve history: they shape public understanding of it. In Georgia, the battle over Stalin's legacy is still visible not only in politics and public debate but also in the galleries, labels and silences of its museums.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de/exhibitions/georgia/sites-of-memory-sites-of-contestation-the-tbilisi-museum-of-soviet-occupation?type=9999" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de">[uni-jena.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-jena.de.</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
