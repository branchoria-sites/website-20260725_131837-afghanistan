## Introduction

The most striking sight at [Ghana]({{ 'where-ghanas-strangest-objects-still/' | relative_url }})'s Nkyinkyim Museum is not an ancient artefact or a reconstructed slave dungeon, but a vast and growing landscape of individually sculpted clay faces. Known as the **Ancestor Project**, this open-air installation transforms remembrance into a physical environment rather than a conventional exhibition. Thousands of heads emerge from earth and water, each standing for an African life disrupted or destroyed through enslavement, colonial violence or forced displacement. Rather than claiming to recreate identifiable historical individuals, the sculptures operate as symbolic ancestors, inviting reflection on historical trauma, continuity and healing. This approach makes Nkyinkyim one of Ghana's most unusual cultural destinations: a place where contemporary art, memorial practice, education and ritual coexist in the same landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span></span></span>

## Why thousands of clay faces rise at Nkyinkyim

The installation was created by Ghanaian artist **Kwame Akoto-Bamfo**, who began sculpting memorial heads in 2009 before establishing the museum and its non-profit Ancestor Project. What began as a handful of sculptures has grown into an evolving field containing more than 3,500 clay heads, with a long-term ambition of reaching 11,111. The project is deliberately unfinished, reinforcing the idea that remembrance is an ongoing responsibility rather than a completed monument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span></span></span>

Every face is modelled individually. Some wear blindfolds, shackles or collars. Others bear marks of royalty, age or suffering. Hairstyles, expressions and physical features vary widely so that the installation avoids reducing millions of enslaved Africans to anonymous statistics. Instead, visitors encounter an imagined community of distinct human beings whose individuality was erased by the slave trade but restored symbolically through sculpture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/mar/03/people-cry-get-angry-remembering-the-enslaved-in-ghanas-remarkable-sculpture-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Established by artist Kwame Akoto-Bamfo in 2019, the museum aims to eventually showcase 11,111 heads, each representing an individual anc...</span></span></span>

The museum describes this work as an archive of African history and heritage rather than a conventional sculpture garden. Tours are often led by trained cultural interpreters, known as griots, who explain the historical, philosophical and symbolic meanings embedded within the installation and wider museum landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span></span></span>

## The Ancestor Project and its expanding field of heads

The clay heads are only one part of the Ancestor Project, but they are its emotional centre. The museum presents them as tools for restorative education and remembrance rather than decorative artworks. Educational programmes, workshops and commemorative events encourage visitors to think about how the transatlantic slave trade continues to shape identities, memory and conversations across Africa and the wider African diaspora.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span></span></span>

The installation has itself followed a symbolic journey. Before finding its permanent home near Ada, groups of sculptures were exhibited at significant historical locations including the Kwame Nkrumah Mausoleum, [Ussher Fort]({{ 'ussher-fort/' | relative_url }}) and Cape Coast Castle, places already associated with colonial rule and the history of enslavement. Some related sculptures were later installed at the National Memorial for Peace and Justice and the Legacy Museum in Montgomery, Alabama, linking Ghanaian remembrance with memorial landscapes in the United States.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span></span></span>

Because the project continues to expand, repeat visitors may encounter new sculptures, altered arrangements and additional educational spaces. The museum therefore functions as a living memorial rather than a static collection.

## How symbolism differs from archaeological evidence

One of the most important distinctions for visitors is that these heads are **not** archaeological reconstructions or portraits based on identified historical victims. They are contemporary memorial sculptures inspired by historical research, oral traditions and artistic interpretation. Their authority lies in symbolism and emotional truth rather than forensic identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span></span></span>

This difference matters because the installation can easily be mistaken for an excavation site or a reconstruction of discovered remains. Instead:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the sculptures represent collective ancestral memory rather than excavated individuals;
* facial expressions and physical details are artistic interpretations, not historical documentation;
* the installation seeks to communicate the scale and humanity of the slave trade through repetition, diversity and setting rather than through original artefacts.

</div>

Understanding this distinction helps explain why the work belongs equally to contemporary art, public history and memorial culture. It complements, rather than replaces, archaeological evidence or documentary collections relating to slavery.

## What visitors experience across the museum grounds

The emotional effect of the installation comes partly from its setting. The heads are distributed through open ground, among trees and around water, creating changing perspectives as visitors walk through the landscape. Some appear partially submerged, while others emerge from the earth in dense clusters, encouraging slow movement rather than quick viewing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/mar/03/people-cry-get-angry-remembering-the-enslaved-in-ghanas-remarkable-sculpture-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Established by artist Kwame Akoto-Bamfo in 2019, the museum aims to eventually showcase 11,111 heads, each representing an individual anc...</span></span></span>

The wider museum combines sculpture with traditional architecture, performance, storytelling, drumming and educational programmes. Museum staff describe the site as a place where tangible heritage, such as artworks, and intangible heritage, including ceremony and oral tradition, are experienced together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nkyinkyimmuseum.org.</span></span></span>

Certain areas are treated as sacred rather than simply photographic attractions. During ancestor-veneration events, visitors are encouraged to observe respectful behaviour, with guidance covering dress, movement and conduct inside ceremonial spaces. This reinforces that the installation functions simultaneously as an artwork, a memorial and, for many participants, a place of spiritual reflection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/celebrating-our-ancestors-nkyinkyim-museums-2024-ancestor-veneration-ceremony/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">celebrating our ancestors nkyinkyim museums 2024 ancestor veneration ceremony</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim MuseumCelebrating Our Ancestors: Nkyinkyim Museum’s 2024 Ancestor Veneration Ceremony – Nkyinkyim Museum...</span></span></span>

## Why the memorial stands apart in Ghana's curiosity landscape

Many unusual museums preserve rare objects from the past. Nkyinkyim reverses that pattern by creating new objects to remember people whose physical remains and personal histories were often erased. The clay heads are therefore unusual not because they are mysterious artefacts, but because they deliberately fill a historical absence.

Within Ghana's broader landscape of museums, forts and memorial sites connected to the Atlantic slave trade, the installation offers something distinct. Rather than focusing on surviving chains, documents or architecture, it visualises the missing millions through contemporary sculpture. That makes the field of clay ancestors one of the country's most memorable examples of how art can become both a public collection and a living memorial, encouraging visitors to move beyond historical facts towards reflection on memory, dignity and ancestral continuity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nkyinkyimmuseum.org/about-us/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nkyinkyimmuseum.org">[Nkyinkyim Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nkyinkyimmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nkyinkyim Museum About Us – Nkyinkyim Museum</span></span></span>
