## Introduction

Jeju Stone Park is one of [South Korea]({{ 'what-makes-south-koreas-museums-so/' | relative_url }})’s most distinctive heritage attractions because it treats volcanic rock not simply as geology, but as the foundation of an entire culture. Rather than presenting stones as scientific specimens alone, the park combines landscape, folklore, architecture, archaeology and oral tradition to explain how the island’s volcanic environment shaped everyday life and local identity. At its centre is the creation story of the giant grandmother Seolmundae Halmang, whose mythical actions are said to have formed Jeju itself. Visitors therefore encounter a place that functions simultaneously as an open-air museum, a cultural landscape and a carefully curated interpretation of how myth and physical geography became inseparable on Korea’s largest volcanic island.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CONT_000000000500547" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitjeju.net">[visitjeju.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitjeju.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit JejuVisit JejuMay 30, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 30, 2016</span></span></span>

## How Did Jeju's Stones Become Guardians?

Unlike many museums devoted to folklore, Jeju Stone Park begins with the island itself. Jeju was created by volcanic activity, leaving an environment dominated by black basalt, lava fields and scattered volcanic cones. For centuries, these abundant stones became the islanders' principal building material because timber was comparatively scarce and powerful coastal winds demanded durable construction. The park presents stone not as a decorative feature but as the material from which homes, walls, shrines, monuments and protective figures emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/iggp/jeju-island-unesco-global-geopark?hub=67817" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jeju Island UNESCO Global Geopark</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jeju Island UNESCO Global Geopark - International Geoscience and Geoparks Programme...</span></span></span>

The museum's guiding narrative revolves around Seolmundae Halmang, the legendary giant grandmother believed in Jeju tradition to have created the island and its landscape. According to the best-known versions of the story, she also became linked with the Five Hundred Generals, a tragic cycle of myths that explains unusual rock formations and gives emotional meaning to the island's volcanic terrain. The park adopts these stories as an interpretive framework rather than presenting them as historical fact, allowing visitors to understand how oral tradition helped people explain an extraordinary landscape long before geological science described volcanic island formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=76736" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english.visitkorea.or.kr">[or.kr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english.visitkorea.or.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Imagine Your KoreaJeju Stone Park (제주돌문화공원)- VISITKOREA...</span></span></span>

This approach makes the park unusual within South Korea's museum landscape. Instead of separating natural history from folklore, it deliberately places mythology alongside geology, encouraging visitors to see how each has influenced the island's identity.

## Volcanic Geology as Museum Material

Most geological museums display minerals inside cases. Jeju Stone Park instead turns an entire volcanic landscape into part of the exhibition.

The park occupies a large area of Gotjawal forest, an ecosystem where lava flows created a rugged mixture of exposed rock, woodland and underground water channels. Rather than extensively reshaping the site, planners integrated paths, galleries and outdoor displays into this existing terrain. Around seventy per cent of the park remains natural forest, reinforcing the idea that the surrounding landscape is itself an exhibit rather than merely a setting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=76736" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english.visitkorea.or.kr">[VISITKOREA - Imagine Your Korea]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english.visitkorea.or.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Imagine Your KoreaJeju Stone Park (제주돌문화공원)- VISITKOREA...</span></span></span>

## Guardian Figures, Creation Stories and Island Homes

Perhaps the best-known objects associated with Jeju are the island's carved basalt guardian statues, commonly recognised by their rounded hats and calm expressions. These figures, often called grandfather statues in English-language tourism material, have become symbols of Jeju itself.

The park brings together examples of many stone figures from across the island instead of leaving them scattered in their original locations. Seen together, they reveal considerable variation in carving style, size and purpose. Some historically marked village entrances or official sites, while others acquired reputations as protective guardians against misfortune or harmful spirits. Their precise historical origins remain debated, but their cultural importance is unquestioned, and the park presents them within the broader story of Jeju's stone-working traditions rather than as isolated curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CONT_000000000500547" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitjeju.net">[Visit Jeju]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitjeju.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit JejuVisit JejuMay 30, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 30, 2016</span></span></span>

Traditional Jeju houses reconstructed within the grounds demonstrate another practical consequence of volcanic geology. Basalt walls enclosed homes, fields and livestock while allowing the island's powerful winds to pass through gaps between stones instead of toppling the structures. What might appear decorative to outsiders was largely an ingenious environmental adaptation developed over generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://m.visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CNTS_200000000012527&amp;menuId=DOM_000001832000000000" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: m.visitjeju.net">[Visit Jeju]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">m.visitjeju.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitjeju.net.</span></span></span>

Throughout these displays, the recurring message is that Jeju's mythology emerged from lived experience. The stories do not merely decorate the landscape; they explain why particular rocks, mountains and stone formations mattered to communities whose daily survival depended upon them.

## Heritage Interpretation Versus Tourist Mythology

Jeju Stone Park occupies an interesting position between heritage museum and visitor attraction.

Its dramatic sculptures, forest trails and monumental installations can easily be appreciated as a scenic destination, and tourism agencies actively promote the site as one of Jeju's signature attractions. At the same time, the park has earned repeated recognition through South Korea's public museum evaluation system and was developed explicitly to preserve Jeju's stone culture rather than simply entertain visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CONT_000000000500547" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitjeju.net">[Visit Jeju]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitjeju.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit JejuVisit JejuMay 30, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 30, 2016</span></span></span>

The exhibitions also distinguish between folklore and scientific explanation. Geological displays explain the volcanic formation of Jeju using modern earth science, while mythological galleries preserve oral traditions in their own right. Rather than asking visitors to choose between myth and geology, the museum demonstrates that both are valuable: one explains physical processes, the other explains how generations of islanders understood their place within that landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://m.visitjeju.net/en/detail/view?contentsid=CONT_000000000500547&amp;menuId=DOM_000001817000000004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: m.visitjeju.net">[Visit Jeju]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">m.visitjeju.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitjeju.net.</span></span></span>

This balanced presentation avoids treating local legends as either literal history or mere fantasy. Instead, they become cultural evidence—records of how communities interpreted an extraordinary volcanic environment long before formal geological knowledge existed.

## Why It Matters Within South Korea's Curiosity Culture

Among South Korea's unusual heritage sites, Jeju Stone Park stands apart because its central collection is not simply a group of rare artefacts but an entire relationship between people and landscape. The basalt statues, reconstructed homes, field walls and mythological galleries all illustrate how a volcanic island generated distinctive forms of architecture, storytelling and identity.

For readers interested in strange museums and unusual collections, the park demonstrates that curiosity does not always come from bizarre objects.       try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
