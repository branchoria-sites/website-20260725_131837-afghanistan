## Introduction

Dark tourism asks people to visit places associated with death, injustice or disaster, but there is an important difference between remembering tragedy and turning it into entertainment. Few American places illustrate that tension more clearly than Salem, Massachusetts. Every year, millions of visitors arrive expecting witches, ghosts and Halloween festivities, yet the events that made Salem famous were not supernatural. In 1692 and 1693, a judicial panic led to the execution of 20 innocent people and the imprisonment of many more. Today, museums, memorials, theatrical attractions and commercial experiences coexist within the same small city, forcing visitors to decide where education ends and spectacle begins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/salem-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicEverything you need to know about the Salem witch trials &#124; National GeographicOctober 7, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2024</span></span></span>

Salem therefore offers a useful case study for understanding how tragic history becomes themed tourism. It demonstrates that commercial success does not automatically trivialise the past, but it also shows how easily real suffering can be overshadowed by branding, costume and entertainment. The most responsible visitor experiences are those that clearly distinguish documented history from folklore, modern witchcraft, ghost stories and fictional retellings.

## Salem's museums, memorials and staged attractions

Salem's visitor landscape is unusually varied. Within a short walk, travellers can encounter solemn memorials, scholarly museums, theatrical presentations, ghost tours, escape-room style attractions and Halloween-themed shops. The city openly embraces its identity as "Witch City", particularly during October, while also preserving sites directly connected with the 1692 trials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.salem.org/things-to-do/museums-and-attractions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salem.org">[Destination Salem]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salem.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Destination Salem Visit Historical Museums and Attractions in Salem, MA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Destination Salem Visit Historical Museums and Attractions in Salem, MA</span></span></span>

This mixture means not every attraction serves the same purpose.

* **The Salem Witch Trials Memorial** was designed as a place of remembrance rather than spectacle. Granite benches commemorate each victim by name, execution date and method of death, while quotations taken directly from court records are deliberately interrupted to symbolise voices that were ignored. The memorial's designers intended visitors to reflect on injustice rather than supernatural folklore. It was dedicated in 1992 by Elie Wiesel during the tricentenary commemorations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salemwitchmuseum.com/locations/witch-trials-memorial/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salemwitchmuseum.com">[Salem Witch Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salemwitchmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salem Witch Museum Salem Witch Trial Memorial &#124; Historic Documents &amp; Location</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salem Witch Museum Salem Witch Trial Memorial &#124; Historic Documents &amp; Location</span></span></span>
* **Historic interpretation** is supported by surviving court records, original documents and nearby historic locations, allowing visitors to connect individual victims with documented evidence rather than legend. Massachusetts has also invested in preserving and digitising many of the original trial papers, emphasising their value as legal and historical records rather than curiosities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us/salem-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us">[digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ma.us.</span></span></span>
* **Commercial attractions**, by contrast, often combine historical themes with theatrical storytelling, dramatic lighting, actors or horror aesthetics. Many openly advertise themselves as entertainment rather than academic history, reflecting the city's broader tourism economy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.salem.org/things-to-do/museums-and-attractions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salem.org">[Destination Salem]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salem.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Destination Salem Visit Historical Museums and Attractions in Salem, MA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Destination Salem Visit Historical Museums and Attractions in Salem, MA</span></span></span>

The result is not simply a choice between "good" and "bad" museums. Instead, Salem contains multiple ways of engaging with the same history, ranging from reflective remembrance to family entertainment.

## Authentic records versus theatrical interpretation

One reason Salem attracts debate is that its history is unusually well documented. Hundreds of surviving depositions, warrants, examinations and court papers allow historians to reconstruct events with considerable confidence. Unlike many ghost stories or local legends, the suffering of the accused is supported by extensive archival evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us/salem-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us">[digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ma.us.</span></span></span>

That evidence also corrects many popular misconceptions.

The people executed in 1692 were not practitioners of modern witchcraft. They were ordinary colonists accused under a legal system that accepted unreliable testimony, including so-called spectral evidence. The victims included both women and men, and the accusations reflected social tensions, fear, religious conflict and local politics rather than genuine magical practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/salem-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicEverything you need to know about the Salem witch trials &#124; National GeographicOctober 7, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2024</span></span></span>

Commercial storytelling sometimes compresses these complexities into a simpler narrative of witches versus persecutors because it is easier to dramatise. Fiction has reinforced this tendency. Arthur Miller's *The Crucible* transformed the trials into a broader allegory about political persecution, while Halloween culture has further merged Salem with imagery of broomsticks, pointed hats and occult symbolism that bears little resemblance to the historical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salemwitchmuseum.com/locations/witch-trials-memorial/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salemwitchmuseum.com">[Salem Witch Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salemwitchmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salem Witch Museum Salem Witch Trial Memorial &#124; Historic Documents &amp; Location</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salem Witch Museum Salem Witch Trial Memorial &#124; Historic Documents &amp; Location</span></span></span>

Modern Salem also supports active Pagan and contemporary witch communities, but these are modern religious movements rather than direct continuations of the people accused in 1692. Confusing the two risks obscuring the fact that those executed consistently denied practising witchcraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/salem-witch-trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicEverything you need to know about the Salem witch trials &#124; National GeographicOctober 7, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2024</span></span></span>

## How visitors can judge responsible presentation

The same city can contain both excellent historical interpretation and highly commercial attractions. Visitors can usually judge the difference by asking a few practical questions.

**Does the attraction identify its evidence?** Responsible museums explain where information comes from, refer to court records, archaeological evidence or contemporary documents, and distinguish between documented facts and later folklore.

**Are individual victims treated as people rather than props?** Memorials and serious exhibitions usually name the accused, explain their lives and acknowledge the legal failures that led to their deaths instead of presenting executions merely as dramatic episodes.

**Are folklore and history clearly separated?** Ghost tours and supernatural experiences can be enjoyable forms of storytelling when they openly acknowledge that they are presenting legend or entertainment rather than established historical fact.

**Does the presentation encourage reflection?** Sites such as the Salem Witch Trials Memorial use architecture, inscriptions and silence to encourage visitors to consider intolerance, fear and miscarriages of justice instead of simply consuming tragedy as spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salemwitchmuseum.com/locations/witch-trials-memorial/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salemwitchmuseum.com">[Salem Witch Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salemwitchmuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salem Witch Museum Salem Witch Trial Memorial &#124; Historic Documents &amp; Location</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salem Witch Museum Salem Witch Trial Memorial &#124; Historic Documents &amp; Location</span></span></span>

## Why the debate matters beyond Salem


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Salem has become one of America's best-known examples of dark tourism because it demonstrates both the opportunities and risks of interpreting painful history. Tourism has helped preserve historic sites, fund museums and keep the stories of the victims in public memory. Without sustained public interest, many archival collections and commemorative projects might have received far less attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/c61fc1d8aadae74dca07edb57a233a00" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These 527 documents, preserved for over four decades at the museum, need specific environmental conditions to ensure their continued cons...</span></span></span>

</div>

At the same time, commercial success can reshape collective memory. Costumed attractions, novelty merchandise and Halloween branding are often what first attract visitors, even though the historical events involved wrongful imprisonment, fear-driven prosecutions and judicial killings. The challenge is therefore not whether tourism should exist, but whether entertainment overwhelms remembrance.

Salem's strongest historical sites show that dark tourism can remain respectful when it keeps authentic evidence at the centre, acknowledges the humanity of the victims and makes clear where history ends and modern mythmaking begins. In doing so, they transform a famous tourist destination into a place that also encourages visitors to think critically about justice, mass panic and the consequences of believing accusation without evidence.
