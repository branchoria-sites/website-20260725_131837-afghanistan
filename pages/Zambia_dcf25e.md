## Introduction

Zambia does not have a large network of dedicated “weird museums” in the style of European cabinets of curiosities or paranormal attractions. These collections are unusual because they preserve things that are difficult to classify — objects that may be scientific evidence, religious material, cultural memory or reminders of beliefs that remain debated today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nmb.co.zm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nmb.co.zm">[NMB Bank]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nmb.co.zm</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NMB Bank National Museums Board</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NMB Bank National Museums Board</span></span></span>

## The Lusaka National Museum’s Witchcraft Collection

### Why this exhibit attracts unusual attention

The most famous strange collection in Zambia is the witchcraft section of the Lusaka National Museum. Unlike many museum displays that separate “science” from “belief”, this exhibition explores a part of social life where spiritual ideas, accusations, healing traditions and fears about supernatural harm have historically existed alongside Christianity and modern institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zm.chm-cbd.net/lusaka-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zm.chm-cbd.net">[zm.chm-cbd.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zm.chm-cbd.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span></span></span>

For visitors, the fascination lies less in whether magic works and more in what the objects reveal about how communities have explained misfortune, illness, conflict and personal relationships.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/lusaka-national-museum-s-witchcraft-exhibit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Lusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in Lusaka</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas Obscura Lusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in Lusaka</span></span></span>

The museum acts as a cultural archive of ideas about power, danger and protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zm.chm-cbd.net/lusaka-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zm.chm-cbd.net">[zm.chm-cbd.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zm.chm-cbd.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span></span></span>

The unusual character of the exhibit comes from the fact that beliefs surrounding witchcraft have not disappeared from Zambian society. ## Archaeological Curiosities: Zambia’s Deep Human Past

Zambia’s unusual collections are not limited to folklore. Some of the country’s most remarkable museum objects come from archaeology, where ordinary-looking fragments reveal extraordinary stories about human origins and ancient technology.

The Livingstone Museum holds major archaeological collections covering the Stone Age, Iron Age and later history. Among its notable material are finds connected with prehistoric Zambia, including evidence from Broken Hill (Kabwe), one of Africa’s most important early human fossil sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://livingstonemuseum.org/research-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livingstonemuseum.org">[livingstonemuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livingstonemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research &amp; Collections &#124; Livingstone Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research &amp; Collections &#124; Livingstone Museum</span></span></span>

The museum’s archaeology galleries present tools, pottery, early metalworking and evidence of changing human lifestyles over thousands of years. Objects that might appear simple — stone tools, fragments of pottery or pieces of worked copper — become clues in a much larger story about migration, trade and technological change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://livingstonemuseum.org/archaeology-gallery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livingstonemuseum.org">[livingstonemuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livingstonemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Gallery &#124; Livingstone Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Gallery &#124; Livingstone Museum</span></span></span>

This gives Zambia a different kind of “strange collection”: not objects claimed to be magical, but ancient items that force visitors to reconsider how long humans have lived, adapted and created societies in the region.

## Livingstone Museum: Relics, Archives and Colonial-Era Curiosities

The Livingstone Museum is also unusual because it combines several different types of collection under one roof. Established in 1934, it is Zambia’s oldest and largest museum, with holdings in archaeology, ethnography, history and natural history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://livingstonemuseum.org/temporary-exhibits/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livingstonemuseum.org">[livingstonemuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livingstonemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Temporary Exhibits &#124; Livingstone Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Temporary Exhibits &#124; Livingstone Museum</span></span></span>

One of its distinctive features is its material connected with Scottish missionary and explorer David Livingstone. The collection includes letters, documents and personal objects associated with his travels in the region. For some visitors these items are fascinating historical relics; for others they raise questions about colonial collecting practices and whose stories museums choose to emphasise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://livingstonemuseum.org/research-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livingstonemuseum.org">[livingstonemuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livingstonemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research &amp; Collections &#124; Livingstone Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research &amp; Collections &#124; Livingstone Museum</span></span></span>

The museum therefore belongs within Zambia’s curiosity landscape not because it contains “mysteries” in the supernatural sense, but because it preserves objects surrounded by complicated historical meanings. A notebook, a garment or an expedition record can become an object of curiosity when it represents exploration, empire, religion and changing interpretations of the past.

## Regional Collections with Unusual Cultural Stories

### Moto Moto Museum and northern traditions

The Moto Moto Museum in northern Zambia preserves archaeological, ethnographic and historical collections connected with communities of the Northern Province. Its origins trace back to collections made by Catholic missionary Jean Jacques Corbeil in the 1940s before becoming a national museum in 1974.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nmb.co.zm/moto-moto-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nmb.co.zm">[NMB Bank]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nmb.co.zm</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NMB Bank Moto-moto museum – National Museums Board</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NMB Bank Moto-moto museum – National Museums Board</span></span></span>

Its interest for curiosity-focused visitors lies in the way everyday objects become cultural evidence. Tools, crafts, household objects and historical materials show how communities organised work, ritual, social relationships and adaptation to changing conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nmb.co.zm/moto-moto-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nmb.co.zm">[NMB Bank]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nmb.co.zm</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NMB Bank Moto-moto museum – National Museums Board</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NMB Bank Moto-moto museum – National Museums Board</span></span></span>

Rather than displaying “oddities” removed from context, Moto Moto provides a reminder that unusual collections often emerge from ordinary objects carefully preserved over time.

## What Makes Zambia’s Strange Collections Different?

Zambia’s curiosity heritage is distinctive because it sits between several worlds:

* **Folklore and belief:** The Lusaka witchcraft exhibit documents ideas about magic, protection and supernatural explanations without requiring visitors to accept those beliefs as fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/lusaka-national-museum-s-witchcraft-exhibit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura Lusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in Lusaka</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas Obscura Lusaka National Museum&#x27;s Witchcraft Exhibit in Lusaka</span></span></span>

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Archaeological mystery:** Fossils, tools and ancient settlements reveal deep human histories that are scientifically investigated but still full of unanswered questions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://livingstonemuseum.org/archaeology-gallery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livingstonemuseum.org">[livingstonemuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livingstonemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Gallery &#124; Livingstone Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Gallery &#124; Livingstone Museum</span></span></span>
* **Colonial and missionary archives:** Historical objects connected with explorers and administrators preserve both important records and difficult questions about collecting and representation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://livingstonemuseum.org/research-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livingstonemuseum.org">[livingstonemuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livingstonemuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research &amp; Collections &#124; Livingstone Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research &amp; Collections &#124; Livingstone Museum</span></span></span>
* **Living cultural memory:** Regional museums preserve traditions that continue to influence identity and community life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nmb.co.zm/moto-moto-museum/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nmb.co.zm">[NMB Bank]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nmb.co.zm</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NMB Bank Moto-moto museum – National Museums Board</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NMB Bank Moto-moto museum – National Museums Board</span></span></span>

</div>

The result is a different kind of strange museum culture from countries famous for paranormal attractions or novelty museums. Zambia’s unusual collections are compelling because they show how societies preserve uncertainty: beliefs people debate, objects whose meanings change, and evidence of lives separated from the present by thousands of years.

## Visiting Zambia’s Unusual Collections

For travellers interested in offbeat heritage, the strongest starting points are the Lusaka National Museum and Livingstone Museum. Lusaka offers the country’s most direct encounter with folklore and witchcraft-related material, while Livingstone provides a wider journey through archaeology, natural history and historical archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zm.chm-cbd.net/lusaka-national-museum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zm.chm-cbd.net">[zm.chm-cbd.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zm.chm-cbd.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lusaka National Museum &#124; Zambia Biodiversity</span></span></span>

Visitors should approach these collections as cultural interpretation rather than sensational entertainment. The most interesting questions are often not “is this object magical?” but “why did people believe this?”, “who preserved it?”, and “what does it reveal about the society that created or used it?”

That approach places Zambia firmly within the wider world of unusual collections: a country where curiosity comes not from imaginary creatures, but from the real human fascination with belief, memory and the unexplained parts of history.
