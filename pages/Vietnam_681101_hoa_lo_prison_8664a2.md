## Introduction

Hoa Lo Prison in Hanoi is one of [Vietnam]({{ 'vietnams-strange-museums-and-hidden/' | relative_url }})’s most unsettling heritage sites because it preserves the physical remains of a prison built by French colonial authorities to control political opposition. Today, the Hoa Lo Prison Relic is not simply a museum of objects; it is a preserved fragment of a colonial detention system where walls, cells, shackles and reconstructed spaces form part of the historical evidence. Built in 1896 as the French Maison Centrale, the prison later became associated with Vietnamese revolutionary prisoners and, during the Vietnam War, with American prisoners of war who called it the “Hanoi Hilton”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/290/the-process-of-the-prison-establishment.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The process of the prison establishment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relicJune 30, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2015</span></span></span>

For visitors interested in unusual museums and difficult histories, Hoa Lo is distinctive because the “collection” is the place itself. Rather than presenting distant artefacts behind glass, it asks visitors to move through spaces designed for confinement and to consider how the same building acquired different meanings under colonial rule, wartime captivity and modern remembrance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/289/permanent-exhibitions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Permanent exhibitions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relic...</span></span></span>

## The prison building and preserved spaces

Hoa Lo’s unusual power comes from its architecture. The surviving sections are remnants of a much larger prison complex constructed by the French in Hanoi during the period of French Indochina. The site was built on the former Phu Khanh village area, replacing local buildings and craft spaces with a colonial institution designed for imprisonment and surveillance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/290/the-process-of-the-prison-establishment.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The process of the prison establishment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relicJune 30, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2015</span></span></span>

Unlike a conventional museum where historical objects are separated from their original environment, Hoa Lo uses the prison structure as an exhibit. Visitors encounter recreated detention rooms, prisoner clothing, everyday items, punishment areas and displays explaining the lives of those held there. The surviving architecture gives these objects their meaning: a metal shackle or narrow cell is understood not as an isolated artefact but as part of a system of control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/289/permanent-exhibitions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Permanent exhibitions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relic...</span></span></span>

### Why the site feels different from an ordinary museum

The unsettling quality of Hoa Lo comes from the relationship between place and memory. The visitor is not only learning about imprisonment; they are standing inside a space originally intended to make prisoners feel powerless. The remaining corridors and rooms create a direct connection between historical evidence and physical experience.

The prison’s survival is also unusual because much of the original complex no longer exists. Large parts of Hoa Lo were demolished during Hanoi’s urban redevelopment, leaving a preserved section that functions as a historical relic within a modern city. This partial survival makes the site both a museum and a reminder of how urban change can erase difficult places from public memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/289/permanent-exhibitions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Permanent exhibitions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relic...</span></span></span>

## Colonial imprisonment and wartime stories

The first major chapter of Hoa Lo’s history is its role under French colonial rule. The prison was intended especially for Vietnamese political prisoners and independence activists. Exhibitions at the site describe overcrowding, harsh discipline, forced labour and punishment practices used against prisoners during the colonial period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/291/imprisoned-regulations-political-prison.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Imprisoned Regulations Political Prison</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relic...</span></span></span>

For Vietnamese visitors, Hoa Lo is strongly connected with memories of anti-colonial resistance. Many prisoners held there were involved in nationalist or revolutionary movements, and the site presents the prison as a place where political activists continued organising, studying and maintaining solidarity despite imprisonment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/292/the-patrotic-revolutionary-school.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The patrotic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">revolutionary school - Hoa Lo Prison relicJune 30, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2015</span></span></span>

The prison’s history later changed direction after the end of French colonial rule. During the Vietnam War era, part of the complex was used to hold captured American pilots and other prisoners of war between 1964 and 1973. This period created the internationally recognised nickname “Hanoi Hilton”, a name used by American prisoners that became part of the prison’s global identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/289/permanent-exhibitions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Permanent exhibitions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relic...</span></span></span>

One of the most internationally known prisoners associated with Hoa Lo was US Navy pilot John McCain, who was captured in 1967 after his aircraft was shot down over Hanoi. His imprisonment later became a major part of his public biography in the United States.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/sailor-statesman-symbol-reflecting-john-mccain-and-vietnam-war" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: americanhistory.si.edu">[National Museum of American History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">americanhistory.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Museum of American HistorySailor, statesman, symbol: reflecting on John McCain and the Vietnam War &#124; National Museum of American...</span></span></span> The site therefore attracts visitors with very different historical connections: some come to understand Vietnamese anti-colonial struggles, while others focus on American prisoner-of-war experiences.

## Different memories of the same site

Hoa Lo Prison is fascinating because it demonstrates how a single historical location can hold competing memories. The prison is not interpreted in exactly the same way by everyone who encounters it. Vietnamese official presentations emphasise colonial oppression, resistance and sacrifice, while many American accounts focus on the experiences of prisoners of war during the Vietnam War.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hoalo.vn/EN/Articles/49/289/permanent-exhibitions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hoalo.vn">[hoalo.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hoalo.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Permanent exhibitions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoa Lo Prison relic...</span></span></span>

This does not make the site unusual only because of disagreement. Many historic prisons around the world carry layered memories, and former sites of imprisonment often become places where nations decide how to remember suffering, resistance and conflict. Hoa Lo’s importance lies partly in this tension: it shows how heritage sites are not neutral containers of history but carefully curated interpretations of the past.

For visitors, the most valuable approach is to recognise that Hoa Lo preserves several historical periods at once. The colonial prison, the revolutionary memory site and the wartime detention centre are connected, but they are not identical stories. Understanding the different perspectives makes the visit more meaningful than viewing the site only through one national narrative.

## Why Hoa Lo matters in Vietnam’s unusual heritage landscape


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Within Vietnam’s wider landscape of unusual collections and historical attractions, Hoa Lo stands apart because it turns a place of confinement into a public archive of memory. Its importance does not come from mysterious objects or unexplained claims, but from the uncomfortable preservation of a real site where political power, colonial rule and wartime conflict left physical traces.

</div>

The prison shows why unusual heritage is often found in places that were never designed to become museums. A cell, a wall marking, a prisoner’s possession or a surviving section of architecture can become a historical collection when society decides that the past must be confronted rather than forgotten.
