## Introduction

The Ramón Elías Mythological Museum in Capiatá is [Paraguay]({{ 'where-paraguay-keeps-its-strangest/' | relative_url }})'s most distinctive public collection devoted to folklore. Rather than treating legendary beings as abstract stories, it gives them physical form through life-sized sculptures, allowing visitors to encounter creatures that for generations existed mainly in oral tradition, family storytelling and popular literature. The museum is unusual because it is neither a conventional ethnographic institution nor a theme park. It is the life's work of artist, collector and restorer Ramón Elías, whose aim was to preserve Paraguay's mythological heritage by making it visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agendarte.cultura.gov.py/evento/museo-mitologico-ramon-elias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agendarte.cultura.gov.py">[cultura.gov.py]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agendarte.cultura.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cultura.gov.py.</span></span></span>

Within Paraguay's landscape of unusual museums, the collection occupies a unique position. It bridges folklore, art, historical collecting and cultural memory, presenting supernatural traditions alongside colonial religious art and artefacts connected with the country's major wars. The result is a museum that reflects how mythology sits within the wider story of Paraguayan identity rather than existing as isolated fantasy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agendarte.cultura.gov.py/evento/museo-mitologico-ramon-elias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agendarte.cultura.gov.py">[Agendarte]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agendarte.cultura.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cultura.gov.py.</span></span></span>

## How Ramón Elías Turned Oral Tradition into a Museum

Ramón Elías (1929–1981) was an artist, teacher, restorer and enthusiastic collector rather than a university folklorist. During years of travelling around Paraguay, he collected antiques, religious objects and historical material while also listening to local accounts of supernatural beings. His ambition was to create a place where visitors could encounter the creatures that many Paraguayans had previously known only from stories told by parents, grandparents and rural communities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

The museum opened in 1979 in Capiatá. Following Elías's death in 1981, his family continued operating the institution, preserving both the collection and his original vision. Official museum directories still identify it as a privately managed museum, illustrating how much of Paraguay's folklore preservation has depended upon individual initiative rather than large state institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agendarte.cultura.gov.py/evento/museo-mitologico-ramon-elias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agendarte.cultura.gov.py">[cultura.gov.py]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agendarte.cultura.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cultura.gov.py.</span></span></span>

What makes the museum historically significant is not that it claims to prove the existence of mythical beings. Instead, it established visual conventions that have become familiar to many Paraguayans. Before Elías created his sculptures, several famous mythological figures lacked widely recognised physical representations. His artistic interpretations helped fix their appearance in the public imagination while remaining explicitly artistic reconstructions rather than historical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

## The Monsters Visitors Meet

The museum's principal gallery contains human-sized sculptures representing the best-known figures from Paraguayan mythology. Rather than presenting isolated monsters, the displays recreate scenes from interconnected legends rooted in Guaraní tradition and later Paraguayan folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

Among the best-known figures are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Pombero**, the elusive forest spirit associated with mischief, rural landscapes and unexplained events. In popular belief he rewards respect but punishes carelessness, making him one of Paraguay's most enduring supernatural figures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>
* **Jasy Jateré**, often portrayed as an unusually fair-haired child who wanders the countryside and lures children away. His stories combine enchantment with cautionary lessons about wandering alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>
* **Kurupí**, remembered for his exaggerated physical features and role as a dangerous symbol of uncontrolled sexuality within traditional stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>
* **Ao Ao**, a fearsome beast said to hunt humans in the forest, representing one of the darker creatures in Guaraní mythology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>
* Other legendary beings, including Moñái, Luisón, Teju Jagua, Mbói Tu'ĩ, Mbói Jagua, Mala Visión, Jagua Ru, Kuarahy Ra'y and treasures linked with the legend of buried colonial wealth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

</div>

The displays are theatrical rather than clinical. Dim lighting, carefully arranged scenery and life-sized figures encourage visitors to experience the atmosphere of the stories instead of simply reading about them. That immersive presentation distinguishes the museum from a conventional folklore gallery, while still keeping its focus on cultural heritage rather than horror entertainment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

## The Seven Monstrous Children of Taú and Keraná

Many of the museum's sculptures are connected through one of Paraguay's central mythological narratives: the story of Taú and Keraná.

According to the traditional account, Taú embodies destructive and malevolent forces. After defeating the protective spirit Angatupyry, he abducts the beautiful Keraná. Their union produces seven monstrous children, each representing different dangers, behaviours or natural forces. This family of supernatural beings forms the backbone of much Paraguayan mythological tradition and explains why creatures that appear unrelated are displayed together within the museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.portalguarani.com/detalles_museos_otras_obras.php?id=27&amp;id_obras=2400&amp;id_otras=369" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portalguarani.com">[Guaraní Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portalguarani.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guaraní PortalPortal Guaraní - TAU Y KERANA - Escultura del MUSEO MITOLÓGICO RAMÓN ELÍAS - Compilación de Mitos y Leyendas del Paraguay...</span></span></span>

Rather than presenting these figures as cryptids awaiting scientific discovery, the museum places them within a living cultural tradition. Their importance lies in storytelling, moral teaching, regional identity and the preservation of Guaraní-influenced folklore. The sculptures therefore visualise a narrative system rather than documenting alleged encounters with unknown animals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

## More Than a Monster Museum

Although its mythology gallery attracts most attention, the museum deliberately expands beyond supernatural folklore.

Additional rooms contain:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* colonial-era Franciscan and Jesuit religious art and altarpieces;
* historical objects connected with the Paraguayan War and the Chaco War;
* local historical material collected by Ramón Elías during decades of research and restoration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agendarte.cultura.gov.py/evento/museo-mitologico-ramon-elias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agendarte.cultura.gov.py">[cultura.gov.py]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agendarte.cultura.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cultura.gov.py.</span></span></span>

</div>

This combination reflects Elías's understanding of national identity. Rather than separating religion, warfare, folklore and local history into different institutions, he treated them as interconnected expressions of Paraguayan cultural memory. The museum therefore resembles a traditional collector's cabinet, where apparently unrelated subjects coexist because they formed part of one person's lifelong effort to preserve the country's heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agendarte.cultura.gov.py/evento/museo-mitologico-ramon-elias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agendarte.cultura.gov.py">[Agendarte]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agendarte.cultura.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cultura.gov.py.</span></span></span>

## What Visitors Can Expect

The museum is located beside Route PY02 in Capiatá, a short journey east of Asunción. It remains a privately managed attraction and is generally visited through guided or semi-guided tours rather than large-scale museum programming. Official tourism information continues to list public admission, while museum directories note that advance arrangements may sometimes be advisable because opening hours can vary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visitparaguay.travel/places/museo-mitologico-ramon-elias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitparaguay.travel">[visitparaguay.travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitparaguay.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visit Paraguay Museo Mitológico Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit Paraguay Museo Mitológico Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

Visitors should expect:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an intimate rather than expansive museum;
* life-sized sculptural scenes rather than digital or interactive exhibits;
* displays devoted equally to mythology, colonial religious heritage and Paraguayan history;
* a collection shaped by one collector's artistic interpretation rather than an academic ethnographic exhibition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agendarte.cultura.gov.py/evento/museo-mitologico-ramon-elias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agendarte.cultura.gov.py">[cultura.gov.py]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agendarte.cultura.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cultura.gov.py.</span></span></span>

Its relatively modest scale is part of its appeal. The museum feels personal, reflecting the vision of a single creator whose work has influenced how generations of Paraguayans imagine their legendary beings.

## Why the Museum Matters

Many countries possess folklore museums that explain legends through text panels and manuscripts. The Ramón Elías Museum instead transformed intangible tradition into physical encounters. That achievement has made it one of Paraguay's most recognisable cultural curiosities and arguably its clearest visitor-facing institution devoted entirely to mythological heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical_Museum_Ram%C3%B3n_El%C3%ADas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mythical Museum Ramón Elías</span></span></span>

Its importance lies less in proving supernatural beliefs than in preserving stories that continue to shape Paraguayan culture. By giving Pombero, Jasy Jateré and the descendants of Taú and Keraná recognisable faces and bodies, Ramón Elías created a visual language that complements oral tradition rather than replacing it. For visitors interested in Paraguay's unusual museums, the collection demonstrates how folklore can become a museum experience without losing its roots in storytelling, imagination and cultural identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.portalguarani.com/967_ramon_elias__/22679_mitos_y_leyendas_del_paraguay_1978__por_ramon_elias.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portalguarani.com">[portalguarani.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portalguarani.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guaraní PortalPortal Guaraní - MITOS Y LEYENDAS DEL PARAGUAY, 1978 - Por RAMÓN ELÍAS - Ramón Elías (+)...</span></span></span>

      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }

      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
